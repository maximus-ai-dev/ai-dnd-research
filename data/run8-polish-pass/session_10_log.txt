**SESSION 10: THE DROWNED CHOKEPOINT**

**REST & RESUPPLY**

The three days following Mercer's sacrifice pass in a blur of preparation and grim determination. Cora, Garrick, and the Grimhold engineers work tirelessly in the fortress above the Drowned Chokepoint, reinforcing the temporal stasis field's power conduits, installing monitoring equipment, and preparing for what comes next. The air hums with the low-frequency vibration of the stasis field—a constant reminder that their friend is frozen in time below, holding back the Sleeper's advance.

Garrick spends hours at the Cold Forge, hammering replacement armor plates and sharpening weapons until his hands ache. Cora pores over the Twisted Overseer's captured schematics, cross-referencing them with the Military Empire's containment data, her workspace littered with half-finished dampener prototypes and chemical equations. The metallic sweetness of Dreamstone radiation has become a permanent scent in the fortress, clinging to clothes and lingering in the back of the throat.

On the morning of the fourth day, Reyna Steelforge arrives from Grimhold with the promised reinforcement materials: crates of dwarven-grade iron ingots, barrels of alchemical reagents, and a team of six engineers who stare in awe at the scale of the ancient architecture. She finds Cora and Garrick in the transit hub, studying the minecart network map.

"The pumps are running," Reyna says without preamble, her practical tone cutting through the heavy air. "The lower Dwarven Layer is draining. And as the water recedes, the Twisted's secrets are being exposed."

She unrolls a schematic on the stone table—the Pump Station Operations Manual, annotated with her own observations. "There's a critical location called the Drowned Chokepoint. Every drainage pipe, every pump circuit, every overflow channel in the Dwarven Layer converges at a single junction point directly above the Giant Capstone. It's been underwater for centuries. Now it's accessible."

Her finger taps a diagram of a massive domed cavern. "The Twisted have been using it as a staging area. Protected by the water from above and the capstone from below. They've built something there. A mining mech, modified with their bioengineering. They call it the Drowned Juggernaut."

She looks from Cora to Garrick, her expression grave. "It's designed to drill through the Giant Capstone. To breach the containment below. And according to the drainage timetables, it's nearly charged."

**THE DESCENT BEGINS**

The lower Dwarven Layer is a revelation. As the water has receded over the past week, corridors and chambers that have been submerged for centuries are now accessible—slick with mineral deposits, coated in bioluminescent algae that glows with an eerie blue-green light, and eerily preserved by the cold, mineral-rich water. The air is warm and heavy with moisture, every surface gleaming with residual water that drips from the ceiling in a steady, rhythmic patter.

Cora, Garrick, and the Grimhold engineers take the minecart network's deep mining route south from the transit hub. The rails are corroded but navigable at slow speed. The tunnel descends steeply—sixty feet below the forge level, then eighty, then a hundred. The metallic sweetness grows stronger with every foot of descent, becoming a physical pressure behind the eyes, a faint ringing in the ears that even Garrick, who has no arcane sensitivity, can feel.

As they descend, modifications to the dwarven tunnels become visible. Rails have been extended with crude but functional additions—new track sections made from salvaged dwarven iron, joined with organic black resin that pulses faintly. Side tunnels have been widened. Loading platforms have been added at irregular intervals. The Twisted have been building their own logistics network within the dwarven one.

Deeper still, biological material appears. Black resin coats the walls in thick patches, forming structures that look almost architectural—buttresses, channels, conduits that seem to breathe. In places, the resin has grown into the stone itself, fusing organic and mineral into a single, living infrastructure. Mercer would have identified these as extensions of the Twisted's bioengineering—living infrastructure that grows and maintains itself. His absence is a hollow space in the group.

The deep mining tunnel finally opens into the Drowned Chokepoint.

It is enormous: a domed cavern roughly one hundred feet across and fifty feet high, carved by the dwarves as the convergence point for their entire water management system. Massive pipes—ten feet in diameter—enter the cavern from every direction, some still dripping, others dry. The floor is a network of drainage channels radiating from a central elevated platform like the spokes of a wheel. The platform is fifteen feet above the channel floor, accessible by stone ramps.

The water has drained to knee level in the channels, but the central platform is dry. And on it, the Twisted have built their workshop.

Mechanical components stripped from other dwarven installations lie scattered—gears, pistons, hydraulic assemblies. Organic resin structures serve as workbenches, storage racks, and structural supports. Tools—dwarven tools modified with biological grips and extensions—are arranged with precise, unsettling order. Stone tablets etched with a script that combines Dwarvish engineering notation with something organic and angular are propped against the resin workbenches.

And at the center of the workshop, partially disassembled for modification, is the Drowned Juggernaut.

It is larger than Mercer's glimpse suggested. Eighteen feet tall, roughly humanoid, built from dwarven iron and stone in the shape of a mining automaton—a machine designed for boring through solid rock. Its arms end in rotating drill assemblies. Its torso is a furnace, cold now, designed to be powered by elemental fire or geothermal heat. Its legs are pistons and hydraulic rams, built for brute stability.

The Twisted have modified it extensively. Organic resin fills the gaps between iron plates, serving as both adhesive and conduit. Black fluid runs through transparent resin tubes like blood vessels, connecting the mechanical systems to a biological control node mounted where the automaton's head should be—a pulsing, iridescent mass of corrupted tissue that serves as the Juggernaut's brain.

The machine is not active. It is being charged—thick resin cables connect it to the organic infrastructure throughout the cavern, feeding it energy from the Anomaly's radiation below. The control node pulses with a slow, rhythmic light. Seventy percent charge, according to Reyna's estimates.

The drill assemblies are aimed at the floor. Specifically, at a section of the central platform that is different from the surrounding stone—darker, denser, inscribed with Giant-era runes that glow faintly even without magical detection. The Giant Capstone. The Juggernaut was designed to drill through it.

From your vantage at the tunnel entrance, you can see the workshop clearly. The Juggernaut dominates the space. The air thrums with latent power. The metallic sweetness is so strong it tastes like blood.
