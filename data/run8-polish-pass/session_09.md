# Session 9: The Drowned Chokepoint

*An entry from the chronicles of the Asymmetrical Mountain, recorded by an observer in the Grand Archive*

---

The restored transit network hummed with new life, but the map projected in the [[Eastern Transit Hub]] told a troubling story. A blue stain of flooding marked the northern branch to [[Pump Station East]], the station itself showing as offline. [[Cora Flint]] stood before the controls, her alchemist’s mind already calculating pressure differentials and geothermal forces. “If those pumps fail completely,” she stated with clinical precision, “we could get catastrophic backflow. This isn’t just water—it’s a building pressure.”

[[Garrick Kade]] hefted his maul, his rune-inscribed armor catching the forge-light. “Then we go north. Clear the line.” His pragmatism was a blunt instrument, but effective.

[[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] consulted his awakened spellbook, the spectral quill darting across pages as he cross-referenced transit logs with geological records. “Three months of silence coincides with a minor seismic event from the [[Giant foundation]],” he reported, his academic tone giving way to genuine concern. “And the logs mention ‘unusual water displacement patterns’ and ‘audible movement through submerged conduits.’ Something is down there. Something large.”

Their maintenance cart splashed into the rising water of the northern tunnel, the warm, mineral-rich flood climbing to their knees as they descended. The air grew thick with humidity and the scent of deep earth. They found [[Pump Station East]] partially submerged, its massive iron-bound doors slightly ajar. From within came a rhythmic **CLANG. CLANG. CLANG.**—metallic, precise, and ominous.

Garrick didn’t hesitate. With a roar, he activated his [[Giant’s Might]], swelling to Large size, and slammed his shoulder into the doors. They crashed open to reveal a chamber of dwarven engineering glory—and its systematic destruction.

Three massive pump assemblies stood dead in a triangle, each fifteen feet tall. [[Pump Station East]] had been murdered. Black organic resin fused valves shut, pipes were severed, control mechanisms lay scattered. And standing between the pumps was a [[Twisted Overseer]]—a corrupted dwarf with iridescent [[Dreamstone]]-sheened skin and black veins pulsing beneath. It wore heavy plate and held a warhammer that glowed with the same sickly energy.

It moved with shocking speed, its warhammer catching Garrick in a bone-jarring impact. From a flooded side passage, four [[Twisted Saboteurs]] rose, carrying tools instead of weapons—pry bars, stone chisels, coils of black resin. They ignored the fight, moving with single-minded purpose toward the pumps to continue the sabotage.

“They’re here to destroy the pumps, not fight us!” Cora shouted, already analyzing the resin. “This is intelligent sabotage!”

Mercer’s [[Manifest Mind]] hovered near the ceiling, giving him a bird’s-eye view. He cast [[Web]], entangling saboteurs near [[Pump One]], while Cora applied concentrated acid to dissolve the resin sealing its intake valve. Garrick traded brutal blows with the Overseer, his maul meeting corrupted plate in showers of sparks and black ichor.

But more saboteurs emerged from the water. The Overseer made sharp gestures, directing them with cold calculation. They flanked Garrick, not to attack, but to reach [[Pump Three]]’s scattered controls. From the flooded passage, a deep humming vibrated through the stone—something massive was rising.

“Garrick, fall back!” Cora called, applying a healing salve to his wounds. “We’ve secured one valve—strategic retreat!”

But Garrick saw an opening. As Mercer cast [[Hold Person]], paralyzing the Overseer, Garrick brought his maul down in a non-lethal strike that knocked the corrupted dwarf unconscious. “We’re taking it for interrogation,” he grunted, heaving the Overseer over his shoulder despite his injuries.

Their retreat became a desperate race. Mercer cast [[Wall of Force]] across the flooded passage as a fifteen-foot corrupted mining mech—a dwarven excavation machine covered in black resin—surfaced and slammed against the magical barrier. The cart splashed back up the tunnel, leaving the humming horror behind.

Back in the transit hub, they secured the unconscious Overseer with dwarven manacles. Mercer’s examination with [[Detect Magic]] revealed the corruption was integrated, not controlled remotely. “They’re not puppets,” he said, awe and dread mixing in his voice. “They’re transformed beings with retained knowledge and skills.”

When the Overseer’s eyes fluttered open, it spoke in rough, guttural Common. “My name… was [[Borin Stonehand]]. Master Engineer of the [[Deep Delvers]].” The words were pulled from centuries of silence. “We mined too deep. Broke through the Giant containment. Found… what they had sealed away. Not the [[Sleeper]]. Something older.”

The revelation unfolded like a geological fault shifting. The dwarves hadn’t accidentally flooded the lower levels—they’d done it deliberately. The water was a hydraulic lock, a prison for something that “sleeps in the stone.” The [[First World Sleeper]] came later, a parasitic consciousness that offered the dwarves transformation and survival in exchange for becoming jailers. They maintained the flooding to keep the older entity contained.

“The Sleeper… tests the containment,” Borin explained, urgency in his corrupted eyes. “Drills through the foundation. Amplifies its signal. Wants whatever is sealed to wake. To join with it.”

As if responding to his words, the mountain shuddered. Iridescent fluid—darker, thicker than water—began seeping from cracks in the tunnel wall, transforming stone on contact. The psychic voice from below grew louder, forming words that pressed directly into their minds: **COME. JOIN. BECOME WHOLE AGAIN.**

They raced to the [[Drowned Chokepoint]], following Borin through maintenance tunnels where entranced Twisted carved resonant patterns into the walls, turning the mountain itself into a psychic antenna. The Chokepoint was a cavern of staggering scale, its master control platform partially submerged, surrounded by fifty-foot crystalline amplifiers pulsing with iridescent light.

At the cavern’s far end, a hundred-foot section of wall bulged outward, stone grinding against stone as something pushed its way through. The entity was waking.

Cora found the emergency controls—red levers for reservoir pressure, blue wheels for lock reinforcement, and a black lever marked for permanent flooding. But there was a third option: silver controls for [[Temporal Containment]], a Giant-era stasis field that would slow time around the seal. The runes warned: **OPERATOR MUST REMAIN WITHIN FIELD TO MAINTAIN STABILITY.**

“I’ll stay,” Garrick said, his voice rough but resolved. “You’re the brains. You need to find a better solution.”

“No,” Mercer cut in, his academic composure shattering into something fiercer. “You lack the arcane training. This requires a chronomancer.” Before they could protest, he cast [[Wall of Force]] as a dome around the platform, sealing himself inside with the silver controls.

Through the transparent barrier, they watched as Mercer’s movements slowed to glacial precision. The bulging wall froze mid-collapse. Stone fragments hung motionless. The psychic voice stretched into silence. Time inside the dome moved at a fraction of normal speed—a day outside might be a minute within. Mercer would live a subjective eternity maintaining the stasis, buying them objective years to find a solution.

His final thought reached them, slowed but intelligible: **TELL THEM… I FOUND MY LIFE’S WORK. NOW FIND A BETTER SOLUTION.**

They returned to the transit hub in silence, transforming it into a fortified monitoring station. But as they prepared to return to [[Grimhold]], the control panel flashed a corrupted message from three months ago—from Borin himself, before his transformation was complete:

**…SLEEPER ACTIVITY DETECTED AT FOUNDATION LAYER… DRILLING OPERATIONS ACCELERATING… ESTIMATED TIME TO SEAL BREACH: 47 DAYS…**

Borin stared at his own warning. “The Sleeper knows about the Chokepoint. It will come for it. And it has… 47 days. From three months ago.”

The forge-light flickered. The mountain held its breath. They had contained one ancient threat with the sacrifice of time itself, but another countdown had already begun—and the [[Sleeper]]’s drills in the foundation layer continued their work, counting down to a breach that could shatter Mercer’s stasis and unleash something older than memory.

The temporary victory felt hollow. The real race was just beginning.
