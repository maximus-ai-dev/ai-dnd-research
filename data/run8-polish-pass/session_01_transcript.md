**DM:** The wind carries the smell of woodsmoke and cold earth as you crest the final rise. Below, nestled in the shadow of the Sentinel Mountains, lies Grimhold — a frontier town of maybe three hundred souls, where the last vestiges of civilization give way to the wild Starmetal Hills.

From this distance, you can see the mix of simple log buildings and crumbling stone walls overgrown with ivy — a town built atop something much older. Thin trails of smoke rise from chimneys into the slate-gray sky. The air bites with the promise of winter, and the muddy main road leading into town is rutted with wagon tracks. To the northwest, the Starmetal Hills roll away toward a distant, off-center peak that dominates the horizon — a mountain that doesn't sit right, as if dropped there by some careless hand rather than grown from the earth.

Each of you arrives separately, drawn by different needs but the same reality: this is where the work is.

**Cora Flint**, you've been traveling for days, your alchemist's supplies clinking in your pack and your ledger of debts growing heavier with each mile. The Second Wind Inn's sign creaks in the wind ahead — warmth, food, and hopefully someone willing to pay for your skills. You can already calculate the cost of a night's stay versus what you have left.

**Garrick Kade**, the weight of your chain mail feels familiar, but the memories it carries are not. Emberfell is an ocean away, but some things follow a man. The town guard at the gate gives you a long look — strangers with weapons aren't unusual here, but men with your bearing get noticed. You need coin, and you need to stay moving.

**Professor Thaddeus Mercer**, you adjust your spectacles and pull your journal from your pack. The geological anomaly of that mountain is already fascinating — the erosion patterns are all wrong. This could be the evidence you need for your paper on anomalous formations. But first, you need shelter and someone who might fund further study.

The sun hangs low in the afternoon sky as you enter Grimhold proper. The streets are muddy, the buildings sturdy but worn. People move with purpose — miners with picks over their shoulders, merchants loading wagons, a pair of armed guards watching the northern road with grim expressions. The air smells of woodsmoke, stew, and the sharp tang of cold metal from the nearby smithy.

At the center of town stands a single ancient stone marker, carved with worn runes nobody seems to understand. The Prospectors' Guild building nearby has a weathered bounty board nailed to its front wall. The largest notice reads in bold letters: **"50 GOLD BOUNTY — Bandits raiding from the Starmetal Hills. See Reyna Steelforge inside."**

The Second Wind Inn's door stands open, warmth and the sound of conversation spilling out into the cold street. Across the way, a sign depicting a griffon marks the Griffon's Hold — weapons and armor for sale. A small chapel with a simple hearth symbol over its door stands quietly nearby.

**What do you do?**

**CORA:** I pull my cloak tighter against the biting wind, my eyes scanning Grimhold with the clinical detachment of a cost-benefit analysis. The ledger in my pack feels heavier than my alchemist's supplies — 37 gold pieces left, and that's before tonight's lodging. I can already calculate the expenses: room at the Second Wind Inn, 5 silver. Meal, 2 silver. Supplies replenishment, maybe 10 silver if I'm frugal. That leaves 36 gold, 3 silver. Not enough. Not nearly enough.

My gaze goes immediately to the bounty board. **50 gold.** That's the number that matters. Bandits in the Starmetal Hills mean risk, but 50 gold means breathing room. Means I don't have to sell my tools. I approach the board, ignoring the other notices, my fingers tracing the bold letters. "Fifty gold," I mutter to myself, already calculating. "Split three ways if I find competent help, that's sixteen gold, six silver, six copper each. Minus expenses for healing salves, antitoxins, rations..." I pull out my ledger, flipping to a fresh page. "Let's see: basic field kit, 5 gold. Potion ingredients, 3 gold. That leaves eight gold profit. Acceptable."

I turn from the board, my eyes scanning for the two most likely candidates I noticed entering town. The armored man with the bearing of a soldier — Garrick, if I overheard correctly at the gate. He looks like he can take a hit, which reduces my medical supply expenditure. And the academic with the spectacles — Professor Mercer. Annoying, but educated types sometimes have useful knowledge about terrain and local flora. Both look like they need coin as badly as I do.

I head toward the Prospectors' Guild door, pausing to address them both with the efficiency of a business proposition. "You two look like you're calculating expenses versus income. "Standard split: expenses come out of the pot first, then equal shares. I keep a ledger. Anyone wastes supplies or takes unnecessary risks, that comes out of their share. **GARRICK:** I grunt, my eyes narrowing as I look Cora up and down. I need it, and bandits are simpler than whatever political mess I left behind.

**MERCER:** I adjust my spectacles, peering at the stone marker with academic intensity. "Fascinating! I trail off, realizing Cora has addressed me with some sort of mercenary proposition.

I turn from the marker, my journal still open. "Madam Flint, while I appreciate your... pragmatic approach to resource allocation, I must correct several assumptions. I pause, examining both of them more carefully. "Actually, bandit activity in the Starmetal Hills could be connected to my research. Disturbances in ancient sites often attract opportunistic elements. And I *do* need funding for proper excavation equipment." I make a note in my journal. "Very well. I accept your proposal with the following amendments: Any texts, tablets, or artifacts of historical significance are to be documented and preserved for proper study before any... financial considerations. I'll handle translation, identification of traps, and analysis of architectural features."

I glance at the bounty notice. "Twenty-three gold, approximately, after expenses? Adequate for initial supplies. But we should first investigate the Prospectors' Guild for local maps and geological surveys. The bandits' choice of location may reveal something about the area's subterranean structures."
