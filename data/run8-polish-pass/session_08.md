# Session 8: The Lightning Coil

*The following account was compiled from field notes, personal observations, and the recovered journal of Professor Thaddeus Mercer, chronicling the eighth expedition into the Asymmetrical Mountain.*

---

The warmth of the reignited [[Cold Forge]] had begun to seep into the surrounding stone, a slow but undeniable thawing of centuries-old cold. For three days, the rhythmic pulse of [[Ignathar]]'s elemental heart had echoed through the dwarven halls, and with that warmth came awakening. Not just of stone, but of purpose. The prototype [[Psychic Dampener]] had proven the [[Twisted]] could be freed, but to build more, to truly challenge [[The Sleeper]]'s dominion, the mountain's veins needed to flow again. And from the east, beyond the apprentice workshops, a rhythmic *clack-clack-clack* whispered of something already moving in the dark.

We stood at the threshold of a precisely engineered tunnel, twelve feet wide, its walls carved with geometric patterns that were functional, not decorative. Along the floor, twin lines of iron rails gleamed faintly in the torchlight, vanishing into the eastern darkness. Two rusted but intact minecarts sat at a loading platform. This was no mere passage—it was the arterial network of [[Kazad-Khrom]], the means by which ore had once flowed to the forges.

[[Cora Flint]] knelt immediately, her artificer’s hands tracing the rails. “Search protocol,” she declared, ledger already open. [[Garrick Kade]] grunted, his smith’s eye on the crane brackets, but his warrior’s attention fixed on the tunnel mouth and the source of the clattering. “That noise isn’t maintenance diagrams. That’s something moving down there.”

It fell to [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] to decipher the walls. His spectral quill flew as he translated the geometric patterns. “Maintenance diagrams, load-bearing calculations, safety protocols,” he announced with academic triumph. “And here—a maintenance alcove, fifty feet down on the left. We proceed systematically.”

He was, as ever, correct. The alcove held dwarven rail tools and, most importantly, a hand-crank maintenance cart. Further down the line, at a junction where three rails converged, we found a derailed ore cart and, wedged within its copper load, a preserved dwarven log.

Its final entry was written in a shaking hand: *“Day 47 of the Silence. Eastern hub sealed by order of [[Foreman Kellak]]. Apex predator confirmed—Kellashen, the lightning coil. No transit until beast is cleared. Request hunter team from [[Deepwatch]] garrison. No response from garrison for twelve days. May the Stone protect us.”*

The clattering ahead was not machinery. It was movement. And the ozone scent growing stronger was the breath of the predator that had ruled these tunnels for decades.

Mercer’s spectral mind drifted ahead to scout the Eastern Transit Hub. The vision it returned was of a vast, destroyed gallery—toppled cranes, overturned carts, and bones. And coiled among the wreckage, a serpentine form twenty-five feet long, with twelve powerful legs and a ridge of crystalline spines that pulsed with blue energy. [[Kellashen]] was a behir, an apex predator of lightning and constricting strength. It slept, but as the spectral mind observed, one vertical-slit eye flicked open. It knew we were there.

Back in the Junction Hall, we prepared a battlefield. Garrick broke a rusted turntable free with a groan of metal. Cora mixed alchemical pastes, creating a conductive path along a repaired section of rail, leading to the turntable as a grounding rod. “We channel its lightning into the rails,” she explained. Mercer, recalling his mind, warned of its intelligence and the five-second recharge of its devastating breath.

We had thirty seconds before it moved.

Initiative was a blur of motion and pain. The creature scaled the wall with terrifying grace, avoiding our trap. Mercer took to the air with a *Fly* spell. Garrick swelled with *Giant’s Might*, growing to Large size. Then Kellashen struck.

Its maw opened, and a twenty-foot line of pure lightning erupted. Mercer’s *Counterspell* fizzled uselessly against the natural ability. The bolt arced through him and down into Garrick. The thunder was deafening, the smell of ozone and scorched flesh overwhelming. Garrick took the full force, roaring in agony but staying on his feet through sheer defiance. Mercer, though wounded, remained aloft.

We were on the back foot from the first second. Garrick, bleeding and enraged, charged. Mercer, hovering at death’s door with but 9 hit points remaining, made a desperate, brilliant play. Instead of attacking the behir, he cast *Polymorph* on Garrick. The warrior’s form shimmered and expanded into a massive, roaring Giant Ape.

The battle became a brutal tug-of-war. The behir constricted and bit the ape, its dagger-teeth sinking deep. Garrick, in his simian form, would break free with thunderous strength, slam his fists into the creature’s skull, and try to drag it onto the conductive rails. Cora hurled acid and prepared the trap. Mercer, maintaining three spells through a haze of pain, used illusion and fire to manipulate the beast’s movements.

The climax was a masterpiece of applied theory. After a feigned collapse using *Minor Illusion*, the behir dropped from the wall to avoid “falling masonry.” Garrick seized it and, with a final heave, positioned it fully on the supercharged conductive section Cora had prepared. Mercer, in a stroke of inspired adaptation, used his Awakened Spellbook to cast *Shatter* as lightning damage, supercharging the copper ore on the rails.

Trapped and sensing doom, Kellashen did the only thing it could—it unleashed its lightning breath. The energy erupted from its maw… and immediately grounded through the prepared circuit. But the feedback was imperfect, violent. Lightning crackled back up through the behir’s own legs in a searing feedback loop. The creature convulsed as its own weapon turned against it, scales blackening, spines flaring with catastrophic light.

In that moment of stunned agony, we finished it. Cora’s acid splashed across its face. Garrick’s ape fists came down in a crushing, two-handed blow that drove its skull into the stone. Mercer’s *Fire Bolt* shattered its remaining spines. Kellashen, the lightning coil, apex predator of the eastern tunnels, thrashed once and was still.

The silence that followed was heavier than the thunder. We were victorious, but Garrick and Mercer hovered at the edge of consciousness, sustained only by Cora’s swift administration of healing potions.

With the hub secured, we explored. The control room held a stone transit map, which Mercer’s *Comprehend Languages* revealed in full. It showed our route from the forge, the eastern mining branch, a northern pump station critical to preventing flooding, and a southern branch marked: **“QUARANTINE — DO NOT ACCESS.”**

More chilling than the map were the fresh minecart tracks we found on the rails leading deeper east. They were recent, systematic, and as Mercer’s *Detect Magic* revealed, carried a faint, *controlled* psychic signature—nothing like the wild corruption of the [[Twisted]].

The final piece of the puzzle came from a crystal recording device, unlocked by [[Foreman Kellak]]’s seal we found. His holographic message was grim. The quarantine was for a “black growth” from the fungal farms—not a fungus, but a *consciousness* that spread through stone, air, and thought. The pumps were failing. And he warned, with desperate urgency, about the dreamstone deposits in the mines: *“It amplifies thoughts. It amplifies the growth.”*

We returned to [[Ignathar]] with the behir’s lightning gland. The elemental accepted the integration, and the forge’s heat intensified markedly, a stepping stone to the temperatures needed for adamantine. The minecart network was now operational, turning hours of travel into minutes.

But Mercer’s test run to the mining branch confirmed our deepest unease. The tracks were there, fresh. Something intelligent, something using controlled psychic energy, was actively using the network we had just cleared. It had been moving through the mountain even as we fought Kellashen.

The Eastern Transit Hub was ours. The forge burned hotter than it had in centuries. Yet, as we stood in the forge’s renewed glow, the mountain felt no safer. We had slain an apex predator only to uncover older, stranger threats: a thinking infection sealed in quarantine, failing infrastructure that could drown the deeps, and an unknown intelligence quietly running its own operations in the dark.

The veins of [[Kazad-Khrom]] were opening, but what else would flow through them?
