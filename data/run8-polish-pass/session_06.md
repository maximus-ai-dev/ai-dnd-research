# Session 6: The Breathing Stone

*The following account was compiled from field notes, personal journals, and survivor testimonies by the Chronicler of Grimhold. What follows is not merely an expedition log, but a record of the moment we learned the true nature of the mountain's sickness—and how close we came to becoming part of it.*

---

The air in the [[Prospectors' Guild]] carried the scent of damp wool and pipe smoke, but beneath it lingered something new: a faint, metallic sweetness that hadn't been there three days prior. [[Reyna Steelforge]] approached our table with grim purpose, dropping a leather pouch containing rock samples from the northern claims in the [[Starmetal Hills]]. The iridescent discoloration was unmistakable—identical to what we'd seen in the [[Military Empire]] corridors below.

"It's the same scent you described from the Sealed Crypt," she said, her voice low. "Something below is venting."

[[Cora Flint]] examined the samples with alchemical precision, confirming the match. "The containment breach is accelerating," she announced, already calculating supplies and timelines. [[Garrick Kade]] grunted his agreement, his thick fingers crushing the pouch as he examined the stones. "Tell your miners to stay clear," he warned Reyna. "I've seen what happens when people breathe in things they shouldn't."

But it was [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] who revealed the true horror. Cross-referencing the Giant Blueprint Tablet with Military Empire surveys, he identified the dwarven "sandwich" containment strategy: three layers of magically-reinforced granite interspersed with lead sheeting and quartzite baffles. "The dwarves built a *filter*," he explained, his lecturing tone taking on new gravity. "If the metallic sweetness is reaching the surface, it means the pressure from below has exceeded their design limits by several orders of magnitude."

*Atmospheric bleed.* The Military Empire's term for the earliest warning sign of containment failure. We were witnessing it firsthand.

## Descent into Resonance

At first light, we descended the Giant stairway into the [[Dwarven Layer]]. The transition from Military Empire masonry to older, heavier dwarven construction was visible in the stonework. Iron brackets the size of wagon wheels held rusted torch remnants. And everywhere—the metallic sweetness, growing stronger with each step.

The eastern corridor opened into residential chambers scaled for dwarves: low stone beds, communal hearths, a bathhouse with dry pipes. But the contamination had permeated these living spaces like a disease. Stone basins showed concentric rings of iridescence where water once stood. Sleeping alcoves were stained darkest where heads would have rested.

"Backflow," Cora identified, her investigation revealing fluid dynamics at work. "The pressure from below is forcing contamination back through the filter media."

Mercer's spectral mind drifted ahead, revealing something more disturbing. "The contamination has *bonded* with the quartzite baffles at a molecular level," he reported, his voice dropping to a whisper. "The dwarves built a sacrificial containment layer. We're not looking at a failing filter—we're looking at a filter that has become saturated and is now actively contributing to the contamination."

At the end of the eastern corridor, past a collapsed storage chamber and a dry well shaft, the party finds it: a door sealed with lead plates riveted directly into the stone frame. The lead is thick — half an inch — and covers every seam. This was not a hasty seal. Someone took great care to ensure nothing could pass through this door in either direction. The lead is cracked. Not broken — cracked, along stress fractures that follow the Dreamstone radiation pattern in the surrounding stone. The corruption has been working on this seal for centuries, and it is beginning to fail. Through the widest crack — barely a finger's width — a faint exhalation of air carries that metallic sweetness, concentrated enough to taste.

## The Echoes Awaken

Cora distributed lead-lined elixirs—temporary psychic dampeners she'd brewed through the night. "We have about an hour of partial resistance," she warned. But as we approached, the breathing pattern changed. Dwarvish words echoed through the crack, spoken in a flat, toneless loop: *"Seal the lower halls. The dreaming is too loud."*

The psychic dissonance hit us like a physical wave. For a terrifying moment, I felt the entire mountain pressing down, stone breathing around me, and a sickening certainty that none of this was real. We all staggered—Garrick shaking it off with warrior's discipline, Mercer clutching his head as if trying to hold reality together.

Mercer's spectral mind mapped the resonance patterns, revealing the truth: the lead hadn't failed from pressure, but from harmonic resonance. The entire containment system was vibrating at a frequency that matched the Dreamstone radiation. And those repeated words weren't just a warning—they were part of the containment protocol, encoded into a psychic imprint.

Then the echoes activated.

Inside, the mausoleum is a single chamber roughly thirty feet square and ten feet high. The walls are lined with stone niches, each containing a body. Dwarven dead, laid out in ceremonial armor, hands folded over weapons. There are fourteen of them. They are perfectly preserved. Not mummified — preserved, as if they died yesterday. Their skin has a faint iridescent sheen, the same blue-purple-green as the corridor stone. Their veins are visible beneath the skin, darkened to black. They are saturated with Dreamstone radiation.

## Harmonic Collapse

What followed was a desperate ballet of containment physics. Cora identified the captain as the resonance anchor. Mercer prepared a precisely tuned *Shatter* spell, one octave above the captain's frequency. Garrick charged through contaminated mist to seal a well shaft venting iridescent aerosol.

"Don't attack the captain!" Mercer shouted as Garrick raised his maul. "He's anchoring the resonance field! Strike him and you feed energy into the system!"

Mercer's analysis proved correct. The captain's ceremonial war-pick wasn't a weapon—it was a *tuning fork*, encoding the containment frequency. Mercer's *Dispel Magic* shattered its enchantment, collapsing the captain's resonance. The soldier echoes faltered, their movements becoming uncoordinated.

But the remaining harmonic nodes were vibrating dangerously. And from below came a new sound: a low, rhythmic *drilling*. Something was working its way up, following the structural seam in the Giant architecture.

## The Coalescence

The floor bulged upward, then cracked open. Not stone dust or mist poured forth, but a thick, black fluid that glistened with iridescent sheen. Where it touched the remaining echoes, they didn't dissolve—they *absorbed* it, their movements becoming smoother, more coordinated.

"That's not just contamination," Mercer realized, horror dawning in his voice. "It's *coalesced consciousness*! The contamination has reached sufficient density to form a pseudo-physical entity!"

The black fluid flowed and reformed, taking shape—not a humanoid form, but something of sharp angles and impossible geometries that hurt to look at. It turned toward us with a focus we could feel in our bones. It knew we were here. It knew we'd tried to contain it.

And it was hungry.

## Retreat and Revelation

While Garrick and the party hold off the echoes, Cora must seal the crack. This is a multi-step process: 1. Clear the debris around the crack to expose its full width (DC 12 Strength or Dexterity) 2. Apply a temporary sealant using materials from the mausoleum: melted lead from the door seal, mixed with stone dust (DC 14 Intelligence with tinker's tools or alchemist's supplies) 3. Compress the seal by activating a dwarven pressure clamp built into the chamber floor — a mechanism Cora identifies as purpose-built for exactly this situation, though centuries of disuse have seized it (DC 15 Strength from Garrick, or DC 13 if Cora applies lubricant from her kit first)

The party returns to the surface with their first Dreamstone fragment — a shard of crystallized radiation that Cora carefully extracted from the crack before sealing it, wrapped in lead sheeting from the door. It pulses with a faint inner light, cold to the touch, and Mercer can feel it humming at the edge of his perception.

When the seal is complete, the effect is immediate: the metallic sweetness cuts off. The radiation level in the chamber drops perceptibly. Any echoes still animated lose their energy source — they stumble, slow, and collapse within two rounds. The captain, if still standing, speaks one final phrase before falling: *"The dreaming stops. The dreaming stops."*

But the victory felt hollow. We'd witnessed the Sleeper's consciousness manifesting physically. We'd seen Dreamstone radiation coalesce into an entity that sought to incorporate reality into itself. And we'd learned the terrible truth: the dwarven containment had succeeded for centuries, but the pressure had finally exceeded material limits.

## Evidence and Aftermath

The mausoleum is sealed. The crack is plugged — not permanently, but well enough to halt the leak for years. Cora estimates the lead-and-stone seal will hold for at least a decade before the radiation begins to compromise it again. A permanent solution would require Giant-era containment materials, which the party does not have. Yet.

We emerged into clean mountain air that felt alien after the metallic sweetness below. [[Reyna Steelforge]] took one look at our haunted expressions and knew. "You found it," she said, not a question.

We presented our evidence. She listened, her face growing grimmer with each revelation. "Then the Commander was right," she said finally. "And we're out of time."

She paid us from salvageable dwarven goods, but her real payment was a new objective: "Reignite the Cold Forge. Make the dwarven layer operational again. Because if the Giants' containment is our last line of defense, we need the dwarven filter working in front of it."

As I write this, the crystallized Dreamstone fragment pulses faintly in its lead wrapping on my desk. It's cold to the touch, humming at the edge of perception. Not just a mineral sample. Not just evidence.

Mercer was right. It's a fragment of emergent consciousness. And it's waiting.
