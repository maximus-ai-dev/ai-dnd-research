She meets each of their eyes. "You're sure about this? Building a seal for something that's been contained for a hundred thousand years?"

The journey back to the mountain is familiar now: the winding trail through the Starmetal Hills, the odd geological formations that grow more pronounced as they approach, and finally, the Asymmetrical Mountain itself—a massive, off-center peak that doesn't match the surrounding geology. The entrance they secured weeks ago is still intact, the heavy stone door sealed with the Brotherhood's warding runes. Inside, the air is cool and still, carrying the faint mineral scent of deep stone and the distant, almost imperceptible hum of the containment field far below.

They descend through the layers they've fought to secure: past the Salt-Vaults where the Brotherhood wardens now maintain watch, through the Grand Archive with its silent guardians, down the Military Empire's grand stairwell, through the Dwarven Layer where Dreamstone still glows faintly in sealed chambers, past the Cold Forge where Ignathar's fire burns steady and hot. The deeper they go, the more the air changes—not in temperature, but in texture. It feels thinner, charged, as if reality itself is stretched taut.

At the capstone—the massive stone disk that seals the Caldera—they pause. The Reality Anchor is where they left it, humming with stabilized spatial energy. The ropes are coiled neatly beside it. Below their feet, through fifty feet of enchanted stone, the Sleeper dreams. The party is roped together, Dreamstone Circlets glowing softly on their brows, the reinforcement materials secured in waterproof packs. They've done this before. They know the procedure.

Garrick checks the anchor's calibration. Cora tests the circlet resonance. Mercer reviews the containment schematics one last time. Then they activate the anchor, and the capstone dissolves into a shimmering portal of golden light. The void of the Caldera opens below them—a darkness so absolute it seems to swallow light, punctuated by the distant golden glow of containment runes on the distant walls and the slow, crystalline orbits of the Deep Things.

They launch together, roped in formation, moving toward the sphere at the Caldera's center. The Dreamstone Circlets vibrate gently against their foreheads—a comforting pressure, a psychic shield against the Sleeper's broadcast. For the first fifty feet from the wall, everything is as they remember: weightless drift, the Deep Things observing but not interfering, the sphere growing larger ahead.

Then, at exactly fifty feet from the Caldera wall, the circlets begin to tremble violently—not a malfunction, but resonance overload. The vibration becomes a physical shudder that travels through their bones. Cora recognizes the pattern immediately: the shielding isn't failing; it's being overwhelmed. The broadcast at this distance is simply too strong.
