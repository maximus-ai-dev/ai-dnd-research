# Session 10: The Drowned Chokepoint

*The metallic sweetness of Dreamstone radiation had become a permanent scent in the fortress above the Drowned Chokepoint—a constant reminder of the sacrifice below. For three days, [[Cora Flint]] and [[Garrick Kade]] worked alongside the [[Grimhold]] engineers, reinforcing conduits and preparing for what came next. The air hummed with the low-frequency vibration of the temporal stasis field, a testament to [[Mercer]] holding back the [[Sleeper]]'s advance. On the fourth morning, [[Reyna Steelforge]] arrived with reinforcements and grim news: the pumps were running, the lower [[Dwarven Layer]] was draining, and as the water receded, the [[Twisted]]'s secrets were being exposed.*

*At a place called the [[Drowned Chokepoint]], every drainage pipe in the Dwarven Layer converged above the [[Giant Capstone]]. The Twisted had built something there—a mining mech modified with their bioengineering. They called it the [[Drowned Juggernaut]]. And according to the drainage timetables, it was nearly charged.*

---

The descent into the newly drained lower Dwarven Layer was a revelation. Centuries of submerged corridors now lay exposed, slick with mineral deposits and glowing with eerie bioluminescent algae. The air was warm and heavy with moisture, every surface gleaming with residual water that dripped in a steady, rhythmic patter. The metallic sweetness grew stronger with every foot of descent, becoming a physical pressure behind the eyes, a faint ringing in the ears.

As they descended via the minecart network, modifications to the dwarven tunnels became visible. Rails extended with crude additions, side tunnels widened, loading platforms added at irregular intervals. The Twisted had been building their own logistics network within the dwarven one. Deeper still, biological material appeared—black resin coating walls in thick patches, forming structures that looked almost architectural, fusing organic and mineral into living infrastructure.

The deep mining tunnel finally opened into the Drowned Chokepoint.

It was enormous: a domed cavern one hundred feet across, carved by the dwarves as the convergence point for their entire water management system. Massive pipes entered from every direction, some still dripping, others dry. The floor was a network of drainage channels radiating from a central elevated platform like the spokes of a wheel.

And on that platform, the Twisted had built their workshop.

Mechanical components stripped from other dwarven installations lay scattered among organic resin workbenches. Tools—dwarven tools modified with biological grips—were arranged with precise, unsettling order. Stone tablets etched with a script that combined Dwarvish engineering notation with something organic and angular were propped against the resin.

At the center, partially disassembled for modification, was the Drowned Juggernaut.

Eighteen feet tall, roughly humanoid, built from dwarven iron and stone in the shape of a mining automaton. Its arms ended in rotating drill assemblies. Its torso was a furnace, cold now. Its legs were pistons and hydraulic rams. The Twisted had modified it extensively—organic resin filled gaps between iron plates, black fluid ran through transparent resin tubes like blood vessels, and a pulsing, iridescent mass of corrupted tissue served as the machine's brain where its head should have been.

The machine was not active. It was being charged—thick resin cables connected it to the organic infrastructure throughout the cavern, feeding it energy from the Anomaly below. The control node pulsed with a slow, rhythmic light. Seventy percent charge, according to Reyna's estimates.

The drill assemblies were aimed at the floor—specifically, at a section of the central platform that was different from the surrounding stone. Darker, denser, inscribed with Giant-era runes that glowed faintly even without magical detection.

The Giant Capstone.

The Juggernaut was designed to drill through it.

From their vantage at the tunnel entrance, the party could see the workshop clearly. The air thrummed with latent power. The metallic sweetness was so strong it tasted like blood.

Then the machine noticed them.

---

The Drowned Juggernaut didn't hesitate. It didn't observe. It processed the party as rock to be drilled.

Hydraulic legs pistoned forward with shocking speed. Drill assemblies spun up to a deafening scream—a high-pitched metallic shriek that drowned out all other sound. Water sprayed from drainage channels as the machine plowed through them.

"Garrick, get down!" Cora shouted, but her foot caught on a loose stone. The spinning drill head caught her square in the chest. Ribs cracked audibly as she was thrown backward against the tunnel wall.

Garrick heard the warning too late. The drill clipped his shoulder, spinning him around like a ragdoll. He skidded across slick stone, armor scraping, pain exploding through his arm.

Professor Mercer—academic robes catching on protruding stone—stumbled as he tried to evade. The drill missed him by inches, but the concussive force sent him sprawling backward into the tunnel wall. The impact knocked the wind from his lungs.

All three were prone, battered, and facing a machine that showed no sign of stopping.

"Garrick, wait!" Mercer gasped, pushing himself up. "Don't just charge it—that's a modified dwarven mining automaton! The biological interface is vulnerable to psychic disruption!"

But Garrick was already moving. "Enough of this!" he roared, activating his Giant's Might. His body surged with power as he grew to Large size. He charged up the stone ramp, maul already swinging.

The Juggernaut's drill slammed into Cora again before she could catch her breath. Forty pounds of spinning metal and hydraulic force—more ribs gave way, blood filled her mouth. She was down to 17 hit points, and the damned thing was turning back to the capstone.

"Garrick!" she choked out, spitting blood. "The charging cables—six resin conduits! Sever them! Mercer, the drainage controls—flood this chamber if you have to!"

Garrick's maul connected with the machine's hydraulic leg joint. Iron plates buckled, black fluid sprayed from ruptured resin conduits. The machine staggered slightly, but the organic resin immediately began to knit itself back together.

Mercer reached the control panel, his fingers flying over levers in a sequence he understood from centuries of studying ancient engineering. The machinery groaned deep within the stone. Water in the drainage channels began to churn, flowing backward against its natural course. The level rose—from knee-deep to thigh-deep in seconds.

The Juggernaut noticed the water. Its biological control node pulsed faster, processing the new environmental variable. It didn't care about Garrick's attack, didn't care about Mercer at the controls. It turned back to the capstone.

**SCREEEEEEE—CRUNCH.**

Stone dust filled the air. The Giant runes flared with golden light as the drill bit into them. The groove in the capstone deepened—three feet became three feet, two inches. The containment was being eroded, millimeter by millimeter.

---

Cora dragged herself through chest-deep water toward the nearest resin conduit. Her alchemist's supplies were watertight, thank the gods. She pulled out a concentrated acid compound—Melf's Acid Arrow, modified for maximum corrosion. She needed to hit that biological control node where the cables connected.

Garrick took another drill slam to the side, necrotic corruption seeping through his chainmail like ice water. He grunted but didn't give ground. "You want to drill? Drill this!" he shouted, swinging his maul at the drill assembly itself. The impact showered sparks, the drill's scream dropping in pitch.

"The cables are feeding it energy from the Anomaly below!" Mercer shouted, his spectral mind floating near the Juggernaut's flank. "They're not just power conduits—they're regenerative systems!"

He wove his hands through complex somatic gestures. Arcane symbols flared to life in the air. The resin conduits began to glow brighter, then flicker erratically. The Juggernaut's drilling slowed for a moment as its control node pulsed with confused light. Mercer had created feedback in the energy transfer—the machine's healing was halved.

The water continued to rise. It was now waist-deep in the drainage channels, lapping at the edges of the central platform. The Juggernaut's hydraulic legs were partially submerged. The machine processed this, recalculating. It didn't retreat. It simply adjusted its stance and resumed drilling.

**SCREEEEE—CRUNCH.**

The drill bit deeper, but Garrick's damage to the assembly showed. The scream was less piercing, the progress slower. The groove deepened to three feet, four inches. The Giant runes flared brighter, fighting back. But they were losing.

Cora reached the conduit. The water was chest-deep and rising fast. She could feel the Dreamstone radiation pulsing through the resin—like holding a live nerve. She used her alchemist's tools to create a precise injection point, her acid flowing with the black fluid toward the biological control node.

Garrick was down to 28 hit points, bleeding into the rising water. "Not... today..." he grunted through the pain, swinging his maul at the leg joints, trying to knock the machine off balance.

Mercer panicked. The water was chest-deep now, churning violently. Garrick was bleeding, Cora was working on the cables, and the Juggernaut was still drilling. Three feet, four inches from breaching the containment. From making Mercer's sacrifice meaningless.

"No more analysis," he muttered through gritted teeth. "Just stop."

He cast **Fireball**, but through his Awakened Spellbook, he swapped the damage type. Not fire—that would superheat the water. **Cold damage.** A sphere of freezing energy erupted around the Juggernaut's upper torso. The biological control node pulsed erratically as frost formed across its surface. The resin conduits turned brittle.

The machine's drilling slowed to a crawl.

---

Cora's acid reached the control node. The concentrated compound, enhanced by her catalyst, burned through the corrupted tissue. The node pulsed erratically, smoking, its iridescent light flickering like a dying candle.

Mercer completed the cascade failure. The arcane feedback loop he created combined all damage sources: freezing cold, corrosive acid, thermal differential, Dreamstone radiation reflecting back on itself.

The control node—already at 2 hit points, smoking, flickering—could not withstand the combined assault. It pulsed one final time, a brilliant flash of sickly light, then went dark. The resin conduits cracked apart, black fluid spraying into the water.

The Juggernaut froze. Its drill assemblies stopped spinning. Its hydraulic systems locked. For three seconds, it stood motionless in chest-deep water.

Then the furnace—partially submerged superheated metal meeting cold water—exploded.

**BOOM.**

Steam erupted in a violent cloud. Metal shrapnel flew in all directions. The machine's torso ruptured, iron plates tearing apart. It staggered, legs buckling, and fell with the slow, inevitable collapse of a mountain.

The Drowned Juggernaut lay broken in the rising flood, its drill assemblies still aimed at the capstone, but no longer spinning. No longer a threat.

---

"Garrick first," Cora gasped, wading through chest-deep water toward him. He was slumped against the wall, bleeding into the flood. She used Healing Word—just enough to keep him conscious.

The water continued to rise. Mercer fumbled at the control panel, trying to reverse the flood, but only made it worse. Water accelerated from another pipe.

"Never mind the controls!" he shouted. "Grab the schematics! The stone tablets with the Twisted script!"

His spectral mind floated over the workshop area. He spotted critical items: a stone tablet with Juggernaut modification schematics, tools with crystalline biological grips, and—most importantly—a sealed resin case containing rolled vellum.

"The case! The sealed resin case!"

He used Mage Hand, the invisible force grasping the waterproof container from the submerged workbench.

The mountain's hum grew louder. A deep, resonant *crack* sounded from below the capstone. Not stone breaking. Something *stretching*. Testing its limits.

The water above the capstone bulged upward, forming a dome. The Giant runes blazed with frantic light. The containment held—but it was being tested. Actively tested.

"Everyone to the tunnel!" Cora shouted, fighting the current.

Cora created a glowing rope with her magical tinkering. Mercer grabbed it and executed a perfect fluid dynamics maneuver, riding the current to the tunnel entrance rather than fighting it. Garrick shielded Cora as they followed.

They hauled themselves into the tunnel, soaked, bleeding, exhausted, but alive. Below them, the Drowned Chokepoint had become a lake. The water swirled, forming a slow whirlpool centered on the capstone. The entity below wasn't trying to break out yet. It was testing. Learning. Understanding the damage to its cage.

---

The minecart carried them upward, away from the flood, away from the thing that beat like a heart below the scarred capstone. After twenty minutes, they reached the transit hub beneath their fortress. Familiar sounds greeted them: the clang of hammers at the [[Cold Forge]], the hum of the pump system, the murmur of the [[Grimhold]] workforce.

They were home.

Cora began proper medical protocols. "Infirmary. Both of you. That case goes to the secure research lab. Full contamination protocols."

Garrick nodded, leaning heavily on his maul. "The capstone holds. The Juggernaut's destroyed. We have their intelligence. Today was a win."

Professor Mercer carefully examined the sealed resin case through its translucent material. The layered script was visible: Dwarvish engineering notation, Twisted angular script, and beneath both, something older. Something that might predate the [[Giant]]s themselves.

"Mercer bought us years," he said quietly. "Today, we bought understanding. The Twisted weren't mindless slaves. They were researchers. They breached something ancient, and they've been documenting it. Studying it. Perhaps even... communicating with it."

He looked from the case to his companions to the tunnel leading downward. "The question is whether understanding will be enough. Or whether some things should remain unknown."

The transit hub bustled around them. They were safe. They were home. They had the intelligence. And they had time.

But the mountain still hummed. And below, something ancient tested its damaged cage.

*The Drowned Juggernaut lay silent in the flooded chamber far below, a monument to failed ambition. The machine was dead. The battle was over. But in stopping the drill, they had glimpsed the true scale of what was imprisoned beneath the Asymmetrical Mountain—and learned that their enemies were not mindless slaves, but researchers documenting something older than the Giants themselves. Time had been bought, but at what cost of understanding?*
