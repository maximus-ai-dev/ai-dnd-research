# Session 20: The Mountain's Wrath

**Date:** Winter's Deep, 15th Day of Frostfall

*The chronicle of the Asymmetrical Mountain continues, as our three caretakers return to the peak where ancient nightmares dream. The quiet before the storm has ended, and the mountain itself has begun to fight back.*

---

The air in [[Grimhold]] tasted of cold stone and woodsmoke, a familiar comfort that felt increasingly fragile. Three days had passed since [[Cora Flint]], [[Garrick Kade]], and [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] returned from the [[Caldera]]—three days of solid food, warm beds at the [[Second Wind Inn]], and the simple reality of a frontier town going about its business. Their wounds had healed, their spellbooks were replenished, their gear mended at the [[Griffon's Hold]]. The deep psychic weariness that clung to them after facing their own echoes in the broadcast zone had faded, replaced by a grim, focused clarity.

They stood now in the [[Prospectors' Guild]], before [[Reyna Steelforge]]'s desk. Morning light slanted through high windows, illuminating dust motes dancing in the air. Reyna looked at them—not as a guild leader to contractors, but as one caretaker of the mountain to another.

"The materials are ready," she said, her voice low. "The [[Wardstone Wardens]] sent what they could spare. [[Battlecrypt]]'s scholars included their latest resonance-stabilization formulae. It's everything you asked for." She paused, her eyes moving between them. "The mountain has been quiet since you returned. Too quiet. The tremors have stopped. The foul drafts from the old shafts have ceased. It feels like the calm before a storm I can't see."

Cora opened her ledger and began making notes. "Let's inventory the materials first," she said, moving to the sealed crate. "Cold-forged iron ingots, purified quartz crystals, alchemical reagents."

Garrick hefted the crate onto his shoulder with a grunt. "Quiet's worse than noise. Means something's building up. Or waiting." He looked at his companions. "We've got what we need. Standing here won't seal anything."

Professor Mercer watched them with academic detachment. "The silence isn't failure or buildup—it's equilibrium. The containment field is currently at 258% of baseline after our work with the [[Deep Things]]. Think of it as a spring compressed to its maximum before release." He examined the materials from a distance. "We're not racing against collapse—we're racing against the [[First World Sleeper|Sleeper]]'s adaptation. Every moment we delay gives it more time to analyze the reinforced containment and find new weaknesses."

Reyna had arranged for a wagon—a sturdy, iron-reinforced cart hitched to two patient draft horses, driven by a grizzled prospector named Torben. The journey back to the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] would take most of the day, winding through the [[Starmetal Hills]] toward that wrong-shaped peak against the northern sky.

As they climbed, something became visible from the overlook halfway up the trail. The fortress ruins on the peak—where the [[Emberfell Rejects]] once hid—now showed signs of recent activity. Smoke rose from cookfires. Movement was visible along the walls. The [[Wardstone Wardens]] had secured the upper ruins, as promised.

But there was something else. A faint, golden glow pulsed from deep within the mountain itself—not from the peak, but from below it. The containment runes, active at 258% of baseline, were visible even from miles away.

Mercer's monitoring crystal pulsed rapidly. "Observe the pattern of the glow—it's not uniform. There are darker patches where the containment is weaker. The 258% baseline is an average, not a constant." He squinted at the mountain. "The movement on the walls concerns me less than what we're not seeing. Where are the [[Twisted]]? If the Wardstone Wardens have secured the upper ruins without resistance, it means the Twisted have withdrawn deeper. They're consolidating around the Caldera."

*He was right,* the chronicler notes. *The quiet was not peace, but preparation.*

---

The wagon reached the peak fortress, where [[Warden Aldric]] and a dozen of his soldiers stood waiting at the repaired iron-bound gates. The commander they had rescued from stasis in the [[Salt-Vaults]] was now dressed in full Warden plate armor.

"Your fortifications are impressive," Mercer said, "but they're irrelevant to our actual problem." He gestured toward the mountain beneath their feet. "The containment field has reconfigured itself. There are four distinct weak points—the structural anchors of the [[Giant foundation]]. The Twisted aren't trying to break out through random breaches anymore. They're attempting to control the anchors themselves."

Aldric unfolded a vellum map showing a cross-section of the mountain. "You're right about the weak points. Our own monitoring equipment shows the same pattern. The Twisted have withdrawn from the upper layers completely—they're concentrated at these four anchor points."

He presented three choices for descent: the main stairwell through the [[Iron Warlords]]' corridors (passing directly through [[Anchor Point Two]]), the minecart network (vulnerable to sabotage), or the ventilation shafts (slow but defensible).

After debate, they chose Mercer's hybrid strategy: ventilation shafts to the [[Military Empire]] barracks, then minecarts to the [[Dwarven Layer]], then down through the [[Cold Forge]] to activate the [[Fire Gate]] with [[Ignathar]].

The descent began.

---

The ventilation shaft was a tight, claustrophobic space. Garrick led, his broad shoulders nearly brushing the damp stone walls as he pulled the crate behind him. Cora followed, calling out adjustments. Mercer's spectral mind scouted ahead, its blue light revealing the passage.

But as they approached the Military Empire barracks, something changed. The golden glow from deep within the mountain intensified. The pattern shifted. The four weak points flowed like dark currents in a golden sea.

Then they felt it: a vibration through the stone. Not a tremor or collapse—something subtler. A resonance. The mountain itself seemed to hum, a deep, sub-audible frequency they felt in their bones.

Mercer's monitoring crystal flashed urgently. "The containment field is fluctuating. The Sleeper is... testing the boundaries. Probing the anchors."

The vibration grew stronger. Dust sifted down from the tunnel ceiling. The crate of materials rattled against the stone floor.

Garrick started down the ladder toward the barracks. "We keep moving. If it's testing, we're running out of time."

Mercer called after him, "This isn't just testing—it's calibration! If the Sleeper finds the right frequency, it could induce sympathetic resonance throughout the entire containment structure!"

But it was too late.

---

The psychic resonance erupted with catastrophic force.

A wave of shimmering distortion tore through the corridor, visible as a ripple in reality itself. Stone cracked with deafening reports. The air filled with dust and the smell of ozone and something older—the scent of void energy given form.

Cora was slammed against the barracks wall. Garrick braced, his feet digging into stone, but the wave still tore at him. Mercer was thrown backward like a rag doll, his Shield spell shimmering into existence a fraction too late.

From deep within the distortion, a shape began to form. Not the [[Avatar of the Slumber]] itself—that remained locked in the Caldera—but a projection of its will. A towering figure of crystallized void energy, fifty feet tall in perception though the corridor could not physically contain it. Its form flickered, sometimes solid, sometimes transparent, with the [[First World]] visible inside it like light through stained glass.

Its eyes were spheres of broadcast energy, and looking into them was like looking into the Sleeper's dream.

The projection didn't speak. It didn't need to. Its presence was the broadcast made flesh, and its message was clear: **This path is closed.**

---

Combat was joined in earnest.

The projection's Void Grasp wrapped around Mercer, lifting him from cover. Stone cracked behind him. The psychic assault tore through his mind, leaving him stunned and vulnerable.

Cora scrambled for her alchemist's supplies, mixing a disruption elixir. Mercer cast Counterspell, his arcane energy momentarily disrupting the projection. Garrick, even stunned, swung his maul in blind fury, cracking the crystallized void form.

But the projection struck again. A shimmering tendril of void energy—Void Grasp—lashed out and wrapped around Mercer, lifting him from cover. The world vanished around the professor, replaced by the First World—a kaleidoscope of impossible colors, shifting geometries, and the Sleeper's dreaming mind. He was effectively blinded and deafened to the physical world, trapped in the dream.

The barracks shuddered with renewed violence. A section of ceiling collapsed near the ventilation shaft entrance, blocking the way back up. The crate of reinforcement materials was trapped somewhere above, lost.

Cora created a perfect psychic severance formula, her hands moving with impossible precision. Garrick's maul became a blur of destruction, a critical hit sending void-crystal shards spraying through the collapsing barracks. Inside the vision, Mercer didn't fight the torrent of ancient dreams—he analyzed it, then cast Dispel Magic at the highest level he could muster, targeting not the projection, but the psychic tether connecting it to Anchor Point Two.

Simultaneously, he triggered his One with the Word feature. As the Void Grasp's psychic damage tore at his consciousness, threatening to reduce him to zero hit points, he sacrificed spell slots—a third-level, then a fourth—to stay at 1 HP. Knowledge as armor.

The effects were catastrophic and simultaneous.

Mercer's Dispel Magic severed the psychic tether. The feedback loop intensified, and Anchor Point Two experienced harmonic dissonance so severe that deep within the mountain, something cracked. The projection, caught between Garrick's physical assault and the collapsing magical conduit, fragmented completely.

The crystallized void body shattered, exploding in a burst of void energy that washed through the collapsing barracks. The psychic resonance field collapsed. The corridor to the minecart network was suddenly, violently clear—though partially blocked by rubble.

The projection was gone. Destroyed.

But the cost was terrible. Cora stood frozen, trapped in a First World vision from a Dream Gaze attack. Mercer lay against the wall, breathing shallowly at 1 HP, having sacrificed precious spell slots to stay alive. Garrick stood amid the destruction, his maul still raised.

The barracks continued to come apart. More stone crashed down. The way back was completely sealed. The crate was lost forever.

---

They dragged themselves to the minecart network, Cora fighting her way free from the psychic prison using Mercer's illusion of her ledger as an anchor. "The numbers won," she gasped as reality snapped back into focus.

The descent to the [[Dwarven Layer]] was a blur of rattling tracks and planning. "The lost materials were specifically calibrated for the original Seal design," Mercer said, sketching alternative architectures in his spellbook. "Without them, we must adapt. The forty-two-hour ritual may be impossible now. We may need to create a smaller, temporary Seal—something that buys us a century rather than a hundred thousand years."

The minecart arrived at the Cold Forge transit hub. The familiar scent of forge-smoke and hot metal filled the air. The massive stone doors stood ahead, a faint orange glow seeping from beneath them—Ignathar was active.

The doors trembled, then swung inward. Heat washed out in a wave—the living, breathing warmth of elemental fire. [[Ignathar]] stood within, its form a towering column of white-hot flame.

"You return," the elemental rumbled. "The bargain was for reinforcement materials. I see no materials."

Garrick stepped forward. "The materials are lost. Buried under collapsed stone. But we're here to finish what we started."

"The materials were for a Seal," Ignathar continued. "A reinforcement of ancient architecture. Without them, your Seal will be... imperfect. Temporary." The elemental's flames reached toward the massive stone arch at the rear of the forge—the Fire Gate, currently dark and inert. "But temporary may be enough. A century of stability. Time for proper materials to be gathered."

The flames withdrew. "I will open the Fire Gate. Not for your lost materials. Not for future service." Ignathar's blue-white eyes fixed on Mercer. "For knowledge. Tell me of the Sleeper. Tell me of the thing that dreams beneath this mountain. Tell me what you have learned in your descent. That is my price now: knowledge of what you fight."

The elemental's form settled. "The gate will open. The ritual can begin. But first, you will tell me everything. And then you will descend to the Caldera, and you will face what waits there with my fire at your backs."

---

*Thus ends the twentieth chronicle. The caretakers have reached their destination, but at terrible cost. Their materials are lost, their strength depleted, their timeline compressed. The Fire Gate awaits activation, but the price has changed from materials to knowledge—and the true Avatar of the Slumber waits below, aware of their presence and their progress. The final confrontation approaches, and whatever Seal they forge now will be a compromise, a temporary fix born of desperation rather than design. The mountain has tested them, and they have survived—but the true test still lies ahead, in the Caldera where ancient nightmares dream.*
