# Session 14: The Maker's Rest

*The following chronicle was compiled from the field notes of the expedition members and the observations of the Prospectors' Guild chronicler stationed at the forward camp. It records the events of the fourteenth expedition into the Asymmetrical Mountain, wherein the party sought the tomb of Korathan, Master Runecarver of the Architect's Council.*

---

The air in the [[Prospectors' Guild]] carried the familiar scents of old parchment, woodsmoke, and the faint metallic tang of ore samples. Three days had passed since the return from the [[Giant foundation]] level — three days of rest, resupply, and quiet preparation. [[Garrick Kade]]'s wounds from the amplifier sabotage had healed into fresh scars. [[Cora Flint]]'s alchemical supplies had been restocked from [[Grimhold]]'s modest market. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]]'s spellbook had been meticulously updated with observations from the containment architecture.

[[Reyna Steelforge]] stood before the large map table in the Guild's main hall, her expression more serious than we'd ever seen it. The blueprint tablet lay open beside the monitoring crystal, both pulsing with faint light. "The data is conclusive," she said, her voice low. "The containment field is decaying at a predictable rate. The Giants built it to last one hundred thousand years. We're at the halfway point." She tapped a location on the tablet — a symbol Mercer recognized from his Giant studies. "This mark here — '[[The Maker's Rest]].' It's not part of the containment network. It's a tomb. The tomb of [[Korathan]], the master architect who designed the entire system."

She looked at each of us in turn. "The tablet shows the tomb's location in the deepest section of the outer ring. If we're going to understand how to reinforce the containment — or build something new to replace it — we need the knowledge Korathan took with him. The scaling techniques, the design principles, the things the Giants considered too important to lose and too dangerous to share freely."

The journey back to the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] was familiar now — the winding trail through the [[Starmetal Hills]], the looming silhouette of the off-center peak against the gray sky, the entrance to the maintenance corridors we'd secured. Hours passed as we navigated the Giant-scale passages, the forty-foot-high ceilings and thirty-foot-wide corridors now feeling less alien, more like a workspace built for beings of unimaginable scale.

We reached the location marked on the tablet. The corridor ended at a door that took our breath away.

Forty feet tall and twenty feet wide, carved from a single slab of polished obsidian so black it seemed to drink the light from our lanterns. The surface was covered in flowing Giant script — not the functional rune sequences we'd seen throughout the foundation, but narrative text carved with exquisite precision. The door stood in a frame of white marble, and beside it, built into the stone wall, was a lever mechanism clearly designed for Giant hands — fifteen feet above the floor, with counterweights and gears the size of wagon wheels.

## The Ritual of Entry

Cora immediately began systematic examination, her artificer's mind analyzing the mechanism and surrounding area for traps. "Garrick, with your Giant's Might, you could reach that lever, but I'm not pulling anything until we know what it does. This is a tomb, not a maintenance corridor — there will be security measures."

Garrick grunted, looking up at the massive lever mechanism. "You're right about one thing, Cora — I can reach it. But waiting around for traps while that containment field decays doesn't sit right with me. The Giants built this place to be maintained, not to kill their own people."

Mercer approached the obsidian door with academic reverence, his eyes tracing the flowing Giant script. "Stand back, both of you. This isn't mere decoration — it's a formal epitaph written in High Giant, the ceremonial language of their architect-priests."

His fingers hovered over the polished surface as he began translating aloud: "Here lies Korathan, Master Runecarver of the [[Architect's Council]], whose hands shaped the bones of the world and whose mind contained the geometry of containment. He who walked with the First Dreamers and spoke with stone as others speak with flesh. His knowledge sleeps with him, guarded by the silence he earned."

The wizard stepped back, his scholarly excitement tempered by professional caution. "The phrasing is significant — 'guarded by the silence he earned.' That suggests passive defenses rather than active traps. The Giants respected their dead, but they also understood the value of what was buried with them."

Garrick's attempt to force the lever revealed its true nature — not frozen, but magically sequenced. Mercer's Detect Magic revealed a breathtaking complexity: three layers of enchantment intertwining — preservation, security, and structural reinforcement. Forcing the lever wouldn't just break the mechanism; it would collapse the preservation field and possibly trigger a failsafe that would seal the tomb permanently.

Cora's examination of the bronze handle revealed the truth: four distinct wear patterns, each corresponding to a Giant's finger placement, with a fifth for the thumb. "Look here," she said, tracing the patterns with a delicate tool. "First grip: index and middle fingers here, thumb here. Second grip shifts to ring and little fingers. Third returns to all four. It's a sequence, three movements total."

Mercer's spectral mind floated near the mechanism, its ethereal form humming with analytical magic. "You're absolutely right, Cora. And I've identified the frequencies. The security lattice requires three specific magical resonances, delivered in sequence. The first is a containment frequency — the same base resonance used throughout the Sleeper's prison. The second is an architectural frequency — the harmonic signature of Giant construction magic. The third... is a personal frequency. Korathan's own magical signature."

The puzzle was solved in principle but not in practice. We needed Korathan's personal magical signature, which Mercer realized wasn't separate from the containment frequency — it was the harmonic overtone that emerged when the containment and architectural frequencies interacted. "We don't need to extract his signature — we need to recreate the conditions that produce it," Cora observed. "We play the first two frequencies in the proper sequence, and the third emerges naturally from their interaction."

The execution required perfect coordination. Garrick, enhanced by Giant's Might, would operate the lever with the precise grip sequence. Mercer would produce the containment frequency from the monitoring crystal. Cora would manage the architectural frequency from the blueprint tablet. With Death Ward and Freedom of Movement cast on Garrick as precautions, we began the ritual.

Cora's countdown echoed in the vast corridor: "Three... two... one... now!"

The two magical frequencies intertwined, and as Mercer predicted, a third resonance emerged — a complex harmonic that carried the unmistakable signature of a master craftsman. Korathan's personal magical essence filled the air, sweet and sharp like ozone and forge-smoke.

The lever moved.

Not with resistance, but with smooth, oiled precision. Gears that were frozen for fifty thousand years turned silently, counterweights shifted, and the obsidian door began to slide inward. No grinding, no protest — just perfect engineering responding to perfect understanding.

## The Architect's Trials

The corridor beyond activated as the door opened. Massive stone tiles — each ten feet square — clicked into place along the floor. The walls, carved with continuous illustrations of Korathan's life, began to glow with soft bioluminescence, illuminating the descent.

"Pressure plates," Cora said, her voice low. "Look at the seams — some tiles have hairline fractures, others don't. This is the first trap layer. Giants would step on one tile at a time. We'll have to navigate multiple tiles per person."

Mercer's analysis of the wall carvings revealed the key: "The safe path follows his achievements, not his biography. See here — the tile beneath the carving of his first successful containment field shows a matching rune pattern in the stonework. That's the key: we step on tiles that correspond to his architectural triumphs, not personal milestones."

We navigated the pressure corridor with careful precision, Cora marking safe tiles with chalk, Mercer casting Feather Fall to reduce our weight impact. But as we progressed, new hazards emerged — a gas trap section with concealed nozzles at Giant nostril height, releasing paralytic compounds designed for Giant physiology.

Mercer identified the solution: "The safe path continues with professional achievement tiles, but there's an additional pattern here. See how the border runes on these tiles match the ventilation schematics in the carvings? Those aren't just achievement markers — they're airflow control points. Stepping on them in the correct sequence might actually prevent the gas release."

The coordination became a dance — Garrick stepping on control tiles to activate ventilation sequences, Cora following immediately behind, Mercer calling the timing through Message spells. We moved through air curtains rather than gas clouds, the Giants' engineering responding to our understanding.

Beyond the gas corridor lay the Gravity Stairs — fifty feet of descent on three-foot-tall steps, with every third step containing active gravity reversal runes. At Giant scale, these would cause a brief lurch; at our scale, they would launch us violently upward.

Garrick spotted the solution: inspection alcoves every ten feet along the staircase, positioned at Giant knee height but reachable for us. "Maintenance access," he reported. "Each alcove contains controls to temporarily disable the gravity runes."

We descended in coordinated stages — reaching each alcove, disabling the next set of runed steps, then carefully navigating past them before the temporary disablement expired. The staircase was a test of systematic thinking, another demonstration that the Giants expected maintainers to understand their systems, not just avoid them.

## The Resonance Chamber

At the base of the staircase, we entered a new chamber — thirty feet square with a vaulted ceiling sixty feet high. The walls were carved with rune sequences in concentric circles, glowing with soft blue light. As we entered, a deep, resonant hum began building in intensity.

"Resonance chamber," Mercer said, his voice barely audible over the building hum. "The runes are building harmonic energy. At Giant scale, this would be tuned to a Giant's vocal range — they could stop it by speaking a counter-tone, a security passphrase."

Cora felt the vibration in her bones. "At our scale, we can't produce the correct frequency. Human vocal ranges are too high. The resonance is building. If it reaches peak intensity..."

Mercer finished the thought: "It becomes damaging. Thunder damage, area effect. The room will fill with sound that shakes us apart."

We had approximately five rounds before the resonance reached damaging levels. Mercer and Cora worked in perfect synchronization — he programming the monitoring crystal to produce the exact inverse frequency with precise phase alignment, she making micro-adjustments with her tinker's tools.

"Now!" Mercer shouted.

Cora activated the crystal at maximum output.

For a terrifying moment, the air in the chamber seemed to *tear*. The building hum met its exact inverse, and the two frequencies collided in a violent interference pattern. The concentric rune circles flared blindingly bright, then began to flicker erratically. The stone floor vibrated so intensely that dust rose in clouds.

Then silence.

Absolute, perfect silence.

The rune circles dimmed to a soft glow, then went dark entirely. The far door clicked open, sliding inward with smooth precision. The resonance chamber was disarmed.

## The Final Choice

The passage ahead split into three parallel tunnels, each thirty feet wide and forty feet high, each marked with a different Giant rune carved above its entrance. The left tunnel bore a rune that meant "Strength." The center: "Knowledge." The right: "Endurance."

Cora examined the floor at each entrance. "Trap triggers. Only one passage is safe. The other two..." She didn't need to finish the thought.

Mercer studied the runes. "Strength, Knowledge, Endurance — qualities the Giants valued. But which did Korathan consider the foundation of containment?" He thought back to everything we'd learned. "The containment wasn't about strength — brute force failed against the Sleeper. It wasn't about knowledge alone — they understood the entity but couldn't destroy it. It was about endurance — persistence, longevity, the willingness to maintain a vigil for a hundred thousand years."

We chose the Endurance tunnel. As we entered, Cora's testing weight triggered not a trap, but a hidden panel that slid open to reveal a fifteen-foot-tall stone golem standing in an alcove. Its surface was carved with containment runes so dense they looked like text. The golem's eyes began to glow with soft blue light.

"The final guardian," Mercer whispered. "Not a trap, but a test. The golem evaluates intruders. If we demonstrate understanding of the containment runes, it should let us pass. If not..."

Mercer approached with academic authority, producing the same three-frequency harmonic we'd used at the door. The golem's stone form vibrated in perfect harmony. Its glowing eyes pulsed in time with the harmonics, studying us with ancient intelligence.

Then, with a deep grinding sound of stone on stone, the golem stepped aside. It raised one massive hand in a slow, deliberate salute — a gesture of respect from one guardian to another — before returning to its alcove. The panel slid shut.

## The Vigil Chamber

The Endurance tunnel ended at a plain stone slab with a single rune carved at its center: "Rest." The rune glowed with soft blue light, but there was no hum, no vibration, no sense of power. Just peace.

The door opened at a touch — not with mechanisms or magic, but with simple counterbalance. It swung inward silently, revealing the chamber beyond.

The space took our breath away. Circular, sixty feet in diameter, with a domed ceiling that rose to a point forty feet above. The walls were covered in runes — but not the containment runes we'd seen throughout the Giant level. These were different. Smaller, denser, more intricate. Korathan's personal runes — the master techniques developed over three centuries of work, preserved on the chamber walls as a permanent record.

At the center, on a raised stone platform, lay Korathan.

The Giant was intact. Twenty-two feet tall, lying in ceremonial armor on a stone bier, hands folded over a runic chisel that glowed with faint residual power. The armor was inscribed with containment runes so dense they looked like text. Korathan's face was peaceful, eyes closed, features perfectly preserved. Not dead, not alive — in stasis. The "long watch" described in the epitaph.

A soft hum filled the chamber — the stasis field maintaining the vigil.

Mercer stepped inside, his eyes wide with wonder. "He's been here for fifty thousand years," he breathed. "Waiting. Watching. In case the containment failed and needed repair." He looked at the wall runes. "And he left everything here. The scaling techniques, the design principles... everything we need to continue his work."

## The Architect's Message

Mercer began transcription with his Wizardly Quill, his spectral mind floating beside him to double the speed. Four hours of meticulous work passed in reverent silence. Cora examined Korathan's equipment without touching anything, understanding that the runic chisel was part of the stasis matrix. Garrick maintained watch at the entrance, though the chamber's profound peace lulled even his vigilance.

As Mercer touched the final rune sequence on the parchment, completing the transcription, something extraordinary happened.

The chamber responded.

The runes on the walls illuminated in sequence — not all at once, but in a cascading activation that ran from floor to ceiling, from wall to wall, until the entire chamber glowed with soft blue light. The illumination built, then focused, projecting images and emotional impressions directly into our minds.

**The Architect's Message began:**

1. **The Discovery:** Korathan finding the [[Sleeper]] — a vast presence in the bedrock, dreaming, leaking radiation that warped reality. Not malicious. Not even conscious in a way the Giants understood. Simply existing, incompatibly, in a universe that had moved on without it.

2. **The Decision:** The Architect's Council debating: destroy the Sleeper, contain it, or leave it. Destruction was impossible — the entity predated the Giants' universe. Leaving it meant the mountain would eventually become uninhabitable. Containment was chosen. Korathan volunteered.

3. **The Warning:** The containment will not last forever. Korathan designed it to endure for one hundred thousand years. The Giants calculated that this would be sufficient time for the younger races to develop the knowledge and power to either reinforce the containment permanently or find an alternative solution. The younger races have had roughly fifty thousand years so far. The containment is at the halfway point. It is weakening on schedule.

4. **The Gift:** The [[Scaling Runes]] are deliberately placed in the tomb because Korathan anticipated that the eventual caretakers would be smaller than Giants. The tomb is not just a burial — it is a technology transfer. Korathan gave the younger races the tools to continue the work.

The message ended. The runes dimmed. Korathan lay still, watching from behind closed eyes, continuing the long vigil.

Mercer lowered his quill, his hands trembling. "A hundred thousand years," he whispered. "We're at the halfway point. The containment is weakening exactly as designed."

## The Return

The journey back through the tomb was a silent, reverent procession. The traps remained disabled — the pressure plates inert, the gas nozzles silent, the gravity runes dark, the resonance chamber quiet. The guardian golem's alcove stayed sealed as we passed.

We emerged into the familiar Giant-scale corridors, the transition jarring — from sacred stillness to functional architecture. Hours later, we reached the forward camp near the minecart network entrance.

Garrick was the first to break the silence as we prepared to ascend to the surface. "Fifty thousand years," he said, his voice rough in the quiet. "That's how long the cage is supposed to hold. And we're already halfway through."

Mercer nodded, his fingers tracing the edge of the transcription scroll. "The question is not whether it will fail. The question is what we build before it does." He looked at Cora. "The scaling runes change everything. We can build containment devices at our scale now. Portable fields. Enhanced circlets. The Dreamstone Seal becomes possible."

Cora opened her ledger, already making plans. "First priority: apply the scaling techniques to our existing Dreamstone circlets. Double their effectiveness. Then design portable containment emitters — small enough to carry, powerful enough to suppress the broadcast in localized areas. Then..." She looked up, her eyes sharp with focus. "The Seal. The final containment device. With these runes, we can build it."

On the walk back to the forward camp, Garrick was quiet for a long time. Finally: "A hundred thousand years. That is how long the cage is supposed to hold. And we are already halfway through."

Mercer nodded. "The question is not whether it will fail. The question is what we build before it does."

*Chronicler's Note: The expedition returned with Korathan's Scaling Runes — the critical technology needed to build upon the Giants' work. The party now understands the true nature of their task: not to stop something that's breaking, but to reinforce something that's aging exactly as predicted. The vigil continues. The responsibility has been passed.*
