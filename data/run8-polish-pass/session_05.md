# Session 5: The Empire's Warning

*Spring, 5th Year of the Frontier Reclamation*

The silence that followed the [[Amalgamation]]'s defeat in the [[Grand Archive]] was not peace, but the quiet of a tomb holding its breath. For three months, winter settled over the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] with heavy snow, and the explorers used the time to fortify their position. The surface camp became a proper fortress, walls patched and watchtowers manned. [[Cora Flint]] coaxed green shoots from ancient grain seeds in a sheltered courtyard. [[Garrick Kade]] drilled the workforce in combat. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] transcribed everything—the [[Commander's logbook]], the [[Giant]] command words, the mountain's structural anomalies—into a growing archive of his own.

When spring arrived with thawing streams, a letter came from [[Grimhold]], sealed with [[Reyna Steelforge]]'s personal mark. The [[Prospectors' Guild]] historian requested a map of the Military Empire layer below the Archives, specifically structural analysis of what held the mountain up. Payment in gold and supplies, of course.

[[Warden Aldric]] stood with them at the top of the [[Grand Stairwell]], looking down into darkness. "The [[Brotherhood]] never went deeper," he said quietly. "They said the lower levels were someone else's problem. But if the Empire knew something about what's below... we should know it too."

The air that rose from 120 feet below smelled of damp stone and something faintly metallic—like very old blood remembered rather than smelled.

---

**Cora** pulled out her ledger as they prepared to descend. "Parameters first. Garrick, your armor's still damaged from the Amalgamation fight—that's coming out of your share. Mercer, mapping supplies ready? Reyna wants structural analysis, not just corridors."

**Garrick** hefted his maul, ignoring her comment about his share. "Inventory's fine. Maul's ready, axes are sharp. That's all that matters." He moved to the stairwell edge, peering down. "Aldric, if your Brotherhood said 'someone else's problem,' that means they knew there was something down there worth avoiding." He started down the ancient stairs. "We're not avoiding it."

**Mercer** examined the transition point with scholarly fascination. "Look at the stonework. The Empire didn't just build *below* the Brotherhood—they built *through* them. Brutal efficiency, no attempt to match existing masonry."

As they descended, the architecture revealed itself. At the 60-foot mark, the steps widened, the ceiling rose, and carved relief panels began. Soldiers in full plate marched on the left wall; officials oversaw tribute processions on the right. The work was masterful, details still crisp after centuries.

Garrick reached the landing 120 feet down—a single slab of polished granite with hairline cracks radiating from its center like a spiderweb. Above the iron-banded door ahead, carved in elaborate Military Empire script: ***THE IMPERIAL LOWER PALACE. LET ALL WHO ENTER SERVE THE EMPIRE'S MEMORY.***

The metallic scent was stronger here, with a faint sweetness beneath it.

**Cora** knelt to examine the cracks. "Structural instability confirmed. These radiate from the center—weight distribution issues below." She examined the door. "Garrick, don't touch that yet. If the Empire sealed something they couldn't destroy, they likely didn't want it getting out."

**Garrick** ignored her, testing his weight on the granite. "Traps won't matter if the floor gives way."

**Mercer** traced the crack pattern. "These aren't random. They form a perfect radial pattern. This suggests either a massive weight concentrated below us, or..." He looked up at the door. "Or something pushing *up* from beneath."

From above, Aldric's voice echoed down. "Everything stable? The air feels... different."

**Cora** called back, "What do Brotherhood records say about pressure seals in Empire architecture?"

Aldric's reply was thoughtful. "Brotherhood records mention the Empire used pressure seals in deep vaults—especially where they stored volatile materials or... contained things. If the pressure equalizes or reverses..."

**Mercer** cast *Detect Magic*. His eyes widened. "Fascinating. The Empire didn't use magic to seal the door. They used it to reinforce the foundation. This landing... the cracks would be much worse without this enchantment. It's ancient, fading, but still active."

**Garrick** had heard enough. "Enough analysis. Magic's holding the floor together, air's moving, door's not locked." He braced his shoulder against the massive oak door. "Stand back. If this is a pressure seal, breaking it might be violent."

He put his full strength into pushing, and with a sudden *WHOOSH*, the door swung inward. A gust of stale, metallic-scented air rushed past, carrying centuries of dust. The pressure differential had been significant.

Simultaneously, **Cora** spotted a recessed panel at the base of the right wall—a bronze plate cleverly patinated to match the stone. **Mercer** found control glyphs on the door frame: pressure regulation and containment status indicators.

The door now stood open, revealing a wide corridor beyond. Brass torch brackets shaped like eagle heads lined the walls every thirty feet. From somewhere deep in the corridor came a faint, distant *drip... drip... drip...* of water on stone.

**Garrick** moved into the corridor, his eyes adjusting to the darkness. "Panel and glyphs can wait. First, we secure this corridor." He peered around a corner, seeing a barracks room with stone platforms and a mural of Military Empire soldiers. On a stone table sat a ceramic flask, sealed with wax, as if placed there yesterday.

**Cora** examined the bronze plate, placing a copper coin in its depression. With a soft *click*, a compartment slid open, revealing a crystal lens mounted in a bronze frame. It was warm to the touch.

**Mercer** found a hidden viewing port beside the glyphs. Looking through it revealed a vertical shaft descending into blackness. At the limit of his vision, perhaps fifty feet down, something glowed with a faint, sickly green light. It pulsed slowly, rhythmically, like a heartbeat.

When **Mercer** held the crystal lens to the viewing port, something remarkable happened. The darkness transformed into a diagram of glowing lines and symbols. The green light resolved into a three-dimensional representation of a spherical chamber, with lines of force radiating upward, pressing against the stone above. The diagram was annotated in Giant script—a structural monitoring system showing stress distribution.

**Garrick** brought the flask to Cora. "Heavy, no sloshing. Imperial seal."

**Cora** analyzed it with her alchemist's tools. "Military-grade stimulant tincture. Preserved by the dry cold. Probably still functional, but... high concentration of something volatile. Fifty percent chance of severe side effects. Two doses."

**Mercer** studied the diagram through the lens. "It's testing the containment. Not just erosion—active probing. The Giant architecture was designed to contain something that exerts constant pressure. But over millennia, that something has learned. It's found the weak points."

From above, Aldric called, "Everything all right? You've been quiet a long time."

**Mercer** searched for the monitoring system's archives, finding a magically concealed compartment. Inside: an iron scroll case, a glowing crystal disk, and folded vellum sealed with the Imperial eagle emblem.

**Cora** checked for traps. "No dangers. Preservation spells only. The Empire wanted these found."

**Mercer** examined the crystal disk through the lens. "It's a monitoring log—still recording. This crystal has been documenting the containment breach progression for centuries. The green light at its center is a real-time link."

He carefully opened the vellum. The writing was in crisp Imperial Common:

***To whatever Empire Officer commands this garrison when the corruption reaches the lower levels:***

*The containment architecture below our foundation is failing. Giant-era seals are compromised. What they contained is testing its prison, and it is learning.*

*Our engineers confirm: the dwarven vaults directly beneath us are already contaminated. The corruption spreads upward through stone like roots through soil.*

*My recommendation is full evacuation of this mountain. My orders are otherwise.*

*The Crypt will not hold indefinitely. Monitor the progression. When the green light in the monitoring crystal reaches the third ring, the containment has six months at most. When it reaches the fifth ring, it has weeks.*

*Do not open the Sealed Crypt. The corruption within is beyond our ability to contain, let alone destroy. Our only option is to delay. Seal this level. Let the mountain bury it.*

*— [[Commander Valerius]], 7th Imperial Garrison*

**Mercer** looked through the lens at the crystal disk. The green light pulsed at its center... with faint concentric rings just visible. The light had reached the second ring.

From the barracks room, the dripping changed—faster now, like rain beginning to fall. From the drain came a gurgling, as if water was backing up from below.

**Cora** packed the archives. "Second ring means we have time, but not infinite. The drain backing up means the water table is contaminated. We need to map what we can and get out."

**Garrick** grabbed the crystal and vellum. "Second ring means we have time, but that drain backing up means something's changing now." The gurgling grew louder; the water in the pool began bubbling.

They retreated, **Garrick** and **Mercer** forcing the massive door closed with combined strength and magic. As they ascended, the thumping began—deep, resonant impacts felt through stone rather than heard. Then silence. Then a slow, grinding scrape from beyond the sealed door.

At the surface camp, **Aldric** secured the Brotherhood door with iron bars. "These will hold against casual force. But if whatever's below really wants through..."

**Mercer** examined the scroll case documents. In margin notes, he found the Empire's observations: *"The contained entity exhibits properties of both mineral and biological matter. It spreads through stone via capillary action. It appears to possess a form of distributed consciousness. The Giants referred to it as '[[The Sleeper]] in their containment inscriptions."*

The thumping resumed: three beats, pause, two beats. The pauses grew shorter.

They traveled to [[Grimhold]], the [[Monitoring Crystal]] pulsing in Mercer's pack with accelerating rhythm. At the [[Prospectors' Guild]] building, [[Reyna Steelforge]] listened in complete silence as they presented their findings.

When **Mercer** placed the crystal on her desk, its green pulse illuminating Commander Valerius's warning, something changed in her eyes.

"This changes everything," she said quietly. "Payment for the map, as agreed. Two hundred gold. But the reinforcement materials... The Guild can provide them at cost. Transport it yourselves. And keep this quiet."

She looked at the crystal, its pulse seeming to quicken even as they watched. "The Guild archives contain fragments of Giant-era records. I'll have them searched for any reference to 'Sleeper.'" She met Garrick's eyes. "You're right—this isn't just your problem. But sending more people poking around could make it worse."

"Take your payment. Secure your mountain. And keep that crystal close. If the pulse reaches the third ring..."

She didn't finish the sentence. She didn't need to.

*Chronicler's Note: The explorers now understand the true nature of the mountain they call home. The [[Military Empire]] did not merely build atop ancient ruins—they built a tomb over a prison. [[The Sleeper]] stirs below, learning its cage with each passing hour. The Empire's warning is clear, but unlike the Empire, these explorers cannot simply walk away. Their home is the prison wall, and the prisoner is testing the bars.*
