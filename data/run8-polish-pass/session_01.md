# Session 1: The Asymmetrical Mountain's Secret

**Date:** 15th of Frostfall, 1273 AE

*The chronicle of three strangers drawn to the frontier town of [[Grimhold]], and what they discovered beneath a mountain that should not exist.*

---

The wind carried the smell of woodsmoke and cold earth as the last light of civilization gave way to the wild [[Starmetal Hills]]. Below, nestled in the shadow of the Sentinel Mountains, lay [[Grimhold]]—a frontier town of three hundred souls where stone walls older than memory stood beside simple log buildings. And to the northwest, dominating the horizon with wrongness, rose the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]].

Three travelers arrived separately that gray afternoon, each drawn by different needs but the same reality: this was where the work was.

[[Cora Flint]] calculated expenses with every step, her alchemist's supplies clinking in her pack, her ledger of debts growing heavier. [[Garrick Kade]] carried the weight of chain mail and memories from an ocean away, his bearing drawing notice from the town guard. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] adjusted his spectacles and recorded geological anomalies, already fascinated by the mountain's impossible erosion patterns.

At the center of town, a weathered bounty board nailed to the [[Prospectors' Guild]] bore the notice that would bind their fates: **"50 GOLD BOUNTY — Bandits raiding from the Starmetal Hills. See [[Reyna Steelforge]] inside."**

Cora approached with the efficiency of a business proposition. "You two look like you're calculating expenses versus income. The bounty's fifty gold. I handle wounds, supplies, and logistics. You handle the hitting and the thinking."

Garrick grunted his assent. Mercer adjusted his spectacles. "While I appreciate your pragmatic approach, I must correct several assumptions. First, I am not here merely for 'coin'—I am conducting field research on anomalous geological formations..."

Thus began the negotiation that would lead them inside the Guild, where Reyna Steelforge awaited with flint-colored eyes and no patience for pleasantries. "You're standing in front of my bounty board. Either you're here for the job or you're blocking the door. Which is it?"

The bandits called themselves the [[Emberfell Rejects]]. Eight to twelve strong, poorly armed, stealing not treasure but supplies: blankets, grain, salt, and—most tellingly—tools. They fled northwest toward the Asymmetrical Mountain after each raid.

When Garrick heard the name, his blood ran cold. "I know them. Kregg leads the Rejects." The personal vendetta had found him, even here on the frontier.

Reyna offered ten gold advance for supplies. "Take it or leave it. The supplies you need are at the [[Griffon's Hold]]—[[Elara Stormwind]] runs it. She knows the trails."

* * *

Elara proved as knowledgeable as promised. In her shop smelling of oiled leather and woodsmoke, she spread maps and spoke warnings. "The mountain does something to people. Prospectors come back talking about whispers, bad dreams. Some don't come back at all. It's not haunted—it's just... wrong."

She outfitted them with cold-weather gear, rations, and a warning about the winds: "They sound like they're coming from the wrong direction. You'll be walking northwest, but the wind will feel like it's blowing from below your feet."

Thus equipped, they followed a blood trail into the gathering storm—bootprints and crimson dots leading northwest, fresh enough that the falling snow hadn't fully covered them. The ascent into the Starmetal Hills revealed the mountain's true wrongness: strata tilted at impossible angles, wind swirling in unnatural eddies, and a metallic scent that grew stronger with each step.

They found the bandits' camp at a frozen stream, abandoned in haste. More importantly, they found clues: stolen pickaxes with unusual crystalline deposits on their tips, and a leather-bound journal belonging to a prospector named [[Harlan]]. His final entry read: *"The mountain isn't a mountain. It's a capstone."*

Prisoner in tow—a terrified bandit named [[Jace]]—they pursued [[Kregg]] to the peak, to ruins of dark, glassy stone that felt older than the hills themselves.

* * *

The fortress at the peak was a collection of collapsed buildings surrounding a central courtyard. Kregg and his remaining bandits were there, but they weren't preparing to fight—they were fortifying against something *below*. A boarded-over section of flagstones covered stairs descending into darkness.

"Three of my men went down there a week ago," Kregg called down to them, his voice carrying on the unnatural wind. "We heard them screaming for about an hour. Then... that." He pointed as a deep *thump* vibrated through the mountain. "It's been getting louder. Closer."

In a move of desperate calculation, Kregg ordered his terrified men to open the stairwell. "You want me, Kade? Come and get me! But you'll have to go through whatever's down there first!"

What emerged were not bandits, but something far worse.

Dwarves—or what had once been dwarves. Their skin was pale and veined with black, their eyes completely dark, black fluid leaking from noses and mouths. They moved with jerky, unnatural grace, and when they touched the bandits, black veins spiderwebbed across living flesh.

Garrick charged into the courtyard, maul swinging. Cora's crossbow bolt went wide in the blowing snow. Mercer's magic missiles struck true but failed to stop the advancing horrors. The fight was brutal and brief—Kregg's bandits fell quickly, infected and twitching, while Garrick swept Kregg's legs out from under him with a satisfying crack of bone.

Then something remarkable happened.

From the stairwell came a deep, wet inhalation—something large breathing in the darkness below. The dwarf-things paused, turned, and began dragging their infected victims back down the stairs. They were withdrawing. On command.

"They're not supposed to retreat," Kregg whispered, blood flecking his lips. "They're supposed to kill you..."

* * *

The night passed in a barricaded stone building, the mountain humming around them, that deep thump echoing up through the stone at irregular intervals. Kregg, feverish with a broken leg, muttered about "the Sleeper's dreams." Jace provided the crucial timeline: "Three nights. The dark of the moon is three nights from now. That's when they'll come out again, if the door stays broken."

At dawn, they descended through snow two feet deep, carrying Kregg between them, returning to [[Grimhold]] as the sun dipped behind the Sentinel Mountains. The townsfolk stared as they passed through the east gate—Garrick carrying the broken bandit leader, Cora with her ledger, Mercer with his journal, Jace trailing like a whipped dog.

Reyna Steelforge counted out fifty gold pieces in the lamplit [[Prospectors' Guild]]. "Proof enough," she said, her eyes on Kregg's unconscious form. "He'll hang once Brother Aldwin patches him up enough to stand trial."

Then her gaze moved to the +1 dagger at Garrick's belt, to Harlan's journal, to Mercer's intense expression. "But this isn't just about bandits, is it? What did you find up there?"

They stood in the warm room, the smell of ink and stone in the air. Outside, night fell. Somewhere to the northwest, the Asymmetrical Mountain waited under the rising moon. And deep beneath it, something that should have slept forever—the [[Sleeper]], the [[First World Sleeper]]—took another slow, steady breath.

Three strangers had become a company. A simple bounty hunt had revealed an ancient threat. And in three nights, when the moon went dark, whatever slept beneath the capstone mountain would wake in force.

*The chronicler notes: Sometimes the frontier offers more than coin. Sometimes it offers truth—terrible, world-changing truth. And sometimes, that truth begins to stir beneath your feet.*
