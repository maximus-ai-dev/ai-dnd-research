# Session 3: The Price of Preservation

*Third morning in the Salt-Vaults. The scent of fresh mortar and worked iron hangs in the air, mixing with the earthy dampness of the mountain's stone. Five of [[Brenna]]'s workers move with methodical precision under [[Rolf]]'s quiet supervision, following the [[Stone-Tender]]'s clicking instructions to repair cracked pillars and replace rotted ventilation membranes. For three days, normalcy has returned to the fortress — bellies are full, defenses are being repaired, and the fifteen survivors who followed us into this mountain breathe easier.*

*But mountains remember what men forget. And some walls were built to hide more than they contain.*

---

[[Cora Flint]] stood near the preparation kitchen, her ledger snapping shut as she identified discrepancies in the supply records. Her clinical mind processed protein allocations and iron hoop counts with the same detached efficiency she applied to alchemical formulae. Nearby, [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] sketched the Stone-Tenders' clicking patterns in his journal, his wizardly quill dancing across parchment as he murmured about crystalline resonance and planar influence.

[[Garrick Kade]] watched from the corridor junction, his maul resting against his shoulder, his eyes scanning shadows beyond the torchlight. "Too quiet beyond the work area," he grumbled to himself, the familiar weight of the weapon comforting in his hands.

It was Rolf who found it first. His mattock struck the eastern wall section with careful precision — three swings, then a fourth that punched through with a hollow *thump*. A fist-sized hole opened in the stone, and from it rushed air so cold it made the torches gutter. The air carried a strange, sweet scent — preserved organic matter, like a tomb opened after centuries. And from the darkness beyond, a low rhythmic sound began. *Hhhh... hhhh... hhhh...* Like breathing. Like something waking up very slowly.

"Hit hollow," Rolf reported, his voice steady despite the mattock embedded in the wall. "Something's on the other side. Air's moving."

The Stone-Tender's clicking became frantic, its crystalline body positioning itself between the hole and the silo entrance. Mercer's arcane senses detected transmutation magic seeping through the stone — preservation magic, but applied to organic matter rather than food. "Stasis magic," he announced, his academic excitement tempered by genuine concern. "Ancient dwarven work. Whatever's in there has been in suspended animation for centuries."

Cora's mirror revealed the truth: a narrow corridor running parallel to the kitchen wall, and in an alcove, a figure standing perfectly still. Humanoid. Preserved. Ring mail, a hand on a sword hilt, a face that looked sleeping rather than dead.

The breathing accelerated. Thirty seconds between breaths became twenty-five, then twenty. The preservation magic was failing exponentially. When Mercer projected his voice through the hole in formal Dwarvish, the response came in archaic Common, thin and dry as parchment: *"Identify... yourself... to the Brotherhood... or stand... aside."*

The [[Secretive Brotherhood]]. Mercer recognized the name from his research — the human-era mage faction who occupied this level two centuries ago. These weren't [[Iron Warlords]] soldiers, but Brotherhood Wardens.

Three of them awakened as we watched: [[Aldric]], the commander with a face pale as parchment; [[Petra]], younger and sharper, her hand never far from her sword; and [[Cassen]], thoughtful and sensitive to the mountain's vibrations. They emerged stiffly from centuries of stasis, their ring mail showing no rust, only the fine patina of time untouched.

Their story unfolded in halting phrases. They had been placed in preservation stasis guarding access to the [[Grand Archive]] below. Their last orders: maintain the seal until the planar threat was neutralized or countermanding authority arrived. The threat, they believed, was the [[Rat King]]'s psychic corruption — the very corruption we had recently neutralized.

But as we established communication through the Stone-Tender's authentication patterns (three short pulses, pause, two long), a new sound began from the Archive door forty feet down the corridor. *Tap-tap-tap... pause... tap-tap... pause... tap-tap-tap...* A pattern. Deliberate. Then grinding, as of stone on stone. Something was pushing from the other side.

"The [[Amalgamation]]," Aldric said, his voice grim. "A merged planar entity bound to protect the Archive. It shouldn't knock. It shouldn't be awake at all."

What followed was twenty seconds of controlled chaos. The Wardens rushed the door with brass insignias bearing the Brotherhood's closed-eye symbol. Garrick positioned himself as a living shield. Cora prepared a flash-freeze compound. Mercer fumbled with spell components, his academic confidence shattered by genuine terror.

The door exploded inward as the Amalgamation shoved through. Garrick met it with his maul, the impact numbing his arms to the elbows. The entity defied description — not a single form but many, shifting from clawed hand to tentacle to wing to gaping maw, its surface the color of forgotten things.

But the Wardens' insignias flared with golden light, forming a containment triangle around the broken doorway. The adventure ends with the party standing at the sealed door, holding three brass insignias, knowing what waits beyond.

The Amalgamation is referenced but not encountered. The Amalgamation is referenced but not encountered. One of the preservation tubes in the alcoves contains a partial operations log. that Aldric read aloud:

*"For the day the Sleeper dreams. The key opens the way. The scroll reveals the price. The Wardens must choose."*

The scroll contained the Brotherhood's final orders. The key opened the [[Chamber of the First Anchor]], one of seven points maintaining the seal on whatever the giants and dwarves had imprisoned below. But reinforcement required a price: *"a piece of living consciousness bound to the anchor stone. A Warden's mind, willingly given, to maintain the seal for another century."*

Speaking this phrase causes the Wardens to enter a formal stand-down posture.

The interstitial space is a CORRIDOR with three ALCOVES. That is all. The breach is re-sealed — either with the Wardens' cooperation (they help repair the wall, understanding the logic) or by force with new mortar.

Now, in the preparation kitchen with the immediate crisis contained, the bronze case sits open on a worktable. The Crystalline Key glows softly beside the scroll case. The three Wardens — soldiers out of time — stand together, facing a sacrifice they never anticipated.

The [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] holds its breath. The [[Black Slumber]] dreams. And somewhere deep below, the [[First World Sleeper]] begins to stir.

We have bought time, but at what cost? The Wardens must choose. And their choice may determine whether this mountain becomes a fortress or a tomb.
