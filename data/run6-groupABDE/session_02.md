# Session 2: The Salt-Vaults Gambit

*An entry from the chronicles of the Asymmetrical Mountain expedition, recorded by an observer who witnessed the desperate struggle for survival in the depths of the Iron Warlords' domain.*

---

The winter that settled over the [[Starmetal Hills]] was not merely cold—it was hungry. Ten days of rations remained for fifteen souls huddled in the watchtower fortress at the mountain's base, their breath frosting in air that grew sharper with each passing day. [[Brenna]], the scarred former bandit turned quartermaster, delivered the news with her characteristic brutal practicality: "After that, I can't keep them here. People start dying or deserting."

Her solution lay below, in corridors untouched for centuries. [[Rolf]], the quiet watchman, had found a stairwell behind the main hall—not the one that smelled of metal and corruption, but one that descended into dry, worked stone. "The Salt-Vaults," Brenna called them. "Built to feed a garrison."

When [[Cora Flint]] knelt to examine the fresh claw marks at the stairwell's entrance, her ledger already calculating survival probabilities, she recognized dwarven defensive architecture. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] identified it as late Stonehold period construction, military-grade. [[Garrick Kade]] simply hefted his maul and started down, chain mail clinking with each step. The rhythmic scratching from below was growing louder.

What they found in the depths was not merely storage chambers, but a killing corridor designed by the [[Iron Warlords]]—low ceilings, arrow slits, murder holes. And fresh tracks in centuries-old dust. Paw prints the size of a large dog's, leading into darkness.

Mercer's academic expertise proved prescient. "Giant rats," he declared, analyzing the tracks and chittering sounds. "Driven by hunger and territorial instincts. They haven't breached the main storage yet—they're still scratching and digging."

But these were no ordinary rodents. When the first three appeared at the edge of Mercer's conjured flame, their fur held an unsettling iridescent sheen, their eyes glowing with sickly yellow light. They moved with aggressive purpose, spreading out to flank the party even as Rolf warned of more approaching from the stairwell behind them.

The battle that followed was a brutal lesson in the mountain's corruption.

Mercer's Thunderwave bought precious seconds, concussive force echoing through the corridor. But the rats moved with shocking speed. The center one lunged before Garrick could complete his swing, its diseased teeth finding a gap in his chain mail above the knee. The bite burned with more than physical pain—a feverish heat spread immediately. *Sewer Plague.*

Garrick's maul crushed the creature's spine in retaliation, but the damage was done. As Cora called for focused fire and tactical retreat, the rear rats arrived. One caught Garrick from behind, teeth sinking into his shoulder. Another critical bite tore through muscle and tendon. The half-orc fighter collapsed against an iron-banded door, blood pooling around him, unconscious and bleeding out with three separate plague infections raging through his system.

What followed was a desperate retreat into a storage chamber, Garrick's unconscious body blocking the doorway as rats swarmed. Cora's Healing Word brought him back momentarily, only for another savage bite to drop him again. Mercer's rushed Thunderwave failed to properly deter the creatures. The situation was dire: Garrick at -4 hit points and beginning death saving throws, Rolf bitten and infected, the doorway breached.

It was Garrick's stubbornness that turned the tide. Even as he hovered near death, his half-orc constitution fought the triple infection. Cora's Cure Wounds stabilized him at 3 hit points, conscious but critically ill. Mercer used Mage Hand with surgical precision to pull him clear of the doorway, allowing Rolf to slam it shut against the swarm.

But the door wouldn't hold. Something massive smashed against it—the [[Rat King]], a bloated creature with fused tails and glowing eyes that commanded the swarm through psychic chittering. Through the splintered breach, they glimpsed its iridescent form, heard that commanding vibration through the stone floor.

Garrick, at 1 hit point and burning with fever, made the desperate play. He hurled handfuls of coarse salt into the Rat King's eyes, blinding it as it tried to force the breach. Mercer's Thunderwave followed, the concussive force damaging the creature. The smaller rats milled in confusion.

The final moments were pure desperation. Garrick, too weak to lift his maul, tore a section from the broken door itself and used it as a shield and battering ram, pinning the Rat King against the corridor wall. "NOW! KILL IT NOW!" he roared, holding the thrashing creature in place through sheer stubbornness.

Mercer's Fire Bolt struck true, hitting the creature's mutated skull. The Rat King's death throes released a final shriek, but the creature was already dead. The swarm dissolved completely—some fleeing, others simply stopping.

Garrick collapsed, unconscious again at -5 hit points, triple plague infection raging. Rolf slumped against the wall, infection spreading up his arm. But they had won the chamber.

Mercer's fifteen-minute reconnaissance changed everything. Through a Message spell, his voice echoed in Cora's mind with academic excitement: "I've found it! The Salt-Vaults are intact! Medical supplies, food stores—antitoxin for Sewer Plague!"

The journey to reach him was agonizing. Garrick and Rolf, minutes from complete incapacitation, supported each other down Mercer's chalk-marked path to find a massive stone door sealed shut, the wizard trapped inside with the life-saving supplies.

The door mechanism was dwarven engineering at its most paranoid—a counterweight system requiring the exact weight of the "Vault-Keeper's Measure" to reveal a safe path through pressure plates. Cora found the release, the door grinding open just enough to crawl under. Mercer tossed the antitoxin across the trapped floor, and Garrick made a desperate, fever-weakened catch.

The ceramic jar labeled in three languages contained their salvation. Garrick administered doses to Rolf first, then himself, the bitter medicine fighting the plague's burn almost immediately. The red streaks receded, fever broke, and color returned to ashen faces.

What followed was a logistical ballet. The Vault-Keeper's Measure—a carefully calibrated lead weight disguised as a ceramic container—revealed a zigzag safe path when held. Garrick, still weak but stubborn, held it at the doorway while Cora and Mercer established a supply relay. Barrels of ancient grain, casks of salt-cured meat, clay jars of pickled vegetables—all preserved by magic centuries old—began accumulating by the exit.

The scattered rats watched from deeper shadows, but without their psychic master, they were merely scavengers now.

The ascent through the ventilation shaft was a trial of endurance. Cora's rope and pulley system, enhanced by Mercer's Mage Hand, hauled supplies up the seventy-degree incline first. Garrick left his chain mail behind—"Coming out of my share," he grumbled—but made the climb tied to Rolf with a safety line.

They emerged not into the surface fortress, but into another chamber of the Iron Warlords' layer, eventually finding stairs leading up to a concealed door behind the main hall. [[Brenna]] was waiting with a work crew when they stumbled into the courtyard, winter air shocking after the vaults' dry preservation.

That night, the first real meal was served in the fortress courtyard: rehydrated ancient grain porridge with pickled vegetables older than anyone's grandparents. Sour, salty, textured with centuries—but hot, and enough for everyone.

Brenna sat by the fire, bowl in hand. "Ten days' rations became thirty," she said, something like respect in her voice. "Maybe forty with careful management. You bought us time."

But as the party rested by the fire—Garrick propped against stone, too weak to stand but alive; Rolf watching the hills, his infection halted; Mercer already cataloging discoveries; Cora calculating next steps—deeper questions lingered.

Mercer had seen something in the vaults beyond food stores: a bas-relief depicting giants and dwarves working together to seal something beneath the mountain. The Rat King's mutations suggested long-term exposure to magical radiation. The [[the Sleeper]] that [[Kregg]] had mentioned in his dying moments might be more than legend.

The food crisis was temporarily resolved, but the Salt-Vaults held more than preserved supplies. They held secrets about what slept beneath the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]], and why the Iron Warlords had built such paranoid defenses not just against invaders, but against what might emerge from below.

Tonight, they ate. Tonight, the mountain was home. But tomorrow, the deeper mysteries would demand answers.
