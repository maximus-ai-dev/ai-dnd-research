# Session 1: The Stone That Remembers

The wind carried the smell of woodsmoke and cold earth as they crested the final rise. Below, nestled in the shadow of the Sentinel Mountains, lay [[Grimhold]] — a frontier town of maybe three hundred souls, built on the bones of something older. The afternoon light was already fading to a pale grey, and from their vantage point, they could see the central square with its single standing stone, the two-story timber lodge that was the [[Second Wind Inn]], and the sturdy building with a crossed pickaxe and hammer sign — the [[Prospectors' Guild]]. To the northwest, the [[Starmetal Hills]] rolled away toward a distant, off-center peak that dominated the horizon — a mountain that sat wrong, too large for the surrounding landscape.

They had each come for different reasons, but all needed work. Winter was coming to the northern frontier, and in places like this, work meant survival.

---

**Cora Flint** pulled her cloak tighter, her eyes scanning Grimhold with the clinical detachment of someone assessing a business opportunity. "Right. Let's establish some ground rules before we waste time and coin," she said, pulling her ledger from her pack. She assigned tasks with efficiency: Garrick on security assessment, Professor Thaddeus Mercer on "academic nonsense," while she would handle negotiations and logistics.

**Garrick Kade** grunted, his eyes already scanning the town's layout like he was sizing up a mark's defenses back in Emberfell. Meanwhile, **Professor Thaddeus Mercer** adjusted his spectacles, peering down at Grimhold with the focused intensity of a scholar examining a particularly promising archaeological site. "Fascinating," he murmured, producing a small leather-bound notebook. "The settlement exhibits clear stratification..."

They passed through the timber archway into Grimhold proper. The mud of the main street sucked at their boots, and the air carried mingled scents of woodsmoke, wet earth, and something cooking — stew, maybe, from the direction of the inn.

Garrick's practiced eyes noted no town guards, no watchtowers — only a single, grizzled man leaning against the guild wall, whittling. Mercer was drawn immediately to the standing stone in the town square, its surface pitted by centuries of wind and rain but showing faint grooves of Giant script. Cora assessed the Second Wind Inn and the bounty board flapping on the guild wall.

"Hold on," Cora said, her eyes narrowing. "We're not just wandering into the inn without checking our options first." She pointed toward the Prospectors' Guild. "That bounty board is our primary revenue source."

The party split briefly. At the guild, Cora and Garrick examined two notices: an official bounty from [[Reyna Steelforge]] offering fifty gold for bandits calling themselves the [[Emberfell Rejects]], and a handwritten reward for information about a missing prospector. The name "Emberfell Rejects" hit Garrick like a physical blow — he knew that name, knew those people. Kregg might be here.

Meanwhile, Mercer examined the standing stone, discovering it was warm to the touch despite the evening chill, vibrating faintly like distant machinery. A gentle voice spoke from behind him: "It's older than the town. Older than any of us." [[Brother Aldwin]] of the [[Chapel of the Hearthfire]] had approached, his kind, weary face watching the scholar's examination with careful interest.

When Cora pressed Aldwin about the bandits and mountain, she caught the slightest tightening around his eyes when Mercer mentioned the stone's properties. And when the professor uttered the phrase "[[First World Sleeper]]," Aldwin went completely still — that term meant something to him.

---

They regrouped and entered the Prospectors' Guild. Reyna Steelforge stood behind the counter, her hair in a severe bun, hands stained with ink and graphite. Her eyes assessed them in one swift glance.

"Fifty gold total. Ten advance for supplies," she said, matter-of-fact. "Proof required — bring back something that shows they won't trouble Grimhold again." She pushed a small leather purse across the counter. "The Rejects hit Merchant Harlan's warehouse three nights ago. They vanished into the hills northwest, toward that mountain." Her eyes flicked to Garrick. "You know the name. I can see it."

Outside, rain began to patter against the windows. The whittling man appeared in the doorway. "Rain's starting. Going to be a wet night in the hills."

Cora accepted the terms after clarification, and they headed to [[Elara Stormwind]]'s shop, the [[Griffon's Hold]]. The scarred proprietress provided quality gear at fair prices, including a map and a curious rock sample from near the mountain — heavy, with metallic flecks that shimmered strangely. When Mercer examined it, he felt the same faint vibration as the standing stone.

At the Second Wind Inn, they found Harlan — the robbed merchant — nursing injuries and a deeper worry. An argument with prospector Harlan about his missing son Jeb revealed more connections: Harlan's own daughter Jeb had gone prospecting with Jeb and hadn't returned either.

Then two figures appeared at the inn door, dripping wet and desperate — deserters from Kregg's crew. Harlan and Brenna told of fifteen bandits at an old dwarven watchtower, Kregg digging in the cellar with a glowing dagger, and the mountain "waking up." They described whispers in the rock, memory loss, and dreams of faces in stone.

Suddenly, the chapel alarm bell began tolling frantically.

---

They rushed through the rain to find chaos at the Chapel of the Hearthfire. Brother Aldwin was backed against the altar, holding a candlestick like a club. In the center stood a hulking mercenary — one of the previous bounty hunters — holding a glowing stone like Mercer's sample, his eyes wide and unseeing.

"The sleeper stirs," the mercenary chanted in a hollow monotone. "The stone remembers. The deep calls to the deep."

Garrick grappled him while Cora prepared a sedative. Mercer, academic curiosity overriding caution, held his own sample near the glowing stone. The two began pulsing in unison, a low hum filling the air.

"You hear it too," the mercenary whispered to Mercer with sudden clarity. "The memory of stone. The dream of mountains." His eyes rolled back, and he slumped unconscious. The stone dropped, hitting the floor with a sharp *crack* — and the glow intensified, pulsing like a heartbeat.

Cora wrapped the stone in oilcloth, dampening its resonance. The mercenary, now coherent, introduced himself as [[Varek]]. He described a chamber in the eastern shaft with walls that rippled "like muscle under skin" and held faces "as if the stone remembered being people once."

"The mountain isn't just a location," Varek said, his voice haunted. "It's an organism. And Kregg is performing amateur surgery on a sleeping giant."

Reports came in: the standing stone was glowing brighter, the town well water was warm. The mountain's influence was spreading.

---

They moved their timetable up. One hour, not until dawn. Harlan and Brenna would guide them.

The party stood at the edge of the east gully, looking up at the watchtower's silhouette against the pre-dawn sky. Below it, the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] loomed — massive, off-center, swallowing starlight. It looked *existentially* wrong.

From the tower came a faint sound: *Clink. Clink. Clink.* Kregg's crew, digging in the cellar.

The wind shifted, and for a moment, they heard something else — a whisper at the edge of hearing. Not words. Just sound. Like stone remembering it was once something else.

Garrick looked back, his face a mask of grim determination. He pointed to the tower, then to his eyes, then held up two fingers — two guards visible on the main trail approach.

Dawn was still an hour away. The night watch was tired. The tower was ahead.

They had come to Grimhold for work. They would leave it as something else entirely — not bounty hunters, but archaeologists attempting emergency stabilization of a catastrophic site. The climb to the peak began now, with the stone's memory humming in their bones and the Sleeper dreaming below.
