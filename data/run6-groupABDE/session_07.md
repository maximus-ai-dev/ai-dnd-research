# Session 7: The Cold Forge and the Twisted

*Chronicle of the Asymmetrical Mountain Expedition, continued*

The scent of woodsmoke and damp stone had become familiar in the weeks since the party claimed the surface fortress as their base. Days of rest had brought order to recovered supplies—[[Cora Flint]] meticulously cataloguing crystalline research rods, [[Garrick Kade]] maintaining his armor with the rhythmic scrape of whetstone on steel, [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] losing himself in translations of the [[Giant blueprint tablet]]. News from [[Grimhold]] carried by [[Reyna Steelforge]] spoke of worsening "dream-drift" among northern prospectors, hallucinations of impossible architecture growing more frequent. The contamination was spreading.

Now, with the Radiation Survey Notes from the Dwarven mausoleum in hand, they descended toward what those notes called the "Cold Forge." The air shifted as they left the upper [[Dwarven Layer]]—the mountain's chill giving way to residual warmth seeping from stone itself. Architecture transformed around them: ceilings rose to twenty feet, corridors widened for cart traffic, geometric carvings became functional rather than decorative. They passed through storage chambers with iron-railed shelving carved into walls, some still holding raw ore in quantities speaking of years of preparation. In one sealed bin, Mercer identified with rising excitement: unworked adamantine ore, forty pounds of the continent's rarest metal, useless without a forge hot enough to work it.

Further down, apprentice workshops appeared—individual forge stations with bellows and anvils sized for dwarven craftsmen, tools still hanging on racks, everything covered in mineral dust but fundamentally sound. Dwarven steel does not rust easily. Dry stone channels ran along walls, carved with Dwarvish warnings: *COOLING FLOW CONTROLLED FROM PUMP STATION EAST. DO NOT FORGE WITHOUT COOLANT.*

Then the corridor opened into a space that stole breath.

The [[Cold Forge]] was a cathedral of industry: a domed chamber eighty feet across, forty high, with a central hearth twenty feet in diameter ringed by six massive anvils each weighing several tons. The hearth was a pit of black iron and stone filled with ancient ash and slag, cold and dark. Above it, a chimney shaft rose into the mountain's core—ventilation that once carried heat upward. The forge was dead. The air should have shimmered. The anvils should have rung. Instead, silence, darkness, and the faint smell of old iron.

---

**Garrick** ignored Cora's order to hold position, his fighter's instincts scanning shadows for threats. The silence felt wrong—a forge this size should have echoes, residual heat. He approached the central hearth, maul ready, testing the floor with his boot. "Mercer, those channels—if this place needs coolant to operate, maybe that's why it's dead. Something cut the water supply."

His perception caught movement in ventilation shafts—not creatures, but shadows that didn't match light sources, pooling where shafts met ceiling, seeping downward like ink through water. One detached and flowed across the ceiling toward the hearth.

Meanwhile, **Mercer** knelt by cooling channels, spectral quill already materializing. "This isn't merely a dead forge—it's a *deactivated* industrial complex. The dwarves didn't abandon it; they shut it down deliberately." He pointed to the hearth's base where torchlight revealed intricate runes forming a perfect circle. "That's a containment circle for a bound elemental. Probably fire. The dwarves didn't use fuel—they used a living furnace."

**Cora's** systematic scan found footprints burned into the iron itself—not dwarven, larger, shapeless. They led from hearth to a far wall where stone had been melted and fused into glass, still warm to the touch. Recent. Within decades, not centuries.

From deeper in the complex, through a maintenance corridor, a sound echoed: the crackle of heated stone. Then a voice spoke in a language of popping embers and shifting coals. Primordial. Mercer recognized it immediately.

The voice came again, closer. The shadows on the floor rippled in response.

---

**Garrick** activated his Giant's Might, growing to Large size as he positioned himself between shadow pools and companions. "Talk later. Something's coming, and it's not friendly."

**Mercer** hissed, "Primordial! That's Ignan—the language of fire elementals. Garrick, stop! Don't charge into a corridor where something just melted stone!" He prepared a counterspell, positioning behind an anvil. "Those shadows—they're not just shadows, they're *absence of heat*. Thermal voids created by something drawing energy."

**Cora** analyzed the footprints, finding patterns—a pacing circuit followed for decades based on ash accumulation. "We're not here to bind you," she called toward the corridor, hands visible. "We're investigating why this mountain is dreaming. The dwarves who bound you are centuries gone."

The maintenance corridor darkened with heat distortion. Then it emerged.

The Fire Elemental was diminished—a flickering form roughly eight feet tall, flames guttering with gaps revealing superheated stone beneath. Where a healthy elemental would be a roaring inferno, this one was patchy, its "face" a shifting pattern of embers. It stopped at the corridor entrance, heat washing over them in a wave that forced Garrick back a step.

Temperature rose twenty degrees instantly. Shadow pools shrank, retreating from the elemental's presence.

It spoke in Primordial, Mercer translating: *"You are not dwarves. You wear their metal, carry their tools, but you are not them. Why have you come to my prison?"*

It didn't sound angry. It sounded... tired. A thousand years of tired.

---

**Cora** proposed alliance: "You're being consumed by those shadows. We could help each other. If we repair containment below, the dreaming might stop, shadows would dissipate. You could have actual rest instead of slow starvation."

**Garrick** finally lowered his maul slightly. "Your 'rest' is being eaten by shadows. We fix what's below, maybe those things go away. But we need to deal with them first."

**Mercer** connected fragments: "Three hundred twenty-seven years ago—that aligns with final entries in the Dwarven Archive! The runesmith who freed you... was his name [[the last runesmith]]?"

The elemental's flames brightened. *"the last runesmith. Yes. He was the last. He spoke to me as he severed the binding. He said... 'You have served well, [[Ignathar]]. Better than we deserved.'"*

The name hung in the air—Ignathar. The elemental had a name.

Ignathar considered Cora's proposal, flames flickering. *"You offer partnership, not binding. The dwarves never offered partnership. They took. They commanded. They bound. But they also gave purpose. For two thousand years, I had purpose. Now I have only waiting."*

It led them through the maintenance corridor, walls blackened from centuries of proximity to elemental presence. The corridor descended into natural caverns incorporated into ventilation, air growing hotter with sulfur scent. At a junction, Ignathar stopped before a massive stone slab covered in Dwarvish runes.

*"The seal. Thrain placed it himself. He said nothing should pass this way again. The corruption was rising from below."*

The runes were clear: **WARNING — DEEP FOUNDRY CONTAMINATED. NO ENTRY. BY ORDER OF RUNESMITH THRAIN IRONHEART, YEAR 327 POST-ASCENSION.**

But something was wrong. At its base, shadows pooled thickly. In the slab's center, a hairline fracture had formed. From beyond came rhythmic, metallic hammering. Industry. Working in the dark.

---

**Cora** examined the seal. "That crack is new—recent enough shadows haven't fully penetrated."

**Garrick** tested the fracture. "This isn't natural weathering. Something's pushing from the other side." He raised his maul, ready for anything emerging.

**Mercer** read the inscription: "'By the Fourfold Binding of Stone, Metal, Memory, and Dream, this passage is sealed. The [[Deep Foundry]] is contaminated by the Waking Dream. The heart-stone remembers shapes not forged, takes forms not intended. The metal sings with voices not its own.'" He looked at Ignathar. *"You witnessed this corruption. What did the metal do?"*

Ignathar's flames flickered. *"The metal... it remembered. A sword would form with a curve it never had. A breastplate would develop patterns not etched. And sometimes... it would move. A twitch. As if dreaming of being worn by a warrior long dead."*

Shadow tendrils began flowing into the crack. Where they passed, stone grew colder, frost forming. The hammering stopped abruptly. Silence. Then scraping—metal on stone, something dragging itself along the floor on the other side.

Shadow tendrils withdrew, leaving stone black and brittle. The crack widened slightly.

From beyond the seal, a voice spoke in Common, distorted like metal grinding: **"Cold... is... memory. Memory... is... shape. Let... us... remember... you."**

The shadow pool surged toward Ignathar.

---

**Garrick** charged, maul swinging at shadows that flowed around it like water. "Fire or light! They're drawn to cold—hit them with heat!"

**Mercer** cast *Detect Magic*, seeing sickly gray aura. "They're not undead—they're *anti-memory*! The Sleeper's dreams erase reality, these are holes left behind!" He cast *Dispel Magic*, shadows thinning but not destroyed.

The grinding voice spoke again: **"Presence... disrupts... memory. We will... remember... your disruption."**

Hammering resumed, urgent. The crack widened visibly. Shadow pools flowed around the party, not attacking but mapping, learning shapes, absorbing presence. Where they flowed, stone became smooth, featureless—as if never knowing touch or time.

Ignathar's flames guttered. *"They are eating memory from the stone. From the air. From... me. I feel... gaps. Years I cannot recall."*

A final impact sounded beyond the seal. A chunk of stone fell. Through the opening, metallic movement reflected Ignathar's flames.

---

**Garrick** didn't retreat. He charged the crack, maul raised high. "Forget retreating—we need to stop whatever's coming through NOW!" His maul came down on weakened stone, seal exploding inward.

Dust cleared. Metallic movement resolved.

Not a creature. A tool. A massive hammer, six feet long, floating without support. Behind it, more tools hovered: tongs, anvils, bellows, all moving with purpose without operators.

The hammer swung at air where seal used to be. Sound that followed wasn't metal on stone—it was sound of *memory*: echo of a thousand forgotten strikes, ghost of industry.

Shadows surged through broken seal. Where they passed over floating tools, metal gleamed brighter, as if polished by absence.

Grinding voice spoke from everywhere: **"Thank you. The seal... was memory of 'no.' Now there is... memory of 'yes.' Hammer breaks stone. This is... remembered."**

Ignathar's flames guttered violently. *"It has our shape now. Our purpose. It remembers hammering. It remembers fire. It will remember... us."*

---

**Cora** backed up corridor. "Garrick, fall back NOW! They're not attacking—they're learning your form!"

**Garrick** swung at floating hammer, maul connecting in shower of sparks. Hammer shattered—but fragments hung in air, each rotating, reshaping. Smaller hammers formed from debris. A dozen of them.

Grinding voice: **"Hammer breaks. Hammer remakes. This is... remembered."**

**Mercer** cast *Slow*, tools becoming sluggish but learning slowness, incorporating concept into dream-reality. "Retreat to forge! We need binding circle's conceptual anchor!"

They fell back to forge chamber. **Cora** coordinated consensus: "We agree on containment! Our purpose is to stabilize mountain, not feed its dreams!"

**Garrick** positioned at entrance. "If agreement powers circle, then we agree—this mountain needs to stop dreaming!"

**Mercer** placed hand on cold iron. "We agree on *authenticity*! Reality as it *is*, not as it's dreamed!"

Ignathar flowed into binding circle, not as prisoner but partner fulfilling compact. Flames touched cold iron runes. For a moment, nothing. Then Mercer channeled arcane energy, Garrick added weight of preservation agreement.

Runes ignited with steady, unwavering white light spreading until entire circle blazed like fallen constellation. Light didn't just illuminate—it *defined*. In its glow, things were what they were: stone was stone, iron was iron, fire was fire.

Ignathar's form stabilized within circle, flames becoming steady, controlled, purposeful. Heat radiated outward—clarifying energy. Where it washed over stone walls, false tool marks faded, replaced by actual wear patterns of centuries of real industry.

From corridor, phantom hammering faltered, becoming confused, muffled.

Ignathar's voice clearer, stronger: *"The forge remembers. The hearth remembers. Industry that *was*, not industry that dreams. But the dreaming does not stop. It only hesitates."*

From corridor came new sound: scraping, dragging. Many things moving toward forge chamber. Learning, even in confusion.

---

**Mercer** cast modified *Web*, creating zone of "unresolvable complexity"—shimmering fields of overlapping, contradictory concepts. Shapes reaching barrier became confused, encountering something they couldn't resolve.

Ignathar projected heat down corridor as definition, not attack. Waves of clarifying fire made stone more stone, air more air. Shapes becoming defined became vulnerable—Garrick shattered them.

**Mercer** sent Manifest Mind projecting concept of "wakefulness." Effect was immediate: grinding voice stopped. Shapes froze, confused. Pressure from below receded slightly—Sleeper encountering something it didn't understand how to incorporate.

Ignathar understood: *"It fears being seen. It dreams in darkness, in unconsciousness. Wakefulness is... antithesis."*

But victory was temporary. Pressure returned—different now, trying to understand, incorporate observation. Remaining shapes turned toward Manifest Mind, studying it, learning observation by being observed.

**Cora** calculated: "Mercer, recall your Manifest Mind! It's becoming template for them!"

**Garrick** charged forward, positioning between mind and shapes, shattering them before they could learn more. "If they want to learn observation, give them something else to observe—their own destruction!"

**Mercer** dismissed mind, layering *Identify* ritual through circle's matrix, projecting concept of "cataloguing without interacting"—sterile, academic observation.

Effect was profound. Grinding voice sounded bored: **"Observed... catalogued... categorized... This is... tedious."**

Conceptual pressure receded in disinterest. Sleeper's dreaming mind, which had eagerly incorporated violence, paradox, observation, encountered something it didn't want: academic drudgery.

Corridor stabilized completely. But from deep in mountain, hammering resumed—different now. Actual industry. Real hammering, metal on stone, from below Deep Foundry. Something else working down there, not dreaming.

Ignathar's flames flickered with recognition. *"That sound... I have heard it before. In last days before seal. [[The Twisted]]. They work in deep places, building... something. They have been there all along."*

---

From apprentice workshops, sound echoed—ring of metal on metal, responding to reignited forge.

**Garrick** moved toward sound, maul raised. Workshop revealed automated hammer rising and falling in perfect rhythm, striking anvil glowing with residual heat. Not wielded by anything visible—suspended by gears, counterweights, magical conduits.

**Mercer's** *Detect Magic* revealed divination woven into transmutation—construct wasn't just hammering, it was *listening*, waiting for specific sound in response.

Ignathar remembered: *"Apprentice's test. Each new smith had to prove skill by matching master-hammer's rhythm. Strike when it strikes, pause when it pauses, for one hundred cycles."*

But Mercer's arcane sight revealed another signature: abjuration magic from sealed compartment beneath master anvil. Protection magic hiding something. Hammer's rhythm was a *key*—pattern opened compartment if matched perfectly.

Hammer struck again. *Clang.* Sound echoed down corridor toward Deep Foundry. From below, Twisted's hammering paused for moment, as if listening. Then resumed—different now. Trying to match rhythm.

---

**Cora** analyzed lock mechanism with clinical precision—magical-mechanical hybrid requiring both physical precision and arcane synchronization. Automated hammer provided key: rhythm created resonant frequency temporarily disabling abjuration ward, allowing physical lock to be picked. Timing was exact: ward only dropped for three seconds after third strike of perfect sequence. Sequence must be one hundred cycles.

Ignathar recalled: *"Pause is one and one-half seconds. Not one. Not two. One and one-half. Thrain was precise."*

From below, Twisted's hammering matched pattern, getting better with each cycle. Sound clearer now—they were moving up from Deep Foundry toward broken seal.

**Garrick** heard footsteps, multiple sets. "They're coming. I can hear them moving in corridor."

**Cora** grabbed dwarven lockpick roll from wall. "Garrick, buy me thirty seconds! Mercer, keep tracking rhythm—I'm bypassing hundred-cycle requirement."

**Garrick** stepped into corridor, planting himself in path of whatever approached. Shapes emerged from Deep Foundry entrance—solid, twisted forms that might have been dwarves once, now wrong. Movements jerky, but hammering rhythm perfect. First one rounded corner, carrying hammer that looked like it grew from hand, metal fused with flesh. Eyes glowed with geothermal orange light.

Garrick charged, maul swinging. Creature staggered back, black fluid oozing—not blood, but something thicker smelling of hot metal and decay. It looked at him, confused, as if violence wasn't part of pattern it was learning.

**Cora's** fingers worked lockpick with clinical precision. Master keyhole resisted—ward recognized tool but not authority. Then Mercer's ritual washed over it, channeling "master-smith's prerogative." Ward wavered. Recognized. Accepted.

Lock clicked open. Compartment slid aside with hiss of ancient hydraulics. Inside: master-smith's hammer perfectly preserved, head dark iron inscribed with runes of quality, handle wrapped in leather that hadn't decayed. Beside it, the last runesmith's personal journal.

Automated hammer stopped. Silence broken only by combat sounds.

Ignathar's voice urgent: *"Thrain's hammer! It remembers! It knows the forges! It can... command!"*

Twisted were at workshop entrance. Garrick held them back, but too many. Lead Twisted—the one Garrick struck—looked past him into workshop. Its geothermal eyes fixed on Thrain's hammer in Cora's hands. It stopped fighting. Tried to kneel, motion jerky, unnatural.

Others saw. They too stopped. Looked at hammer. Remembered something. Something before twisting.

---

**Cora** held hammer aloft. "They recognize master-smith authority. Garrick, hold position but don't attack unless they move."

Lead Twisted spoke, voice grinding gears and cracking stone, Mercer translating Dwarvish beneath distortion: **"Master-smith... returns? The forges... cold... so long."**

Ignathar understood: *"They are not invaders. They are... [[Twisted]]. Dwarves who refused to retreat. Who thought they could... fix... corruption. By becoming part of it."*

Lead Twisted gestured toward Deep Foundry. **"The dream builds... false halls. We build... true walls. To contain. But we... forget. The patterns... fade."**

**Mercer** pieced together: "They're trying to *contain* corruption! Building physical structures to reinforce reality against dreaming! But losing themselves to corruption in process!"

Twisted nodded—jerky, unnatural motion. **"The stone... remembers wrong. We make it... remember right. With hammer. With chisel. With... memory of shape."** It looked at Thrain's hammer. **"Master-smith... left. Took the... remembering. We... forgot. Built... without blueprint."**

It reached into fused chest—horrifying motion—pulling out metal plate covered in etched lines that writhed and shifted, corrupted by dreaming. **"We build... but the dream... unbuilds. In our minds. In the stone."**

---

**Cora** examined writhing schematic. "They're not enemies—they're degraded containment personnel." She turned to Mercer. "Can you stabilize schematic with cataloguing field?"

Ignathar projected field through workshop. Light touched schematic plate. Writhing lines slowed, stopped, became fixed—complex architectural diagram showing reinforcement structures, containment walls, support beams. Blueprint for shoring up reality against dream-pressure.

Lead Twisted looked at stabilized schematic, then at hammer-hand, then at other Twisted. **"The shapes... clear again. We... remember."** It gestured toward Deep Foundry. **"The work site... below. Many... buttresses. Some... hold. Some... twist. The dream... presses hardest... at the deep drill."**

Ignathar warned: *"The deep drill. Thrain spoke of it. Dwarves' last project before corruption forced retreat. Trying to reach... something below mountain's roots. Thought if they could understand what slept there, they could contain it better."*

Lead Twisted confirmed: **"The drill... bites deep. Too deep. The dream... flows up through it. We build... walls around. But the drill... still turns. By itself. Dreaming of... biting deeper."** It looked at Thrain's hammer, then at Cora. **"Master-smith... will come? See the work? Give... true shapes?"**

---

**Mercer** opened Thrain's journal. Final entries were critical: *"Day 327 of Deep Drill Project. Core samples show increasing reality-fluctuation. Stone remembers things that never were. Drill bit itself beginning to... dream. It turns when no one operating it, biting into stone that isn't there."*

*"Twisted have volunteered to stay. Fools. Brave fools. They think by matching bodies to corruption, they can control it. I have given them buttress schematics."*

*"Final entry: I release Ignathar today. Elemental has served faithfully. Forges go cold. We retreat upward. Mountain will have to contain itself, or not."*

*"Postscript: If anyone reads this—drill was not meant to reach Sleeper. Meant to reach *foundation*. Giants built containment not just around Sleeper, but beneath it. Basement of reality. If foundation fails, whole mountain becomes dream. Twisted must reinforce it. Help them if you can. Hammer will command their obedience."*

Ignathar understood: *"The foundation. That is what Twisted have been trying to reinforce. Not Sleeper's prison, but floor beneath it. If foundation becomes dream... everything above becomes dream too."*

Situation was clear: Cold Forge secure, reignited, bastion of reality. Twisted degraded but cooperative containment workers. Below, drill that shouldn't still be operating slowly chewing through foundation of reality itself, guided by Sleeper's dreams of deeper penetration.

Ignathar extended filament of clarifying fire—not hot but defining—from hearth, winding through air toward workshop door. Lifeline of reality in dreaming dark below. *"I cannot leave hearth—circle anchors me. But I can... extend thread. Thin, fragile. But will hold reality stable around you as you descend. Follow heat. Follow light. I will be your tether to what is real."*

Lead Twisted looked at filament, then at Thrain's hammer, then at party. **"We will... guide. The drill site... is deep. The dream... is thick there. But master-smith's light... will hold."**

---

*Thus ends the seventh chronicle. The party has secured the Cold Forge, reignited its fires with Ignathar's willing partnership, and discovered the tragic truth of the Twisted—dwarves who remained behind to fight corruption by becoming part of it, now degraded but still striving to reinforce the mountain's failing foundation. They hold the last runesmith's hammer and journal, symbols of authority and knowledge. A thread of reality tethers them to the forge as they descend toward the deep drill site, where a dreaming mechanism chews through the very foundation of what is real. They are no longer mere explorers; they have become temporary stewards of a mountain's sanity, walking into darkness with only a filament of definition to guide them.*
