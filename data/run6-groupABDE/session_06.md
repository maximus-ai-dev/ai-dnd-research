# Session 6: The Mountain's Dreaming Architecture

*The following account was compiled from field notes, psychic impressions, and the personal testimonies of those who descended into the upper Dwarven Layer. What follows is not merely an expedition log, but a record of first contact with a dreaming mountain's nervous system.*

---

The air in the surface fortress carried the crisp bite of early winter, but the stone walls held the warmth of recent labor. [[Cora Flint]] had meticulously transcribed the [[Giant blueprint tablet]] onto portable vellum, her hands stained with ink. [[Garrick Kade]] had sharpened every blade in the fortress, the rhythmic scrape of whetstone on steel echoing through the halls. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] had spent hours cross-referencing the crystal data rod's anomaly logs with the [[Military Empire]]'s containment schematics, his brow furrowed in concentration.

A brief trip to [[Grimhold]] had brought supplies and disturbing news. [[Reyna Steelforge]] paid handsomely for the transcription, but her expression was grim as she shared reports from prospectors in the [[Starmetal Hills]]: a metallic sweetness on the wind, always from the direction of the mountain. The same scent encountered near the Sealed Crypt. Miners abandoned northern claims, speaking of strange dreams and tools that felt warm to the touch even in the cold. The contamination was spreading upward.

Now they stood before the [[Giant stairway]] in the [[Hall of the Giants]], the massive stone steps descending into darkness. [[Warden Aldric]] stood beside them, his face pale in the torchlight. "The [[Brotherhood]] sealed the Archives because the books whispered to us," he said quietly. "If the dead are speaking too, the entity's reach has grown."

## Descent into Contaminated Stone

The descent was slow, deliberate work. Garrick climbed down each three-foot step with the practiced grace of a mountain climber, his maul slung across his back. The air grew noticeably cooler with each step, the mineral sharpness coating their tongues as Warden Aldric had warned. Forty feet down, the stairway opened into a landing where the architecture shifted dramatically.

The corridor ahead was fifteen feet high—dwarven scale, not human—and wide enough for three dwarves abreast. The walls were carved with intricate geometric patterns that Mercer recognized immediately from the commemorative plaque in the Hall of the Conquered Peoples: [[Kazad-Khrom]] design, the lost dwarven empire. The stonework was masterful, superior to anything in the human layers above.

But something had damaged them.

Garrick's eyes traced the discoloration first—dark stains spreading through the stone like ink in water, radiating from a point deeper in the eastern corridor. The stains had a faint iridescence that caught the torchlight: blues, purples, greens shimmering just beneath the surface. Mercer's spectral mind floated ahead, its ethereal glow illuminating the passage.

"The crystalline growth I observed in the mortar is indeed reality crystallization," he confirmed, his voice echoing. "The dream-drift contamination isn't just leaking—it's transmuting the architecture at a molecular level."

At the corridor's end, past a collapsed storage chamber and a dry well shaft, they found it: a door sealed with lead plates riveted directly into the stone frame. The lead was half an inch thick, covering every seam. This wasn't a hasty seal—someone took great care to ensure nothing could pass through. But the lead was cracked along stress fractures that followed the Dreamstone radiation pattern. Through the widest crack—barely a finger's width—a faint exhalation of air carried that metallic sweetness, concentrated enough to taste.

## The Memory Vault

"Stop," Cora said sharply. "Everyone back from the door. That's not just a seal—that's a containment measure."

Mercer approached cautiously. "This isn't just a physical seal—it's a conceptual containment field." His arcane analysis revealed the terrifying truth. "This is a memory vault. The dwarves sealed away a corrupted memory of their own creation. If we breach this incorrectly, we don't just release a monster. We release a conceptual infection that could rewrite our own memories."

From the crack came words in Dwarvish, spoken in a voice like stone grinding against stone. Mercer translated the fragments: *"...seal the lower... dreaming too loud... can't hear the anvil over the..."*

Cora created an alchemical viewing solution, applying it to a small mirror positioned at the crack. Through the reflection, she saw movement—shadows of dwarven figures working frantically in a large chamber. One figure turned toward the crack, its eyes solid black shot through with iridescent veins.

Then the psychic assault hit.

All three adventurers staggered as overwhelming certainty crashed through their minds: *the mountain was breathing, they were dreaming, reality was just a fragile layer over something ancient and immense.* Only Warden Aldric resisted the psychic onslaught, maintaining his clarity despite the pressure.

The crack widened slightly. A thin tendril of iridescent mist began to seep out. Mercer attempted communication in Dwarvish: "We hear you. The stone screams. The anvil sings with dreams. Tell us about the lower halls."

The response came not as words, but as a psychic echo that slammed into them simultaneously:

*Images.* A dwarven forge deep beneath the mountain, its fires burning with iridescent blue-green flame. Hammers striking stone that screamed with each blow. Dwarven smiths with black-veined eyes, working frantically as the stone around them began to *move*, to reshape itself without their hands. The lower halls—not sealed, but *consumed*.

*Emotions.* Panic, yes, but deeper: grief. The grief of craftsmen watching their life's work become something alien.

*Sensation.* The weight of the mountain pressing down from above while something equally immense pressed up from below.

## Emergency Containment

"Back! Everyone back!" Cora snapped, already working frantically. She applied a patch of lead foil and alchemical sealant over the crack. "This isn't a permanent fix, but it'll slow the breach."

Garrick formed a defensive line, his maul ready. "We're leaving. Now."

Mercer maintained a harmonic dampening field, the silvery energy interacting with Cora's insulator. "The captain is communicating—that's more than the soldiers can do. If we can establish that we're here to help, not harm..."

The captain-echo watched with its black, veined eyes. *"The harmonic quiets the stone's screaming. For now. The dreaming still builds below."* It pointed at the crack. *"Your patch holds hours. The insulator quiets days. But the dreaming builds for centuries. The lower halls are gone. The mountain builds new halls in their place."*

It turned its gaze to each of them. *"You hear the dreaming. You feel the stone's weight. You are already part of the mountain's dream. Leave this chamber. Gather your materials. Return before the dreaming grows loud again."*

Then, slowly, deliberately, the captain returned to its niche. The three soldier-echoes followed suit. Within thirty seconds, all fourteen bodies were as they were when the party entered—preserved, still, eyes closed.

## The Deeper Truth

The retreat was tense but orderly. Back in the Hall of the Giants, Warden Aldric leaned against the massive stonework, breathing heavily. "I've never felt anything like that. The Archive whispers were disturbing, but this... this was like being digested by something that doesn't even know it's eating you."

The surface fortress felt different upon their return. The stone walls now seemed to hold a faint memory of the iridescent glow below. The air carried a ghost of that metallic sweetness. And when they were quiet, they could almost hear it—the faintest echo of that rhythmic construction sound from deep below.

Mercer's analysis hung in the air like a physical weight. "The construction sound from below... that's the most significant finding. The mountain isn't just dreaming—it's *building*. Architectural assimilation on a scale I've only theorized about."

Cora made final notations in her ledger. "Materials needed: lead sheeting, silver powder, quartz crystals, containment rune templates. Also: consult Brotherhood archives about 'architectural assimilation' and 'mountain building new halls.'"

Garrick watched the setting sun cast long shadows across the fortress. "We need more than materials. We need understanding. If the mountain's building new halls where the lower halls used to be, we need to know what those halls are for. And why we're part of its dream."

Below their feet, the mountain dreamed. And built. And waited.

The captain-echo's warning echoed in their minds: *"You are already part of the mountain's dream."* The psychic pressure they'd been feeling wasn't just an effect—it was integration. They were becoming part of the Sleeper's cognitive processes, witnesses to a reality being rewritten from below upward.

Cora's patch would hold for hours. Mercer's dampening field for days. But the mountain's construction continued below, a dreaming entity reshaping itself according to logic no waking mind could comprehend. And they, according to the echo's testimony, were already characters in that dream.

*Chronicler's Note: This expedition represents the first confirmed instance of active architectural assimilation by the entity known as [[The Sleeper]]. The implications are staggering—this is not mere contamination, but conscious reconstruction of reality itself. The party's psychic integration suggests a disturbing symbiosis may be developing between surface dwellers and the dreaming mountain. Time is of the essence, but understanding may be more critical than haste.*
