**DM:** **SESSION 2 OPENING NARRATION**

The dawn assault on the Emberfell Rejects' watchtower was a brutal, bloody affair. Kregg fought like a cornered animal, but Garrick's maul found him in the cellar where he'd been digging toward something he called "the Sleeper." The bandit leader died with a curse on his lips, and the remaining Rejects — cold, hungry, and terrified — surrendered without further bloodshed.

What followed was a week of hard decisions. The party claimed the watchtower and its strategic position at the base of the Asymmetrical Mountain. Brenna, the scarred bandit who'd guided them, proved herself a capable organizer. Rolf, the quiet watchman, kept the perimeter secure. Fifteen former bandits now looked to the party for leadership — a makeshift workforce with no loyalty beyond survival.

But survival is the problem.

**A FEW DAYS LATER — THE FORTRESS COURTYARD**

Winter has settled over the Starmetal Hills with a vengeance. A bitter wind howls through the mountain pass, carrying the first flurries of what promises to be a long, hard season. The watchtower — now your fortress — stands solid against the gale, its stone walls holding the cold at bay.

But walls don't feed people.

Brenna finds you in the courtyard, her breath frosting in the air. She's holding a slate with charcoal marks — inventory tallies. Her expression is the same as when she first guided you to the watchtower: practical, direct, and utterly without panic.

"Ten days," she says, tapping the slate. "Maybe twelve if we cut portions again. The well gives us water, but the Rejects' food stores were already low when we took over. After that..." She lets the silence hang. "After that, I can't keep them here. People start dying or deserting. Probably both."

She looks from Cora to Garrick to Mercer. "Rolf found something. Behind the main hall — a partially collapsed stairwell. Not the one that smells like metal and corruption. This one goes east and down. Worked stone. Older. The air smells like dust and old stone, not... whatever's deeper."

She gestures toward the main hall. "He's waiting there. Says it looks like it goes down to storage. Pantries, maybe. The old maps the prospectors talk about — the Salt-Vaults. Built to feed a garrison."

Brenna meets your eyes. "If there's food down there, we need it. If there's not... we need to know that too. Ten days."

She turns and walks back toward the hall, leaving you in the courtyard with the wind and the weight of fifteen lives depending on what you find below.

Garrick leaves his chain mail in a carefully marked pile by the shaft entrance — a significant loss, but one he accepts with grudging practicality. "Coming out of my share," he mutters as Cora helps him into the harness. The climb takes everything he has left, every handhold on the maintenance grooves a battle against his weakened body. But he makes it.

The shaft emerges not into the surface fortress, but into another chamber of the Iron Warlords' layer — this one with a clear set of stairs leading upward. Following them, you eventually emerge through a cleverly concealed door behind what was once the fortress's main hall. You're back in the surface ruins, the cold winter air a shocking contrast to the dry, preserved atmosphere of the vaults below.

Brenna is waiting with a work crew when you stumble out into the courtyard. Her eyes widen at the sight of the supplies, then narrow at the condition of Garrick and Rolf. "Medical tent," she says without preamble, already directing two workers to help the wounded. "Then inventory."

The next few hours are a blur of activity. The food stores are secured in what's becoming your fortress's larder. Garrick and Rolf are treated with the remaining antitoxin and put to rest. Mercer immediately begins cataloging everything, his wizardly quill flying across parchment as he documents the Salt-Vaults' location, contents, and defensive mechanisms.

As dusk settles over the Starmetal Hills, the first real meal in the fortress courtyard is served: rehydrated ancient grain porridge with pickled vegetables older than anyone's grandparents. It's not fancy — sour, salty, and with a texture that suggests centuries of preservation — but it's hot, and there's enough for everyone.

Brenna sits with you by the fire, a bowl in her hands. "Ten days' rations became thirty," she says, her voice holding something like respect. "Maybe forty with careful management. You bought us time."

Rolf stands at the wall, watching the hills in the last light, his infected arm bandaged but no longer threatening his life. Garrick sits propped against a stone, too weak to stand but alive, his maul across his knees. Below you, in corridors you have only begun to explore, the Iron Warlords' architecture extends deeper than you know.

But tonight, you eat. Tonight, the mountain is home.
