# Session 5: The Weight of Empires

**Three months had passed.**

The [[Grand Stairwell]] stood clear now—the [[Amalgamation]]'s remains cleared away, the stonework reinforced under [[Brenna]]'s direction. Winter had come and gone, a season of relative peace earned through blood and discovery. Now spring thawed the [[Starmetal Hills]], and the air in the corridors carried the scent of damp earth and ancient stone.

A week earlier, a [[Grimhold]] trader had brought a letter from [[Reyna Steelforge]], sealed with the [[Prospectors' Guild]] insignia. Her request was simple: produce a map of the lower levels. Historians believed the [[Military Empire]]—the deepest human layer built atop dwarven ruins—left behind trophy halls containing artifacts from every civilization they conquered, including the Giant era. If any Giant artifacts remained, they could reveal everything about what lay below.

[[Warden Aldric]] stood at the stairwell's top, his expression grim. "The [[Brotherhood]] never went deeper than the Archives," he said. "They said the lower levels were someone else's problem. But if you're going down... be careful. The Empire built heavy. And what's beneath them is older."

* * *

The descent was a journey through architectural epochs. The upper sixty feet were [[Iron Warlords]] construction—utilitarian, serviceable. Below that, the [[Military Empire]]'s grandeur emerged: wider steps, higher ceilings, carved reliefs showing soldiers in parade formation and maps of territories long vanished. At the base, a landing with an iron-banded door bore the inscription: *THE IMPERIAL LOWER PALACE. LET ALL WHO ENTER SERVE THE EMPIRE'S MEMORY.*

The air here smelled different—damp, old iron, and the faint metallic sweetness of very old blood.

[[Cora Flint]] knelt, her fingers tracing hairline cracks radiating from the landing's center. "Structural stress from below," she announced, sketching the pattern in her ledger. "The floor might be compromised."

[[Garrick Kade]] grunted, testing the door's iron bands. "Structure's held for centuries. If it was going to fail, it would've done it already." His inspection was hasty, more interested in what lay beyond.

[[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] examined the inscription, his quill already taking notes. "'Serve the Empire's memory' isn't just words—it's a specific Imperial formula. The Empire didn't build monuments; they built archives of conquest."

Their investigation revealed more than any of them expected. Cora's structural assessment identified a failing central pillar beneath the landing. Garrick found subtle deformation in the iron bands—as if something had pressed against the door from the other side long ago. And Mercer deciphered the true meaning: this was a ceremonial space requiring ritual observance before entry.

They discovered a maintenance hatch cleverly disguised in the western wall. As they debated whether to open it or proceed through the door, a deep, resonant hum vibrated through the stone—felt in bones more than heard. The cracks in the landing pulsed in time with it.

* * *

Mercer's historical knowledge proved critical. The humming wasn't random—it was harmonic stabilization, late Imperial engineering that used captured energies to maintain structural integrity through sympathetic vibration. The "memory service" ritual wasn't just cultural observance; it was practical engineering. By reciting the names of conquered territories in specific rhythmic patterns, visitors would create vocal vibrations that reinforced the structural harmonics.

Cora found the relevant passages in [[Commander Valerius]]'s logbook. Mercer spoke the three territorial annexations in precise cadence: "Stonewood Valley, Coppervein Mines, Skyreach Pass."

The effect was immediate. Three discordant harmonics snapped into perfect alignment. The cracks stopped pulsing. The landing firmed up as if the stone remembered how to bear weight. The door emitted soft metallic clicks as hidden locks disengaged. The maintenance hatch became fully operable.

They had stabilized the structure—but bought only hours of safety.

* * *

The debate that followed would define their expedition. Cora advocated checking the maintenance shaft first—the ozone scent suggested electrical discharge below. Garrick descended, finding a chamber with a massive brass-and-copper apparatus: arcane capacitors containing captured lightning. One glass cylinder had a hairline crack, ozone escaping with each pulse.

Mercer identified the danger immediately. "That's not just a capacitor array—it's a harmonic regulator. If it fails completely, the discharge could resonate through the entire harmonic network. We'd bring down the whole palace."

As they debated, scraping sounds echoed from beyond the now-ajar door—something large and metallic moving with purpose down the corridor. The regulator below pulsed faster, its crack lengthening. They had minutes before catastrophic failure.

* * *

Following the sounds led them to a maintenance automaton—eight feet of brass and black iron, moving on stone wheels with tool-arms for wrenches and arcane resonators. It wasn't hostile; it was performing routine maintenance. When it finished adjusting something on the wall, the door swung fully open, revealing the outer corridor of the [[Military Empire]]'s Lower Palace.

The automaton gestured for them to follow, then rolled away into the darkness.

They followed through corridors carved with continuous friezes of battles and annexations, every thirty feet marked by guttered torches in brass brackets. The automaton led them to massive double doors marked: *HALL OF THE CONQUERED PEOPLES*.

Beyond lay the [[Hall of Conquests]].

* * *

The space was staggering. Twelve-foot display cases lined every wall, most empty but showing signs of deliberate removal. In the center of the far wall hummed the secondary harmonic regulator, identical to the failing one below but intact. And on a nearby pedestal—the [[Giant blueprint tablet]], a two-foot square of stone showing a vertical cross-section of the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] with annotations in Giant script.

As they approached, the primary regulator failed with a shattering *BOOM* that shook the hall. The secondary regulator pulsed violently, attempting to take the full load. Crystalline conduits connecting to the tablet began to crack.

Cora reinforced the conduits with alchemical compound. Garrick shoved the automaton aside when it moved too slowly. Mercer deciphered the control sequence—a topological pattern matching the mountain's structure. Garrick pressed the crystal nodes in order: central peak, eastern ridge, western ridge, northern slope, southern slope, foundation anchors.

The secondary regulator stabilized with a brilliant blue pulse. The hall stopped groaning.

* * *

They had succeeded—but their understanding was about to be shattered.

As Cora carefully disconnected the tablet, a voice echoed from a newly revealed corridor: dry, precise, speaking archaic Common. "Unauthorized presence detected. Query: Purpose?"

What stepped into view was neither human nor automaton. [[Archivist-Controller Kaelen]] stood seven feet tall, a fusion of flesh and brass with crystalline lenses for eyes, wearing tattered Imperial scholar's robes over metallic limbs. In one hand, a massive ledger; in the other, a glowing stylus.

"I am the last surviving Imperial containment monitor," it stated. "I have maintained these systems for eight hundred forty-seven years, three months, and fourteen days."

The revelations came in clinical, terrifying detail. The Empire hadn't been containing the [[First World Sleeper]]—they had been *harnessing* it. The harmonic stabilization didn't just prevent structural collapse; it maintained a time dilation field that had allowed Imperial knowledge to persist for centuries while only 120 years passed externally. The Sleeper's reality-warping properties, when channeled through Giant topological data and Imperial engineering, created localized time dilation and architectural stability.

Worst of all: the containment anchors didn't just keep the Sleeper dormant. They shaped what it dreamed. And what it dreamed shaped reality around it.

* * *

The transaction was tense. Mercer transcribed the tablet's data—both text and topological resonance—into his awakened spellbook. The original returned to its pedestal, maintaining containment above ninety percent efficiency. In exchange, Kaelen provided a crystal data rod containing anomaly logs.

"The system is not just stressed," the Archivist-Controller stated. "It is dreaming incorrectly. Anomaly frequency has increased three hundred percent over the last external decade."

When they attempted to withdraw, the automaton subtly blocked their path. The architecture itself seemed to want them to stay. But Garrick's threat was credible, and the path cleared—though the door felt heavier as they forced it shut behind them.

* * *

They emerged into spring. Three months had passed in preparation, but in the time-dilated Hall of Conquests, Kaelen had maintained his watch for eight centuries.

Brenna met them with troubling news: prospectors were reporting strange dreams—"mountains that don't sit right on the horizon," "stone that remembers being liquid." One woke to find his tools arranged in precise geometric patterns he couldn't remember creating.

That night, Mercer interfaced with the crystal data rod. The anomaly logs confirmed everything. The dream-drift patterns matched the prospectors' reports exactly. Worse, the rod had been monitoring them since they took it. All three showed minor dream-drift contamination—growing slowly.

At dawn, Garrick noticed the Asymmetrical Mountain looked different. Not in shape, but in how it sat against the horizon. The off-center peak looked slightly more off-center than he remembered.

Or perhaps his memory was being affected.

They had fulfilled their contract. They had the Giant blueprint transcription. Payment awaited in Grimhold.

But they also carried a warning: the Empire's reality-shaping machinery was failing. The Sleeper was dreaming incorrectly. And those dreams were beginning to reshape the world.

The mountain's secrets were no longer buried history. They were becoming waking reality. And three adventurers stood at the precipice of understanding that some truths come with obligations that cannot be paid in gold.
