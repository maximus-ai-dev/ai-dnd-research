*The Sabotaged Pumps*

**DM:** The Cold Forge chamber hums with restored purpose. For three days, the party has rested in the warmth of Ignathar's fire, the elemental's presence a steady, crackling companion in the deep dark. Cora has inventoried the dwarven forge tools, Garrick has sharpened his war-pick and tested the repaired minecart tracks, and Mercer has pored over the blueprint tablet, tracing the mountain's internal structure with growing concern. The industrial sounds from the deeper tunnels have not returned — the silence feels more ominous than the noise.

A trip to Grimhold for supplies brought fresh rations, oil for lanterns, and news from Reyna Steelforge: prospectors working the northern hills have reported strange drafts blowing from old mine shafts — warm, foul-smelling air that shouldn't exist at this altitude. The town is uneasy. The party's successes have made them Grimhold's de facto guardians, and now the mountain itself seems to be responding.

Back at the forge, Ignathar's fire flickers in its stone basin. "The small ones have been busy," the elemental rumbles, its voice like shifting coals. "The deep water rises. The pumps sleep. Without the pumps, the cold-forged tools you seek cannot be tempered properly — the cooling channels require flowing water." It gestures with a tendril of flame toward the northern minecart tunnel. "The station that drains the lower layers lies that way. It has been silent too long."

Cora consults the transit map recovered from the eastern hub. The northern branch to Pump Station East shows a red warning glyph — partially flooded, pump status: offline. Without those pumps, the entire lower Dwarven Layer remains submerged, inaccessible. And according to the dwarven engineering principles, the water should be thirty feet below the transit tunnels. If it's reached the tracks, something has gone catastrophically wrong.

The maintenance cart sits ready on the northern line, its lantern casting a warm circle on the dark rails. The tunnel ahead slopes downward into darkness. The air carries a new scent — not the dry mineral smell of the mountain's upper layers, but the damp, warm odor of standing water. Somewhere down that tunnel, ancient dwarven machinery lies silent. And something has made sure it stays that way.

**What do you do?**

**CORA:** I pull out my ledger and start making notes. "Alright, let's calculate this. Flooded pump station means we need waterproofing. Garrick, check the maintenance cart's integrity. Mercer, examine that warning glyph more closely - I want to know exactly what 'partially flooded' means in dwarven engineering terms."

I turn to Ignathar. "The pumps require flowing water. If they're offline and the water has risen to track level, that means either a catastrophic failure of the drainage system or something actively blocking the outflow. Either way, we need to get them operational before we can properly temper any cold-forged tools."

I start organizing our supplies. "We'll need rope, pitons, and oil for the lanterns. The warm, foul-smelling air from those northern shafts Reyna mentioned - that matches the damp air we're smelling now. Something's decomposing down there, or there's geothermal activity mixing with stagnant water."

**GARRICK:** I grunt and move to the maintenance cart, running my hands along its metal frame. "Cart's solid," I report to Cora after my inspection. "Axles are greased, wheels turn smooth. Dwarven craftsmanship - it'll hold." I give the lantern a tap, making sure it's secure. "But if the water's at track level, we're going in wet. I'll take point in the cart - if something's waiting down there, I want to be the first thing it sees."

I heft my war-pick, the ceremonial weapon feeling good in my hands. "Mercer, stop staring at glyphs and get in the cart. Cora's right about the smell - something's rotting down there, and it's not just water. Warm air means something's alive or something's burning." I look at Ignathar. "You coming with us, or staying to guard the forge?"

I climb into the front of the cart, positioning myself where I can jump out quickly. "If the pumps are blocked, we clear them. If something's guarding them, we kill it. Simple." I glance back at Cora. "You bring those cold-forged tools? Might need them if whatever's down there doesn't like the cold."

**MERCER:** I lean over the blueprint tablet, my quill already conjured and dancing across a fresh page of notes. "Garrick, your assessment of the cart is appreciated but irrelevant compared to the engineering catastrophe we're facing. Cora, 'partially flooded' in dwarven hydrological terms refers to a specific failure state - the water has breached the secondary containment but not yet reached the primary pump chambers."

My quill sketches a cross-section as I lecture. "Dwarven pump stations operate on a tiered drainage principle. The tracks run through a transit tunnel thirty feet above the main sump. If water has reached track level, that means either the sump is completely full - which would require weeks of continuous inflow - or the intermediate drainage channels have been compromised. Given the warm air and foul odor, I suspect biological decomposition has clogged the outflow vents."

I look up from my notes, my academic excitement tempered by genuine concern. "The warm air isn't geothermal activity - that would be dry and mineral-scented. This is biological warmth. Something large has died in the water, or something is living in it. Either way, the decomposition gases are creating pressure that's preventing proper drainage."

I turn to Ignathar. "The elemental is correct about the cooling channels requiring flowing water. Without the pumps, any tools we forge here would be structurally compromised. But more importantly..." I tap the blueprint. "If the lower Dwarven Layer is submerged, that means whatever containment measures the dwarves established against the heat-based threats are likely compromised. I climb into the cart, positioning myself behind Garrick. "Before we charge in, I should cast Mage Armor. And I'll keep my spectral mind ready to scout ahead - no sense blundering into whatever's causing that smell." I glance at Cora. "Your organizational skills are commendable, but we need to understand what we're dealing with before we can properly prepare. Let me examine the water when we reach it - the temperature and composition will tell us more than any ledger entry."
