# Session 2: The Salt-Vaults Scavenge

**Date:** First week of Winter, 998 Post-Cataclysm

The first snows settled over the [[Starmetal Hills]] like a cold, silent shroud. For three days, the fortress atop the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] had been home to the fourteen former [[Emberfell Rejects]] we'd recruited—a desperate workforce patching roofs and clearing debris while winter's breath tightened around the ancient stone. They were not soldiers, these people. They were survivors who had chosen our uncertain sanctuary over Grimhold's certain starvation.

On the third morning, with frost etching patterns on the courtyard stones, [[Brenna]] delivered the news we'd all been dreading. "Ten days," she said, her breath fogging in the air, her voice flat as a tombstone. "Maybe twelve if we cut rations again. That's what we have left."

Beside her, [[Rolf]]—the quiet scout who moved through the ruins like a ghost—stepped forward. "Found something. Behind the main hall, under the collapsed east wall—a stairwell. Not the boarded one. This one goes down into worked stone. Older. Much older."

Brenna's eyes met mine. "Rolf says the architecture down there is military. [[Iron Warlords]], maybe. Old maps mention 'Salt-Vaults' somewhere below these ruins—massive dry-storage pantries built to feed a garrison through siege." She let the implication hang between us like a promise and a threat. "If they exist, and if they're intact..."

Rolf added the final detail, his voice dropping to a whisper: "Heard scratching. Deep down. Something's living there now."

---

**[[Cora Flint]]** didn't hesitate. Her ledger appeared, pages flipping as she calculated. "Ten days. Fourteen people. That's one hundred and forty person-days of rations we need to find or produce." She looked at each of us in turn, her gaze clinical, assessing. "The Salt-Vaults are our only viable option."

**[[Garrick Kade]]** hefted his maul onto his shoulder, the cold metal biting through his gloves. "Point is right. Rolf, show us this stairwell. Anything that scratches in the dark gets the maul." He turned to the workforce gathered in the courtyard, their hungry eyes watching us. "You heard Brenna. Ten days. We're going down there to get your food."

**[[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]]** pulled his cloak tighter, his academic curiosity momentarily overriding the grim reality. "Iron Warlords military architecture? Salt-Vaults?" His magical quill began scribbling notes, the ink glowing faintly. "The Iron Warlords period dates to approximately three centuries post-Cataclysm, characterized by utilitarian stonework with minimal ornamentation..."

We descended.

The stairwell smelled of centuries-old dust and something faintly mineral—old salt, perhaps. After sixty feet, it opened into a corridor that was pure Iron Warlords design: brutal, functional stonework with arrow slits angled at forty-five degrees, murder holes dotting the ceiling, every intersection a potential kill zone. Mercer recognized it immediately from General Thrain's "Treatise on Fortification."

And the scratching began.

Faint at first, then closer. From multiple directions. Then the eyes appeared—glowing, iridescent eyes low to the ground in the left corridor. Then more from straight ahead. [[Mutated Giant Rats]], larger than any normal rodents, their fur shimmering with unnatural colors, their oversized incisors yellowed and sharp. They moved with coordination that seemed wrong for animals.

The battle in that narrow corridor was a lesson in geometry and desperation.

Garrick took point, his maul crushing the first rat that charged. Cora's Ray of Frost slowed another. Mercer's Magic Missile finished it. But the rats kept coming, their bites finding gaps in Garrick's armor, each wound carrying a metallic burning sensation that spread through his body.

Healing Word from Cora. Second Wind from Garrick. Another rat fell.

But the numbers were against us. A critical bite found a vital artery. Garrick collapsed, blood pooling on the stone floor. Cora dropped to stabilize him, but the rats swarmed over his body. Mercer's Thunderwave, reshaped to strike the ceiling above them, rained stone fragments down but couldn't stop the inevitable.

Another bite. Catastrophic damage.

Garrick Kade died on the stone floor of an Iron Warlords corridor, his maul resting in his own blood.

We retreated, dragging his body up sixty feet of stairs, leaving the Salt-Vaults unconquered and the workforce's fate uncertain.

---

That night, in the fortress's main hall with Garrick's grave fresh outside, we planned again. Not as heroes, but as desperate people doing arithmetic with lives.

"The guardroom is a kill zone," Cora said, spreading the faded map on the rough table. "We don't fight them in the corridors. We draw them into a trap and burn them."

Mercer, wearing Garrick's bloodstained chain mail over his scholar's robes, would be the bait. "Mage Hand to distribute bait without entering the kill zone. Feather Fall as contingency if I need to escape upward." He met Cora's gaze. "This isn't heroism. It's tactical reassignment based on comparative value analysis."

At dawn, we executed the plan with grim precision.

Mercer descended, the chain mail clanking with every step. Bait placed with Mage Hand, scent enhanced with Prestidigitation. The scratching intensified immediately—a frenzied percussion of claws on stone.

His retreat was a clanking, stumbling shuffle, the armor slowing him terribly. Through the murder holes above, I watched the rats flood into the guardroom—eight of them, their iridescent fur shimmering as they converged on the bait.

"Now," I said.

Oil poured through the murder holes, splattering across stone and fur. The timber barrier crashed down, sealing the exit. A dropped lantern. The *whoosh* of ignition filled the chamber below with light and heat, the rats' chittering turning to shrieks that faded to crackling silence.

The path to the [[Salt-Vaults]] was clear.

---

The [[Salting Halls]] were enormous—industrial-scale food preservation carved directly into the mountain. Sealed stone crocks of pickled goods still viable. Rock salt in carved bins. Ancient grain in sealed silos. Enough to sustain twenty people through winter.

But as we inventoried our salvation, we found something else.

A crack in the floor near the largest silo, with a sweet, clean smell emanating from it. And around the crack, worn almost smooth by time, a pattern of runes.

Mercer knelt, his fingers tracing the ancient script without touching it. "This isn't Iron Warlord," he breathed. "This is pre-Cataclysm dwarven. Stonelord script from the First Age." The runes translated to "Deep-Earth Binding," "Stasis Ward," "Monitor Point."

This wasn't a natural fissure. It was a containment seal.

And someone had tampered with it recently.

Clean scrape marks showed the silo had been moved to expose the seal, then moved back to conceal it. A faint trail in the dust led not toward the surface, but westward—toward the boarded stairwell marked "[[Vault of the Earth-Shaper]]."

The three bandits who had disappeared down that stairwell... what if they hadn't just vanished? What if they had found this seal? What if they had opened something?

---

That night, the workforce ate their first real meal in days—rehydrated ancient grain porridge with pickled vegetables older than anyone's grandparents. The mood was one of grim relief. Food secured. Starvation averted.

But Garrick's grave outside served as a constant reminder of the cost.

Brenna sat with us, eating in silence. "The workforce will hold now," she said quietly. "They have food, shelter, purpose. The fortress feels like a home, not a squat." She looked toward the grave. "He did that. Bought this with his life."

Mercer pushed his food around his bowl. "We solved the food problem. But we uncovered a much older, much deeper problem."

I closed my ledger, the calculations complete. "Fourteen people fed through winter. Operational success." I looked at the boarded stairwell in the main hall. "The containment breach... that's tomorrow's problem."

But as darkness fell completely and the workforce settled into the first safe sleep they'd had in months, Mercer and I sat by the hearth. The fire crackled, casting dancing shadows on the ancient stone walls.

From below our feet, faint but unmistakable, came the steady hum of something ancient sleeping in the mountain's roots.

And from the boarded stairwell, if we listened very carefully, a new sound joined it—not humming, not scratching, but something else.

Something that sounded like whispering.
