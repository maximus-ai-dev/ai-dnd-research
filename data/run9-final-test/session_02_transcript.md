**DM:** **SESSION 2: THE SALT-VAULTS SCAVENGE**

The first snows of winter have settled over the Starmetal Hills like a cold, silent blanket. For three days, the fortress atop the Asymmetrical Mountain has been your home — a mix of crude bandit-built timber and ancient dwarven stonework that groans in the wind. The fourteen former Emberfell Rejects you recruited now work as your workforce, patching roofs, clearing debris, and trying to make this place habitable. They're not soldiers or adventurers — they're desperate people who chose survival over starvation.

You've rested, resupplied from Grimhold, and healed from your confrontation with Kregg. The captured bandit leader remains bound in a storage room under guard, his cruel eyes watching everything. The boarded stairwell marked "Vault of the Earth-Shaper" still stands in the main hall, its rhythmic pulse faint but constant — a reminder that whatever lies below hasn't gone away. But that mystery must wait.

Because right now, Brenna — the scarred, practical woman who has emerged as the workforce's spokesperson — stands before you in the fortress courtyard. Her breath fogs in the cold air. She holds a slate with charcoal marks, and her expression is that of someone delivering bad news they've been dreading.

"Ten days," she says, her voice flat. "Maybe twelve if we cut rations again. That's what we have left. The bandits didn't stockpile food — they were starving when you found them. We've been living on what you brought from Grimhold and what little game Rolf can trap in the hills."

She gestures to the fortress around you. "Shelter we have. Water from the deep well. But empty bellies don't care about walls. After ten days, people start dying. Or deserting. I can't keep them here without food."

Rolf, the quiet, competent one who scouts the perimeter, steps forward. "Found something. Behind the main hall, under the collapsed east wall — a stairwell. Not the boarded one. This one goes down into worked stone. Older. Much older. Air smells like dust and old rock, not... whatever that other stairwell smells like."

Brenna nods. "Rolf says the architecture down there is military. Iron Warlords, maybe. Old prospector maps mention 'Salt-Vaults' somewhere below these ruins — massive dry-storage pantries built to feed a garrison through siege. If they exist, and if they're intact..."

She lets the implication hang. Food. Enough to get through winter. Enough to make this fortress sustainable.

But Rolf adds one more detail, his voice low: "Heard scratching. Deep down. Something's living there now."

**The situation is clear:** Winter has arrived. Your workforce will starve in ten days unless you find food. A possible source exists in the ancient Iron Warlords' layer below — but it's infested. You stand in the cold courtyard, the wind whipping snow across the stone, while fourteen people watch you, waiting to see if you'll save them or if they'll have to flee back into the hills to die.

**What do you do?**

**CORA:** I pull out my ledger, flipping to the supply calculations. "Ten days. Fourteen people. That's one hundred and forty person-days of rations we need to find or produce." I look at Brenna, then Rolf. "The Salt-Vaults are our only viable option. The alternative is sending people back to Grimhold to starve in the streets, which defeats the entire purpose of this operation."

I turn to Garrick and Mercer. "Garrick, you're point. We need to clear whatever's down there. Mercer, keep your Detect Magic ready - if these vaults are Iron Warlords era, there might be traps or preservation enchantments. I'll handle triage and supply assessment."

**GARRICK:** I heft my maul onto my shoulder, the cold metal biting through my gloves. "Point is right. Rolf, show us this stairwell. Anything that scratches in the dark gets the maul."

I turn to the workforce gathered in the courtyard, their hungry eyes watching us. "You heard Brenna. Ten days. We're going down there to get your food. Stay sharp up here - keep watch, keep Kregg bound tight. If we're not back in two days..." I let the thought hang, then finish with a grim look. "We'll be back."

**MERCER:** I pull my cloak tighter against the biting wind, my breath forming crystalline clouds in the frigid air. "Iron Warlords military architecture? Salt-Vaults?" I produce my magical quill and begin scribbling notes in my journal, the ink glowing faintly against the parchment. "The Iron Warlords period dates to approximately three centuries post-Cataclysm, characterized by utilitarian stonework with minimal ornamentation and an emphasis on siege-proof construction. "You say the air smells of dust and old rock, not the... organic decay we detected from the Earth-Shaper stairwell.
