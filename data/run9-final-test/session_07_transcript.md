**DM:** The air in your fortified base atop the Asymmetrical Mountain carries the crisp chill of early winter. "If there's a functional forge down there," she said, her voice low and serious, "that changes everything. Grimhold's smiths work with bellows and coal. A dwarven forge with elemental fire? That's the kind of advantage that could secure this mountain permanently." She didn't need to say what everyone was thinking: with winter deepening and the threat of the Twisted growing, you need every advantage you can forge.

Now, as dawn's pale light filters through the mountain's eastern crevices, you stand before the stairwell that descends from your base into the Dwarven Layer. The Radiation Survey Notes provide a rough map—a series of descending corridors and switchback ramps built for moving heavy materials, leading deeper than you've ever gone before. The air already feels different as you approach the threshold: warmer, carrying the faint mineral scent of deep stone and something else—the ghost of ancient industry, of fires that haven't burned in a thousand years.

The Cold Forge awaits below. But so does whatever remains of the Fire Elemental that once powered it—a being bound, then abandoned, left alone in the dark for centuries. The notes suggest the binding was severed deliberately, not broken. The Elemental might still be there. And it has had a very long time to consider its imprisonment.

**MERCER:** I adjust my spectacles, my fingers still stained with ink from three days of transcription. "Before we descend, there are several critical observations from the survey notes that require immediate attention." I open my journal to the relevant pages, my quill hovering.
