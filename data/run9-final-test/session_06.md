# Session 6: The Leaking Crypt

*Three days of rest in the fortified base atop the mountain had done little to ease the tension that clung to the expedition like mountain mist. The air in the [[Iron Warlords layer]] tasted cleaner now—the metallic sweetness that haunted the [[Military Empire]] corridors was gone, for the moment. But [[Reyna Steelforge]]'s warning hung heavy in the cool air: prospectors in the [[Starmetal Hills]] still reported that same scent on the wind, always from the direction of the mountain. Something below was venting, and it was leaking downward.*

*Now they stood before the Giant stairway discovered in the lower archives—the same descent that would take them into the [[Dwarven Layer]]. The Commander's letter about the Sealed Crypt lay heavy in [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]]'s pack. If the dead were speaking too, as [[Warden Aldric]] feared, then the entity's reach had grown beyond books and into preserved flesh.*

*The descent awaited.*

---

## The Descent into Memory

[[Cora Flint]] pulled out her ledger with practiced efficiency. "Three days rest, three hundred gold payment from Reyna, and now we're back at the Giant stairway. Let's do this properly." She turned to the others. "[[Garrick Kade]], you're point. Chain mail's recovered, but check those bloodstains aren't compromising the links. Mercer, keep that Commander's letter accessible—if the crypt's leaking, we need to know what was sealed."

Garrick hefted his maul, checking the chain links where the giant rat had torn through. "Point it is," he grunted. "Chain's solid. Won't stop a clean hit but it'll turn a glancing blow." He moved to the stairway's edge, peering down into darkness where the air carried that mineral sharpness Reyna had warned about.

Mercer carefully unrolled the Commander's letter, his hands trembling with academic anticipation. "Listen to this passage again: 'The crypt's seals are failing. The dead speak in languages we cannot comprehend, but the resonance... it's the same as the books.'" He looked up, spectacles catching the faint torchlight. "The Military Empire didn't just seal a crypt—they sealed a *linguistic anomaly*. The dead aren't merely restless; they're communicating in something that shouldn't exist."

Cora measured powdered silver and crushed quartz into a crucible. A drop of distilled water, a whispered incantation, and the mixture glowed with soft amber light before solidifying into a viscous elixir. "Resonance dampener," she explained. "Should help if we encounter that psychic dissonance the Commander mentioned."

The descent began. Each step was a climb down rather than a walk—three feet of polished stone worn smooth by millennia of disuse. After forty feet, the stairway opened into a landing where the ceiling rose to fifteen feet—dwarven scale, not human. A corridor ran east and west, wide enough for three dwarves abreast. The walls were carved with geometric patterns Mercer recognized immediately: [[Kazad-Khrom]] design, the lost dwarven empire.

Garrick's trained eye spotted it first: the stonework was superior to anything in the human layers—built to withstand siege. But something had damaged it. Not age, not combat. The stone itself was discolored, darkened as if stained from within. The discoloration followed a pattern, radiating from a point deeper in the eastern corridor.

Cora knelt, running her fingers along the staining. "This isn't corrosion or water damage. It's contamination spreading through the stone's crystalline structure."

Mercer's arcane senses tingled. "[[Dreamstone]] radiation," he whispered. "Not raw ore, but the ambient effect of prolonged exposure."

From the eastern corridor, faint but unmistakable: the metallic sweetness. Stronger here. Not coming from above—coming from ahead.

Garrick ignored the analysis. "Enough studying. Something's leaking, and it's that way." He moved down the corridor, his maul coming off his shoulder. The air tasted wrong, like licking a rusted blade.

"Garrick, wait!" Mercer hissed, his voice dropping to a whisper as he examined the discoloration more closely. "This isn't just contamination—it's a *linguistic pattern*. Look at the radial spread—it follows the same geometric principles as Kazad-Khrom script, but inverted. The dwarves didn't just build containment; they built a *syntactic barrier*."

He traced the pattern with his wizardly quill. "The stone isn't stained—it's *remembering*. [[Dreamstone]] radiation doesn't merely corrupt; it imprints. Whatever's leaking from the crypt is communicating through the architecture itself. The dead aren't speaking in unknown languages—they're speaking in *architecture*."

## The Mute Seal

The corridor led through chambers clearly designed for dwarven habitation: low stone beds carved into alcoves, communal cooking hearths, a bathhouse with stone basins. Everything was sized for people four feet tall—a constant reminder they were trespassing in a civilization's domestic spaces.

At the corridor's end, past a collapsed storage chamber and a dry well shaft, they found it.

A door sealed with lead plates riveted directly into the stone frame. The lead was thick—half an inch—covering every seam. This wasn't a hasty seal. Someone had taken great care to ensure nothing could pass through in either direction.

The lead was *cracked*. Not broken—cracked, along stress fractures that followed the [[Dreamstone]] radiation pattern in the surrounding stone. Through the widest crack—barely a finger's width—a faint exhalation of air carried that metallic sweetness, concentrated enough to taste.

Cora examined the seal with her alchemist's tools. "The lead's failing because it's being transmuted from within. This isn't just corrosion—it's molecular restructuring."

Garrick stepped up to the door. "Structural failure's already happening. That thing's been working on this seal for centuries." He raised his maul, aiming for the largest crack. "Stand back. If it's breathing, it can die."

"Garrick, STOP!" Mercer's voice cracked with genuine alarm as he stepped between the warrior and the door. "This isn't a containment vessel—it's a *linguistic quarantine*! The lead wasn't chosen just to block radiation—it was chosen because lead is *mute*. It doesn't resonate, doesn't transmit, doesn't *remember*."

He pointed at the cracks, his hand trembling. "The dwarves didn't seal away a creature—they sealed away a *language*. That pulse you hear? It's not breathing—it's *syntax*. The Anomaly communicates through resonance, and it's been teaching the stone to speak its grammar for centuries. If you break that seal, you're not releasing a monster—you're releasing a *concept* into the mountain's architecture."

From the largest crack, something new: a voice. Not dwarven, not human. It spoke in Dwarvish, but the pronunciation was wrong—consonants too sharp, vowels too long, as if spoken by something that learned the language from stone rather than living throats.

***"Seal the lower halls. The dreaming is too loud. Seal the lower halls."***

The same phrase, repeated without inflection or awareness. A loop. A memory preserved in corrupted tissue, playing back.

Cora shook off the psychic dissonance. "That's not a warning—it's a recording. The dwarves left a monitoring station, and it's telling us [[the dreaming]] has reached critical volume." She pulled out her ledger. "Controlled entry is the only permanent solution. We cut a small opening, enter, and silence whatever's broadcasting that phrase."

Garrick nodded, shifting his grip on his maul. "Controlled entry it is. That thing's already talking—means it's awake enough to be dangerous."

Mercer's vertigo hit him like a physical blow—his Wisdom save failed spectacularly. The mountain's weight became an oppressive consciousness, and he staggered back, clutching his head. "Wait... that phrase isn't just a recording—it's a *command structure*. 'Seal the lower halls'—that's what the dwarves did. 'The dreaming is too loud'—that's the diagnosis. But listen to the *syntax*..."

He forced himself to focus. "Let me use my Manifest Mind—I can send a spectral observer through the crack to assess without risking physical exposure."

## The Philological Station

Cora administered the resonance dampener elixir, clearing the psychic fog from Mercer's mind. Garrick positioned himself at the crack, his maul held ready but not yet striking. "Manifest Mind first. But if there's something in there that needs killing, we're not waiting."

Mercer's spectral form slipped through the finger-width gap. What it saw inside was immediately relayed in a hushed, clinical tone:

"The mausoleum is a single chamber roughly thirty feet square. The walls are lined with stone niches—fourteen of them. Each contains a body. Dwarven dead, laid out in ceremonial armor, hands folded over weapons."

He paused, his spectral observer moving deeper. "They're perfectly preserved. Not mummified—preserved, as if they died yesterday. Their skin has that same iridescent sheen as the corridor stone. Their veins are visible beneath the skin, darkened to black. They're saturated with [[Dreamstone]] radiation."

The voice repeated from inside. Mercer's spectral observer identified the source: a larger figure in a captain's ceremonial plate, lying in the central niche. Its lips didn't move, but the voice emanated from it nonetheless.

Cora's assessment via the spectral observer: the chamber floor had a crack—narrow but deep—descending at least twenty feet before curving out of sight. The metallic sweetness poured from this crack. The mausoleum had been built directly above a seam in the Giant containment network that had shifted over millennia.

While Mercer's spectral mind observed, Garrick noticed something through the physical crack. The captain's hand had shifted from its chest to its side. Then the captain opened its eyes. They were solid black, shot through with iridescent veins.

The captain sat up slowly, turned its head toward the spectral observer, and spoke again—the same phrase, same wrong pronunciation. After thirty seconds of repetition, it lay back down and closed its eyes.

"These are not animated in the traditional sense," Mercer reported as the spectral observer returned. "They're echoes—psychic residue imprinted on saturated tissue. They replay memories, not malice."

Cora marked the weakest section of lead with chalk. "We cut a controlled opening here. Large enough to enter but small enough to reseal. I can fashion a temporary patch from the lead sheeting itself."

Garrick's maul struck along the chalk line. The sound was a dull *thud* rather than a metallic ring—the transmuted lead had lost its resonance. On the third strike, a section of lead roughly two feet square folded inward, creating a ragged opening.

The metallic sweetness flooded out, concentrated and thick. The captain's eyes opened. Solid black, shot through with iridescent veins. It stood, lifting its ceremonial war-pick. Three other bodies followed—soldiers in corroded chain, rising with mechanical precision.

## The Dreaming Learns

Garrick grew to Large size, his form filling the dwarven-scale chamber as his maul came down in a crushing arc. The captain's ceremonial plate cracked under the impact, but the echo didn't stagger—it swung its war-pick in return.

Mercer's Web erupted across the floor, thick sticky strands coating two of the soldier echoes. The third broke free, advancing toward Cora as she worked frantically at the floor crack.

"First round: mixing complete! Two more rounds to apply and compress!" The crack pulsed rhythmically beneath her hands.

From the niches, two more soldier echoes activated—their eyes opening black and iridescent. The captain swung its war-pick at Garrick again. The weapon bit deep into his chain mail, but he shook off the psychic whisper of the captain's loop phrase.

The free soldier echo reached Cora, raising its rusty axe. The blade cut across her shoulder as she worked.

Cora applied the sealant to the crack, a thick paste of lead powder and silver dust that smoked as it contacted the [[Dreamstone]] radiation. "Sealant applied! One more round to compress with the dwarven pressure clamp!" She pointed to a mechanism in the chamber floor—a heavy iron lever connected to a plate. "Garrick! Next round, you need to activate that! It's seized from disuse!"

Mercer, still reeling from psychic pressure, saw the situation deteriorating. More echoes were activating each round. The captain spoke again, not its loop phrase but something new: ***"The dreaming... learns."***

"'The dreaming learns'—that's not a threat, it's a *warning*!" Mercer shouted, his Arcana check revealing the horrifying truth. "The captain isn't speaking to us—it's reporting data! [[The dreaming]] is learning from our presence, from our tactics, from our very thoughts! These echoes aren't just fighting us—they're *studying* us!"

Cora gritted her teeth against her wound, working frantically to loosen the seized lever with her tinker's tools. The centuries of corrosion flaked away. "Lever's free! Garrick, now!"

Garrick's maul came down with devastating force. The captain's ceremonial plate shattered, its body collapsing to the stone floor. As it fell, it spoke one final phrase, its voice fading: ***"The dreaming... stops."***

The captain's collapse had an immediate effect. The soldier echoes stumbled, their movements becoming jerky, uncoordinated. Garrick moved to the lever, his Large form gripping the iron bar, muscles straining. The mechanism activated with a deep, resonant *THUMP* that vibrated through the chamber floor.

The effect was immediate and profound.

The metallic sweetness cut off abruptly. The rhythmic pulse from below ceased. The iridescent discoloration in the chamber walls dimmed. The soldier echoes collapsed where they stood, their animating energy source severed. The chamber fell silent.

## Aftermath and Implications

Cora worked methodically, her ledger open as she inventoried the chamber. She left the fourteen dwarven dead undisturbed—their armor and weapons had value, but disturbing radiation-saturated corpses was both disrespectful and potentially hazardous. [[Torvan Deepwatch]]'s ceremonial war-pick, however, was salvageable: a +1 weapon of dwarven make, its enchantment intact.

The sealed iron box contained the **[[Dwarven Radiation Survey Notes]]**—systematic records mapping the upper [[Dwarven Layer]], marking which crypts were "active" and which were "stable." A prominent landmark was noted: **the [[Cold Forge]]**, with an annotation: *"Fires extinguished when fuel source cut off. Reignition required for operational status."*

The **[[Dreamstone Fragment]]** was carefully wrapped in multiple layers of lead sheeting. It pulsed with faint inner light, cold to the touch—a phoneme crystal containing a single syllable of the dreaming's language.

Mercer knelt beside the captain's body, his Arcana check revealing the final truth. "Torvan Deepwatch wasn't just a captain—he was a *linguist*." He gently turned the body, revealing dwarven script etched into the ceremonial plate: *'Syntax recorder. Dreaming frequency: 14.7 cycles. Containment threshold: 22 cycles.'*

He looked up, his voice hushed with academic awe. "This wasn't a military outpost. It was a *philological* monitoring station. The dwarves weren't just measuring radiation—they were measuring the dreaming's *grammar*. Fourteen point seven cycles—that's the frequency at which [[the dreaming]] communicates. Twenty-two cycles is the threshold where it becomes coherent enough to teach."

He examined the sealed crack, his scholarly excitement tempered by grim realization. "We haven't solved the problem. We've just bought time. [[The dreaming]] is still down there, still learning, still trying to teach the mountain to speak its language. And now it's learned something new: how we fight."

The ascent up the Giant stairway felt longer than the descent. When they emerged into their fortified base atop the mountain, the change was palpable: the air tasted clean, cold, and empty.

[[Warden Aldric]] met them at the [[Salt-Vaults]] entrance. He listened to their account, becoming visibly shaken when Mercer explained the philological nature of the monitoring station. "The Brotherhood sealed the Archives because the books whispered to us," he said, his voice tight. "If the dead are speaking too, the entity's reach has grown. And if it's learning from exposure..."

*The expedition had contained the leak, but at what cost? They had silenced a warning system that had operated for centuries, obtained critical intelligence about the [[Dwarven Layer]]'s structure, and confirmed their worst fears: [[the dreaming]] wasn't just a force—it was a language, and it was learning to speak through the mountain itself. The [[Cold Forge]] awaited deeper in the layer, its fires extinguished, its purpose unknown. And somewhere below, in the mountain's deepest bones, something dreamed... and learned.*
