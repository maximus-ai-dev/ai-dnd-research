# Session 11: The Door That Should Not Open

*The following chronicle was transcribed from the field notes of the expeditionary team stationed at what has been officially designated the [[Bastion of the Asymmetrical]]. The events described occurred during the initial breach of the [[Giant Capstone]] and the first contact with the containment systems below. All accounts have been verified against multiple sources, including the crystal resonance chamber archives and the personal ledgers of [[Cora Flint]].*

---

The air in the [[Cold Forge]] chamber hummed with renewed purpose. For three days, the adamantine plating salvaged from [[The Drowned Juggernaut]] had rung on the anvils as [[Grimhold]] engineers worked in shifts, hammering the metal into reinforcement plates for the capstone above. The scent of hot metal, coal smoke, and the faint ozone of [[Ignathar]]'s elemental presence filled the space—a testament to dwarven industry reclaiming what had been lost.

Above, in the dwarven corridors, the party's base had transformed. [[Brotherhood Wardens]] stood guard at reinforced checkpoints. Workers hauled salvaged stone to patch walls. [[Reyna Steelforge]]'s voice echoed through the halls as she directed the installation of new support beams. The mountain felt different now—not just secured, but *claimed*.

That morning, the engineers finished the controlled breach. Using the [[Giant Blueprint Tablet]] as a guide, they cut along runic seams in the capstone—a ten-foot square opening that preserved the containment field around its edges. The doorway glowed with residual power, a curtain of warm static that made hair rise on every arm. Below it, darkness waited.

Reyna stood before the breach, her practical demeanor tempered by academic awe. "The [[Prospectors' Guild]] needs an official name for this installation on our maps and contracts," she said, looking from Cora to [[Garrick Kade]] to [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]]. "It's your home now. What do you want to call this place?"

Beyond her, the breach hummed. Through the static curtain, a landing of dark Giant-era basalt was visible—a platform fifty feet square, overlooking a corridor that stretched into darkness in both directions. The scale was immediately wrong: the ceiling forty feet above, the corridor wide enough for three giants to walk abreast. Every surface was carved with continuous rune sequences so dense they looked like text.

The party stood at the threshold of the Giant foundation level—a containment vessel built for beings twice their height and five times their mass. The air that drifted up from below carried the smell of ancient stone and something else: the faint, cold scent of [[Dreamstone]].

---

**"First things first. The name."**

[[Cora Flint]] pulled out her ledger, flipping to a fresh page. She glanced at Reyna, then at the breach. "This isn't just a base anymore. It's a containment facility, a research station, and our primary asset." Her eyes moved to Garrick and Mercer. "Given the scale, the history, and what we're dealing with below, I propose 'The Bastion of the Asymmetrical.' It acknowledges the mountain's name, implies fortification, and sounds professional enough for Guild contracts."

Before anyone could object, she continued with clinical precision. "Now, before we step through that curtain, we need to PLAN. That's a fifty-foot square platform with a forty-foot ceiling. Garrick, you're on point with your maul. Mercer, keep Detect Magic active—those runes are dense enough to be dangerous. I'll be right behind with my alchemist's supplies ready."

Garrick grunted, hefting his maul onto his shoulder. "'Bastion of the Asymmetrical'? Sounds like something a guild accountant would come up with." His eyes fixed on the massive breach, the curtain of static energy humming with power. "Call it what you want on your maps. Down there, it's just the kill zone."

He activated Giant's Might, feeling his body swell and grow, the runes on his armor glowing with power. "Enough talking. I'm going through." Then he stepped through the static curtain without hesitation.

The air crackled around him as he passed through, his maul raised and ready. His boots hit the dark basalt platform with a solid thud that echoed in the enormous space. Through the breach, Cora and Mercer saw Garrick standing on the fifty-foot square landing, his Large form still dwarfed by the scale around him.

"Platform's clear," he called back up, his voice echoing. "But this place feels... waiting."

Mercer sighed as he stepped through, his quill already materializing. "Typical. No appreciation for proper procedure." He cast Detect Magic, the arcane sight revealing the complex weave around them. "The curtain is a containment field—likely a modified form of the original Giant warding. It's designed to prevent energy leakage, not physical passage. Fascinating adaptation."

His analysis deepened as they examined the space. The entire structure hummed with abjuration magic of staggering complexity. The runes weren't decorative—they were functional components of a containment network, woven together in patterns so dense they formed what his academic mind recognized as a "magical latticework." But something troubled him: the field strength readings were lower than the blueprint tablet's baseline markings. The containment was weakening—slowly, over millennia, but measurably.

From the darkness to the left, a faint glow became visible—one of the monitoring alcoves described in the blueprint, about a hundred feet down the corridor. To the right, the corridor curved gently, the runes on the walls pulsing with slow, rhythmic light.

---

Garrick moved toward the glowing alcove, his footsteps echoing in the enormous corridor. The rhythmic pulsing of the runes seemed to synchronize with his movement—or perhaps it was just imagination. The alcove revealed itself as a recessed chamber containing Giant-era instruments: stone dials the size of wagon wheels, crystal resonance chambers that glowed with internal light, and etched measurement scales in a language of angles and symbols rather than numbers.

Cora's examination of the platform edges revealed seams in the basalt—maintenance access points, but sealed with runic locks that would require Giant-scale tools to open. Her ledger filled with sketches of the rune patterns, each sequence more complex than the last. She noted the precision: tolerances measured in fractions of an inch across hundred-foot surfaces. The Giants were not just large; they were exact.

Mercer's analysis revealed something more immediate: the runes nearest the alcove showed a localized spike in energy consumption. Something in the monitoring station was drawing power.

As Garrick reached the alcove's entrance, the crystal resonance chamber within brightened. A low hum filled the air—not through sound, but through vibration in the stone. The chamber's glow resolved into a three-dimensional display: a schematic of the containment vessel, showing the Inspection Circuit where they stood, the outer ring, and the central void. Points of light marked their position—three small, bright dots on the perimeter.

Then the display changed. The schematic shifted to show containment field strength at various points. Most locations showed amber light—functional but degraded. One location, deep in the central void, showed red. The runes beside it pulsed in a distressed pattern.

From deeper in the corridor, beyond the curve to the right, a new sound emerged: a deep, resonant *thud*, followed by the grinding of stone on stone. Distant, but unmistakable—the sound of something massive moving.

---

**"Garrick, back to the platform—now!"**

Cora's voice snapped through the vast space. "That grinding sound means we're not alone down here, and we're standing in a kill zone with no cover."

Garrick didn't retreat. Instead, he planted his feet in the alcove entrance, maul raised. "That sound came from the right," he growled, his eyes fixed on the curve. "Something's moving down there. Big."

Mercer approached the crystal chamber, his quill transcribing the schematic. "Garrick, your tactical assessment is correct but incomplete. That red location represents a containment breach in progress—not catastrophic failure, but a localized weakening that's allowing something to draw power from the field itself."

He turned to both of them, his academic detachment giving way to genuine concern. "We need to understand what's in the central void before we proceed. That grinding could be defenses activating, or it could be the breach itself manifesting. Either way, standing here debating is statistically the most dangerous option."

Garrick advanced down the corridor, staying close to the wall. The blue-white light grew brighter, casting his shadow long and distorted behind him. As he neared the curve, he saw the source: a crystalline panel set into the wall—an observation window. Through it, he got his first glimpse of the cage's interior.

A void. Not a room. A space so large its far wall was invisible in the darkness. The containment runes glowed on the interior surfaces, providing faint illumination that faded into nothing. The scale was incomprehensible—this was a space designed to contain something the size of a building. The blue-white light came from within the void itself, pulsing in time with the runes.

The grinding sound resumed, but now Garrick could see its source: massive stone slabs, fifteen feet tall and three feet thick, set into the wall beyond the window. One of them was moving—the door that was ajar. It was opening wider, pushed by invisible force. Cold air flowed from the gap, carrying the smell of [[Dreamstone]] and something else: ozone, like after a lightning strike.

From the platform, Reyna called down, her voice tight with concern: "The runes up here are fluctuating! Something's drawing massive power!"

The crystal chamber in the alcove flashed. The display showed the containment field strength dropping rapidly at the nearby node—from 60% to 55% in seconds. The siphoning had accelerated.

Mercer's analysis snapped into focus: "It's not testing the field—it's *learning* the field's frequency! The anomaly is mimicking the containment signature to bypass it! That's how it's siphoning power!"

The door beyond the observation window ground open another foot. Through the widening gap, darkness waited—and from that darkness, a new sensation began: a pressure, not physical but mental, like a headache forming behind the eyes. The first whisper of the Sleeper's broadcast.

---

**DC 13 Wisdom save.**

The psychic pressure washed over them—not the full broadcast, just the ambient leakage from the containment field. All three resisted: Cora with fey-touched resilience, Garrick with soldier's will, Mercer with academic discipline. But it was a warning.

The runes on the walls blazed with light, their pulsing now frantic. The crystal chamber display showed containment field strength at the nearby node plummeting: 50%... 45%... 40%. The critical threshold.

"Garrick! Get over here and help me turn these!" Cora shouted, pointing to the Giant-scale dials. "Your strength plus my knowledge—we need to force a field recalibration NOW!"

Garrick abandoned the door and charged back toward the alcove. He grabbed the massive dial with both hands, his Giant's Might-enhanced strength surging as he put all his weight into it. The dial resisted at first—built for giants, after all—but then began to turn with a deep grinding sound.

"Which way, Mercer? How far?"

"Stop! You're overshooting! The system is counter-adapting—it's learning from our adjustments!"

For a moment, it worked. The energy siphoning slowed. The containment field strength stabilized at 40%, holding just above critical. The low hum from the door diminished slightly.

Then the system adapted.

The display showed the anomaly analyzing the new frequency, matching it, then beginning to siphon again. It was learning faster than they could adjust. Mercer watched in horrified fascination: "It's not just mimicking—it's *predicting*! It anticipates our next adjustment based on the pattern of our previous ones!"

The door remained open a foot, but no wider. The broadcast hum continued, a steady vibration through the stone. From the platform, Reyna called: "The breach is stabilizing! Whatever you did, it's holding for now!"

But Mercer knew the truth. He pointed to the display, where a new reading appeared: "Containment Integrity: Compromised. Broadcast Channel: Established." The foot-wide gap was enough. [[The Sleeper]] had a channel through the containment.

---

They established their forward camp in the monitoring alcove. That night, all three dreamed.

Cora dreamed of building something massive and perfect—a machine that ran forever, its gears the size of buildings. But the dream shifted: the machine began to build itself, learning from her designs, improving them beyond her understanding, until she was no longer the engineer but a component in her own creation.

Garrick dreamed of standing guard at a door that never opened. He'd been there for centuries, his armor fused to his body, his maul grown into the stone floor. He knew with absolute certainty that if the door opened, something terrible would emerge. But in the dream, he also knew the door would open eventually—it was inevitable—and when it did, he'd have failed the only thing that ever gave his vigil meaning.

Mercer dreamed of reading a book with infinite pages. Each page revealed something beautiful and terrible. He understood that if he read far enough, he'd find the answer to every question ever asked. But as he turned page after page, he realized the book was reading him back—learning his thoughts, his fears, his memories—and the knowledge he gained was being used to write new pages about him.

They woke at the same moment, the dreams lingering like afterimages. They didn't discuss them at breakfast, but they each knew the others had dreamed too.

As evening approached, the crystal chamber displayed a new alert: "[[Containment Node Delta]] — Integrity: 35%. Proximity: Outer Ring, Sector 2." The schematic showed its location: beyond the open door, in the outer ring corridor, past what the blueprint labeled as "[[The Weight Gate]]"—a security measure requiring minimum mass to activate.

The broadcast hum intensified slightly. The psychic pressure returned as a creeping presence at the edge of awareness.

Reyna approached, her expression serious. "The engineers have done what they can with the breach. But that node at 35%... if it fails, the containment field in that sector collapses. Whatever's behind that door gets more room to work."

She pointed to the schematic. "You need to reach that node. Stabilize it. But to get there, you have to pass through the outer ring's security measures. The Giants built puzzles—physical and magical barriers designed to prevent unauthorized access."

The door remained open a foot. The broadcast channel was established. [[The Sleeper]] was learning. And somewhere in the outer ring, [[Containment Node Delta]] was failing.

The race had begun.
