# Session 18: The Broadcast Zone

The void between worlds is not empty. It is filled with the dreams of something ancient.

From the [[Observation Platform]], the journey into the [[Broadcast Zone]] began as a calculated risk. [[Cora Flint]] had triple-checked the tether lines. [[Garrick Kade]] had secured the equipment. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] had reviewed the intensity projections. The [[Dreamstone Circlet]] on each brow had protected them for days, but now they had to leave its effective shielding range to deploy the [[Dreamstone Seal]] within fifty feet of the sphere's surface.

They launched into the weightless dark, roped together in a line, the [[Reality Anchor]] humming as it maintained their reference frame. At exactly fifty feet from the Caldera wall, the circlets began to vibrate—not failing, but resonating with overload. The broadcast at this distance was simply too strong.

The world dissolved.

**Wisdom saves were called for. All three failed.**

For [[Garrick Kade]], four seconds stretched into eternity as his mind flooded with First World light. The sphere at the center of [[The Caldera]] transformed from a threatening mass to a sleeping mind—vast, serene, unknowably old. Sound became visible, rippling through the void in concentric waves. The containment runes on the distant walls appeared as bars of golden light—a cage seen from inside. His maul drifted from his grip as he tried to orient himself. "Cora! Mercer! Stay... stay together!" His voice sounded wrong, layered, coming from somewhere else.

[[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] experienced two rounds of disorientation, but in that time he perceived something profound. The [[Deep Things]] resolved into shapes that suggested faces, hands, memories from before memory existed. One extended a tendril of perception toward him—not touching, but tasting the edges of his consciousness. The sensation was not violation. It was recognition.

As Mercer's mind cleared first, he saw what the others could not yet perceive: at the edge of each character's vision, a figure began to form. Not a [[Deep Thing]]. Something familiar. A version of themselves.

Cora's echo wore a master artificer's coat she had never owned, her hands moving with precision she would recognize as her own, constructing a device of extraordinary complexity. Garrick's echo stood guard at a door—not a mountain entrance, but the door of a house, a home—and he looked at peace in a way Garrick had never been. Mercer's own echo sat in a library with infinite shelves, every book open, every page revealing something true and terrible and beautiful.

The echoes did not speak yet. They simply watched. Waiting.

---

## The Psychological Gauntlet Begins

When Cora and Garrick's disorientation cleared, the Broadcast Zone revealed its true nature. The echoes spoke.

Cora's echo, with laugh lines instead of worry lines, held up a crystalline structure shimmering with First World light. "You always knew you could build anything," she said. "The question is whether you should."

Garrick's echo turned from the house door. "You could stop," it said, its voice layered with the same resonance as their own. "You have defended enough. When do you put down the maul?"

Mercer's echo closed the infinite book with a soft thud that echoed through the void. "You wanted to know everything," it said. "What will you do when you do?"

Mercer's spectral mind manifested as he analyzed the situation. "They're not tricks," he announced, his wizardly quill taking rapid notes. "They're potentialities. Alternate timelines where different choices were made. The broadcast isn't showing us what we want—it's showing us what could have been."

The [[Deep Things]] drifted closer, their forms now shifting to reflect the psychological concepts being explored. One took on the shape of a door. Another became a crystalline structure. A third manifested as an open book. They were not mimicking—they were *understanding*, translating psychological concepts into physical forms within the broadcast.

---

## Stage 1: The Opening Questions

The broadcast intensified. Each character faced their first challenge.

For Cora, her echo showed the [[Dreamstone Seal]] completed—but not as a containment device. As a key. "If you could open the containment rather than reinforce it," the echo asked, "and the thing inside would simply leave—walk away, dissolve into the cosmos—would you? Even knowing you could not predict what would happen?"

Cora failed her save. The vision of the key-Seal hung before her, beautiful and terrifying. "I need data," she said, her voice tight. "I need projections. I can't make a decision without knowing the variables." The clinical detachment she relied on was cracking.

For Garrick, the echo showed the door—the home he had never had. Behind it: warmth, safety, the sound of someone waiting. "You have defended others your entire life," the echo asked. "Who defends you?"

Garrick failed as well. "They do," he growled, gesturing to Cora and Mercer. "We defend each other." But the vision was powerful. A real home. Not just a place to sleep between jobs.

Only Mercer succeeded. His echo showed the [[Architect's Codex]] complete—every page filled, every question answered. "Was the seeking better than the having?" it asked.

"That's a false dichotomy," Mercer responded. "The seeking *is* the having. Knowledge isn't a destination—it's a methodology." He had maintained psychological ground.

---

## Stage 2: The Deepening Probe

The questions cut deeper.

Cora's echo showed the [[Cold Forge]] burning, [[Ignathar]] in visible agony. "Everything you build costs something," the echo said, her voice now carrying an edge. "What are you willing to pay for perfection?"

Another failure. Cora watched the vision, her hands shaking. "I know costs better than anyone," she said. "My shop burned. My debts remain. I pay every day." But the question wasn't about the forge—it was about what she'd sacrifice for what she wanted.

Garrick's echo shifted focus. Another figure appeared beside the door—[[Kregg]]. Not the bandit leader, but the *idea* of Kregg. The cycle of violence. "You are not Kregg because you chose not to be," the echo said. "But the choice was close. How close?"

This time, Garrick succeeded. "Not close at all," he growled. "Kregg chose power over people. I left because I wouldn't do that anymore." He pointed his maul at the echo. "I know the road he took, and I chose another one."

Mercer's echo showed his academic disgrace—the scandal that drove him from his university. "You were right," it said. "They were wrong. Does being right justify what you lost—and what you would lose again?"

Mercer failed. He watched the memory unfold, seeing something new: his colleagues hadn't just been dismissive. They'd been afraid. "Justify?" he said, his voice losing some academic detachment. "That presupposes a need for justification. Knowledge doesn't require justification—it simply *is*."

---

## Stage 3: The Core Questions

The Broadcast Zone reached peak intensity. The [[Deep Things]] froze, fully transformed into manifestations of the psychological struggles.

Cora's echo showed the fortress centuries later, still standing. Her work—the defenses, the forge modifications, the Seal itself—all functioning perfectly. No one remembered her name. "If your work outlasts you but your name does not," the echo asked, "was the work enough?"

Failure. "No," Cora said quietly. "The work is never enough. The debts are never paid. The shop still burned." She looked at her ledger floating beside her. "But that doesn't mean you stop working."

Garrick's echo showed him old, tired, still standing guard. Still holding the door. Endless vigilance. "When do you get to rest?" the echo asked, its voice weary. "Or is the watch the rest—because without it, you do not know who you are?"

Failure. "The watch ends when the job's done," Garrick said. "When the Seal's deployed. When my people are safe." He tightened his grip on his maul. "Maybe the watch is who I am. But that's not a bad thing. Someone has to stand guard."

Mercer's echo opened the infinite book to its last page. Words appeared in his own handwriting—but he hadn't written them. The question formed: "What if being right isn't enough? What if understanding the Sleeper doesn't change anything?"

Failure. "That's not a tragedy," Mercer said, though his voice wavered. "That's the nature of inquiry. Pain is the price of understanding."

---

## Final Resolution

The psychological gauntlet complete, the echoes stood fully formed before each character.

Cora faced her echo holding the beautiful key-Seal. "Does it matter if no one knows who did it?" the echo asked.

"No," Cora said, her voice clear. "It doesn't matter. The containment holds or it doesn't. My name being remembered won't change that." Her final resolution check failed. The echo would remain in her peripheral vision—a constant reminder of roads not taken. She gained Broadcast Resistance.

Garrick faced his old, weary echo. "What happens when the watch is over?" it asked.

"The watch is never over," Garrick said. "There's always someone who needs guarding." His check failed as well. His echo would persist. He gained Broadcast Resistance.

Mercer faced his echo with the infinite book. "What if some knowledge only brings more questions, and those questions only bring more pain?"

"That's not a tragedy," Mercer said, his voice regaining certainty. "That's the nature of inquiry. You keep asking. You keep seeking. Because the alternative is living in a world where the questions stop." His check succeeded spectacularly. The echo dissolved, absorbed into the broadcast. He gained Broadcast Immunity.

---

## The Deployment Position

With the psychological gauntlet resolved, the party made their final approach. The [[Deep Things]] drifted back to normal orbits, seemingly satisfied. The broadcast pressure eased.

At fifty feet from the sphere's surface—the deployment range—they found a cluster of Giant-era debris: construction platforms and scaffolding left from the containment's original construction fifty thousand years ago. They anchored themselves to the largest platform.

From here, [[The Sleeper]] was breathtakingly close. Its surface rippled with slow, organic motion—a state of matter their physics could not describe. Looking closely, they could see through the surface in moments of transparency:

The interior was the [[First World]]. Not the dead remnant—the living original. A reality of impossible beauty where stone sang, light had weight, and consciousness was the fundamental force. The Sleeper existed in that reality—not trapped, not imprisoned, but simply present. It had been here since before the universe was reshaped.

"The Sleeper is not our enemy," Mercer murmured, his spectral mind recording observations. "It is not our responsibility. It is our neighbor—a neighbor whose house is built on different laws of physics. The Seal does not punish it. The Seal is a better wall."

They had reached the deployment position. The psychological gauntlet was complete. The Broadcast Zone had been navigated. Behind them, the golden lights of the containment anchor points burned steadily on the Caldera wall. Above them, the capstone and the mountain and the world they knew.

Before them, the boundary between worlds pulsed with ancient dreams.

They stood ready to build and deploy the Dreamstone Seal.
