# Session 1: The Desperate Bounty

**Date:** First Frost, Year of the Iron Star

The wind that sweeps down from the Sentinel Mountains carries more than winter’s first bite—it carries the scent of desperation. Three strangers converged on the frontier town of [[Grimhold]] from different directions, each bearing the weight of their own pasts. [[Cora Flint]], her mind already calculating survival costs; [[Garrick Kade]], his eyes scanning for ghosts from [[Emberfell Rejects]]; and [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]], more interested in ancient stones than present dangers. They met at the Marker Stone in the town square, where the wind rattled the bounty board nailed to the [[Prospectors' Guild]].

One notice stood out: fifty gold for the capture or elimination of the Emberfell Rejects, last seen near the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]]. The door to the Guild opened, and [[Reyna Steelforge]] emerged, her assessment as practical as Cora’s. “If you’re interested, come inside,” she said, already turning away. “If you’re not, move along—I’ve got a town to run.”

Inside, the air smelled of ink and cold stone. When Garrick spoke the name “Kregg,” Reyna’s eyes sharpened. “Big man. Heavy. Came through here selling ‘protection.’ I ran him out with a pickaxe handle.” She tapped a map. “He’s holed up in ruins at the mountain’s peak. Ten, maybe fifteen with him. Mostly kids and desperate fools.”

Professor Mercer, meanwhile, examined the Guild’s foundation. “This building incorporates much older stonework—dwarven cut. Grimhold was built atop a dwarven outpost.” His academic excitement contrasted with Reyna’s grim practicality as she counted out ten gold pieces. “Advance for supplies. The rest when you bring proof Kregg’s dealt with.”

* * *

The trio’s preparations were as different as their personalities. At the [[Griffon's Hold]], [[Elara Stormwind]] offered fair prices and a rough map. “The mountain doesn’t sit right,” she warned. “Animals avoid it. Prospectors come back talking about bad dreams.” At the [[Chapel of the Hearthfire]], [[Brother Aldwin]] provided healing supplies and troubling information. “One man came back raving about whispers from the ground. He wasn’t feverish—he was terrified.”

At the [[Second Wind Inn]], they found [[Harlan]], a grizzled guide who’d seen campfire smoke near the peak. “I’ll guide you to the first marker,” he said, nursing an ale. “After that, you’re on your own. Cost you an ale. Or you can take my advice: don’t go. That mountain’s bad luck.”

That night, as snow began to fall, Garrick kept watch from their room. His vigilance proved warranted—two shivering figures whispered in the alley below. “...tonight. They leave at dawn. Kregg wants to know...” Garrick moved with silent efficiency, dropping one bandit with a non-lethal blow and grappling the other. The captured youth, gaunt with hunger, revealed Kregg’s desperation: starving followers, frozen signal horns, and a growing fear of the mountain itself.

Garrick made a choice born of his own past. He freed the bandits with a warning. “Head south. Find work that doesn’t involve stealing from people who have less than you.” As they stumbled into the night, he turned to Cora. “They won’t talk. They’re too scared of Kregg finding out they were captured.”

* * *

Dawn came gray and cold. Harlan waited at the north gate as promised, leading them three hours northwest to the first marker stone. There, he pointed to the distant peak. “That’s as far as I go. Watch for wolves. And giant eagles. And if you hear whispers from the ground... run.”

The Asymmetrical Mountain loomed like a mistake in creation—too sharp, too dark, its angles wrong against the horizon. Cora examined the marker stone, finding faded Giant runes: “Beware the Sleeping Earth.” The journey grew stranger with each mile—the metallic smell strengthened, and a rhythmic pulse vibrated through the ground.

They found an abandoned prospector’s camp beneath a rock overhang. A journal lay open, its last entry reading: “The whispers are getting louder. The ground breathes beneath our feet. We’re leaving at first light.” As Professor Mercer examined the journal, a shadow passed overhead—a giant eagle, territorial and hungry. Garrick met its dive with his maul, injuring its wing and driving it off, but the cry echoed through the hills.

That night, camped on a high ridge, they watched campfires burn at the mountain’s peak. The rhythmic pulse continued—one beat every thirty seconds, felt through stone. “This isn’t superstition,” Professor Mercer whispered. “There’s machinery below us. Or something that functions like machinery.”

* * *

At dawn, they approached Kregg’s fortress—a crude palisade surrounding ancient dwarven ruins. Garrick subdued a young lookout with silent efficiency, learning of the bandits’ misery: starvation, beatings, and a whispered resistance led by a woman named [[Brenna]].

When Garrick kicked open the camp gate, every eye turned toward him. Kregg emerged from his tent, a dagger glowing faintly at his belt. Recognition dawned on his cruel face. “Kade. Come to join my crew? Or come to die?”

Cora acted first, her Ray of Frost freezing a bandit’s legs as she shouted, “Stand down and you live!” The other bandits hesitated, looking to Brenna, who watched with cold calculation.

Garrick didn’t waste words. His maul connected with Kregg’s chest, then shattered his knee. The bandit captain collapsed screaming, all bravado gone. Brenna stepped forward. “Enough! We’re done, Kregg. Done starving. Done freezing.” The bandits dropped their weapons.

As Kregg lay bound and broken, Brenna presented their new leaders with a stark choice: execute the bandits, turn them in to hang, drive them into the hills to freeze, or take responsibility for fifteen human lives.

Cora chose pragmatism. “You work for us. Help us investigate what’s below this mountain. We vouch for you with Reyna.” Garrick added the weight of his authority. “You’re people who made bad choices. Now you get a chance to make better ones.”

* * *

The camp transformed under Brenna’s direction. As order was established, Professor Mercer examined the boarded-over stairwell that breathed cold, metallic drafts. Cora discovered faded Dwarven runes carved above it: “Vault of the Earth-Shaper” and “Beware the Waking Dream.” Three sets of footprints led down into darkness—none returned.

Night fell on their newly acquired fortress. Kregg lay bound for transport to Grimhold. Fourteen former bandits now worked as a disciplined workforce. But below them, in the depths of the mountain, the rhythmic pulse continued—now one beat every twenty-eight seconds.

Garrick stood watch, his maul resting across his shoulders. “We have a fortress,” he said quietly. “A home beyond Emberfell’s reach.” Cora made final ledger entries, calculating risks and assets. Professor Mercer wrote of discoveries that could define a lifetime—or end it.

The wind howled across the peak. The stars shone cold and sharp. And deep below, in the [[Vault of the Earth-Shaper]], something dreamed of waking.

*The bounty was secured, but the true price of the Asymmetrical Mountain had yet to be paid. The fortress was won, but what ancient tenant slept beneath its stones? And what would happen when—not if—it woke?*
