# Session 4: The Amalgamation

*Chronicled from the field notes of an observer in the Asymmetrical Mountain, winter of the current age*

---

The fortress had settled into a rhythm of winter survival. For three days, the party rested—[[Cora Flint]] brewing restorative elixirs in the preparation kitchen, [[Garrick Kade]] drilling the workforce on basic defensive formations, [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] poring over the [[Brotherhood Operations Log]] by firelight. The captured bandits worked under [[Brenna]]'s watchful eye, hauling stone to patch the curtain walls. The three Wardens—[[Warden Aldric]], Petra, and [[Brother Cassen]]—stood their silent vigil at the sealed corridor, two centuries out of time but adapting to this new reality.

The air in the mountain fortress was cold but stable. The [[Salt-Vaults]] below provided enough preserved meat and grain to last the winter. The immediate crisis had passed. But the deeper mystery remained: what lay behind the Brotherhood door, and what was the [[The Amalgamation]] that guarded it?

Mercer had studied the operations log thoroughly. He knew [[The Grand Archive]] was forty feet below the preparation kitchen, behind a door inscribed with a closed eye and the words: *The Grand Archive. Entry by rank only. The Amalgamation watches.* The party had the three [[Brotherhood Insignia]] from the Wardens—Scholar-rank keys to a place no living person had entered in two hundred years.

Warden Aldric approached as the party gathered their gear. The preserved man's face was grave. "I will not enter the sanctum," he said, his voice echoing with ancient formality. "This is above my authorization level. But I can tell you this: the Grand Archive is divided into three wings. The Amalgamation patrols the [[Central Hall]]. The East Wing contains the primary collections. The West Wing was restricted—Commander's level only. We were never told why."

He paused, looking at each of them. "I have never seen the Amalgamation. I only knew it existed. I do not know what it will do when it encounters you."

## The Doorway

The party moved through the fortress, past the workforce repairing walls, past Brenna giving quiet orders, past the Wardens standing their silent vigil. They descended through the preparation kitchen—the same room where they fought mutated rats weeks ago—and into the interstitial corridor beyond. The air grew colder with each step.

At the corridor's end, the Brotherhood door waited. Three inches of solid stone, fitted with intricate arcane script. In the center, the closed-eye symbol stared blankly at them. Around it, three recesses formed a perfect triangle, each sized exactly for one of the insignias they carried.

Cora approached first, her movements deliberate. She ran her hands along the stonework, feeling for pressure plates, hidden seams, or mechanical triggers. "No mechanical traps that I can detect," she announced. "The stonework is seamless."

Mercer stepped closer, his eyes taking on a faint silver glow as he cast Detect Magic. "The inscriptions are complex. Layered. There's abjuration magic here—powerful preservation wards. And something else... a binding enchantment tied to the insignias. When they're placed, it releases the arcane lock." He squinted, leaning closer. "A secondary layer. Not a trap, exactly. More like... a notification system. When this door opens, something will know."

Garrick stood guard, his back to the door as he watched the corridor behind them. "Notification system means it knows we're coming. No point in being defensive when it's already alerted."

They positioned themselves at the recesses, wax earplugs in place against potential auditory attacks. Cora counted down silently: *Three... two... one.*

Simultaneously, they pressed the insignias into the recesses.

For a heartbeat, nothing happened. Then the arcane script flared with soft blue light, tracing the closed-eye symbol and spreading through the intricate carvings. The light pulsed once, twice, three times—each pulse brighter than the last. The stone vibrated under their hands, a deep, resonant hum that they felt in their bones even through the earplugs.

With a sound like a held breath finally exhaled—a long, slow sigh of stone on stone—the door released. It swung inward, perfectly balanced on hidden hinges that hadn't moved in two centuries.

Cold air rushed out, carrying smells that had been sealed away for generations: old ink, old leather, and something else—a faint animal warmth, like something large had been sleeping nearby for a very long time.

## The Antechamber

Beyond the door lay an antechamber—roughly thirty feet by twenty feet. Stone benches lined the walls, simple and functional. On the wall near the entrance, a rank-insignia board showed Brotherhood hierarchies: Initiate, Scholar, Archivist, Commander. Their insignias were Scholar-rank.

On the benches: personal effects left behind in a hurry. A half-written letter on faded parchment. Two empty lantern hooks on the wall—no lanterns remained.

Mercer carefully lifted the letter, his Wizardly Quill hovering ready. "Listen to this," he said, his voice hushed. "It's dated two hundred and three years ago. The writer says: *'...the deep readings have intensified again. Brother Cassen believes the resonance is climbing toward the [[Iron Warlords]]' level. I believe we have three months before—'*"

He looked up, his face pale. "It stops mid-sentence. They were writing when something happened. Something that made them abandon this letter and evacuate."

From beyond the archway at the far end of the antechamber, in the magical darkness of the Central Hall, there came a sound: a faint, rhythmic *thump*. Then another. Heavy. Regular. Stone floor yielding slightly to each impact.

"That's it," Garrick said, his voice tight. "It's moving. We're not waiting for it to find us."

Before Cora or Mercer could stop him, he stepped through the archway into the magical darkness, maul raised. The darkness swallowed him immediately.

## The Horror Revealed

Garrick's torchlight—which should have illuminated thirty feet—barely reached fifteen in the magical darkness. He could see outlines: a long corridor, sixty feet by ten feet wide, with archways branching left and right. Shelving units lay overturned against the walls. Stone benches were smashed. Personal items—books, scrolls, writing implements—were scattered across the floor, ground under something heavy.

And the claw marks. Massive, wide-set, four-inch deep gouges in the stone floor at regular intervals. The stride pattern was large: roughly eight feet between sets.

The rhythmic thumping had stopped. The silence was absolute, broken only by Garrick's own breathing and the faint crackle of his torch. Then, from somewhere ahead in the darkness, he heard a new sound: a slow, wet, tearing noise, like flesh being pulled apart. Then a heavy footstep. Then another.

Something was moving toward him.

Back in the antechamber, Cora and Mercer heard the footsteps too. They were louder now, more deliberate. The darkness beyond the archway seemed to thicken, to pulse with each footfall.

Mercer's fingers traced frantic patterns in the air. "It's coming," he whispered. "Garrick's in there blind. We need light."

From the darkness, Garrick's voice echoed back, tight with tension: "I can hear it. Eight-foot strides. Heavy. Something's wrong with how it moves—wet sounds. Like it's... coming apart and reforming with each step."

Cora stepped to the edge of the archway, her fingers tracing icy patterns. A beam of freezing energy streaked into the darkness, striking something massive and wet-sounding. A deep, resonant groan echoed through the hall as frost crystals formed on unseen flesh.

Garrick roared as something massive slammed into him in the darkness—a crushing blow that drove the air from his lungs. He swung his maul blindly toward the source of the attack. The weapon connected with a sickening wet crunch.

Mercer's Fire Bolt streaked into the darkness, striking the ceiling fifteen feet above the combat. For one brilliant instant, the hall was illuminated.

What they saw in that flash froze the blood in their veins.

**The Amalgamation** was a nightmare of fused bodies—not a golem of clay or stone, but of flesh. At least seven humanoid forms were visible in its construction, their limbs and torsos merged into a single hulking shape ten feet tall. The bodies were preserved, their skin pale and waxy, veins bulging with black fluid. They faced outward in all directions, arms fused into massive limbs that ended in crude, bony fists. The faces were expressionless, eyes closed, mouths slightly open as if in silent screams.

Where Garrick's maul had struck, a deep gash oozed thick black fluid that steamed in the cold air. Frost crystals from Cora's ray spread across its right side, slowing its movements.

The creature had no head—just a mass of torsos rising to a blunt peak. It turned toward Garrick, the bodies that formed its "front" shifting with a wet tearing sound.

The light faded, plunging the hall back into magical darkness. But now they knew what they were fighting.

## A Desperate Battle

What followed was a brutal, grinding combat that pushed the party to their absolute limits. Garrick, true to his nature, refused to retreat even as the Amalgamation's massive fists shattered his ribs, drove him to the ground, and brought him to the brink of death not once, not twice, but *five times*.

Cora's healing magic flowed into him again and again—Healing Word, Cure Wounds, every spell in her arsenal—only for the creature to slam him back into unconsciousness. Her ledger would later show a catastrophic expenditure of resources: spell slots, healing potions, medical supplies.

Mercer fought with a scholar's precision and a survivor's desperation. He cast Web, entangling the creature in sticky magical strands. He cast Shatter, the thunderous blast tearing at its already-wounded flesh. He cast Magic Missile, force darts slamming home with unerring accuracy. He even tried Hold Person, realizing too late that the creature was made of fused human bodies—perhaps the spell would work.

But the Amalgamation was relentless. Black fluid poured from dozens of wounds—acid burns from Cora's spells, frost damage slowing its movements, deep gashes from Garrick's maul, force impacts from Mercer's missiles. It burned with green flame where Mercer's Fire Bolt had struck. Yet it kept fighting, kept advancing.

Garrick went down for the fifth time, blood pooling around him, his breathing shallow and ragged. Cora was out of spell slots. Mercer had one first-level slot remaining.

"I'm out of spells, but I'm not out of options," Cora said, her voice tight but controlled. She pulled out her healer's kit, her fingers working with clinical precision. "Multiple rib fractures, internal bleeding, pneumothorax. Standard battlefield triage."

She looked up at Mercer. "Buy me sixty seconds. Use whatever you have left to keep that thing occupied."

Mercer cast Mage Hand, directing the spectral hand to scoop up stone fragments and hurl them at the Amalgamation's fused faces. The distraction bought precious seconds as Cora stabilized Garrick at 0 HP—alive, but unconscious and completely helpless.

## The Terrible Truth

As they dragged Garrick's unconscious form back toward the archway, the magical darkness began to thin. For the first time, Mercer saw the Amalgamation clearly in the proper torchlight of the antechamber.

And he understood.

"By the gods," he whispered, his academic mind seizing on the horrifying detail even in this moment of terror. "Those are Brotherhood members. Archivists. Scholars. This isn't a guardian they created—it's what happened to them. The 'forbidden synthesis rituals'—they *became* the Amalgamation!"

The creature stood at the archway, mortally wounded but still following its programming. Black fluid poured from dozens of injuries. Green flames danced across its fused torsos. Frost crystals spread across its side. The seven preserved forms stared with closed eyes and open mouths in eternal silent screams.

Cora and Mercer dragged Garrick the final fifteen feet to the Brotherhood door. Just as they reached the threshold, the Amalgamation's massive fist swung through the archway, catching Cora a glancing blow that cracked more ribs and sent her staggering.

But they were through. Together, they heaved Garrick across the threshold into the interstitial corridor.

Mercer threw his weight against the massive stone door, his slender frame straining. The door groaned on hinges that hadn't moved in two centuries, moving inch by agonizing inch. Through the narrowing gap, they saw the Amalgamation at the archway—the fused Brotherhood members, the guardians who had become what they guarded.

Mercer's Thunderwave finally took full effect, the concussive blast pushing the creature back 10 feet into the Central Hall, buying them the last few seconds they needed.

The massive stone door swung shut with a final, resonant *boom* that echoed through the corridor. The arcane script flared blue once more as the binding enchantment reactivated, then faded. The seal was restored.

## Aftermath

Silence.

Cora collapsed against the cold stone wall, breathing in ragged gasps, clutching her injured side. Mercer sank to his knees, trembling with exhaustion and residual terror. Garrick lay unconscious at their feet, his breathing shallow but steady thanks to Cora's medical skills.

They were safe. The Amalgamation was sealed back in the Grand Archive.

But they had learned terrible truths: the guardian wasn't created—it was the Brotherhood members themselves, transformed by forbidden synthesis rituals, possibly corrupted by the [[First World Sleeper]]'s resonance they were studying.

And they had failed. The Archive remained unclaimed, its secrets still guarded by a nightmare of fused scholars.

Mercer looked at Cora, then at Garrick's broken form. "We have to come back," he whispered, his voice trembling with equal parts terror and desperate curiosity. "When he's healed. When we're prepared. The answers are in there. About the Sleeper. About what happened here."

He paused, the weight of their near-death experience settling upon him.

"But not today. Today... we survive."

*The chronicler notes: The party has retreated from the Grand Archive, severely wounded and with Garrick unconscious. They have learned the true nature of the Amalgamation but failed to secure the Archive's secrets. The cost has been high—nearly a life, all their healing resources, and their confidence. What they do next will determine whether this expedition becomes a footnote in history or the prelude to greater discoveries... or greater tragedies.*
