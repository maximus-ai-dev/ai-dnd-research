# Session 17: The Gravity Well

*The following account is compiled from the field notes of the expeditionary team, supplemented by arcane monitoring data from the containment system itself. The chronicler has attempted to maintain narrative coherence while preserving the disorienting reality of the Caldera environment.*

---

The air in the [[Cold Forge Chamber]] still carried the scent of ozone and heated metal—the lingering memory of [[Ignathar]]'s reignited flames. For three days, the party had rested while [[Grimhold]] sent supplies down the repaired access shaft: fresh rations, replacement climbing gear, and specialized tools from [[Elara Stormwind]]'s shop. [[Brother Aldwin]]'s note had been terse: "The town is quiet. Too quiet. Whatever you're doing down there, finish it soon."

Now they stood before the repaired capstone—the massive, rune-carved seal that separated the known mountain from the void below. [[Cora Flint]]'s engineers had completed the airlock-style passage, its lead-lined walls humming with containment energy. The [[Dreamstone Circlet]]s rested cool against their brows. The [[Capstone Resonator]] charged with a low thrum. [[Garrick Kade]]'s runic maul vibrated in his hands, its runes glowing in sympathetic resonance. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] held the [[Architect's Codex]] open to Korathan's diagram of the [[Caldera]]—the cylindrical void, the anchor points, the observation platform. [[Korathan's Chisel]] replica hung at his belt.

The airlock door opened downward. Not into darkness, but into a warm, shadowless luminescence that seemed to come from the stone itself. The metallic sweetness that had haunted their descent for months was absent here. In its place: nothing. A perfect sensory void.

"Containment readings stable," Cora reported, checking the [[Monitoring Crystal]] one last time. "The capstone holds. The runes burn." She secured the [[Reality Anchor]] to her pack. "This is it. The threshold. Beyond this point, gravity fails. Direction is arbitrary. The broadcast is the atmosphere."

The passage descended ten feet through solid capstone before opening into the transition zone. The walls curved visibly here—the beginning of the Caldera's cylindrical shape. Giant handholds, each the size of a dinner plate, were carved into the stone at regular intervals. The containment runes burned with golden light so intense they overlapped, creating a continuous barrier of magical energy.

Fifty feet down, the Threshold ended. Their weight began to lessen—first by a quarter, then by half. Garrick tested his footing, pushing off gently and floating upward before catching himself on a handhold.

Then the Gradient began.

"Down" shifted. Not gradually, but in sudden, jarring reorientations. The floor became the wall became the ceiling. Garrick lost his grip during a forty-degree rotation, tumbling sideways—which was now "down"—before catching a Giant handhold ten feet away. The impact was solid but not debilitating.

Cora deployed the Reality Anchor immediately. The device hummed to life, projecting a fifteen-foot sphere of stable gravity around itself. Within the bubble, "down" pointed toward the Anchor, regardless of the surrounding distortion. "Stay within the field. Move as a unit. The Gradient gets worse before it gets better."

They descended further, crawling along what was once a wall, now the floor, now another wall—always with the Anchor as their gravitational center. The containment runes grew brighter, the cylindrical curvature more pronounced.

At the bottom of the Gradient, gravity failed entirely.

They reached the lip of the Caldera proper. One moment they were crawling along a curved surface with the Anchor providing orientation. The next, they were floating.

The void opened before them.

Two hundred feet across—a cylindrical space carved into bedrock so deep its bottom could not be seen. The walls were covered in containment runes that cast golden light into the emptiness, illuminating floating debris: chunks of stone from ancient collapses, fragments of Giant tools left during construction fifty thousand years ago. And other things. Crystallized formations of impossible geometry that twisted in non-Euclidean patterns. [[Dreamstone]] growths that spiraled in ways no natural crystal should allow. Small pools of liquid suspended in midair, reflecting light that did not come from the runes.

At the center of the Caldera—a direction they had to choose arbitrarily, for there was no "down" here—the glow. Not golden. Something else. A presence that pulled at the eye the way gravity should pull at the body. The [[First World Sleeper]]'s surface expression.

Garrick stared, unable to look away. His grip on his maul slackened. For one full round, he was motionless, transfixed by the scale, the beauty, the terrible wrongness of it all. When the moment passed, he shook his head, breathing heavily. "It's... bigger than I imagined."

They anchored to the Caldera wall by ropes and the Reality Anchor, floating at the edge of the void. Cora secured their position, driving pitons into the containment rune arrays. "This is our base. We need to scout. Find the observation platform [[Korathan]] mentioned. Locate the anchor points. Understand how to move in... this."

She gestured at the void, where crystalline formations drifted in slow, windless orbits around the distant glow.

* * *

**Systematic Observation**

Mercer floated away from the wall, his quill dancing across the Codex pages. "Interference patterning," he murmured. "The [[Giant]]s didn't just inscribe runes—they created a resonant field. Each rune reinforces its neighbors, creating a continuous barrier stronger than the sum of its parts."

He turned to the crystalline formations. "They're antennas. Not passive formations. They're receiving the broadcast, resonating with it. The growth patterns follow the intensity gradient—denser near the center, more diffuse here at the edge."

Cora methodically searched the immediate area, finding what she sought tucked behind a dense cluster of runes: a metal box, lead-lined and sealed with wax hardened to stone-like consistency over millennia. Inside: a set of crystalline lenses designed to fit over the observation platform's viewing panels, and a slate tablet covered in Giant script—a calibration guide for measuring the Anomaly's energy output, pulsation frequency, and dimensional stability.

As she examined her find, Garrick noticed movement in the debris field. Not the slow, random drift of inert objects. Purposeful rearrangement. The crystalline formations had rotated—all of them—to face their position. The suspended liquid pools merged into larger masses and began to pulse with rhythmic light.

"The broadcast intensity just increased," Mercer said quietly. "Not dramatically, but... deliberately. As if something noticed our examination."

The Reality Anchor hummed steadily. Outside its sphere, the void felt different now. Watchful. The golden light of the containment runes dimmed slightly, as if something was drawing energy away from the barrier.

* * *

**Harmonic Protocol**

The formations began displaying a pattern: a perfect, miniature replica of the Caldera itself, complete with glowing center and aligned formations. A map. Or a message.

"Five seconds," Cora snapped, already moving toward the third formation—the calibration point pulsing with distinctive amber light. Through the lenses, she saw equations: [[Korathan's scaling runes|Korathan's seventh modulation pattern]]. "The system wants proper observation protocol. This isn't an attack—it's a security measure."

Mercer's quill flew across the Codex. "The numbers counting down aren't a timer for failure—they're the input sequence! Each number corresponds to a specific harmonic node in the alignment!"

The countdown reached two. One.

Cora's hands moved with artificer's precision, adjusting the calibration lenses as she watched the harmonic frequencies. She didn't just match the frequency; she input the entire septenary array, aligning the lenses' crystalline filters with each harmonic node.

Zero.

The third formation pulsed once, intensely bright, then stabilized to a steady amber glow. The other formations shifted their displays—the countdown numbers vanished, replaced by a complex three-dimensional diagram showing the Caldera with a clear, glowing path marked along the wall. The path led to a structure labeled in Giant script: "Observation Platform."

The containment runes stopped dimming. Their golden light stabilized. The system had recognized them as authorized observers.

But at the far end of the illuminated path, where the observation platform should be, there was a problem. The containment runes in that section were dark. Completely extinguished. And floating near that dark section were shapes that weren't crystalline formations. They were geometric, polyhedral, shifting between transparent and opaque. Drifting in slow, coordinated patterns around the dark area.

[[Deep Things]]. The Sleeper's dreaming given temporary form.

* * *

**Containment Breach**

Through the calibration lenses, Cora counted eight [[Deep Things]]—each roughly three feet across, their surfaces shifting between crystalline transparency and opaque blackness in rhythmic pulse. They drifted in complex orbital patterns around a dark section about twenty feet across where twelve containment runes should be burning. Instead, the stone was cracked and blackened. Through the largest crack, a faint, sickly green glow emanated from within the wall itself.

"The damage is physical," she reported. "Twelve runes extinguished. The stone is cracked—badly. There's something glowing green inside the wall."

One of the [[Deep Things]] broke formation, drifting closer to the damaged section. It extended a tendril of crystalline material and touched the green glow within the crack. The green light pulsed brighter, then the Deep Thing withdrew, its surface shimmering with new energy patterns before returning to formation.

"They're not just feeding," Mercer whispered. "They're interacting with whatever that green energy is. That's not broadcast energy—that's something else. Something coming from inside the wall."

Mercer's [[Manifest Mind]] revealed the truth: the green glow was a network. Fine tendrils of sickly green light spread through the cracked stone like roots, branching from a central nexus deep within the wall—a miniature version of the [[fleshy amplifiers]] the [[Twisted]] had been building. A seed. An anchor point.

The [[Deep Things]] weren't just feeding on broadcast energy leaking through the breach. They were protecting this anchor point. Nurturing it.

"This isn't just containment failure," Mercer said, his voice tight. "This is active sabotage. From inside the containment field itself."

* * *

**Battle in the Void**

Garrick charged, his maul blazing as he activated Giant's Might. But the [[Deep Things]] were faster in their native environment. Eight crystalline polyhedra intercepted him simultaneously, surrounding him in a three-dimensional cage of shifting geometry. Their surfaces pulsed with the same sickly green light as the anchor point, and they began emitting the broadcast in a concentrated field.

The world dissolved for Garrick. He didn't see the Caldera, didn't see the [[Deep Things]], didn't see his companions. He saw the [[First World]]: liquid stone flowing in impossible patterns, singing crystal formations that vibrated with alien music, consciousness as a shared medium where individual thought didn't exist.

Cora's [[Stinking Cloud]] erupted between Garrick and the [[Deep Things]], but the crystalline forms drifted through unaffected. Mercer's [[Wall of Force]] sprang into existence around the green-glowing crack itself, containing the anchor point—and any energy release from its destruction.

The [[Deep Things]] noticed the barrier. All eight turned simultaneously, their surfaces shifting to opaque black. They emitted a harmonic whine that rose until it was almost unbearable.

Then they attacked the Wall of Force.

Not with physical force—with void energy. Green-black tendrils of corrosive force lanced from each [[Deep Thing]], striking the invisible barrier. The Wall of Force shimmered into visibility under the assault, glowing with strained magical energy. Cracks appeared—fine, spiderweb fractures that spread with each impact.

"The Wall won't hold long!" Mercer shouted. "Cora, we need to extract Garrick and retreat!"

But Garrick snapped out of the hallucination with a violent shudder. "Don't touch me!" he roared, intercepting his drifting weapon. "I'm fine! Get back to the corridor!"

Mercer began another incantation. "I'm going to try to sever the connection! When I do, the [[Deep Things]] might lose coordination! That's when you strike, Garrick! Not before!"

The Wall of Force shattered with a sound like breaking glass. The anchor point was exposed—and it had grown. The fleshy nexus had swollen to twice its previous size, pulsing with intense green light.

Mercer completed his [[Dispel Magic]]. A wave of dispelling energy washed over the anchor point, severing the magical bonds connecting it to the Sleeper's broadcast.

The green light flickered. The tendrils retracted slightly, their glow dimming. The connection severed.

The [[Deep Things]] reacted instantly. All eight stopped their assault, their crystalline surfaces shifting to transparent confusion. Their perfect coordination broke. They drifted aimlessly for a second, disoriented.

Then they turned. Toward Mercer. The source of the disruption.

Garrick saw his opening. He raised his maul and charged.

* * *

**Anchor Point Destroyed**

Garrick's maul connected with the fleshy nexus in a spray of green ichor and shattered crystal. The anchor point imploded, its sickly light winking out as the tendrils retracted and withered into desiccated threads.

Cora's [[Melf's Acid Arrow]] streaked across the void, striking the lead [[Deep Thing]] with sizzling impact. Mercer's [[Shatter]] spell erupted between three advancing formations, the thunderous boom echoing through the Caldera as concussive sound shattered two of them to dust.

But reinforcements were coming—a dozen more polyhedral shapes from the Caldera's center, moving with coordinated purpose. And the containment runes remained dark, their magical channels corrupted by the anchor point's residue.

"Structural repairs are done, but the runic channels are corrupted," Cora assessed. "Purge and reignition will take too long with reinforcements that close."

Mercer's hands flew through the air as he cast [[Stone Shape]]. The cracked stone flowed like liquid, fractures sealing themselves as the wall remembered its original integrity. But the runic channels remained dark, their magical pathways still corrupted.

The reinforcements were forty feet away. Sixteen [[Deep Things]] total, spreading out in a three-dimensional formation that surrounded them from multiple angles.

"Garrick, you can't hold sixteen of them alone," Cora snapped, pulling the [[Capstone Resonator]] from her pack. "Mercer, I'm using the Resonator's pulse—it'll clear the [[Deep Things]] and provide the energy surge we need to reignite the runes. You need to channel that energy into the runic channels the moment the pulse hits."

* * *

**Containment Restored**

The [[Capstone Resonator]] activated perfectly. A pulse of pure, structured magic erupted outward—a wave of golden energy that washed over the Caldera. The sixteen [[Deep Things]] disintegrated, their crystalline forms dissolving into fine dust.

Mercer's hands were already weaving through the air as the energy wave hit. His [[Magic Missile]] darts streaked into the first rune's channel, creating a perfect resonant pathway. The Resonator's massive energy surge followed, flowing through the interconnected channels with exquisite precision.

The first rune ignited—a point of golden light so intense it cast sharp shadows in the void. Then the second. The third. The energy cascaded through the harmonic sequence. Fourth. Fifth. Sixth.

Garrick stood his ground as the wave passed through him, the runes on his maul blazing in sympathetic resonance.

Seventh. Eighth. Ninth. The repaired section of wall became a blaze of golden light. Tenth. Eleventh. Twelfth.

All twelve runes burned with renewed power. The containment field stabilized, its energy flowing smoothly through the repaired channels. The breach was sealed.

* * *

**Strategic Retreat**

They retreated along the illuminated path, moving back through the stabilized corridor toward the transition zone. The watching [[Deep Things]] at a hundred feet made no move to follow—they simply hung in the void, observing.

The airlock door sealed behind them with a solid thunk. The transition zone felt almost homely by comparison. Cora immediately set up camp, tending to Garrick's wounds—deep bruises from force damage, but nothing broken.

Mercer studied the [[Deep Things|Deep Thing]] fragment Cora had collected. The crystalline shard glowed with soft internal light, pulsing faintly.

"It's a compass," he murmured. "Not just a fragment—it retains a directional connection to the Sleeper's core. It always points toward the Caldera's center. And it's... learning. The pulsing is adapting to our presence. It recognizes us as part of the system now."

Cora checked the [[Capstone Resonator]]. "Twenty-three hours to full recharge. We have time for a proper long rest before returning."

The monitoring equipment chirped—a status update. The containment field readings showed something new: a harmonic signature that wasn't there before. *Their* signature. The party's magical energies, their life forces, their recent repairs—all had been integrated into the containment system's observational data. They weren't just caretakers anymore. They were part of the containment.

Garrick watched the airlock door, his maul across his knees. "When we go back down... that observation platform. What do you think we'll see?"

Mercer looked up from his studies. "Everything. Magnified views of the Sleeper's physical manifestation. Monitoring equipment that's been waiting fifty thousand years for someone to use it. And answers. About what the Sleeper is. About what we're supposed to do next."

Cora secured the last of her gear. "Rest first. Questions later."

The transition zone fell into watchful silence. Above them, the capstone held firm. Below them, the Caldera waited. And at its center, something dreamed with newfound focus.

*The expedition has successfully descended into the Caldera, navigated zero-gravity for the first time, defeated Deep Things, repaired a containment breach, and established themselves as recognized caretakers of the system. They retreat to rest and recover before proceeding to the observation platform—the final vantage point from which they will behold the Sleeper in its dreaming prison.*
