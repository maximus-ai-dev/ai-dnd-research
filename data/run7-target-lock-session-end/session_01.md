# Session 1: The Mountain That Sits Wrong

*An excerpt from the field journal of Arannis the Chronicler, attached to the Grimhold Prospectors' Guild for the season of Deepfrost, Year 342 of the Third Age*

---

The wind that sweeps down from the [[Sentinel Mountains]] carries more than winter's first bite—it carries the scent of desperation and forgotten stone. Three travelers approached [[Grimhold]] as the sun dipped behind the peaks, their silhouettes stark against the fading light: a woman clutching a ledger like a shield, a warrior whose eyes held ghosts, and a scholar already lost in the geometry of ruins.

They came separately but found common cause in the muddy streets of the last true outpost before the wilderness claims everything. [[Cora Flint]], her alchemist's supplies clinking with each step, calculated survival in columns of income and expense. [[Garrick Kade]] moved with the heavy grace of a man who knows violence intimately, his chain mail whispering secrets of battles left behind. And [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]]—ah, the professor—saw not a frontier town but a puzzle, his spectacles catching the last light as he sketched the ancient standing stone in the square.

The bounty board at the [[Prospectors' Guild]] offered their first answer: fifty gold pieces total for ending the threat of the [[Emberfell Rejects]], a band of fifteen desperate souls from the [[Thieves Woods]] who had been raiding supply caravans. Not for treasure, the notice specified—for survival supplies. Winter comes early in the [[Starmetal Hills]], and hunger makes criminals of us all.

[[Reyna Steelforge]] emerged from the Guild as if summoned by their need. Flint-eyed and practical, she offered ten gold advance and credit at the [[Second Wind Inn]]. "The bandits disappeared toward that mountain nobody goes near," she said, nodding northwest where an unnatural peak broke the horizon's symmetry.

Garrick's jaw tightened at the name "Emberfell Rejects." "I know these men," he said, the words tasting of old betrayal. "Kregg leads them now."

Mercer, meanwhile, had wandered to the standing stone, tracing worn runes with academic reverence. "Third Age Dwarvish," he murmured to no one in particular. "This town is built atop something much older."

---

The [[Second Wind Inn]] welcomed them with stew-steam and firelight. There they found Harlan, a grizzled prospector who spoke of the mountain in hushed tones. "Camped near there once," he said, staring into his tankard as if it held dark water. "Woke up screaming three nights in a row. Dreams of falling. My partner heard whispers in the wind."

Elara Stormwind joined them, the scar running from temple to jaw speaking of her own frontier history. She ran the [[Griffon's Hold]]—weapons, armor, supplies. "I can draw you a map to the base," she offered. "Two days' walk if the weather holds."

It was Mercer who connected the threads with scholarly precision. "The 'dark water' motif appears in dwarven cosmology as reference to the Underdark or deep places of the earth. If prospectors are experiencing auditory hallucinations..." He trailed off, making notes about "geologically-induced psychic phenomena."

Their planning was interrupted by shouts from the loading dock. Two [[Emberfell Rejects]] were attempting to break into Guild storage—not hardened criminals, but starving men named Tobin and Jax who surrendered with desperate relief. "Kregg doesn't feed us unless we bring back supplies," Tobin gasped as Cora bandaged his broken ribs. "And the mountain... the dreams every night. Falling into black water."

The prisoners gave them everything: fifteen bandits total, two lookouts at a choke point halfway up the mountain, guard changes at dawn, noon, dusk, and midnight. Kregg himself drank from a metallic-tasting stream that flowed from the mountain's base and "fed something" in a deep well at the camp.

"Things move in the dark down there," Jax whispered. "Too big to be animals."

---

Dawn found them on the trail, the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] growing larger with every step. The air took on a metallic taste, like sucking on a copper coin. They found an abandoned prospector's camp, a journal left behind with increasingly frantic entries: "Strange dreams again. Falling. Always falling." "The mountain is wrong. It watches us." The final entry: "Leaving now."

At the mountain's base, they encountered Finn—a deserter kneeling by the stream, drinking with the blank-eyed devotion of a supplicant. He gave them the same information before fleeing down a side trail, clutching the travel ration Cora offered like a holy relic.

Then the horn sounded from above. Two scouts came down the switchback trail, and violence erupted in the thin mountain air. Garrick's maul broke ribs and splintered a crossbow. Cora's frost magic slowed and numbed. Mercer, fumbling with his component pouch, finally contributed with precise magical force that knocked a warning horn from grasping hands.

But the damage was done. Shouts echoed down from above, and a deep gong began to sound from the peak—a resonant, bone-shaking vibration that seemed to come from the mountain itself.

---

They reached the choke point—a narrow ledge between sheer rock faces—to find lookouts already alerted. Another brief, brutal skirmish left two more bandits defeated and a horn crushed to uselessness. Four reinforcements arrived, but Mercer's magic triggered a rockfall that bought precious seconds.

Then the gong sounded with a new rhythm, and the bandits withdrew with disciplined haste. "Kregg's orders," their leader called. "Fall back to the courtyard. Let them come to us."

Garrick stared up at the peak where a heavyset figure stood watching. Kregg. Even at this distance, the recognition was mutual and charged with old violence.

But the hornman they'd captured offered another path: an old mine shaft, sealed but marked with three stones in a triangle. "Comes out behind the storage tents," he gasped. "Near the well."

---

The mine entrance opened to damp darkness and the smell of wet stone and decay. Ancient timbers groaned under their weight as they descended into the mountain's belly. The thrumming vibration was stronger here, pulsing through veins of dark quartz in the walls.

In a mining gallery, they found something that was no longer human.

It stood swaying to the mountain's rhythm, milky eyes unseeing, black fluid dripping from nostrils and mouth to sizzle on the stone floor. When it turned toward them with a wet gurgle, Garrick didn't hesitate. His maul struck with crushing force, but the thing only staggered before lashing out with black-nailed fingers.

Cora's frost slowed it. Mercer's magic missiles struck with precision. And then the gong sounded—five rapid strikes—and the corrupted figure convulsed, back arching unnaturally before stumbling into a collapsed tunnel and disappearing into darkness.

"Kregg's been feeding something in the well," Garrick said, wiping black fluid from his maul. "I think we just met one of his offerings."

The shaft continued upward to a crude platform beneath the well opening. Torchlight filtered down, and with it came the sounds of bandits preparing defenses. Voices, movement, the ever-present vibration of the gong.

They stood at the bottom of the rope that led to the courtyard, to Kregg and his fifteen desperate men, to whatever moved in the dark waters below. The mountain thrummed around them, a living thing with secrets in its stone heart and corruption in its veins.

Garrick tested the rope. It held.

"This is it," he said, looking at his companions. "We go up, we hit them from behind."

Above, in the torchlit courtyard, the past waited with a notched axe and a mountain's power at his command. Below, in the dripping dark, three strangers turned allies prepared to climb toward a reckoning years in the making.

The [[Asymmetrical Mountain]] watched, patient and wrong, as they began their ascent.
