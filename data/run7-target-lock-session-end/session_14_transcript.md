**DM:** The air in the Cold Forge Chamber still carries the faint, mineral scent of freshly worked adamantine, but the forge itself has been banked for several days. Cora, Garrick, and Professor Mercer have taken the time they needed — a journey back to Grimhold for supplies, a few nights of proper rest at the Second Wind Inn, and long conversations over stew and ale about what comes next.

The Dreamstone ore sits in its lead-lined pouch, a tangible weight of potential and danger. The Giant blueprint tablet rests beside Mercer's notes, its runes now familiar but still holding secrets. And the forward camp in the Giant foundation level awaits their return, a fragile foothold in the most ancient part of the mountain.

Now, gathered once more in the Cold Forge Chamber with packs restocked and minds clear, Mercer unrolls the blueprint tablet's transcription across a workbench. His finger traces the intricate network of containment architecture until it stops at a symbol unlike any they've seen before — a rune that seems to shift in meaning as you look at it, a glyph that represents both "rest" and "vigil."

"Here," he says, his voice quiet with the weight of discovery. "The Maker's Rest. The tomb of Korathan, Master Runecarver of the Architect's Council. The Giant who designed the containment vessel itself."

He looks up at the others, his expression a mixture of scholarly excitement and sober realization. "We have the blueprint. We have the monitoring crystal. We've seen how the containment can be attacked. But we don't understand the principles behind it. Not truly. The scaling techniques, the harmonic calibrations, the fundamental architecture — that knowledge is in there."

He taps the symbol on the transcription. "The Giants buried their master builders with their greatest works. Not just as honor, but as preservation. The tomb isn't just a grave — it's a library. And if we're going to have any chance of reinforcing this containment before it fails completely..."

He lets the sentence hang. The implication is clear: they need what's in that tomb.

The journey back through the familiar corridors of the Giant foundation level takes several hours. The scale still humbles — doorways forty feet tall, corridors wide enough for three wagons abreast, everything built for beings who saw the world from twenty feet above the ground. The forward camp is as they left it: the monitoring crystal humming softly, their supplies neatly organized, the air still carrying the faint ozone scent of the retuned containment field.

And now, following the blueprint's directions to the deepest section of the outer ring, they stand before it.

The door is a single piece of obsidian, forty feet tall and twenty feet wide, polished to a mirror finish that reflects their tiny forms back at them. Its surface is covered in Giant script — not the functional rune sequences they've seen throughout the containment network, but narrative text. A story carved in stone. To the left of the door, built into the frame, is a lever mechanism clearly designed for Giant hands — fifteen feet above the floor, with counterweights and gears that suggest it requires immense force to operate.

The air here is still and cold, with a quality of silence that feels intentional. This place was built to be undisturbed. This is where the architect of the mountain's purpose chose to wait through eternity.

The Giants calculated that this would be sufficient time for the younger races to develop the knowledge and power to either reinforce the containment permanently or find an alternative solution. The younger races have had roughly fifty thousand years so far. The containment is at the halfway point. It is weakening on schedule.

4. **The Gift:** The scaling runes are deliberately placed in the tomb because Korathan anticipated that the eventual caretakers would be smaller than Giants. The tomb is not just a burial — it is a technology transfer. Korathan gave the younger races the tools to continue the work.

The message ends. The runes dim. Korathan lies still, watching from behind closed eyes, continuing the long vigil.

Garrick is quiet for a long time. Finally: "A hundred thousand years. That is how long the cage is supposed to hold. And we are already halfway through."

Mercer nods, his expression solemn. "The question is not whether it will fail. The question is what we build before it does."

Cora looks at her completed notes, then at Korathan's still form. "We have the tools now. The scaling principles, the harmonic calibrations. We can reinforce the containment. We can build what's needed."

The chamber feels different now — not just a tomb, but a beginning. The inheritance has been received.
