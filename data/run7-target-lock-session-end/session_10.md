# Session 10: The Drowned Chokepoint

*An entry from the field journal of an unnamed chronicler traveling with the Emberfell Rejects*

---

The pumps had been running for a week, and the mountain had developed a new heartbeat.

The rhythmic thrum of dwarven engineering echoed through the [[Cold Forge Chamber]], a mechanical pulse that had transformed the once-dead [[Dwarven Layer]] into something approaching livable. Warm air now circulated through ancient [[Cooling Channels]], fed by the newly operational pump network that drained the lower levels. [[Cora Flint]] stood before her restored control panel, watching pressure gauges with the focused intensity of an engineer witnessing her creation come to life. Across the chamber, [[Garrick Kade]] sharpened his [[Torvan Deepwatch's War-Pick]], the scrape of whetstone on metal keeping time with the pumps. [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] hunched over the Giant blueprint tablet, its luminous runes casting blue light across notes that grew more ominous with each passing day.

"We're going down," Cora announced, her voice cutting through the mechanical rhythm. "The pumps are at seventy percent. The lower levels are accessible now."

The descent was steep and unnerving. The minecart creaked along corroded rails, descending through tunnels that grew warmer and heavier with each passing moment. The air thickened with moisture and the unmistakable metallic sweetness of concentrated [[Dreamstone]] radiation. Garrick felt it first—a pressure behind his eyes, a faint ringing in his ears that had nothing to do with the minecart's rumble.

The walls told a story of occupation. Dwarven stonework, once clean and geometric, now showed modifications by [[The Twisted]]. Rails extended with crude additions, side tunnels widened by something that *grew* over them, loading platforms built from black, organic resin that pulsed faintly in the dim light. This wasn't maintenance. This was expansion.

Then the tunnel opened into the [[The Drowned Chokepoint]].

The cavern was enormous—a hundred feet across, fifty high, carved as the convergence point for the entire dwarven water management system. Massive pipes entered from every direction, some still dripping, others dry and silent. The floor was a network of drainage channels radiating from a central platform like wheel spokes, water receded to knee level but still present, reflecting the faint bioluminescence of organic structures.

And on that central platform, the Twisted had built their workshop.

Mechanical components stripped from other dwarven installations lay organized on resin workbenches. Tools modified with biological grips hung from racks. Stone tablets etched with hybrid script—Dwarvish engineering notation merged with something organic and angular—were stacked neatly.

At the center of it all, partially disassembled for modification, stood [[The Drowned Juggernaut]].

Eighteen feet of dwarven iron and stone shaped into a mining automaton. Arms ending in rotating drill assemblies. A torso furnace, cold and dark. Legs of pistons and hydraulic rams. Organic resin filled the gaps between iron plates, serving as both adhesive and conduit. Black fluid ran through transparent resin tubes like blood vessels, connecting mechanical systems to a biological control node mounted where a head should be—a pulsing, iridescent mass of corrupted tissue.

The machine was dormant, connected to the organic infrastructure by thick resin cables. But the metallic sweetness of Dreamstone radiation was overwhelming here, intense enough that even Garrick felt dizzy with it.

"Don't move," Cora whispered, her eyes sweeping the cavern. "That thing's dormant, but those cables are feeding it. If we sever them—"

"Study it after it's scrap metal!" Garrick roared, leaping from the minecart.

He activated Giant's Might as he charged, his body swelling as he became Large. His war-pick arced toward the thickest resin cable connecting the Juggernaut to the wall. The strike landed with a wet *thwack*—not the clean severing he intended. The cable flexed but held, spraying droplets of viscous fluid that sizzled on stone.

The biological control node pulsed with angry light. Crimson to violet and back again. The resin cables began to pulse in rhythm—a slow heartbeat that accelerated with each passing second.

"Garrick, no!" Mercer hissed. "That's not just a power cable—it's a biological interface!"

But the professor's warning came too late. The Juggernaut woke.

It happened all at once—the node flared with blinding crimson light, the furnace ignited with iridescent heat that smelled of burning Dreamstone, and the drill assemblies spun to life with a scream that echoed through the cavern. With hydraulic pistons firing, the machine charged.

Garrick barely dodged the first drill charge, taking a glancing blow that tore through his chain mail. He swung back, his war-pick striking the Juggernaut's iron torso but deflecting off reinforced plating. Sparks flew. The machine didn't flinch.

Mercer scrambled toward the stone tablets, his academic focus laser-sharp. "Twenty-five on my Arcana check," he murmured, his voice dropping to a whisper of awe. "The biological node... it's a translator. It processes the Anomaly's psychic radiation into mechanical instruction. This machine doesn't think—it *dreams* the Sleeper's dream of breaking free."

Cora's eyes darted to the drill orientation. "It's aimed at the floor," she realized. "At the [[Giant Capstone]]."

The Juggernaut's furnace glowed brighter. Iridescent heat radiated from its torso—the Corruption Furnace was charging. Garrick saw the energy buildup, smelled the Dreamstone burning. He charged again, this time with Action Surge, putting everything into a two-handed swing at the biological node.

The war-pick struck true with a wet, sickening crunch. Corrupted tissue ruptured, spraying black fluid. The node's light flickered erratically, and for a moment, the Juggernaut staggered.

But the moment didn't last.

The machine recovered with mechanical determination. Its drill assembly slammed into Garrick with crushing force, lifting him off his feet and hurling him into a drainage channel. Water exploded around him as he crashed down hard.

"It's regenerating!" Cora shouted. Even as the Juggernaut attacked, the organic resin pulsed with renewed vigor. The black fluid pumped faster, feeding energy back into the damaged node. The wound was already closing.

Mercer found what he was looking for—a master control diagram etched into a large stone slab. "The cables!" he shouted over the mechanical roar. "They're not just power conduits—they're *limiters*! The Twisted weren't just modifying the Juggernaut—they were *containing* it!"

The Juggernaut turned toward Garrick again, its furnace glowing brighter. The Corruption Furnace erupted.

A thirty-foot cone of iridescent fire and necrotic energy washed over the workshop. Garrick took the brunt, his already battered body absorbing another wave of searing pain. Mercer screamed, collapsing behind a stone slab. Cora barely managed to dive behind a workbench.

Then Garrick struck the node again.

This time, the war-pick smashed into the biological control node with devastating force—a critical hit that ruptured it completely. Black fluid sprayed in a wide arc. The node's light died.

The Juggernaut entered berserk mode.

The machine shuddered violently, drill assemblies spinning wildly, hydraulic legs pistoning erratically. It lost all tactical behavior, attacking everything randomly. One drill slammed into the stone wall, carving a deep groove. Another swung wildly, smashing through a resin workbench. The machine was a whirlwind of destruction, but it could no longer target specific creatures—it attacked the environment, the walls, the floor, everything.

Mercer, through pain-clouded vision, saw the master diagram. "Containment failure protocol: berserk state lasts three rounds, then system collapse."

Three rounds. A lifetime when facing eighteen tons of berserk dwarven engineering.

The Juggernaut's wild drill swing caught Garrick with a glancing blow. Then a second strike—a devastating critical hit. The drill smashed into Garrick with brutal force, tearing through what remained of his chain mail, through flesh and bone. The impact lifted him off his feet and hurled him against the stone wall with a sickening crunch. He slid to the floor, leaving a bloody smear, and didn't move.

"Garrick, no!" Mercer screamed.

Cora darted forward, calculating the Juggernaut's erratic movements. She slipped past the massive form as it lurched sideways, reaching Garrick's broken body. Her hands pulled out diamond dust for Revivify.

Garrick lay motionless, blood pooling around him. His chest rose and fell in shallow, ragged breaths. His condition worsened, life slipping away.

The Juggernaut turned—not with intelligence, but with berserk instinct. Cora was now the nearest active creature. Its first drill slam caught her in the side as she knelt beside Garrick, tearing through leather armor. She grunted in pain but didn't stop her work.

The second drill slam followed immediately—a devastating critical strike aimed directly at her kneeling form.

"Protego!" Mercer shouted, his voice cracking with strain.

A shimmering, invisible barrier of force sprang into existence between Cora and the descending drill. The Juggernaut's critical strike slammed into the Wall of Force with a sound like thunder, but the barrier held. The impact reverberated through the cavern.

The Juggernaut thrashed against the barrier. Then the berserk state reached its conclusion.

The machine shuddered violently. Its furnace flickered, the iridescent heat dying. Drill assemblies slowed, then stopped. Hydraulic legs locked. With a final, shuddering groan, the Drowned Juggernaut collapsed—eighteen tons crashing to the stone floor with an impact that shook the entire cavern.

Silence followed, broken only by water dripping and Cora's whispered incantation. Golden light flowed from her hands into Garrick's broken body, knitting shattered bones, sealing torn flesh, restarting a heart that had nearly stopped.

Garrick gasped, his eyes flying open. He was alive—battered, broken, but alive.

* * *

In the aftermath, Cora worked with practiced efficiency, stabilizing Garrick's multiple fractures and internal bleeding, then tending to Mercer's burns. The wreckage of the Juggernaut lay scattered—twisted dwarven iron, ruptured organic components, black fluid slowly pooling on stone.

Mercer knelt by the Giant Capstone, his fingers tracing the three-foot groove carved by the Juggernaut's drilling. "They were three feet through," he murmured. "The containment is millimeters thick at this point."

Below the capstone was darkness—not the darkness of an unlit room, but a darkness that moved, that breathed, that had weight. The Anomaly's domain began immediately below this stone.

As they salvaged what they could—[[Adamantine Chassis Plates]], a [[Dwarven Master Forge Key]], a [[Dreamstone Power Cell]] still pulsing with energy, damaged schematics—a sound rose from below. Not the rhythmic industrial noise of the Twisted, not the mechanical breathing of the pumps. Something older. A vibration felt in the bones rather than heard.

The mountain hummed.

Garrick, propped against the wall with his war-pick beside him, said what they were all thinking: "We need to go down there eventually, don't we?"

Mercer nodded. "Not today. But yes. Eventually."

The [[First World Sleeper]] waited below. And now, with the Drowned Juggernaut destroyed and the chokepoint secured, there was nothing left between the Emberfell Rejects and whatever dreamed beneath the mountain.
