# Session 9: The Water That Remembers

*From the personal chronicles of an observer in the service of the Vigilant Brotherhood*

The [[Cold Forge Chamber]] hummed with renewed life on the third day of the expedition's rest. [[Ignathar]]'s fire burned steady in the central hearth, casting dancing orange light across ancient dwarven stonework. [[Garrick Kade]] had spent his days sharpening his maul, [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]] transcribing the intricate details of the [[Dwarven Transit Map]], and [[Cora Flint]] organizing alchemical supplies from her trip to [[Grimhold]]. The forge was operational, the minecart network cleared, and the mountain's industrial heart beat again—but the question remained: what else beat with it?

Mercer spread the transit map across a worktable, his Awakened Spellbook manifesting as a floating spectral quill that illuminated the parchment. "The network is extensive," he said, tracing lines with a finger. "The eastern hub we cleared connects to three major branches: the upper [[Dwarven Layer]] residential sections, the [[Cold Forge]] itself, and... this." He tapped a line that descended sharply northward. "The northern branch leads to [[Pump Station East]]. According to dwarven engineering principles, it should be draining the lower Dwarven Layer."

Cora leaned over the map, her alchemist's goggles pushed up on her forehead. "The water level in the lower levels is wrong. When we were in the mausoleum, the [[Dwarven Radiation Survey Notes]] mentioned flooding. If the pumps are offline, everything below the forge level could be submerged."

From the forge hearth, Ignathar's voice rumbled like shifting coals. "The small ones drain the deep places. The deep things will not be pleased. Water holds secrets. Water hides what should not be seen."

Mercer's investigation of the maintenance notes revealed a chilling truth: the flooding had begun three hundred and forty-seven years ago, and the water should have stabilized at eighteen inches—not continued rising. "We're not looking at a simple pump station repair," he concluded, his academic excitement tempered by grim realization. "We're looking at an archaeological dive into a drowned dwarven city. And something has been living down there, maintaining the infrastructure, for longer than most human civilizations have existed."

---

## The Twisted Work Here

The tunnel to [[Pump Station East]] yawned dark before them, the air thick with the scent of damp stone and something else—something metallic and organic that clung to the back of the throat. Water lapped at the tunnel entrance, not six inches deep as the ancient notes suggested, but a dark, still surface that stretched into darkness. And from deep within came a rhythmic *thump-thump-thump*, like a massive heart beating underwater.

Ignathar's warning echoed in the chamber: "Three hundred and forty-seven years. That is when the deep stone first groaned. When the water began to taste of dreams. The small ones sealed their doors. The small ones drowned their own halls. They chose drowning over what waited below."

Mercer's spectral eye drifted ahead, revealing unnaturally placid water with an iridescent sheen. Beneath the surface, dwarven tools lay neatly arranged on the submerged rail bed—pry bars, stone chisels, hammers—gleaming as if placed there yesterday, not three centuries past. On the tunnel walls, just above the waterline, dwarven containment seals had been carved over with crude, newer markings: stick-figure dwarves standing in water, their bodies distorted with wavy lines. Arrows pointed downward beside figures with extra limbs and lines radiating from their bodies.

"The original dwarven runes say 'Containment Breach—Do Not Proceed,'" Mercer translated, his voice tight. "The newer carvings say 'The Twisted Work Here. The Water Remembers. Do Not Wake The Drills.'"

As he spoke the last word, the thumping rhythm accelerated. From deep in the darkness, something moved in the water—a shadow detaching from the wall and gliding toward them with purpose. It carried a tool.

Cora's analytical mind snapped into focus. "That's not water—it's a containment medium. Those tools are preserved because the water itself is maintaining them." She hurled a flask of alchemical acid at the water around Garrick's legs as he stood knee-deep in the liquid. "Acid disrupts surface tension! If they're held together by water cohesion, we need to break that cohesion!"

But the shadow emerged into Ignathar's firelight, and they saw it clearly: not a water manifestation, but a dwarf. Or what had been a dwarf. Stocky and broad-shouldered, wearing corroded dwarven chain mail, its skin bearing the iridescent sheen of [[Dreamstone]] contamination. Black veins pulsed visibly beneath the skin. Its eyes were open, aware, tracking. It carried a pry bar not as a weapon, but as a tool.

Behind it, three more rose from the water.

---

## Professional Sabotage

The [[Twisted]]—for that was what they were, as Mercer's arcane analysis confirmed—moved with chilling efficiency. Their leader, a field commander with a warhammer that appeared from somewhere within its maintenance gear, assessed the situation with professional detachment. It didn't see enemies; it saw obstructions to its work.

The warhammer swung with brutal precision, catching Cora in the side with a sickening crunch of armor and bone. She staggered back, gasping as ribs cracked. The attack was a combat action by the Twisted Saboteurs. The adventure file states they 'do not speak. They do not negotiate.' and their objective is to 'destroy the pumps, prevent drainage'.

Garrick roared in anger, activating his Giant's Might and surging to Large size. He charged the field commander, slamming it into the tunnel wall with the force of a battering ram. Black fluid sprayed from its mouth as the impact jarred its corrupted body. "You want to sabotage pumps? You deal with me first!"

But the other Twisted didn't attack. They worked around him with coordinated purpose—one climbing the tunnel wall with surprising agility, another dropping into deeper water to swim beneath the surface, two more trying to distract while their pinned comrade scrambled for its tools. According to the adventure file, the Twisted Saboteurs' combat objective includes engaging the party: 'Two will engage Garrick at the barricade as a distraction. Two will attempt to circle... to reach the pumps from behind.'

Mercer's analysis crystallized into horrifying focus as he cast Hold Person on the bypassing Twisted. "They're not maintenance crew responding to system activation! They're saboteurs preventing reactivation! They want the pumps to stay offline!"

Ignathar's fire created a wall of radiant heat at the tunnel threshold, but the Twisted field commander responded by throwing a handful of black organic resin at the barrier. Where the resin hit, it didn't melt—it *grew*, spreading across the heat field like mold on a petri dish, forming a latticework of black filaments that systematically disrupted the thermal energy. The adventure file describes the resin as 'a black organic resin — hard as stone, resistant to tools' that fuses valves shut. It does not describe it as anti-magic or learning.

From deeper in the mountain came a grinding, metallic shriek—the sound of sabotage in progress. The field commander looked at the party one last time, its dark eyes calculating cost versus benefit. Then it turned and disappeared into a maintenance shaft, moving with urgent purpose.

---

## The Warning Shot

Garrick carried the now-unconscious Cora as they retreated, her body convulsing from psychic backlash. The adventure file describes no such psychic defense mechanism or reality anchor. Combat damage came from Twisted attacks (warhammer, Corruption Aura). The humming from [[Pump Station East]] changed, becoming a resonant vibration that shook dust from the tunnel ceiling as they fled.

The adventure file contains no such negotiation tablet. The Twisted 'do not speak. They do not negotiate.' Crude carvings showed stick figures carrying an unconscious figure away from the station. Below it, Dwarven runes: **"Knowledge is contamination. Leave the knowing here."**

The adventure file contains no negotiation or offers from the Twisted. They 'do not speak. They do not negotiate.'

They refused.

The retreat to [[Grimhold]] was tense and silent. Brother [[Aldwin]]'s healing magic flowed into Cora in the [[Chapel of the Hearthfire]], mending broken ribs and coaxing her consciousness back from the edge. [[Reyna Steelforge]] arrived as Cora was waking, her practical miner's eyes taking in the strange tools, the resin samples, the stone tablet with its crude warnings.

"Report," she said, her voice flat.

Garrick met her gaze. "The pumps aren't broken. They're being maintained. By things that used to be dwarves. They're keeping the lower levels flooded for a reason."

Mercer placed the stone tablet on a table. "They call themselves 'the Twisted.' They've been down there for centuries. Maintaining sabotage. Preventing drainage." He pointed to the carvings. "'Do Not Wake The Drills. The Water Must Stay.'"

Reyna studied the tablet, her expression unreadable. "My grandfather's journals mentioned them. The dwarves weren't just mining ore. They were digging toward something. Something that made them flood their own work and seal themselves in with it."

She looked from the tablet to Cora, now sitting up with Aldwin's help. "You didn't fix the pumps."

"No," Garrick said. "We discovered why they shouldn't be fixed."

Reyna was silent for a long moment. "The bounty for securing the mountain stands. You've secured it by discovering a threat and not triggering it. But Grimhold needs those lower levels drained. The ore down there could sustain this town for generations."

She met each of their eyes in turn. "So here's your new objective: understand what the Twisted are containing. Find a way to contain it differently. Or destroy it. Then drain the mountain. Because one way or another, that water is coming down."

*Chronicler's Note: The expedition has shifted from reclamation to investigation. What began as a simple pump repair has revealed a containment system older than Grimhold itself, maintained by corrupted engineers who sacrifice their very humanity to keep something submerged. The "drills" they warn of—are they tools or entities? And what happens when water that remembers meets fire that purifies? The mountain holds its breath, and so must we.*
