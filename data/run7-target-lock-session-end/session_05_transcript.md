Now it is spring. The snow retreats from the mountain's shoulders. The Grand Stairwell beckons downward, into the dark where empires built on empires built on empires.

**ACT 1: THE GRAND STAIRWELL DESCENT**

The timber door groans open on well-oiled hinges. Beyond lies the Grand Stairwell — 120 feet of descending stone, torch brackets empty, the air still and cold. The upper 60 feet are Iron Warlords construction: utilitarian, serviceable, the stonework rough but solid. Below that, the architecture changes.

The steps become wider, the ceilings higher. Carved relief panels appear on the walls — soldiers in parade formation, officials in ceremonial dress, maps of territories that vanished beneath ocean or forest millennia ago. Military Empire builders favored grand entrances, and this stairwell was meant to impress. At the base, a landing with a single iron-banded door. Above it, carved in archaic script that Mercer recognizes as Military Empire Common: *THE IMPERIAL LOWER PALACE. LET ALL WHO ENTER SERVE THE EMPIRE'S MEMORY.*

Garrick tests the landing floor with his boot. Solid, but hairline cracks radiate from the center — ancient, not fresh, but the pattern suggests stress from below. Cora's eyes trace the masonry. The stairwell itself is sound, built to last, but the landing... something under it is settling, or was.

The door opens without resistance. Beyond lies a corridor twelve feet wide, eight feet high — built for formations, not individuals. The walls are carved with continuous friezes showing battles, annexations, tribute deliveries. Every thirty feet, a brass bracket holds a guttered torch. The air here is different: damp, old iron, and a faint metallic sweetness so distant it might be imagination — the ghost of very old blood.

To the left, an open archway leads into a large rectangular room with rows of stone sleeping platforms — a garrison hall, scaled twice the size of the Iron Warlords' barracks above. To the right, another archway reveals a dining hall with long stone tables. On one table sits a sealed ceramic flask, dark liquid visible through the glaze.

And straight ahead, the corridor continues into darkness, ending at another door visible sixty feet distant — the entrance to the Relic Basements.

But in a side corridor off the dining hall, the floor has partially collapsed — a ten-foot section dropped two feet, stone cracked and tilted. Through a finger-width gap below: darkness, and the sound of moving air. Not wind. Circulation. Whatever is below this level is not sealed.
