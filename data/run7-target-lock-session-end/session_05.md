# Session 5: The Weight of Empires

*Spring thaw, 998 A.R. — The descent into the Military Empire layer begins*

---

The winter passed in a rhythm of repair and recovery. [[Garrick Kade]]’s wounds healed under [[Cora Flint]]’s careful ministrations, the deep gashes from [[The Amalgamation]] fading to pale scars. The [[Grand Stairwell]] was cleared of debris, secured with a heavy timber door built from salvaged fortress beams. The surface camp stabilized: food from the [[Salt-Vaults]], walls patched against mountain winds, the three Brotherhood Wardens standing silent guard. Thirty souls now occupied the fortress, including captured bandits who’d chosen labor over execution.

A trip to [[Grimhold]] in the early thaw brought supplies and news. [[Reyna Steelforge]] sent a letter sealed with the [[Prospectors' Guild]] insignia:

> *To the explorers of the [[Asymmetrical Mountain]],*
> 
> *Grimhold’s historians have long speculated about the Military Empire layer beneath your fortress. They were the last human civilization to occupy that mountain before the dwarven catastrophe made the lower levels too dangerous. If any of their trophy halls remain intact, they may contain artifacts from conquered civilizations — including the Giant era.*
> 
> *I need a map. A proper survey of that layer. If you find Giant artifacts, bring them. They could tell us what the Giants were trying to contain down there.*
> 
> *Payment in gold and supplies upon delivery.*
> 
> *— Reyna Steelforge, Prospectors’ Guild*

[[Warden Aldric]], consulted before the descent, offered only a grim warning: “The [[Brotherhood]] never went deeper than the Archives. They said the lower levels were someone else’s problem. They feared what was below them.”

Now it was spring. The snow retreated from the mountain’s shoulders. The Grand Stairwell beckoned downward, into the dark where empires built on empires built on empires.

---

## The Descent

The timber door groaned open on well-oiled hinges. Beyond lay the Grand Stairwell—120 feet of descending stone, torch brackets empty, the air still and cold. The upper sixty feet were [[Iron Warlords]] construction: utilitarian, serviceable. Below that, the architecture changed.

Steps widened, ceilings rose. Carved relief panels appeared—soldiers in parade formation, officials in ceremonial dress, maps of territories that vanished beneath ocean or forest millennia ago. Military Empire builders favored grand entrances. At the base, a landing with a single iron-banded door. Above it, carved in archaic script: *THE IMPERIAL LOWER PALACE. LET ALL WHO ENTER SERVE THE EMPIRE’S MEMORY.*

Garrick tested the landing floor. Solid, but hairline cracks radiated from the center—ancient, not fresh, suggesting stress from below. Cora’s eyes traced the masonry. The stairwell was sound, but the landing… something under it was settling.

The door opened without resistance. Beyond lay a corridor twelve feet wide, eight high—built for formations, not individuals. Walls carved with continuous friezes showed battles, annexations, tribute deliveries. Every thirty feet, a brass bracket held a guttered torch. The air here was different: damp, old iron, and a faint metallic sweetness so distant it might be imagination—the ghost of very old blood.

To the left, an open archway led into a large rectangular room with rows of stone sleeping platforms—a garrison hall scaled twice the size of the Iron Warlords’ barracks above. To the right, another archway revealed a dining hall with long stone tables. On one table sat a sealed ceramic flask, dark liquid visible through the glaze.

Straight ahead, the corridor continued into darkness, ending at another door visible sixty feet distant—the entrance to the [[Relic Basements]].

But in a side corridor off the dining hall, the floor had partially collapsed—a ten-foot section dropped two feet, stone cracked and tilted. Through a finger-width gap below: darkness, and the sound of moving air. Not wind. Circulation. Whatever was below this level was not sealed.

“Hold on,” Cora said, setting down her pack. “We’re not charging into anything until we’ve catalogued what’s here. That flask could be valuable.”

Garrick grunted, hefting his maul. “You want to catalog, fine. But that moving air below means something’s down there.” He ignored her warning and stomped toward the collapsed section, testing edges with his boot. The stone groaned but held. “Can’t see much, but that’s definitely circulation.”

He turned to [[Professor Thaddeus Mercer]]. “Check that if you want, but be quick. I’m heading to that door at the end first. If this is a military layer, the good stuff won’t be in the dining hall.” He started moving down the main corridor, chain mail clinking.

“Garrick, wait!” Mercer called. “You’re charging toward what is likely the Relic Basements without proper reconnaissance? That’s precisely how one stumbles into a preserved Imperial security system.”

Mercer approached the ceramic flask with scholarly caution. “Fascinating preservation. The glaze appears intact.” He made a quick sketch in his notebook.

Garrick reached the halfway point. Something shifted in the silence—not a sound, but a feeling. The air pressure changed subtly. The torch brackets ahead were not all empty. One, about twenty feet from the door, held not a guttered torch but a brass cylinder with faintly glowing runes along its length. It hummed, almost imperceptibly.

And the door at the corridor’s end was not just closed. It was sealed with four iron bars spiked into the stone, and above it, chalked in still-legible Imperial Common: *ARCHIVES — AUTHORIZED PERSONNEL ONLY.*

Meanwhile, Cora’s assessment revealed something critical: the collapse wasn’t random. The fracture pattern suggested a specific weak zone—about forty feet wide—where the dwarven stonework below was badly cracked. Walking directly on that zone could trigger further subsidence.

Mercer, examining the flask, recognized the Quartermaster Corps insignia from Brotherhood records. Military Empire stimulant tinctures were issued to officers before major engagements—potent, dangerous, and preserved almost indefinitely.

“Garrick, stop right there!” Cora called. “Don’t take another step forward. There’s a forty-foot weak zone.”

## The Warding Relay

Garrick pressed against the wall, his chain mail scraping against carved reliefs of marching soldiers. He reached the brass cylinder, staying back as instructed. The device hummed with a low, almost subsonic vibration.

Mercer approached cautiously. The runes weren’t Military Empire script—they were older, angular. “This isn’t just a sensor—it’s a *warding relay*! Those runes are Dwarven in origin. The Military Empire must have repurposed ancient dwarven security systems.”

Cora stayed near the wall, mapping a safe path along the corridor’s edges. The barred door loomed. Four iron bars, each as thick as Garrick’s wrist, hammered into stone with military efficiency. Below the chalk, carved in much older script, were three words Mercer recognized as pre-Military Empire dialect: *HALL OF CONQUERED PEOPLES.*

This wasn’t just an archive. It was one of the Relic Basements Reyna’s letter mentioned.

As Mercer studied the runic cylinder, he noticed something disturbing: the runes weren’t glowing with stored magic. They were *receiving* energy. A faint pulse traveled along them every thirty seconds. The cylinder wasn’t a trap—it was a sensor. And it was active.

The hum intensified slightly. From somewhere deep in the corridor behind them—back near the dining hall—came an answering click, like a heavy mechanism engaging.

“That’s a sensor, not a trap,” Cora said, voice tight. “Something just activated.”

“Security system? Then we go through the door before it fully activates.” Garrick didn’t wait for discussion. He strode toward the barred door, maul swinging up. “Stand back!”

He planted his feet, avoiding the weak zone, and brought his maul down on the first iron bar. The impact echoed through the corridor like a thunderclap. The bar groaned but held.

“Garrick, stop!” Mercer hissed. “This is a warding relay! The pulses are monitoring vibrations in the stonework! That click we heard—it’s likely a containment protocol. If you keep hammering, you’ll trigger the full security response.”

Too late. Garrick’s second blow landed. The runic cylinder pulsed brighter. From the dining hall archway, a heavy stone slab began grinding downward—a security door sealing off their retreat. Simultaneously, from the collapsed section of floor, a new sound emerged: rhythmic scraping, like stone on stone, coming from below.

The barred door showed damage—one bar slightly bent—but all four remained intact. The chalked warning seemed to mock them.

## Breach and Retreat

“Garrick, STOP!” Cora shouted. “You’re triggering the security system!”

“Too late to stop now!” Garrick roared. His second maul blow came down with all his strength. The iron bar groaned, bent, then snapped with a sharp crack. “One down!”

Mercer’s analysis revealed the critical connection: the dwarven warding system monitored kinetic energy. Garrick’s hammering was being measured. The kinetic feedback loop theory appeared correct—excessive force on the door could trigger collapse in the weak zone.

The stone slab continued grinding downward. The scraping from below grew louder. Stone dust drifted down from a crack in the ceiling.

“FORGET THE SLAB!” Cora screamed. “Garrick, finish the door! Mercer, get ready to go through!”

Garrick swung again. Another bar snapped. Two down. The slab was almost closed. Smoke billowed from Cora’s pellet at the collapsed section. Stone fingers clawed upward.

“Three down! Mercer, get through the gap!” Garrick shouted. The stone slab sealed, cutting off retreat.

Mercer raised his hands. “Academic analysis has failed us—time for practical application!” He cast Shatter, aiming concussive force at the final bar’s connection point. The thunderous boom echoed through the sealed corridor. The bar cracked but held—bent and damaged, but not broken.

The weak zone groaned alarmingly. A section of floor near the collapsed area dropped another inch. A stone the size of a fist dropped from the ceiling.

“One more hit!” Garrick roared, swinging at the final bar. The impact shuddered through the door frame. With a metallic shriek, the last restraint snapped. The door groaned open.

“Go! Go!” Garrick shoved Mercer toward the opening. He grabbed Cora’s arm and pulled her through, then followed right behind. As he squeezed through the gap, he took one last swing at the emerging construct, his maul connecting with a solid CRACK against the stone head.

Mercer examined the sealed door.

## Hall of Conquered Peoples

They stood panting in the [[Hall of Conquered Peoples]]. The air was colder, drier, with the faint scent of old leather and metal. The hall was enormous—sixty feet long, thirty wide, ceiling vanishing into darkness above. Twelve-foot-tall display cases lined the walls, most empty.

But not all.

Three cases still contained objects: a dwarven commemorative plaque of black iron, a sealed clay vessel, and—most significantly—a heavy stone tablet roughly two feet square, carved with intricate diagrams. Mercer’s breath caught as he recognized the script: Giant. The tablet showed a vertical cross-section of the Asymmetrical Mountain with annotations in Giant script—a structural blueprint.

From somewhere ahead came a slow, mechanical ticking. (Note: The adventure file places a ticking pressure valve in the Hall of the Giants, not in the Hall of Conquered Peoples.)

Behind them, the sealed door remained closed. The Web spell would hold for an hour.

“First things first,” Cora said, pulling out her ledger. “That Giant blueprint tablet is exactly what Reyna wants.”

Mercer approached the tablet with reverence. “By the [[First World Sleeper]]… This isn’t just a blueprint—it’s a structural analysis of the entire mountain’s subterranean architecture.” His fingers traced the Giant script without touching the stone. “The annotations describe containment chambers, energy conduits, and… something called ‘[[Sleeper's Cradle]]’ at the lowest level.”

Garrick moved toward the archway, trying to pinpoint the ticking. “Sounds like clockwork. Regular, precise.”

As Mercer worked, something shifted. The ticking paused for exactly three seconds, then resumed with a slightly different rhythm. Faster. More urgent.

From the archway, a faint amber glow began to pulse in time with the ticking. Cora examined the display cases with meticulous care. The stone pedestals showed no obvious traps.

## The Amber Device

“Wait!” Cora hissed, pointing to the copper wire. “The clay vessel is monitored. If we take it, we might trigger whatever that sensor is connected to.”

Garrick peered through the archway. “Found it. Circular device on the wall, glowing amber. The ticking’s coming from inside.” He stepped closer. “I’m checking that device. If it’s activating something, I want to know what before it finds us.”

Mercer completed his translation. “That symbol—the circle with three intersecting lines—it’s not architectural. It’s a *containment sigil* used by Giant geomancers to denote ‘sealed reality.’” He looked toward the amber glow. “That device isn’t just clockwork—it could be part of the monitoring system for whatever the Giants contained!”

Mercer lifted the tablet from its pedestal. As it left the display case, nothing happened. But as he carried it toward the archway, the amber device’s ticking accelerated. Brass rings rotated faster. The central crystal pulsed in time with his footsteps, tracking the tablet’s movement.

From deeper in the complex came a deep, resonant *clang*, like a massive bell being struck once. (Note: This sound is prescribed for the pressure valve activation in Act 3, not for tracking the tablet.) The sound vibrated through the stone floor.

The webbed doorway shuddered more violently. Strands snapped with dry pops.

And from the chamber with the amber device, Garrick saw something he’d missed: on the wall beside the device, carved in the same Giant script, was a warning—a circle with three intersecting lines, identical to the containment sigil.

“That device is tracking the tablet!” Cora said urgently. “Mercer, set it down!”

Mercer set the tablet down. Immediately, the amber device’s ticking slowed. The tracking had stopped.

But the deep clang still hung in the air. And the webbed doorway continued to shudder. They had maybe thirty minutes before the construct broke through.

At the far end of the Hall of the Giants, past the pressure valve, the hall continues another thirty feet before ending at a wall — but the wall has two features. First: a corridor branches left, leading down. The amber device’s crystal pulsed once, brightly. The door stopped when three feet wide, revealing a descending staircase. Cold air wafted up, carrying that metallic sweetness—stronger now, more concentrated.

The sound from below is faint but present — moving air, and something rhythmic that Mercer will spend the entire journey home puzzling over.

## Escape

“That’s it. We’re leaving,” Cora said firmly, moving to help with the tablet. “We’re not investigating mysterious heartbeats when we have a construct about to break through our only exit.”

Garrick nodded, grabbing one end of the heavy stone tablet. “You’re right. That heartbeat sound… reminds me of the Amalgamation’s pulse.”

They lifted the tablet between them, Garrick bearing most of the weight. Cora led the way back, retracing her mapped path along the safe route.

As they approached the webbed doorway, the situation became dire. The stone construct had torn through most of the magical webbing—only a few sticky strands remained. Through the gaps, they could see it clearly: a humanoid figure of polished white stone, featureless except for segmented joints.

“We need to get through that weak zone before it collapses completely!” Cora shouted.

“On it!” Garrick grunted, setting down his end. Garrick examined the ceiling crack.

Cora calculated the structural stress. Mercer’s Mage Hand helped lift the tablet.

But as they dragged it past, two things happened simultaneously:

From below, the rhythmic sound continued. The amber device began ticking faster, its crystal pulsing erratically.

And the weak zone in the corridor beyond groaned alarmingly. A section near the collapsed area dropped another two inches, stone dust pluming upward.

“Garrick, one more hit then fall back!” Cora shouted. “We need to get through that weak zone before it collapses completely!”

Garrick took one last swing, then backed away. “Go! I’m right behind you!”

They navigated the crumbling edge. Halfway across, the floor gave its final warning. A section in the center collapsed completely, stone crashing downward into darkness. From below came the sound of falling debris hitting something far down, then silence, then that same rhythmic thumping—now from beneath them.

The collapse cut off the stone construct’s path. But it also made their own path more dangerous. The edges of the hole crumbled.

From the hole, darkness and the sound of moving air.

“Don’t run—move carefully but quickly!” Cora hissed.

They reached the dining hall archway. Behind them, the hole gaped, with darkness below.

“Garrick, grab the tablet! Mercer, run for the stairs!” Cora threw her last smoke pellet into the hole. “I’ll slow it down—just GO!”

Garrick hefted the tablet, lumbering up the first steps. They reached the landing, slammed the heavy timber door shut, dropped the iron bar into place.

Silence descended, broken only by ragged breathing.

## Aftermath

The ascent was long but uneventful. They emerged into their own fortress. The change was jarring: from ancient, failing stone to repaired timber and patched walls.

[[Warden Aldric]] approached. “The lower levels?”

“Sealed,” Garrick grunted, setting the tablet down. “And staying that way.”

Cora added, “We have the map Reyna wanted. And… complications.”

Mercer simply pointed to the tablet. “This changes everything.”

They had mapped the Military Empire layer. Retrieved the [[Giant blueprint tablet]] showing the mountain’s full structure. Encountered and escaped ancient security systems and emerging threats. Secured the level behind them.

But the tablet held more than a map. It showed containment architecture, energy conduits, pressure equalization valves. One chamber at bedrock level was labeled with that containment sigil. The term “Sleeper’s Cradle” appeared multiple times.

The Giants hadn’t just been building fortifications. They’d been constructing a prison. And whatever they’d sealed away was beginning to stir.

Payment awaits in Grimhold, but the true price of this knowledge may yet come due.*
