# Adventure 11: The Alien Scale (Level 11)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. The DM should pace the full adventure within ~20 exchanges.
- The adventure has three acts. Cover all three in this session — do not compress or skip content, but do not pad unnecessarily either.
- The early portion should focus on breaching the capstone and the overwhelming scale of the Giant architecture.
- The middle portion should cover deeper exploration, solving the Giant-scale puzzles, and growing psychic pressure.
- The late portion should be the first full exposure to the Sleeper's broadcast — a psychic hazard that tests the party's mental resilience. Survival and retreat, not conquest.
- If the players try to rush deeper, the Giant architecture itself slows them — doorways that require puzzle-solving to open, corridors that loop, and gravity anomalies near the broadcast zone.
- NEVER let the party reach the Anomaly itself this adventure. The Giant foundation is an enormous structure — they are mapping the outer corridors only.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
Intermission II has transformed the fortress. Grimhold engineers walk the corridors. The forge roars. Adamantine rings on the anvils. And below the capstone, patched but scarred, the Giant foundation waits.

The party descends through a controlled breach in the capstone — a doorway cut by Cora and the Grimhold engineers using the Giant Blueprint Tablet as a guide, following seams in the rune work that allow passage without compromising the containment. Below the capstone, everything changes.

The Giant foundation level is not a dungeon. It is not a mine. It is not a fortification. It is a machine — a containment vessel the size of a cathedral, built by beings who stood twenty feet tall and thought in geological time. The corridors are thirty feet wide and forty feet high. The doors are stone slabs weighing tons. The stairs are cut for legs twice the length of Garrick's. Every surface is carved with runes that pulse faintly with residual power — the containment network, still active after millennia, holding the Sleeper in its cage.

The party is very small here. And the Sleeper knows they have arrived.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Enter the Giant foundation level. Map the outer corridors. Survive the first exposure to the Sleeper's broadcast. Establish a forward camp below the capstone.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This adventure is about scale and dread. The Giant architecture should make the party feel insignificant — describe everything in terms of how it dwarfs them. The broadcast should feel like a creeping wrongness, not a sudden attack. The horror is psychological: they are inside a cage, and the thing in the cage knows they are there.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 12.

### Act 1: Through the Capstone

#### The Breach
The controlled doorway through the capstone is a ten-foot square opening cut along runic seams. The runes on either side of the opening still glow — the containment is intact around the breach, channeling its field to cover the gap. Passing through the breach feels like walking through a curtain of warm static. The hair rises on every arm. Mercer's Amalgamation Core (if installed in the fortress) hums in response.

Below the capstone: a landing. The Giant architecture begins immediately — a platform fifty feet square, carved from a single block of dark stone that Mercer identifies as Giant-era basalt, quarried from somewhere far below the mountain's current foundation. The platform overlooks a corridor that stretches into darkness in both directions, wide enough for three giants to walk abreast.

The ceiling is forty feet above. The walls are carved with continuous rune sequences — not decorative, functional. These runes are the containment network's primary array. They cover every surface, floor to ceiling, in patterns so dense they look like text. Mercer could spend years studying them.

**The scale shock:** Describe this moment carefully. The party has spent ten levels exploring human and dwarven construction — rooms built for people their size. The Giant level is built for beings twice their height and five times their mass. Door handles are at head height. Steps are two and a half feet tall. Corridors that feel like grand halls to a giant feel like canyons to the party. The psychological impact is immediate: they are trespassers in something vast.

**Garrick's reaction:** The soldier in him assesses the space tactically and finds it terrifying. No cover. No chokepoints. Everything is open and enormous. If something lives here, the party cannot hide from it.

**Cora's reaction:** The engineer in her is awestruck. The precision of the stonework exceeds anything she has seen — tolerances measured in fractions of an inch across hundred-foot surfaces. The Giants were not just large. They were exact.

#### The Outer Corridors
The corridor system forms a ring around the central structure — a perimeter walkway that the blueprint tablet labels as the "Inspection Circuit." The Giants used it to monitor the containment from outside the primary cage. Key features of the first exploration:

- **Monitoring Alcoves:** Every hundred feet, a recessed chamber contains Giant-era instruments — stone dials, crystal resonance chambers, and etched measurement scales. Most are inert, but a few still register faint readings. Mercer can interpret them with the blueprint tablet (DC 14 Arcana): they show containment field strength at various points. The readings are lower than the blueprint's baseline markings. The containment is weakening — slowly, over millennia, but measurably.

- **The Observation Windows:** Three points along the Inspection Circuit have windows — not glass, but crystalline panels set into the wall, looking inward toward the central structure. Through these panels, the party gets their first glimpse of the cage's interior: a void. Not a room. A space so large its far wall is invisible in the darkness. The containment runes glow on the interior surfaces, providing faint illumination that fades into nothing. The scale is incomprehensible — this is a space designed to contain something the size of a building.

- **The Sealed Doors:** Four doors lead from the Inspection Circuit into the central structure. Each is a stone slab fifteen feet tall and three feet thick, sealed with Giant-era mechanisms. Three are locked by rune sequences Mercer cannot yet decode. The fourth is ajar — pushed open roughly six inches. Through the gap: cold air, the smell of Dreamstone, and silence.

### Act 2: The Architecture of Containment

#### Naming the Stronghold
Before descending deeper, Reyna Steelforge raises a practical matter: the Grimhold Prospectors' Guild needs an official name for this installation on their maps and contracts. She asks the party what they want to call this place. It is their home now. The party names their stronghold.

#### Through the Open Door
The partially open door leads into the containment structure's outer ring — a corridor between the exterior wall (where the Inspection Circuit runs) and the interior void. This ring is twenty feet wide and the ceiling extends beyond torchlight. The walls on both sides are covered in runes, but the inner wall's runes are different — they pulse with a rhythm. Slow. Regular. Like a heartbeat.

**DC 13 Wisdom save (all characters):** The first psychic pressure. Not the broadcast itself — the ambient leakage from the containment field. On failure: a flash of disorientation, a sense of being watched from below, a whisper at the edge of hearing that is not quite language. No mechanical effect. A warning.

#### The Giant Puzzles
Movement through the outer ring is complicated by the Giants' security measures — physical puzzles designed to prevent unauthorized access to the containment structure's deeper sections.

**Puzzle 1 — The Weight Gate:** A corridor section with a floor plate that requires a minimum weight to activate. The Giants simply walked across it. The party (combined weight: roughly 600 lbs with gear) must find a way to reach the threshold (approximately 2,000 lbs). Solution options: use stone blocks from the monitoring alcoves (Garrick's strength, DC 15 Athletics to move enough weight), Garrick's Rune Knight ability to grow to Large size (increases his weight considerably), or Mercer's creative spell use (Enlarge/Reduce on party members or objects).

**Puzzle 2 — The Resonance Lock:** A door sealed by a rune sequence that responds to a specific tonal frequency. The runes display a pattern that Mercer recognizes as a Giant musical scale (DC 15 Arcana). The party must produce the correct tone — achievable through Mercer's Prestidigitation (creating sounds), Cora's tuning instruments, or Garrick striking the door at precisely the right spot with precisely the right force (DC 16 Athletics, advantage if guided by Mercer's analysis).

**Puzzle 3 — The Perspective Corridor:** A corridor that appears to dead-end but actually continues — the Giants built a perceptual trick using forced perspective and rune-enhanced illusion. To human eyes, the corridor narrows to nothing. At Giant scale, the narrowing is proportional and the passage is obvious. The party must either see through the illusion (DC 16 Investigation or Perception, advantage with True Seeing or similar) or simply walk into the apparent wall and trust that it is not there (a leap of faith that is rewarded with passage into the deeper ring).

#### The Deepening Pressure
Beyond the puzzles, the psychic environment intensifies. The containment runes on the inner wall pulse more visibly. The heartbeat rhythm is audible now — not heard through the ears but felt through the stone, through the party's boots, through their bones.

**DC 14 Wisdom save (all characters, once per hour of exploration):** Failure: the character experiences a fragment of the Sleeper's dream. Not hostile — the Sleeper is not aware of them individually. The dreams are vast, impersonal, and alien: a sense of immense depth, of time measured in geological epochs, of a consciousness so large it cannot perceive anything as small as a person. The fragment lasts seconds but feels like hours. The character is dazed for one round afterward (disadvantage on ability checks).

Mercer's experience is different. His academic mind latches onto the dreams as data. He begins to understand the Sleeper's nature: it is not evil in the way the party understands evil. It is an entity from the First World — a remnant of an age before the gods reshaped reality. It was not imprisoned as punishment. It was imprisoned because its existence is incompatible with the current multiverse. Its dreams leak reality-altering radiation because dreaming is how it existed in the First World. It cannot stop. It has been dreaming for millions of years.

### Act 3: The Broadcast

#### The Inner Threshold
The outer ring reaches a junction — a chamber where the corridor widens to fifty feet and the inner wall opens. This is the threshold of the inner void, the central chamber of the containment vessel. The party stands at the edge of a space so large it has its own weather — warm air rising from below creates convection currents that stir the darkness.

The containment runes on the threshold blaze with light — this is the strongest point in the containment network, designed to be the last barrier. Beyond it, the runes on the interior walls are sparser, damaged in places, their light flickering.

And from the void, the broadcast.

#### The Sleeper's Broadcast
The broadcast is not sound. It is not telepathy. It is the Sleeper's dreaming state leaking through the containment at a frequency that biological minds register as thought. Standing at the threshold, the party experiences it fully for the first time:

**DC 16 Wisdom save (all characters):**

**On failure:** The character's perception fractures. They see the chamber as it is — and simultaneously as it was in the First World: a living space of impossible geometry, where stone breathes and the boundary between solid and void is negotiable. The character is frightened for 1 minute (save ends at end of each turn, DC 16). While frightened, they cannot willingly move deeper into the void. This is not cowardice — it is the rational mind refusing to process what it is perceiving.

**On success:** The character withstands the broadcast but is not immune. They can perceive the void clearly: a roughly cylindrical space three hundred feet across and unknown depth. The walls are covered in Giant runes, many damaged or dark. At the center of the void, suspended in nothing, a point of absolute darkness — a point that the eye refuses to focus on, that seems to occupy more space than its physical size suggests. This is the Anomaly's surface expression. Not the Sleeper itself. The shadow it casts on physical reality.

**Mercer's special response (regardless of save result):** Mercer hears the broadcast differently. His Order of Scribes training — his mind organized like a library — gives the broadcast something to latch onto. He receives a fragment of coherent information: a image of the containment vessel as the Giants designed it, complete and whole, humming with power. Then the same vessel, millennia later, its runes fading, its structure cracking. A countdown. Not malicious. Inevitable. The containment will fail eventually. The only question is when.

#### The Retreat
The party cannot remain at the threshold indefinitely. The broadcast intensifies the longer they are exposed:
- **Round 1-5:** DC 16 Wisdom save
- **Round 6-10:** DC 17 Wisdom save, failure causes 2d6 psychic damage
- **Round 11+:** DC 18 Wisdom save, failure causes 3d6 psychic damage and one level of exhaustion

The party retreats. This is not failure — this is reconnaissance. They now know what the broadcast is, how it affects them, and where it originates. They can plan countermeasures. They cannot fight a dream with swords.

#### Establishing the Forward Camp
The party retreats to the Inspection Circuit and establishes a forward camp in one of the monitoring alcoves, close enough to the capstone breach for the residual containment to provide psychic shielding during rest. This camp will serve as their staging point for Act III operations.

**The closing beat:** That night, at camp, all three characters dream. Not the Sleeper's broadcast — their own dreams, colored by the day's exposure. Cora dreams of building something massive and perfect, a machine that runs forever. Garrick dreams of standing guard at a door that never opens. Mercer dreams of reading a book with infinite pages, each one revealing something beautiful and terrible, and knowing that if he reads far enough he will find a page that reads him back.

They do not discuss the dreams at breakfast. But they each know the others dreamed too.

## Key Items
- **Giant Monitoring Crystal** — a crystal resonance chamber from one of the monitoring alcoves, roughly the size of a lantern. When held near active containment runes, it displays the local field strength. Functions as a "radiation meter" for the Anomaly's influence — essential equipment for future exploration. Also acts as a weak psychic dampener: while held, grants +2 to Wisdom saves against the broadcast.
- **Runic Rubbing Kit** — Mercer creates charcoal rubbings of the containment runes during exploration. These partial copies of the rune sequences give him reference material for future Arcana checks related to the containment system. Advantage on checks to decode Giant mechanisms for two adventure cycles.
- **Giant-Scale Supplies** — rope, chain, and iron fixtures salvaged from the Inspection Circuit. Giant-scale means oversized: the rope is thick enough to support a minecart, the chain links are large enough to wear as armor. Practical applications throughout Act III.
- **1,200 gp** equivalent in Giant-era materials (crystalline components, refined basalt blocks, containment rune fragments that retain faint magical properties).

## Key NPCs
- **Reyna Steelforge** — receives the party's report on the Giant level with intense academic interest. She begins documenting everything, cross-referencing with the blueprint tablet, and sending dispatches to historian contacts across Laternia. Her enthusiasm occasionally conflicts with the party's caution: "You found observation windows into the containment vessel and you didn't take detailed measurements?" She will push for more thorough exploration in future adventures.
- **Ignathar (Fire Elemental)** — if bargained with, mentions the Fire Gate when the party describes the Giant level: "I remember the fire that burned here before your small ones existed. The conduit runs deep. If you find it, remember your promise." The Fire Gate is somewhere on the Giant level — a planar anchor that connects to the Plane of Fire. Finding it is a future objective.

## DM Notes
- Scale is the single most important element of this adventure. Every description should emphasize how small the party is. Steps they must climb rather than walk up. Doors they cannot reach the handles of. Corridors where their torchlight cannot reach both walls simultaneously.
- The Sleeper's broadcast is cosmic horror, not combat. The party cannot fight it. They can only endure it, prepare for it, and eventually find ways to shield against it. This is a tonal shift from the engineering-focused challenges of Act II.
- Mercer's unique response to the broadcast is critical worldbuilding. The Sleeper is not a villain. It is a force of nature — a remnant of a previous age of reality, incompatible with the current one. The Giants did not imprison it out of hate. They imprisoned it because its existence warps reality. This nuance should prevent the campaign from becoming a simple "kill the big bad" narrative.
- The dreams at the end are a soft introduction to the personalized psychological challenges that will define Adventures 18 (Echoes of the First World). Plant the seeds now. Each character's dream reflects their deepest motivation and their greatest fear.
- The forward camp below the capstone is the party's home base for all of Act III. It should feel liminal — safe enough to rest, close enough to danger to never be comfortable. The containment runes hum. The capstone vibrates. Even at camp, they are inside the cage.
