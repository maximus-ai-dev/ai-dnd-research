# Adventure 5: The Weight of Empires (Level 5)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure with approximately 20 exchanges. The DM should pace the full adventure within this one session, covering all three acts.
- The adventure has three acts. Give each act enough breathing room — do not rush through Act 1 to get to Act 3. Each act should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- If the players try to rush ahead, introduce complications: load-bearing walls that crumble under investigation, staircases that collapse mid-use, rooms sealed by military-grade iron bars that screech when forced.
- NEVER let the party open the Sealed Crypt this adventure. Every time they try, something goes wrong — structurally, mechanically, or because something on the other side pushes back.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
Three months have passed since the Amalgamation fell. The Grand Stairwell is clear, secured with a new door the workforce built from salvaged timber. The surface camp is stable — food from the Salt-Vaults, walls patched, the Wardens at their posts. The party has earned a winter of relative peace.

Now it is spring. The stairwell beckons.

The Military Empire layer is the deepest of the three human epochs — built directly atop the dwarven ruins, separated from the Giants' architecture below by fifty feet of dense, stressed stone. The Empire was the last human civilization to occupy this mountain before the dwarves' catastrophe made the lower levels too dangerous. They built here for centuries, piling great halls and garrison quarters and trophy rooms on top of each other until the weight of their own construction began to crack the dwarven vaults below.

The primary hazard is not monsters. It is the building itself. Every wall in the Military Empire layer is a calculation. Some are load-bearing for three floors above. Some are already cracked. Some are held together only by the frozen, compressed rubble around them. The wrong footstep in the wrong place could bring a ceiling down — and that ceiling is the floor of the Iron Warlords' level, and above that is the Brotherhood's sanctum, and above that is the Salt-Vaults, and above that is the party's fortress with thirty people in it.

The party has been asked by Reyna Steelforge (via letter, carried up by a Grimhold trader who found the fortress easily now that it has a cleared trail) to produce a map of the lower levels. Grimhold's historians believe the Military Empire left behind trophy halls containing artifacts from every civilization they conquered — including the Giant era. If any Giant artifacts remain, they could tell the party a great deal about what is below the dwarven layer. And about what the Giants were trying to contain.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Map the Relic Basements of the Military Empire layer. Do not trigger a structural collapse. Locate and mark — but do not open — the Sealed Crypt.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This adventure introduces the concept of environmental stakes. The party is not just fighting monsters — they are navigating a building that is actively trying to fall down. Every room should feel provisional. Mercer's mapping instincts and Cora's engineering mind are as valuable here as Garrick's maul. This is an expedition, not an assault.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 6.

### Act 1: The Grand Stairwell Descent

#### The Stairwell
The Grand Stairwell descends 120 feet from the Brotherhood sanctum door to the Military Empire level. The upper 60 feet are Iron Warlords construction — serviceable, utilitarian, the torch brackets still sound. Below that, the architecture changes. The steps become wider, the ceilings higher, the stonework more elaborate. Military Empire builders favored grand entrances: carved relief panels on the walls showing soldiers in parade formation, officials in ceremonial dress, maps of territories long since vanished beneath ocean.

At the base of the stairwell, a landing with a single iron-banded door. Above the door in carved Military Empire script (Mercer can read it as archaic Common with a DC 10 History check): *THE IMPERIAL LOWER PALACE. LET ALL WHO ENTER SERVE THE EMPIRE'S MEMORY.*

**Garrick's read on the space:** The stairwell is sound. Someone built this to last. The Iron Warlords' section above was retrofitted into an existing opening — the Grand Stairwell predates them by centuries.

**Cora's read:** The landing floor has hairline cracks radiating from the center. Not fresh — ancient. But the pattern suggests the floor is being stressed from below. Something under it is settling, or was.

#### The Outer Corridors
The door opens onto a wide corridor — twelve feet across, eight feet of ceiling. Military Empire scale: built for formations, not individuals. The walls are carved with continuous friezes showing battles, annexations, tribute deliveries. Every thirty feet, a brass bracket holds a guttered torch, long burned out.

The air is different here. The Iron Warlords' layer smelled of dust and old stone. The Brotherhood's sanctum smelled of preservation and ink. This smells of damp and old iron and something that takes a moment to identify: the faint metallic sweetness of very old blood, so distant it might be imagination.

**Structural note for DM:** The outer corridors are the safest part of the Military Empire layer. The masonry here is thick and well-built — the Empire concentrated their construction quality on public-facing spaces. It gets worse as the party moves deeper into residential and storage areas.

**What the party finds in the outer corridors (early exploration):**
- **The Garrison Hall:** A large rectangular room with rows of stone sleeping platforms, identical in layout to the Iron Warlords' barracks above but twice the scale. Long-rotted wooden furniture has left only iron fittings and stone supports. On the far wall, a painted mural showing soldiers in full plate — Military Empire armor is heavier than anything the party has seen, designed for massed formation combat. The paint has flaked but the composition is clear: they were organized, regimented, proud.
- **The Officer's Mess:** A dining hall. Long stone tables, iron rings for hanging lanterns (the lanterns are gone). On one table, a tray of stone cups and a sealed ceramic flask. The flask contains something dark and viscous — preserved by the dry cold. Mercer identifies it as a military-grade stimulant tincture, centuries old, probably still functional (treat as 2 doses of *Potion of Hill Giant Strength* if consumed, but with a Constitution save DC 13 or suffer 1d4 hours of tremors afterward).
- **The First Warning:** In a side corridor off the Officer's Mess, the floor has partially collapsed — a ten-foot section has dropped two feet, the stone cracked and tilted. Below the crack, visible through a finger-width gap: darkness and the sound of moving air. Not a wind. A circulation. Whatever is below this level is not sealed air — something is moving down there.

**DC 14 Engineering (Cora's Investigation):** The collapse isn't random. The dwarven vaults below this section are particularly stressed. There is a zone, roughly forty feet wide, where the dwarven stonework is cracked badly enough that the Military Empire floor above it has lost significant support. Walking on that zone is risky. She can mark it on the emerging map.

#### Act 1 Transition
The party has descended to the Military Empire level, established a base camp at the landing, explored the outer corridors, and encountered the first structural warning. They have a partial map. They know the terrain gets more dangerous deeper in. The Relic Basements are through a second door at the corridor's far end — visible, reachable, but not yet explored.

### Act 2: The Relic Basements

#### Into the Trophies
The second door leads to the Relic Basements proper — a series of interconnected trophy halls where the Military Empire stored the physical evidence of its conquests. These are not storage rooms. They were designed to be walked through, admired, to remind those who served the Empire what they were building.

The halls are staggering in their ambition and melancholy in their current state. Twelve-foot display cases — stone pedestals with iron brackets that once held artifacts — line every wall. Most are empty. Many show signs of deliberate removal: artifacts taken when the Empire evacuated. But not all.

**Key finds in the Relic Basements:**

- **Hall of the Conquered Peoples:** Contains ethnographic trophies — weapons, tools, clothing fragments, personal objects from civilizations the Empire annexed. Most are decayed beyond use, but three items survive: a dwarven commemorative plaque (iron, engraved with a date in Dwarvish reckoning — Mercer can calculate it predates the dwarven occupation of this mountain by 400 years, meaning the Empire conquered dwarves elsewhere and brought a trophy here), a sealed clay vessel containing dried seeds of an extinct grain variety (Cora finds this fascinating — it could potentially be cultivated), and a Giant-era carved stone tablet roughly two feet square. The tablet is heavy. Its surface shows a diagram Mercer recognizes with growing excitement: a vertical cross-section of the Asymmetrical Mountain, showing all five strata, with annotations in Giant script. It is a structural blueprint. It shows, clearly, a large sealed chamber at the bedrock level — the anomaly's location — and the containment architecture around it. This is the most significant single artifact the party has found. It goes in Mercer's pack immediately.

- **Hall of Military Honors:** The Empire's own trophies — armor from generals, standard-bearer poles, commendation scrolls sealed in iron tubes. The scrolls are mostly illegible but one iron tube is sealed with a Commander-rank seal Mercer recognizes from the Brotherhood logbook. It contains a letter, readable, addressed *To whatever Empire Officer commands this garrison when the corruption reaches the lower levels.* The letter is a formal military assessment written by an officer who knew what the Anomaly was and was told to pretend it wasn't happening. It ends: *The Crypt will not hold indefinitely. My recommendation is full evacuation. My orders are otherwise.* This is the first evidence that the Military Empire knew about the Sealed Crypt's contents and chose not to deal with them.

- **Hall of the Giants:** The most impressive hall — and the most damaged. It was clearly the centerpiece of the Relic Basements, dedicated entirely to artifacts from the Giant era. The display cases here are enormous, scaled to Giant objects. Most are empty. The Empire looted them during evacuation. But in the back of the hall, too heavy to move quickly: a Giant-era iron mechanism, roughly the size of a wagon, bolted to the floor. Its purpose is unclear but Mercer's examination (DC 16 Arcana, aided by the Giant blueprint tablet) reveals it is a pressure valve — part of the Giant containment system for the anomaly. It is not active. It is not connected to anything visible. But it hums faintly when Mercer touches it, and the hum corresponds to a specific annotation on the blueprint tablet.

#### The First Structural Incident
Deep in the Hall of Military Honors, Garrick notices the ceiling is wrong. The stones are bowing slightly downward — not catastrophically, but perceptibly. One section, about fifteen feet wide, shows a crack running the full width. When he examines it, small fragments of stone dust drift down.

**DC 13 Athletics or Investigation:** The crack is fresh — within the last few months. The weight of the party's own movement through this level may be redistributing stress that has been in equilibrium for centuries. Moving quickly is fine. Stopping to examine things in certain rooms is not.

Before the party can react to this assessment, the crack widens by a hairline. A stone the size of a fist drops from the ceiling and hits the floor twenty feet away with a sound like a gunshot. Everything goes still. Then, from somewhere above — four floors up — a faint groan of stressed masonry.

Nothing collapses. This time.

**Cora's calculation:** If the party continues moving through the Relic Basements, they are adding stress to an already compromised ceiling. Not enough to cause a collapse right now. But they are approaching a threshold. Whatever they plan to do in the late part of the adventure, they need to do it carefully.

#### The Sealed Crypt
At the far end of the Hall of the Giants, behind the pressure valve mechanism, is a door unlike anything else in the Military Empire layer. It is not Military Empire construction — the stonework is too old, the proportions slightly wrong. This was here before the Empire built around it. The Empire found it and chose to use it.

The door is sealed six times over:
1. The original lock — a mechanism Mercer identifies as Military Empire-era, still functional
2. A lead plate bolted over the keyhole (lead is significant — the same material used to contain the Anomaly's corruption in the Giant architecture)
3. Four iron bars spiked directly into the surrounding stone, clearly added in a hurry
4. A wax seal over the iron bars with a Commander's insignia
5. A chalked warning in Imperial Common (still legible in the dry air): *DO NOT OPEN — CORRUPTION BEYOND — CONSULT GENERAL STAFF*
6. The smell: faint but unmistakable to Mercer — the same metallic sweetness from the outer corridors, concentrated here. Whatever is behind this door, it has been leaking for a very long time.

**This door does not open this adventure.** The party can examine it, document it, argue about it. It will not open. The lead seal means a Knock spell or similar won't work without removing the lead first. The iron bars would take tools and noise and time. And Mercer, if he genuinely analyses the situation, will advise against it: the last thing he wants is to open a door containing corruption while standing below thirty people's worth of structurally compromised floor.

#### Act 2 Transition
The party has mapped most of the Relic Basements. They have the Giant blueprint tablet. They have found the Sealed Crypt and documented it. The ceiling incident has put them on notice. The final act is about completing the map and getting out intact.

### Act 3: The Mapping Crisis

#### The Last Halls
There are three unexplored sections of the Relic Basements that the party can see on their emerging map: a southeastern corridor (labeled on the only Military Empire direction marker they found as *LOWER ARCHIVES*), a room behind a collapsed section they've been routing around, and the far end of the Hall of the Giants beyond the pressure valve.

**The Lower Archives:** A smaller room filled with iron scroll cases, most corroded shut. One opens — it contains a complete architectural survey of the Military Empire layer, annotated with the structural load calculations. This is exactly what Cora needs. With this document, she can identify which walls are load-bearing, which corridors are safe for heavy traffic, and where the dangerous zones are. Completing the map is now possible in a single careful pass.

**The collapsed section bypass:** The room behind the collapse contains nothing of particular value — it was a storage room for lamp oil, most of which evaporated centuries ago. But getting past the collapse reveals that the far wall of this room has been modified — Military Empire stone has been mortared over a much older opening, similar to the false wall Rolf found in the preparation kitchen. Behind it, Mercer detects a faint presence of the same deep aura that permeates the Psychic Guardian's domain. Something was sealed here too, something smaller and older, but the seal is intact and stable. Not a threat. Not for today.

**The far end of the Hall of the Giants:** Past the pressure valve, the hall continues another thirty feet before ending at a wall — but the wall has two features. First: a corridor branches left, leading down. The steps are Giant-scale, three feet high per step, the ceiling rising to accommodate a being twice human height. This is the first visible entrance to the Dwarven Layer. It goes down into the dark and the sound from below is faint but present — moving air, and something rhythmic that Mercer will spend the entire journey home puzzling over. Second: mounted on the wall above the Giant stairway entrance is a carved Giant-era warning, identical in script to the blueprint tablet Mercer is carrying. He can read it with the tablet as reference: *THE LOWER WORKS ARE SEALED BY DECREE OF THE ARCHITECT'S COUNCIL. ACCESS ONLY WITH STRUCTURAL AUTHORITY. THE SLEEPER MUST NOT BE DISTURBED.*

The party has found the Dwarven Layer entrance. They are not going down today.

#### The Breach
While mapping the Lower Archives, Garrick hears it: scratching. Not structural settling. Something alive, moving through the crack in the floor from the First Warning. Then another sound — stone being pried apart from below.

Three Twisted Scouts have climbed up through the structural cracks from the Dwarven Layer. They are corrupted dwarves — gray-skinned, black-veined, moving in jerky mechanical patterns. They do not speak. They do not negotiate. They are scouting the Military Empire layer, testing whether the upper levels are accessible.

**HOSTILE-ONLY ENCOUNTER. The Twisted Scouts attack on sight.**

**Twisted Scouts (3):** AC 14 (corrupted leather + chitin). HP 32 each. Speed 25 ft.
- Pry Bar: +5 to hit, 1d8+3 bludgeoning
- Corruption Aura: DC 13 Con save, 1d4 psychic damage to creatures within 5 ft at start of their turn
- Pack Tactics: Advantage on attack rolls when ally within 5 ft of target
- Vulnerability: Radiant damage

**Combat complication — the structural crisis:** The fighting accelerates the ceiling collapse. Each round of combat, stone dust falls in curtains. After round 3, the ceiling crack from earlier propagates. Mercer's analysis (DC 15 Arcana or Engineering via Cora's architectural survey): the stress from combat has exceeded the threshold. A cascade is beginning — not a catastrophic collapse yet, but a settling process that will become one within minutes if they cannot arrest it.

**The party must fight AND solve the structural crisis simultaneously.**

**The solution is the pressure valve.**

Mercer makes the connection: the Giant mechanism in the Hall of the Giants was a pressure valve for the Anomaly's containment architecture. But it was also, according to the blueprint tablet's annotations, connected to a broader structural stabilization network throughout the mountain's lower levels. If it can be reactivated — even partially — it will redistribute the stress load across the Giant-era foundation rather than letting it concentrate on the compromised dwarven-to-Military Empire interface.

**The problem:** The valve's activation mechanism requires simultaneously triggering three control nodes: one on the valve itself, one in the Hall of the Conquered Peoples (near where they started), and one that Mercer has to locate using the blueprint — it is behind the false-wall seal in the collapsed storage room. The Twisted Scouts are between the party and at least one of the nodes.

**The run:** The party splits or coordinates as they see fit. The ceiling is cracking audibly. Stone dust is falling in curtains. The remaining Twisted are still fighting. From above, a distant crash — something has shifted in the Iron Warlords' layer. Not a catastrophic failure, but a warning.

Mercer handles the valve. Garrick handles the Hall of the Conquered Peoples node (it requires a successful DC 15 Strength check to turn a rusted control mechanism) — and any Twisted between him and it. Cora has to breach the false wall in the storage room quickly and cleanly without creating a shockwave that accelerates the cascade (DC 14 Dexterity with tinker's tools, or DC 12 if she uses a targeted strike with a light tool rather than the crowbar).

When all three nodes are triggered, the pressure valve activates with a deep, resonant CLANG that is felt in the teeth rather than heard. The grinding stops. The dust settles. The ceiling holds. Any surviving Twisted flee back through the crack in the floor.

From below the valve mechanism — from somewhere deep in the Giant architecture — a response hum answers the activation. Mercer writes this down immediately. The containment network still has functioning components. Whatever the Giants built, part of it still works.

#### The Return
The party ascends the Grand Stairwell with:
- A complete map of the Military Empire layer
- The Giant blueprint tablet showing the mountain's full structure
- The architectural survey with load calculations
- The Commander's letter about the Sealed Crypt
- Knowledge of the Giant stairway entrance to the Dwarven Layer
- Knowledge of the pressure valve and its connection to the Giant network

Reyna Steelforge's map request is fulfilled — twice over, with the blueprint tablet. When Garrick brings it to Grimhold on the next supply run and Reyna sees what it contains, her expression is the most unguarded he has ever seen on her face. "This changes everything," she says quietly. "Every historian on Laternia would give their eyes for this."

The party is level 6 now. The Dwarven Layer entrance is marked and secured with a fresh door. The Sealed Crypt is documented and left sealed.

Below the Giant stairway, in the dark, that rhythmic sound continues. Whatever is moving down there has not noticed the valve activation. Or it has, and it is not concerned.

## Key Items
- **Giant Blueprint Tablet** — complete structural diagram of the Asymmetrical Mountain showing all five strata and the containment architecture around the Anomaly. Gives Mercer advantage on Arcana checks related to the mountain's structure for the rest of the campaign. Reyna Steelforge will want a copy — Mercer can transcribe it.
- **Military Empire Architectural Survey** — load calculations and structural maps of the human layers. Cora can use this to safely plan excavation projects, avoiding load-bearing walls (eliminates structural collapse risk from future workforce excavation for two adventure cycles).
- **Commander's Letter re: the Sealed Crypt** — primary source evidence that the Military Empire knew the Crypt's contents were corrupted and chose not to act. Seeds Adventure 6.
- **Preserved Stimulant Tincture (2 doses)** — Potion of Hill Giant Strength with tremor side effect risk.
- **Extinct Grain Seeds** — no mechanical benefit, but Cora can cultivate them. Within one adventure cycle, the fortress has a supplementary grain crop that reduces supply chain dependence on Grimhold.
- **200 gp** in salvageable Military Empire military artifacts (decorative ironwork, commemorative plaques, intact ceramic vessels).

## Key NPCs
- **Reyna Steelforge** — is brought back into the story via her map request. Her reaction to the blueprint tablet is the closing beat of the adventure. She is not just a quest-giver anymore — she is beginning to understand what the party has found.
- **Warden Aldric** — should be consulted before the descent. He knows nothing about the Military Empire layer (below his clearance level) but he knows the Brotherhood feared what was below them. "They never went deeper than the Archives. They said the lower levels were someone else's problem."

## DM Notes
- The structural hazard is the dungeon's identity. Every room description should include one detail about the ceiling, the floor, or the walls that reminds the party they are inside a building that is actively failing.
- The pressure valve crisis should feel earned — not arbitrary. The party made choices (lingering in dangerous rooms, moving heavy objects, triggering the stone-fall incident) that led to this threshold being crossed. Reward careful players by letting them avoid the worst of it if they paid attention to Cora's warnings.
- The Sealed Crypt is important. Make it feel forbidden and correct. The lead, the bars, the wax seal, the chalked warning — this is a door that good people sealed and were right to seal. The party should leave it alone and feel like they made the right call.
- The rhythmic sound from the Dwarven Layer is the Twisted. They are present and active but not in range this adventure. Do not explain the sound yet. Let it be a mystery.
- This adventure ends Act I's "survival mode" permanently. The party has food, shelter, a stable base, a complete map of the human layers, and the Stairwell open. The campaign shifts gear from "keep thirty people alive" to "understand what this mountain is."
