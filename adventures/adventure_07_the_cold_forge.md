# Adventure 7: The Cold Forge (Level 7)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. Pace the full adventure within ~20 exchanges.
- Cover all three acts in this session. Do not compress excessively — each act should receive meaningful screen time — but do not stretch or pad either.
- The early part of the adventure should focus on the deeper descent into the Dwarven Delve, the shift in architectural scale, and the discovery of the Cold Forge chamber.
- The middle part should cover tracking the Fire Elemental through the forge complex, learning its history, and the first negotiation or confrontation attempt.
- The late part should be the climactic bargain or capture — reigniting the forge and dealing with the consequences. The forge lights. The Dwarven Delve changes.
- If the players try to rush ahead, the forge complex is enormous and maze-like. Collapsed tunnels, sealed blast doors, and heat-warped corridors slow progress naturally.
- NEVER let the party descend below the forge level this adventure. The minecart network and deeper infrastructure are gated behind Adventure 8.
- A D&D session should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

## Adventure Overview
The Radiation Survey Notes recovered from the Leaking Crypt identify the Cold Forge as the industrial heart of the upper Dwarven Layer. When the dwarves of Kazad-Khrom occupied this mountain, the forge was their primary production facility — weapons, tools, structural components, everything the settlement needed was smelted and shaped here. The forge was powered by a bound Fire Elemental, a common practice in major dwarven installations. When the corruption from below began to intensify and the dwarves started their long retreat, the Elemental's binding was severed. The forge went cold. It has been cold for over a thousand years.

Reigniting the forge would give the party something no surface expedition can provide: the ability to work metal at industrial scale, deep within the mountain, without depending on Grimhold's limited smithing capacity. Cora has been sketching designs for custom equipment since they found the ceremonial war-pick. Garrick wants proper dwarven-grade armor. Mercer wants to experiment with the Dreamstone Fragment in a controlled heat environment.

The problem is the Elemental. It was bound once. When the binding broke, it did not leave — it could not. The forge complex is its prison as much as its home. It has been alone in the dark for a thousand years, and it is angry.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Enter the Cold Forge complex. Find the Fire Elemental. Convince it to reignite the forge — through negotiation, new binding, or bargain. Light the fires.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** The Fire Elemental is not a monster to be defeated. It is a character — ancient, bitter, and lonely. It was enslaved by the dwarves, abandoned when they fled, and left in a cold stone prison for centuries. The party can fight it, but they cannot kill it permanently in this space (it will reform from the forge's hearth within days). The only lasting solutions are a new bargain or a binding strong enough to hold. Cora's artificer skills and Mercer's arcane knowledge are both relevant.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 8.

### Act 1: The Deep Descent

#### Below the Mausoleum Level
The Radiation Survey Notes provide a rough map of the path from the upper Dwarven Layer to the Cold Forge — a series of descending corridors and switchback ramps built for moving heavy materials. The architecture changes as the party descends: ceilings rise from fifteen feet to twenty, corridors widen, and the geometric wall carvings become more elaborate. This was not residential space. This was industrial.

The air warms as they descend. Not much — the mountain's baseline cold is still present — but the stone itself carries a residual heat that Cora identifies as geothermal. The dwarves built their forge where the mountain's natural heat supplemented their fires. Practical, efficient, dwarven.

**Garrick's observation:** The corridors show signs of heavy use. Grooves in the stone floor from centuries of cart traffic. Scorch marks on walls near ventilation shafts. Iron brackets for enormous lanterns — industrial lighting, not decorative. This was a working facility, and it was busy.

#### The Forge Approaches
Three distinct sections precede the forge proper:

- **The Material Stores:** Enormous chambers with iron-railed shelving carved into the walls, most empty. A few contain raw ore — iron, copper, tin — in quantities that suggest the dwarves stockpiled for years. One sealed bin contains something Mercer identifies with rising excitement: unworked adamantine ore, roughly forty pounds. It is worthless without a forge hot enough to work it. With the Cold Forge lit, it becomes the rarest crafting material on the continent.

- **The Apprentice Workshops:** Smaller forge stations, cold and dark, with individual bellows and anvils sized for dwarven apprentices. The tools are still here — hammers, tongs, measuring gauges, quenching troughs. Everything is covered in a thick layer of mineral dust but fundamentally sound. Dwarven steel does not rust easily.

- **The Cooling Channels:** A network of stone channels that once carried water from a reservoir above to cool the forge's output. The channels are dry. Carved into the wall near the main channel junction, in Dwarvish: *COOLING FLOW CONTROLLED FROM PUMP STATION EAST. DO NOT FORGE WITHOUT COOLANT.* This is the first mention of the pump infrastructure that will become central in Adventure 9.

#### The Cold Forge Chamber
The corridor opens into the largest enclosed space the party has seen inside the mountain. The Cold Forge is a cathedral of industry: a domed chamber roughly eighty feet across and forty feet high, with a central hearth twenty feet in diameter ringed by six massive anvils, each weighing several tons. The hearth is a pit of black iron and stone, cold and dark, filled with ancient ash and slag. Above it, a chimney shaft rises into the mountain's core — the ventilation system that once carried smoke and heat upward through channels the party may have felt as warmth in the corridors above.

The forge is dead. The air should shimmer with heat. The anvils should ring. Instead there is silence, darkness, and the faint smell of old iron.

**Mercer's examination of the hearth (DC 14 Arcana):** The hearth was not fueled by coal or wood. The binding circle is still visible in the iron — a complex series of runes carved into the base of the pit, designed to contain and direct a Fire Elemental's energy. The runes are intact but inactive. The binding was cleanly severed — not broken by force, but deliberately released. Someone freed the Elemental on purpose.

**Evidence of the Elemental's presence:** Scorch marks on the far wall, recent compared to the ancient ash — within the last few decades. A section of the apprentice workshops nearest the forge chamber has been melted, the stone fused into glass. And in the ash of the hearth, footprints. Not dwarven. Larger, shapeless, burned into the iron itself. The Elemental is here. It moves. It is awake.

### Act 2: The Prisoner

#### Tracking the Elemental
The Elemental does not stay in the forge chamber. The scorch marks and heat signatures lead deeper into the complex — through maintenance corridors behind the main chamber, into a series of natural caverns that the dwarves incorporated into their ventilation system. The caverns are warm, almost hot, and the air carries the smell of superheated stone.

**DC 13 Survival or Investigation:** The Elemental follows a pattern. It moves through the same corridors on a roughly six-hour cycle, returning to the forge hearth periodically as if drawn back by the binding circle even though the binding is broken. It is pacing. A prisoner walking the walls of its cell.

#### The Elemental's Lair
The party finds the Elemental in a natural chimney — a vertical shaft roughly fifteen feet across that connects to the geothermal vents below. The Elemental rests here, where the heat from the mountain's core is strongest. It is not the roaring inferno of traditional Fire Elemental depictions. It is diminished — a flickering form roughly eight feet tall, its flames guttering and uneven, like a candle in a draft. A thousand years without fuel, without purpose, without contact has reduced it.

When the party enters the chimney chamber, the Elemental reacts. It flares — a burst of heat that forces the party back five feet — and speaks. Its voice is the cracking of heated stone.

**The Elemental's grievance (in Primordial, which Mercer can understand with a DC 15 Arcana check or a Comprehend Languages spell):** It was bound by dwarven runesmiths three thousand years ago. It served faithfully — it heated their forge, powered their industry, shaped their greatest works. When the corruption began to seep upward and the dwarves started dying, the last runesmith freed it as an act of mercy. But the Elemental could not leave. The chimney shaft connects to geothermal vents, not to the surface. It has been trapped in a cold forge with no one to serve and nowhere to go.

It does not want to be bound again. It does not trust the small ones. But it is so tired of the cold.

#### The Negotiation Begins
The party has three approaches:

**Path A — Negotiate a Bargain:** Convince the Elemental to reignite the forge voluntarily in exchange for something it wants. What does a thousand-year-old Fire Elemental want? Freedom — but true freedom requires a path to the surface or to the Elemental Plane of Fire. Mercer's knowledge of planar theory (and the Giant Blueprint Tablet's annotations on the containment architecture) may offer a lead: the Giant containment network includes sealed conduits that once connected to planar anchors. One may connect to the Plane of Fire.

**Path B — Forge a New Binding:** Cora can study the original binding circle and attempt to reconstruct it. This is a complex artificer challenge (multiple checks) and the Elemental will resist — not with violence initially, but with evasion and sabotage. It will flee through the maintenance corridors if it suspects a trap.

**Path C — Subdue and Contain:** Fight the Elemental into submission. This is possible but difficult — in its diminished state, it is weaker than a standard Fire Elemental, but the confined spaces and geothermal vents give it significant environmental advantages. And even if subdued, a forced binding will degrade over time without the Elemental's cooperation.

**DC 16 Persuasion (any character) or DC 14 if referencing the Elemental's long service:** The Elemental will listen to a bargain proposal. It needs proof the party can deliver on any promise. "The small ones always promise. The small ones always leave."

### Act 3: The Reignition

#### Resolution by Path

**If Path A (Bargain):**
The party must offer the Elemental a credible promise of eventual freedom. Mercer's examination of the Giant Blueprint Tablet (DC 16 Arcana, advantage if he has studied the tablet extensively) reveals a notation near the Giant foundation level: a planar conduit sealed by the Architect's Council, labeled in Giant script as *FIRE GATE — SEALED, MAINTENANCE ACCESS RETAINED.* The conduit exists. It is deep — far below the party's current reach — but it is real.

The Elemental accepts a conditional bargain: it will reignite the forge and maintain it in exchange for the party's oath to unseal the Fire Gate when they reach the Giant level. This is not charity — it is a transaction. The Elemental provides industrial power now; the party provides freedom later. If the party breaks the oath, the forge goes cold permanently.

**Binding the Bargain (DC 15 Arcana from Mercer, DC 14 if Cora assists with the binding circle modification):** Mercer modifies the original dwarven binding circle into a voluntary compact — the Elemental enters the hearth willingly, and the runes hold it gently rather than imprisoning it. The Elemental can leave the hearth to rest in the chimney, but it maintains the forge fire. This requires trust on both sides.

**If Path B (New Binding):**
Cora must succeed on three sequential checks:
1. Reconstruct the damaged rune sequence (DC 15 Intelligence with tinker's tools)
2. Activate the binding circle while the Elemental is in the hearth (DC 16 Dexterity — timing the activation to the Elemental's natural return cycle)
3. Lock the binding before the Elemental can escape (DC 14 Intelligence, contested by the Elemental's Charisma save +4)

If all three succeed, the binding holds. The Elemental is furious but contained. The forge reignites under duress. This works mechanically but creates a resentful prisoner. The Elemental will test the binding constantly, and the party will need to maintain it (Cora's ongoing task, one check per adventure cycle).

**If Path C (Subdual):**
**Diminished Fire Elemental:** AC 13, HP 68, Speed 50 ft (reduced from standard). Slam: +5 to hit, 2d6+3 fire damage. Fire Form (standard). Resistance to nonmagical weapons. Vulnerable to cold damage.

**Environmental hazard:** The chimney vent releases bursts of geothermal heat every 2 rounds (DC 14 Dexterity save, 2d6 fire damage on failure, half on success). The Elemental is immune and uses these bursts as cover.

If reduced to 0 HP, the Elemental dissipates into the geothermal vents and reforms in the hearth within 1d4 hours, weakened further. A second defeat in the same day reduces it to a flickering ember that Cora can contain in the binding circle (DC 12 Intelligence with tinker's tools). The binding holds but the forge fire is weak — adequate for basic smithing but not masterwork or adamantine.

#### The Forge Lights
Regardless of path, the result is the same: the Cold Forge reignites. The hearth fills with controlled flame. Heat radiates outward in waves. The ancient chimney system carries the heat upward through the mountain — the party will feel it in the upper corridors on the way home. The apprentice workshops warm. The anvils, after a thousand years, ring when Garrick tests one with the ceremonial war-pick.

The forge is operational. Not at full capacity — the cooling channels are dry (the pumps are offline, a problem for Adventure 9) and the adamantine requires temperatures the diminished Elemental cannot yet reach — but functional for steel, iron, and basic enchantment work. Cora can begin crafting custom equipment during downtime between adventures.

**The closing beat:** As the forge fire stabilizes, the Elemental settles into the hearth with a sound that might be a sigh. The flames find a rhythm — steady, breathing, alive. Mercer records the moment in his journal: for the first time since entering the mountain, they have built something rather than explored it. The Dwarven Delve is no longer a ruin. It is becoming a workshop.

From deeper in the mountain, below the forge level, a new sound reaches them: the distant clatter of metal on stone. Rhythmic. Industrial. Something else is working down there, and it is not the party.

## Key Items
- **Unworked Adamantine Ore (40 lbs)** — enough for one suit of adamantine armor or two adamantine weapons. Cannot be worked until the forge reaches full capacity (requires the cooling pumps from Adventure 9 and the Elemental at full strength). Store in the Material Stores until ready.
- **Dwarven Forge Tools (master set)** — hammers, tongs, and measuring instruments of exceptional quality. Cora gains advantage on crafting checks when using these tools at the Cold Forge.
- **Runesmith's Journal** — found in a sealed compartment near the binding circle. Written by the last dwarven runesmith, it describes the binding process, the Elemental's temperament, and — critically — the early signs of corruption reaching the forge level. The final entry reads: *"I release Ignathar from service. It has earned its rest. I only wish I could release us all."* The Elemental's name is Ignathar.
- **500 gp** in dwarven forge supplies, raw metal stock, and salvageable tool components.

## Key NPCs
- **Ignathar (Fire Elemental)** — ancient, diminished, bitter, but not evil. It served the dwarves for two thousand years before being freed. It wants to leave the mountain but cannot. A bargain earns its grudging respect. A binding earns its resentment. Subdual earns its contempt. How the party treats Ignathar defines their relationship with the forge for the rest of the campaign.
- **Reyna Steelforge** — when she learns the forge is lit, her reaction is immediate and practical: "I can send you a smith. Not a great one — Grimhold's best work with surface forges. But someone who knows metalwork." This is the seed for Intermission II's workforce upgrade: Grimhold Specialists.

## DM Notes
- Ignathar is the campaign's first morally complex ally. The party cannot simply slay it and move on — they need the forge, and the forge needs the Elemental. How they handle this encounter should have lasting consequences.
- If the party bargains, the Fire Gate promise becomes a long-term obligation that pays off in Act III when the party reaches the Giant level. Note it in campaign state.
- The distant industrial sounds at the adventure's end are the Twisted. They are working the deep minecart network, and the forge's reignition has attracted their attention. This seeds Adventure 8.
- The cooling channel reference and pump station mention are deliberate seeds for Adventure 9. Do not explain them further yet.
- The forge enables a crafting system for future downtime: Cora can produce one uncommon magic item per adventure cycle using the forge and her artificer abilities. This should feel like a reward for securing the forge, not a shopping catalogue.
