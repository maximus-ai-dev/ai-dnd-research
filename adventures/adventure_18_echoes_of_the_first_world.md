# Adventure 18: Echoes of the First World (Level 18)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. The DM should pace the full adventure across ~20 exchanges.
- Cover entering the unshielded broadcast zone and the first echo encounters in the early part, the three-stage psychological gauntlets in the middle, and the echo resolution and approach to the sphere in the late part.
- If the players try to rush through the psychological challenges, the broadcast intensifies — suppressing it requires engagement, not avoidance.
- NEVER let the party touch or interact with the sphere this adventure. Proximity is the goal, not contact.
- A D&D session should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
The Observation Platform is secured. The Caldera is mapped. The party understands the physics of the void and the nature of the Deep Things. Now they must move closer to the sphere — close enough to deploy the Dreamstone Seal when it is built. And the sphere does not want them close.

Not intentionally. The Sleeper's broadcast is not a weapon — it is an emanation, the way a star emanates heat. But fifty feet from the Caldera wall, the Dreamstone Circlets fail. The broadcast penetrates the psychic shielding and interacts directly with the party's minds. The result is not madness. It is worse: clarity.

The broadcast, at close range, resonates with the deepest structures of conscious thought — memories, fears, motivations, the fundamental architecture of identity. It reflects the mind back at itself, amplified and distorted. Each party member will face an echo of themselves — a version conjured by the broadcast that embodies their greatest fear and their deepest desire. Surviving the echo is not a combat challenge. It is a test of self-knowledge.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Navigate the unshielded broadcast zone. Face and overcome the personalized psychological echoes. Establish a position within fifty feet of the sphere — close enough for the Seal's deployment.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This is the most character-driven adventure in the campaign. Combat is minimal. The challenge is entirely psychological. Each party member faces a version of themselves that knows everything they know, wants everything they want, and asks the question they are most afraid to answer. The DM must engage with each character's backstory and motivations to create echoes that feel personal and earned.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 19.

### Act 1: The Unshielded Mind

#### Beyond the Circlets
The party launches from the Observation Platform toward the sphere, roped together, with the Reality Anchor providing navigational stability. At fifty feet from the wall, the Dreamstone Circlets begin to vibrate — a physical trembling that Cora recognizes as resonance overload. The circlets are not failing; they are being overwhelmed. The broadcast at this distance is simply too strong for the shielding to contain.

**DC 18 Wisdom save (all characters) upon circlet failure:** This is not a combat save — it is a transition. On success, the character adjusts to the unshielded broadcast gradually. They feel the pressure but maintain coherence. On failure, the transition is jarring — the character experiences a 1d4 round disorientation as the broadcast floods their unprotected mind. During this time, they perceive the Caldera through the broadcast's lens: the void is not dark — it is luminous, suffused with the light of the First World, and the sphere at its center is not a threatening mass but a sleeping mind, vast and serene and unknowably old.

#### The Broadcast Zone
Beyond the circlet's effective range, the Caldera is transformed. The party's perception shifts — not hallucination, but augmented reality. They see the Caldera as it is and as the Sleeper dreams it simultaneously:

- The containment runes on the distant walls appear as bars of golden light — a cage, seen from inside
- The sphere pulses with dream-logic: it is simultaneously close and infinitely far, small and impossibly large
- The Deep Things, drifting in their orbits, resolve into something almost recognizable — shapes that suggest faces, hands, memories, from a time before memory existed
- Sound becomes visible. The broadcast manifests as ripples in the air — concentric waves spreading from the sphere in all directions, passing through the party like tidal surges

**Environmental effect — Broadcast Immersion:**
- No saving throws in the Broadcast Zone — the effects are constant and unavoidable
- All characters perceive the augmented reality layer permanently while in the zone
- Concentration checks for spells increase by +3 (the broadcast is distracting)
- Psychic damage from any source is doubled (the mind is exposed)
- The party can communicate normally with each other, but their voices sound strange — resonant, layered, as if echoed by something vast

#### The First Echoes
The broadcast interacts differently with each mind. As the party moves deeper into the zone, each character begins to perceive something the others cannot — a figure, familiar, hovering at the edge of their vision. Not threatening. Not hostile. Waiting.

**Cora's Echo:** A version of herself, older, wearing a master artificer's coat she has never owned. The echo is building something — hands moving with precision Cora recognizes as her own, constructing a device of extraordinary complexity. The echo looks up and speaks: "You always knew you could build anything. The question is whether you should."

**Garrick's Echo:** A version of himself standing guard at a door. Not the mountain's door — the door of a house. A home. The echo is at peace — something Garrick has never been. It looks at him with an expression he cannot name and says: "You could stop. You have defended enough. When do you put down the maul?"

**Mercer's Echo:** A version of himself sitting in a library with infinite shelves. Every book is open. Every page reveals something true and terrible and beautiful. The echo is reading with an expression of perfect satisfaction — and perfect horror. It looks up and says: "You wanted to know everything. What will you do when you do?"

Each character has encountered their echo for the first time. The echoes have not attacked, have not demanded anything, have simply appeared and spoken. The party is in the Broadcast Zone, roughly halfway between the wall and the sphere, anchored to floating debris with the Reality Anchor. The echoes drift nearby — always visible in peripheral vision, always watching, always waiting for the characters to engage.

### Act 2: The Questions

#### Engaging the Echoes
The echoes cannot be ignored. As long as the party remains in the Broadcast Zone, they persist — growing more detailed, more vivid, more insistent. They do not attack. They ask questions. And the questions get harder.

**Each character must face a three-stage challenge. Each stage is a DC 16 Wisdom or Charisma save (player's choice — Wisdom represents resilience, Charisma represents force of personality). Failure does not mean death or madness — it means the echo gains ground, and the next stage's DC increases by 2.**

#### Cora's Gauntlet: The Builder's Dilemma

**Stage 1 — The Gift:**
The echo shows Cora a vision: the Dreamstone Seal, completed, perfect. But the Seal is not what she expects — it is not a containment device. It is a key. The echo asks: "If you could open the containment rather than reinforce it, and the thing inside would simply leave — walk away, dissolve into the cosmos — would you? Even knowing you could not predict what would happen?"

The question tests Cora's relationship with control. She builds things to solve problems. What if the solution is to unbuild?

**Stage 2 — The Cost:**
The echo shows her the Cold Forge, burning. Ignathar, in agony. The forge is consuming itself to produce something extraordinary — the greatest work Cora will ever create. The echo asks: "Everything you build costs something. What are you willing to pay for perfection?"

The question tests Cora's pragmatism against her empathy. She uses tools, uses resources, uses the forge. At what point does using become exploiting?

**Stage 3 — The Legacy:**
The echo shows the fortress, centuries later, still standing. Cora's work — the defenses, the forge modifications, the Seal itself — all functioning perfectly. No one remembers her name. The echo asks: "If your work outlasts you but your name does not, was the work enough?"

The question tests Cora's motivation. Does she build for recognition or for permanence?

#### Garrick's Gauntlet: The Guardian's Choice

**Stage 1 — The Door:**
The echo shows Garrick a door — the door of the home he has never had. Behind it: warmth, safety, the sound of someone waiting. The echo asks: "You have defended others your entire life. Who defends you?"

The question tests Garrick's self-worth. He defines himself as a protector. What happens when the one who needs protection is himself?

**Stage 2 — The Enemy:**
The echo shows Kregg. Not the bandit leader from Adventure 1 — the idea of Kregg. The cycle of violence: Garrick was exiled from Emberfell for questioning authority, Kregg was exiled for embracing it. They are mirrors. The echo asks: "You are not Kregg because you chose not to be. But the choice was close. How close?"

The question tests Garrick's honesty about his own capacity for violence and cruelty. He is not a good man by nature — he is a good man by choice. The echo makes him own that.

**Stage 3 — The Watch:**
The echo shows Garrick at the end — old, tired, still standing guard. Still holding the door. The echo asks: "When do you get to rest? Or is the watch the rest — because without it, you do not know who you are?"

The question tests Garrick's identity beyond his function. Is he a person who guards, or is he only a guard?

#### Mercer's Gauntlet: The Scholar's Price

**Stage 1 — The Book:**
The echo shows Mercer the Architect's Codex — but complete, every page filled, every question answered. The Sleeper's nature, the First World's history, the structure of reality itself. All of it, known. The echo asks: "You have spent your life seeking knowledge others said was forbidden. Now you have it. Was the seeking better than the having?"

The question tests Mercer's relationship with curiosity. Is knowledge the goal, or is the pursuit of knowledge the goal? Because they are very different things.

**Stage 2 — The Warning:**
The echo shows Mercer's disgrace — the academic scandal that drove him from his university. But from the echo's perspective, Mercer was right. His theories about the First World Sleeper were correct. The echo asks: "You were right. They were wrong. Does being right justify what you lost — and what you would lose again?"

The question tests Mercer's pride. Being right is his deepest need. But rightness has cost him everything before. Will it again?

**Stage 3 — The Page That Reads Back:**
The echo opens the infinite book to its last page. The page is blank — and then it is not. Words appear, written in Mercer's own hand, but he did not write them. The page asks him a question. The question is different for every version of this adventure — it should be the question Mercer most needs to answer about his own nature, based on how the campaign has played out.

The echo smiles. "The book is not finished. It will never be finished. That is the answer."

Each character has faced their echo's three stages. Some stages may have been passed, others failed. The echoes have not attacked, but the characters are emotionally raw — the broadcast's resonance has forced them to confront truths they normally keep buried. The party regroups at the Reality Anchor, shaken but intact. The sphere is closer. The echoes wait for resolution.

---

### Act 3: The Breakthrough

#### Resolving the Echoes
The echoes must be resolved — not defeated, not destroyed, but acknowledged and integrated. Each character must face their echo one final time and give an answer. Not the right answer. An honest answer.

**Resolution mechanics:** Each character makes a final Wisdom or Charisma check (DC 18, or DC 20 if they failed 2+ stages in Act 2, or DC 16 if they passed all stages). This is not a save — it is a test of character. The roll represents not strength of will but clarity of self-knowledge.

**On success:** The echo nods, dissolves, and is absorbed — not into the character's mind but into the broadcast itself. The character's understanding of themselves strengthens, and the broadcast loses its grip on their psyche. The character gains **Broadcast Immunity**: they can operate in the unshielded zone without psychic interference. Concentration checks return to normal. Psychic damage is no longer doubled. They can perceive the augmented reality layer but it no longer disorients them.

**On failure:** The echo does not dissolve. It remains — a persistent presence in the character's peripheral vision for the remainder of the campaign. It does not interfere mechanically, but it watches, and occasionally it speaks. The character does not gain Broadcast Immunity but gains **Broadcast Resistance**: disadvantage on saves is removed, psychic damage bonus is reduced from double to +50%, and they can function in the zone with effort.

**Either outcome is narratively valid.** Success means the character has made peace with their deepest conflict. Failure means the conflict remains — unresolved, present, human. Both outcomes are honest.

#### Approaching the Sphere
With the echoes resolved (or at least quieted), the party can push deeper into the Broadcast Zone. The sphere grows larger, filling their vision. Its surface ripples with slow, organic motion — not liquid, not solid, but a state of matter that the party's physics cannot describe.

The final approach is ninety feet from the Observation Platform — putting the party within fifty feet of the sphere's surface. At this distance, the containment's anchor points are visible on the Caldera wall behind them — four golden lights, equidistant, the structural framework of the containment vessel.

**The position:** A cluster of Giant-era debris — construction platforms and scaffolding left from the containment's original construction — provides a stable staging area. The party can anchor themselves here with ropes and the Reality Anchor. The sphere hovers in front of them, its surface fifty feet away, its broadcast washing over them in waves that they can now — through resolved echoes or raw endurance — withstand.

This is where the Seal must be deployed.

#### The Observation
From fifty feet, the sphere is not what the party expected. It is not a surface over something solid. It is a boundary — the edge where the Sleeper's reality meets the physical universe. Looking at it closely, the party can see through the surface in moments of transparency:

The interior is the First World. Not the dead remnant — the living original. A reality of impossible beauty where stone sings, light has weight, and consciousness is the fundamental force rather than gravity or electromagnetism. The Sleeper exists in that reality — not trapped, not imprisoned, but simply present. It has been here since before the universe was reshaped. The containment did not capture it. The containment built a wall between its world and ours.

**Mercer's final Codex entry for this adventure:** "The Sleeper is not our enemy. It is not our responsibility. It is our neighbor — a neighbor whose house is built on different laws of physics, and whose presence makes our house unstable. The Seal does not punish it. The Seal is a better wall."

**The closing beat:** The party secures the staging position. They are fifty feet from the boundary between worlds, anchored to fifty-thousand-year-old scaffolding, breathing air that tastes of dreams. Behind them, the golden lights of the containment anchor points burn steadily. Above them (a direction they choose arbitrarily), the capstone and the mountain and the world they know.

They are ready to build the Seal.

## Key Items
- **Echo Insights** — each character's resolved (or unresolved) echo provides a permanent character trait that influences their effectiveness in the final two adventures:
  - Cora (resolved): **Builder's Certainty** — advantage on all crafting checks for the Dreamstone Seal
  - Cora (unresolved): **Builder's Doubt** — no advantage, but the echo occasionally suggests improvements that are genuinely helpful (the doubt produces creativity)
  - Garrick (resolved): **Guardian's Peace** — advantage on all saves against fear, charm, and compulsion effects in the Caldera
  - Garrick (unresolved): **Guardian's Vigil** — no advantage, but the echo stands watch during rests, providing immunity to surprise
  - Mercer (resolved): **Scholar's Clarity** — advantage on all Arcana checks related to the Anomaly and the Seal
  - Mercer (unresolved): **Scholar's Hunger** — no advantage, but the echo provides fragments of information during key moments (the hunger for knowledge produces insight)

**Note:** Both resolved and unresolved outcomes should feel equally valid and useful. Neither is "better" — they are different expressions of the same character truth.

- **Broadcast Zone Survey** — detailed mapping of the broadcast intensity at various distances from the sphere, the Deep Thing orbits, and the environmental phenomena. Critical for planning the Seal deployment in Adventure 19.
- **First World Observations** — Mercer's documented observations of the sphere's interior. These provide the theoretical understanding needed to calibrate the Seal: the Seal must match the frequency of the boundary itself, not the Sleeper's emissions.

## Key NPCs
- **The Echoes** — whether resolved or persistent, they are the party's most intimate NPCs. They know everything the characters know, feel everything they feel, and speak truths no external character could voice. Their role in the narrative is to humanize the cosmic horror — to remind the player-agents that the campaign is about people, not just about a sleeping god.
- **The Deep Things** — curious about the echo interactions. During the gauntlet, they drift closer, observing. They do not interfere. They seem to recognize the echoes as something related to their own nature — fragments of consciousness given form by the broadcast.

## DM Notes
- This is the campaign's most difficult adventure to run. The psychological challenges require the DM to engage with each character's backstory, motivations, and fears at a level that goes beyond tactical play. The echoes must feel personal. The questions must feel real.
- There are no wrong answers to the echoes' questions. The point is not to give the "correct" response — it is to give an honest one. The resolution check is a measure of self-knowledge, not moral correctness. A character who honestly says "I do not know" is being more truthful than one who gives a rehearsed answer.
- The First World glimpse through the sphere is the campaign's most important piece of worldbuilding. It reframes the Sleeper from "cosmic horror monster" to "entity from a previous version of reality." The horror is not the Sleeper — it is the incompatibility between realities. This nuance should make the party's mission feel compassionate rather than violent.
- Do NOT make this adventure about combat. There should be no significant combat encounters. The challenge is entirely internal. If the players feel bored by the psychological focus, the echoes are not personal enough — adjust them to hit harder.
- The echo outcomes (resolved vs. unresolved) should both be equally mechanically useful. The point is not to reward "winning" the gauntlet — it is to reward engagement. A character who fails every check but engages honestly should feel no less capable than one who passes them all.
