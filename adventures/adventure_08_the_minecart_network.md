# Adventure 8: The Minecart Network (Level 8)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. Pace the full adventure within ~20 exchanges.
- Cover all three acts in this session. Do not compress excessively — each act should receive meaningful screen time — but do not stretch or pad either.
- The early part of the adventure should focus on discovering the minecart network, understanding its scale, and the first signs of the behir's territory.
- The middle part should cover tracking the behir through its territory, learning its patterns, and the first confrontation — which should go badly enough to force a tactical retreat.
- The late part should be the prepared assault — using the minecart infrastructure itself as part of the strategy to defeat the behir and reactivate the eastern transit hub.
- If the players try to rush the behir, it retreats into tunnels too narrow for pursuit and ambushes from an unexpected direction. The behir is a territorial predator, not a stand-and-fight creature.
- NEVER let the party access the flooded lower sections this adventure. The water is gated behind the pump system (Adventure 9).
- A D&D session should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

## Adventure Overview
The forge is lit. The Dwarven Delve is warming. And from deeper in the mountain, the industrial sounds continue — the rhythmic clatter of something working in the dark. The party follows the sound east from the forge complex and discovers what the dwarves built to move materials through their underground empire: a minecart network.

The network is vast — iron rails laid into carved stone channels, spanning hundreds of feet through tunnels that branch and merge like arteries. Switch junctions, loading platforms, brake mechanisms — all designed for efficiency, all cold and still. The eastern branch, which connects the forge to the dwarves' primary mining operations and the deeper transit routes, is blocked. Something large and predatory has made the eastern transit hub its lair.

A behir — a twelve-legged serpentine predator with lightning breath — has occupied the hub for decades, drawn by the warmth from geothermal vents and the abundance of cave fauna in the connecting tunnels. It is an apex predator in an ecosystem of cave creatures, and it has no intention of leaving.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Clear the behir from the eastern minecart transit hub. Reactivate the eastern branch of the minecart network.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** The behir is a dangerous CR 11 creature, and the party is level 8. This fight should not be a straight-up brawl in an open room. The party wins by using the environment — the minecarts, the rails, the switch junctions, the narrow tunnels that limit the behir's mobility. Garrick is the damage dealer, but Cora's engineering mind and Mercer's tactical spellcasting should be what turns the tide.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 9.

### Act 1: Iron Veins

#### The Network Entrance
East of the forge complex, past the apprentice workshops and the cooling channel junction, a wide tunnel opens into the first minecart station. The station is a loading platform — a raised stone dock with iron crane brackets and a channel below containing iron rails. Two minecarts sit on the rails, rusted but structurally intact. Each is large enough to carry half a ton of ore.

**Cora's engineering assessment:** The rails are in remarkable condition — dwarven iron, treated against corrosion. The switch mechanisms at the junction ahead are seized but not broken. With a few hours of work, she could free them. The network could potentially be reactivated.

**Mercer's analysis:** The network branches at the first junction. West goes back toward the forge (this is the supply line they just followed). East goes into a longer tunnel that descends slightly — toward the primary mining operations. North branches toward what the blueprint tablet labels as "PUMP STATION EAST." The eastern tunnel is the one producing the sound they have been hearing.

#### Exploring the Network
The party follows the eastern rail line. The tunnel is twelve feet wide and ten feet high — large enough for carts but not for giants. The rails run in paired tracks, allowing two-way traffic. Every hundred feet, a passing bay is carved into the tunnel wall — a widened section where a cart could pull aside to let another pass.

**Key discoveries along the eastern line:**

- **The Maintenance Bay:** A side chamber containing dwarven tools for rail repair — iron spikes, rail jacks, replacement sleepers. Also contains a hand-crank cart, smaller than the main carts, designed for maintenance crews. It rolls freely on the rails and seats two dwarves (or one human uncomfortably). This is a vehicle the party can use.

- **The Junction Hall:** A major intersection where three rail lines converge. The switching mechanism is a massive iron turntable set into the floor. On the turntable, a cart sits at an angle — derailed and abandoned. The cart contains a load of copper ore and, wedged between the ore chunks, a dwarven maintenance log. The last entry: *"Eastern hub sealed by order of Foreman Kellak. Apex predator confirmed. No transit until beast is cleared. Request hunter team from Deepwatch garrison."* The hunter team never came. The garrison was already dying.

- **The First Signs:** Past the Junction Hall, the tunnel shows damage. Claw marks in the stone — deep, parallel grooves from something with many legs and enormous strength. Sections of rail have been bent or displaced. The air carries a sharp ozone smell — the chemical signature of lightning discharge. Something has been hunting in these tunnels.

#### The Behir's Territory
The tunnel opens into a wider gallery — a natural cavern the dwarves incorporated into their network. Here the rails split into four parallel tracks leading to loading docks carved into the eastern wall. This was the eastern transit hub: the main transfer point between the mining operations deeper in the mountain and the forge above.

The hub is destroyed. Loading cranes are toppled. Carts are overturned, their contents spilled across the stone floor. The four rail lines are blocked by debris — some of it stone collapse, some of it deliberately arranged. Bones litter the floor: cave fauna (bats, rats, blind cave fish from the flooded lower levels), but also something larger. A section of dwarven armor, partially melted. And everywhere, the scorch marks of lightning strikes.

**DC 15 Perception:** Movement in the far corner of the hub. A shape, serpentine, twelve-legged, roughly twenty-five feet long, coiled among the toppled cranes. It is sleeping. The party has found the behir.

### Act 2: The Predator's Domain

#### Scouting the Behir
The behir — which the maintenance log identifies by the dwarven name *Kellashen*, meaning "lightning coil" — is a formidable predator. In the confined tunnels of the minecart network, it has enormous advantages: speed, the ability to climb walls and ceilings, lightning breath that can arc down metal rails, and a constrict attack that can crush a fully armored fighter.

**DC 14 Nature or Arcana (Mercer):** Behirs are intelligent — not sapient, but clever hunters with excellent memory and territorial instincts. Kellashen has been here for decades, knows every tunnel and junction, and has mapped escape routes through side passages too narrow for anything larger than itself. A direct assault in the hub is possible but extremely dangerous.

**Cora's tactical assessment:** The minecart network gives the party mobility if they can use it. The maintenance cart on the rails can move quickly — faster than the party on foot, potentially faster than the behir in straight-line tunnels. The switch junctions can be used to redirect carts (and anything riding on them) into different tunnels. And the rails themselves are iron — conductive. The behir's lightning breath could be turned against it if the party can isolate it on a section of electrified rail.

#### The First Engagement
The party's approach to scouting wakes Kellashen. It does not attack immediately — it uncurls, rises to its full height (seven feet at the shoulder), and hisses. Its twelve legs grip the stone floor and the walls simultaneously, giving it an unsettling stability.

When it determines the party is a threat to its territory, it charges.

**Kellashen (Behir):** AC 17 (natural armor), HP 168, Speed 50 ft, Climb 40 ft. Bite: +10 to hit, 3d10+6 piercing. Constrict: +10 to hit, 2d10+6 bludgeoning plus grappled (escape DC 16). Lightning Breath (Recharge 5-6): 20 ft line, DC 16 Dexterity save, 12d10 lightning damage (half on save). Swallow: grappled Large or smaller creature, DC 16 Strength save or swallowed.

**The combat should go badly.** Not a TPK — the behir is territorial, not suicidal. But it should demonstrate that a straight fight in the open hub is near-fatal at this level. After dealing significant damage to the party (or taking moderate damage itself), the behir disengages — it climbs the wall, retreats into a side tunnel barely large enough for its body, and disappears.

**The retreat:** The party falls back to the Junction Hall. They are hurt but alive. They have learned the behir's capabilities. And they have learned something critical: the behir avoids the rail tunnels when it can, preferring the natural cavern passages. Why? Because it knows the rails conduct its own lightning. It can hurt itself in the tunnels.

#### Planning the Assault
The rest of this part of the adventure is preparation. The party has the Junction Hall as a base and several advantages:

- **The rails:** Iron rails running through confined tunnels. If the behir uses lightning breath near the rails, the electrical discharge will arc along the metal, dealing damage to anything in contact with it — including the behir. The party can use insulated materials (rubber-equivalent from Cora's alchemist kit, or simply wooden platforms) to protect themselves.

- **The maintenance cart:** Mobile, fast on rails, and capable of carrying one party member at speed through the tunnel network. Can be used to lure the behir into a prepared kill zone.

- **The switch junctions:** The turntable mechanisms can redirect rail traffic. If the party can lure Kellashen into a specific tunnel section, they can seal both ends by switching the junctions and derailing carts as barricades.

- **The loading cranes:** Toppled but potentially functional. Garrick's strength can right one. A crane dropping several hundred pounds of counterweight onto a behir is effective regardless of its armor class.

**Cora's plan (or the party's variation of it):** Lure the behir into a straight tunnel section using the maintenance cart as bait. Use the switch junctions to close off its escape routes. Use the crane as the opening strike. Finish it in the confined space where its mobility is limited.

### Act 3: The Hunt

#### Setting the Trap
The party's preparations take the first portion of this act. Key setup tasks:

- **Garrick rights the loading crane** at the hub entrance (DC 14 Athletics). The counterweight — a stone block weighing roughly 800 pounds — is chained to the crane arm.
- **Cora rigs the switch junctions** (DC 13 Intelligence with tinker's tools) to redirect the eastern tunnel section into a dead-end siding. The behir enters; the switches close behind it.
- **Mercer prepares the rail trap** — using prestidigitation and minor magical effects to charge a section of rail with residual energy that will amplify the behir's own lightning breath back at it (DC 15 Arcana). This is not a damage spell — it is a conductor. If the behir breathes lightning in the prepared section, the discharge rebounds.

#### The Lure
Garrick takes the maintenance cart down the eastern tunnel, making noise — banging his maul against the cart sides, shouting. The behir responds to territorial challenge. It emerges from its side passages, drawn by the sound and the heat of the forge-warmed air Garrick carries with him.

The chase is tense: Garrick pumping the hand-crank cart through the tunnel at speed, the behir's twelve legs clacking on stone behind him, its hiss echoing off the walls. The passing bays blur past. The Junction Hall approaches.

**DC 14 Athletics (Garrick):** Maintain cart speed through the switch junction without derailing. On success, the cart rockets through the prepared tunnel section and out the other side. On failure, the cart derails in the junction — Garrick tumbles but the behir is still drawn into the tunnel.

#### The Engagement
When Kellashen enters the prepared tunnel section, Cora throws the switch. The junction turntable rotates with a grinding screech, and the tunnel entrance behind the behir is sealed by derailed carts forming an iron barricade. The behir is trapped in a sixty-foot section of tunnel with rails on the floor and no climbing surfaces — the walls here are smooth dwarven masonry, not natural stone.

**Phase 1 — The Crane Strike:** As the behir charges toward the party's position at the tunnel's far end, Garrick releases the loading crane's counterweight. The 800-pound stone block drops from the crane arm on a forty-foot arc. Hit: automatic against a Large creature in a twelve-foot tunnel. Damage: 6d10 bludgeoning. This is the opening.

**Phase 2 — Lightning Rebound:** The injured behir uses Lightning Breath (it will recharge quickly under stress). The prepared rails absorb and redirect the discharge — the lightning arcs along the iron rails back toward the behir. It takes its own Lightning Breath damage (12d10, DC 16 Dexterity save for half — the behir has disadvantage on this save because it is standing on the conductive rails).

**Phase 3 — Close Quarters:** The stunned, injured behir fights in the confined tunnel. Its mobility advantages are gone. Garrick engages in melee. Cora uses alchemist fire and crossbow from the tunnel entrance. Mercer provides spell support from behind the barricade. The tunnel limits the behir to frontal attacks only — no wall climbing, no flanking.

The fight should feel dangerous but winnable. Kellashen fights to the death — it has nowhere to retreat, and its territorial instincts override self-preservation. When it falls, the eastern transit hub goes silent for the first time in decades.

#### Reactivation
With the behir dead, the eastern hub can be cleared. This takes the remainder of the adventure:

- **Clear the debris** from the four rail lines (Garrick's strength, one hour)
- **Reset the switching mechanisms** (Cora's tools, two hours)
- **Test the rail lines** with the maintenance cart (successful test run to the mining operations entrance and back)
- **Catalogue the behir's hoard** — Kellashen nested among its kills, and decades of accumulated prey have left behind valuables dragged in from the dwarven residential areas nearby

The minecart network's eastern branch is operational. The party can now move materials from the deep mining areas to the forge in minutes rather than hours. More importantly, the transit hub connects to routes that lead deeper into the Dwarven Delve — toward the pump station, the flooded lower levels, and eventually the interface with the Giant architecture below.

**The closing beat:** On the first test run of the full eastern line, Mercer rides the maintenance cart into the mining tunnel entrance and back. When he returns, he is pale. "The tunnel goes deeper than the blueprint shows," he says. "And there are tracks on the rails. Recent tracks. Something else has been using this network."

## Key Items
- **Behir Lightning Gland** — harvested from Kellashen. Can be used as a component for lightning-based enchantment. Cora can craft one of: a *Javelin of Lightning*, a *Wand of Lightning Bolts* (3 charges, does not recharge — consumable), or install it in the forge to boost the Elemental's heat output (stepping stone to working adamantine). Player choice.
- **Dwarven Transit Map** — a detailed schematic of the entire minecart network, found in the hub's control room. Shows all branch lines including the northern route to Pump Station East, the deep mining routes, and a sealed southern branch labeled *QUARANTINE — DO NOT ACCESS.* Provides advantage on navigation checks within the Dwarven Layer for the rest of the campaign.
- **Foreman Kellak's Seal** — an iron signet found in the hub control room. Bears the authority of the dwarven Foreman of Transit. May be needed to operate secure doors and mechanisms deeper in the network.
- **600 gp** in salvage from the behir's hoard (dwarven jewelry, a gemstone-encrusted belt buckle, iron trade ingots stamped with Kazad-Khrom marks).

## Key NPCs
- **Kellashen (the Behir)** — not a character in the conversational sense, but a force of nature that defined the eastern hub for decades. Its death should feel significant — the party has removed an apex predator from a closed ecosystem, and the consequences (changes in cave fauna behavior, thermal shifts from the cleared ventilation) will be subtly felt.
- **Ignathar (Fire Elemental)** — should react to the minecart network's reactivation. If the party bargained with it, Ignathar offers grudging approval: "The small ones build. Perhaps they are different." If bound, it remains silent but the forge fire burns slightly brighter when the transit hub connects to the forge supply line.

## DM Notes
- The minecart chase is the set piece of this adventure. Make it kinetic — describe the tunnel walls blurring past, the squeal of iron wheels on iron rails, the behir's twelve legs hammering stone in pursuit. This should feel like an action sequence, not a grid combat.
- The behir fight uses the environment as the primary weapon. The party's damage output at level 8 is not sufficient to brute-force a CR 11 creature. The crane, the rail trap, and the confined space are force multipliers that make the fight fair. Reward clever use of the environment with bonus damage or advantage.
- The "recent tracks" on the deep mining rails are the Twisted. They have been using the minecart network for their own logistics — moving materials from the deep levels upward toward their drilling operations. The party has just cut their supply line. This will provoke a response in Adventure 9.
- The QUARANTINE branch on the transit map is deliberately unexplained this adventure. It connects to areas the party will not reach until Act III. Plant the seed. Do not explain it.
- The pump station route (north branch) is the direct setup for Adventure 9. The transit map shows it clearly, but the route is currently flooded at the far end. The pumps need to be reactivated — and something does not want them reactivated.
