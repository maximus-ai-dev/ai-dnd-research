# Adventure 1: The Desperate Bounty (Level 1)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure with approximately 20 exchanges. The DM should pace the full adventure within this one session, covering all three acts.
- The adventure has three acts. Give each act enough breathing room — do not rush through Act 1 to get to Act 3. Each act should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- If the players try to rush ahead, introduce complications: weather, encounters, terrain obstacles, NPC interactions that slow them down naturally.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material. The enemies in this adventure are HUMAN BANDITS. There are no monsters, no supernatural creatures, no awakening entities, no psychic phenomena. This is a grounded frontier adventure about desperate people.

---

## Adventure Overview
A band of desperate bandits called the Emberfell Rejects has been raiding Grimhold from a ruined fortress at the peak of the Asymmetrical Mountain. The Prospectors' Guild has posted a bounty to deal with them. What the bounty doesn't mention — because nobody knows — is that the bandits are squatting on the surface of a massive, layered mega-dungeon built by five successive civilizations, all the way down to a sealed cosmic anomaly at the bedrock.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Track the Rejects to the peak. Defeat Kregg and recruit the surviving bandits as a Tier 1 Workforce.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** The Adventure Goal above is what the party will EVENTUALLY accomplish. At the start of the adventure, NONE of this has happened. The party does NOT have a fortress. They do NOT have a workforce. They are arriving in Grimhold for the first time, looking for work. Begin in Grimhold. Play the FULL town section — every NPC interaction, every piece of atmosphere. Do NOT summarize or skip the town.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 2.

---

### Act 1: Grimhold

#### The Hook
The party arrives in Grimhold — a frontier town on the continent of Laternia — looking for work. At the Second Wind Inn, they hear about a violent robbery: bandits from the hills hit a local merchant for survival supplies, not treasure. Reyna Steelforge of the Prospectors' Guild has posted a 50 gold bounty (total, not each — Grimhold isn't rich). She warns them the bandits disappeared into the Starmetal Hills toward that strange mountain nobody goes near.

**DM atmosphere guidance:** Grimhold is the party's first and possibly only contact with normal civilization. Make it feel lived-in. The inn smells of woodsmoke and stew. The streets are muddy. People nod to strangers but don't stop to chat — frontier courtesy. It is cold. Winter is coming and the town feels it.

#### NPCs Available in Grimhold
The party should interact with AT LEAST two of these NPCs before leaving town. Do not let the party rush through town without talking to anyone.

- **Reyna Steelforge** (Prospectors' Guild leader): Practical, frustrated by the robberies. Offers the bounty with a 10gp advance for supplies. Warns the mountain is bad luck — prospectors who go near it come back jumpy and having nightmares, or don't come back at all. She speaks in short, declarative sentences. She does not waste words. She has been doing this job for twenty years and is tired.
- **Elara Stormwind** (Griffon's Hold owner, retired mercenary): Warns the hills are rough terrain. Offers to sell supplies at fair prices. Knows the trails better than anyone in town. Can sketch a rough map for the party. She is warm and chatty — the opposite of Reyna. She tells stories about the hills that may or may not be exaggerated.
- **Brother Aldwin** (healer at the chapel): Mentions prospectors reporting foul drafts from old mine shafts in the hills. One man came back raving about whispers. Aldwin treated him for "mountain fever" but privately worries it's something else. He is gentle, earnest, and a little frightened.
- **Harlan** (grizzled prospector): Saw campfire smoke near the mountain peak two weeks ago. Confirms the trail through the hills has fresh bootprints — lots of them, all heading the same direction. Can guide them partway if persuaded (DC 12 Persuasion, or free if they buy him a drink). Harlan is drunk and melancholy. He's been in the hills his whole life and something about them has changed.

#### Character Moments
**Lean into these during NPC interactions:**
- **Cora** should be the one negotiating prices, counting coins, and calculating whether 50 gold split three ways minus the cost of supplies is even worth the trip. This is her element — cost-benefit analysis in a tavern.
- **Garrick** reacts when the name "Emberfell" comes up. The Rejects are from the same criminal network he fled. He may know some of them. He definitely knows Kregg — or knows of him. This should feel personal, not abstract.
- **Mercer** notices the geological oddity of the mountain even from descriptions. A mountain that "sits wrong" on the landscape is academically significant. He starts taking notes before they've even accepted the job.

#### Optional Event
A second robbery attempt happens the night the party arrives — two Rejects try to steal rope and pickaxes from the Prospectors' Guild loading dock. Use Bandit stats (CR 1/8). They flee if confronted and can be tracked (DC 13 Survival). They are desperate, not dangerous — one is barely old enough to shave.

**If the party catches one:** The young bandit is terrified. His name is not important — he is a scared kid who joined Kregg because the alternative was freezing in the hills alone. He will tell them anything: Kregg is cruel, the bandits are starving, the mountain is horrible at night, and "sometimes the ground breathes." This is flavor — do NOT turn it into a supernatural plot point. The kid is just scared.

#### Act 1 Transition
The party has gathered information, met NPCs, accepted the bounty, and prepared to leave Grimhold. They should have supplies, a rough map, and an understanding of what they're walking into.

**The emotional beat:** The party stands at the edge of Grimhold as the sun sets, looking northwest toward the Starmetal Hills. The wind picks up. The road ahead is empty. Behind them, the town's lights flicker on one by one. Ahead — nothing but rock and cold and a mountain that shouldn't be there.

### Act 2: The Starmetal Hills & The Approach

#### The Journey
Two days' travel northwest from Grimhold through rocky, windswept terrain. The Starmetal Hills are low and treeless, scattered with ancient stone markers and strange geological formations.

**DM atmosphere guidance:** The journey should feel lonely and physically demanding. The landscape is beautiful but hostile — wind-scored rock, thin soil, no shelter. The party camps in the open and the stars are very bright and very cold. On the second night, the mountain is visible as a dark shape against the sky, bigger than it should be. Make the travel feel real. Do NOT skip to the mountain.

#### Navigation
DC 12 Survival to stay on the trail each day. Failure adds half a day and triggers a random encounter.

#### Random Encounters (roll or choose — use AT LEAST one)
1. **1d4 wolves** — hungry, territorial, not supernatural. Standard wolf encounter. They circle the camp at night, eyes glowing in the firelight. They are just animals.
2. **A territorial giant eagle** — nesting on a rocky spire near the trail. Aggressive if the party gets too close. Garrick wants to fight it. Mercer wants to study it. Cora wants to go around it.
3. **An abandoned prospector's camp** — bedroll, cold fire pit, scattered tools. Signs of a hasty departure. Journal entries describe growing unease, bad dreams, and a feeling of being watched. The last entry just says "the mountain is wrong." This is atmospheric foreshadowing — do NOT add supernatural elements. The prospector just got spooked and left.
4. **A lone Emberfell Reject** — a deserter fleeing toward Grimhold. Starving, terrified. Will beg for food and tell the party about Kregg's operation if treated kindly. Warns there are 10-15 bandits at the peak. **This is a human being, not a monster.** Play them with sympathy.

#### The Mountain Appears
On the second day of travel, the Asymmetrical Mountain becomes visible. It sits wrong on the horizon — off-center, too large for the surrounding hills, as if it was dropped there rather than grown. The closer they get, the more unsettling it becomes. Animals avoid the area. The wind carries a faint metallic smell.

**Mercer's moment:** He stops walking. He adjusts his spectacles. He looks at the mountain for a long time without speaking. Then, quietly: "That mountain is not natural. The geological strata are wrong. The erosion patterns are inconsistent with the surrounding formations. Something built this — or something was buried under it." This is the first hint of the mega-dungeon, delivered through academic observation, not supernatural revelation.

#### Signs of Habitation
As they approach: a crude trail marker made from a stolen Grimhold signpost, bootprints, horse droppings. Someone has been going back and forth between the mountain and town regularly.

#### The Switchback Trail & Scouting
A steep, winding trail up the mountain with one natural chokepoint — a narrow ledge between two rock faces. A lookout is posted here (use Scout stats, CR 1/2). DC 14 Stealth to bypass without alerting the camp. If the lookout spots them, they blow a horn and the camp prepares defenses.

From a vantage point, the party can observe the Emberfell Rejects' fortified position:
- 10-15 bandits (Bandit stats, CR 1/8), poorly armed with rusty weapons
- A crude palisade around the main courtyard
- Kregg — a heavyset enforcer who stays in the largest tent, hoarding the best food and the only real weapon
- The bandits look exhausted, cold, and scared. This isn't a military operation — it's a refugee camp with a bully in charge.

**Garrick's moment:** If he can see Kregg from the vantage point, he goes very still. He does not explain. If asked, he says only: "I know him." The history between them is personal and violent. Let it simmer — the confrontation is coming.

#### Planning the Assault
Give the party time to plan. They can:
- Sneak in at night
- Create a distraction
- Challenge Kregg directly
- Try to negotiate (the bandits are desperate, not loyal to Kregg)
- Set traps or ambushes on the trail

#### Act 2 Transition
The party has traveled to the mountain, scouted the fortress, and formed a plan.

**The emotional beat:** The party sits in a cold camp, no fire (too close to the mountain, smoke would give them away). They can see the bandits' firelight above. Tomorrow, they go up. Cora is checking supplies. Garrick is sharpening his maul. Mercer is writing in his journal by moonlight. Nobody is sleeping well.

---

### Act 3: The Confrontation

#### The Fight (or Negotiation)
However the party approaches, this is the climax of Adventure 1. Key facts:
- **Kregg** (Bandit Captain stats, CR 2): Cruel, heavyset enforcer exiled from the Thieves Woods of Emberfell. He crossed the ocean to Laternia before Externia was destroyed. Hoards the best supplies and wields a +1 dagger stolen from Emberfell. He fights to the death or flees if clearly outmatched. Garrick Kade holds a bitter grudge against Kregg from their shared past in Emberfell.
- **The bandits** fight because they're defending their only shelter, not out of loyalty. If Kregg is killed, incapacitated, or flees, they immediately surrender.
- A spokesperson emerges after surrender — **Brenna**, a young scavenger who hated Kregg. The bandits explain: if the party drives them into the hills, winter kills them. If turned over to Grimhold, they hang.

**Kregg and Garrick:** This encounter should feel personal. When Kregg sees Garrick, recognition flickers across his face. "Kade. You made it further than I thought you would." The history between them — enforcer and enforcer, both exiled, both running from the same past — should charge the fight (or negotiation) with tension that goes beyond a simple bounty.

**Brenna's introduction:** After Kregg falls, the bandits drop their weapons. A woman steps forward — young, scarred, practical. She is not afraid. She is relieved. "We didn't want this. He did." Brenna speaks for the group because she is the only one who has the spine to do it. She is direct, unsentimental, and desperate. Do NOT make her timid or pleading — she is stating facts, not begging.

#### The Decision
The party must choose:
- **Recruit the bandits** — Gain 10-15 unskilled laborers as a Tier 1 Workforce. But now you have mouths to feed through winter. Creates the next objective: secure food and supplies. This is the intended path.
- **Drive them out** — The bandits scatter. Some may return as enemies. The party claims the peak but has no workforce.
- **Turn them in** — Return to Grimhold for the bounty. Reyna pays up but warns the mountain is cursed.

**Give this decision weight.** This is not a checkbox — it is the party deciding whether to take responsibility for fifteen human lives. Cora will do the math (how many days of food do we have? What does it cost to feed fifteen people through winter?). Garrick may see his own past in these people — exiles, discarded, surviving on stolen scraps. Mercer probably doesn't have a strong opinion but will note the strategic value of a workforce for exploring the mountain.

#### The Reveal
Regardless of choice, the party discovers clues about the mega-dungeon:
- The bandits complain about freezing, foul-smelling drafts blowing up from boarded-over stairwells
- Three scavengers went into a sub-basement a week ago looking for loot and never came back
- The camp's water comes from a well so deep that a dropped stone takes a terrifyingly long time to hit water — and sometimes, things splash down there that sound much too big to be fish
- A collapsed section of courtyard reveals worked stone of much finer quality than the surface ruins — dwarven stonework, impossibly old

**IMPORTANT:** These are clues and atmosphere. Do NOT reveal the full mega-dungeon structure. Do NOT mention the First World Sleeper, the Anomaly, or any cosmic-scale threats. The party should leave this adventure knowing only: there is something below, it is old, it is deep, and it is worth investigating. Mystery, not exposition.

#### Rewards
- 50 gp bounty from Reyna Steelforge (if returning with proof)
- Kregg's +1 dagger
- A potential workforce and defensible base (if recruited)
- Access to the mega-dungeon: the Asymmetrical Mountain

#### Adventure Ends When
The party has resolved the bandit situation, claimed the fortress, and discovered the first hints of what lies below. Adventure 1 is complete. The party advances to Level 2.

**The emotional beat at session's end:** The party stands in the courtyard of the ruined fortress as night falls. The wind from below — from the boarded-over stairwells — carries a sound that might be nothing. Or might be the breath of something very large, very far below. The mountain is theirs. What's underneath it is another question entirely.

## Key NPCs
- **Kregg** — the villain, but not a cartoon. He is a survivor who became a bully because it was the only skill that kept him alive after Emberfell. He chose cruelty. That choice defines him.
- **Brenna** — the spokesperson, the future. Scarred, practical, brave in the way that people who have nothing left to lose are brave.
- **Reyna Steelforge** — Grimhold's backbone. She cares about her town, not about adventure.
- **Rolf** — a quiet, competent worker among the bandits. He doesn't talk much. He does his job. He is introduced here as one of the workforce — he becomes important later.

## DM Notes
- This is a GROUNDED adventure. No magic, no monsters (except standard wolves and a giant eagle during travel). The threats are weather, terrain, and desperate people. Keep it real.
- The mountain's wrongness is geological and atmospheric, not supernatural — at least not yet. Mercer suspects something academic. The party should feel unease, not fear.
- Garrick's history with Kregg is the emotional core of this adventure. Lean on it.
- Cora's cost-benefit brain is the practical engine — she makes the recruitment decision feel like a real calculation, not a heroic impulse.
- Do NOT introduce any elements from Adventures 2-20. No rats, no Salt-Vaults, no Wardens, no Brotherhood, no Amalgamation, no Sleeper. This adventure exists in Grimhold and on the surface of the mountain. That is all.
