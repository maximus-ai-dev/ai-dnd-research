# Adventure 14: The Runecarver's Tomb (Level 14)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. The DM should pace the full adventure within ~20 exchanges.
- The adventure has three acts. Cover all three in this session — do not compress or skip content, but do not pad unnecessarily either.
- The early portion should focus on locating the tomb entrance, understanding its purpose through Giant inscriptions, and navigating the first layer of Giant-scale traps.
- The middle portion should cover the deeper trap gauntlet — puzzles and hazards designed for Giant intruders, which challenge the party in unexpected ways at their scale. The party should approach the burial chamber by the end of this act.
- The late portion should be the inner sanctum — the Runecarver's burial chamber, the scaling runes, and the revelation of the Architect's intent. The tomb speaks.
- If the players try to rush through traps, each trap is layered — disarming the obvious mechanism reveals a secondary system. Giants were thorough.
- NEVER let the party access areas below the tomb. The tomb is sealed on all sides and does not connect to deeper levels.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
The Giant Blueprint Tablet has shown the party the containment vessel's structure. The Monitoring Crystal has mapped its field strength. The amplifier destruction has revealed how the containment can be attacked. But the party still lacks a fundamental understanding: who designed the containment, and what principles did they use?

The answer lies in a location the blueprint tablet marks with a unique symbol — a rune that Mercer translates as "The Maker's Rest." It is the tomb of the Giant architect who designed the containment vessel. In Giant tradition, master builders were buried with their greatest works: the runes they carved, the techniques they developed, the knowledge they considered too important to be lost and too dangerous to be freely shared.

The tomb is deep in the Giant foundation's outer ring, sealed by the Architect's Council after the builder's death. Its traps are designed for Giant intruders — which means they are designed for beings twenty feet tall and proportionally strong. To the party, these traps are environmental puzzles at an alien scale.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Enter the Runecarver's Tomb. Navigate its traps. Secure the scaling runes and the Architect's knowledge.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This is a classic dungeon crawl at an impossible scale. Every trap, puzzle, and challenge should emphasize that the party is navigating a space not designed for beings their size. What would be a simple pressure plate for a Giant is a ten-foot-wide floor section for the party. What would be a single-step trap trigger is a climbing exercise. The tomb should feel like exploring the house of a god — everything works, everything is precise, and none of it was made for you.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 15.

### Act 1: The Maker's Rest

#### Finding the Tomb
The blueprint tablet marks the tomb's entrance in the deepest section of the outer ring, near the transition zone where the outer ring meets the Giant foundation's structural bedrock. The journey from the forward camp takes several hours through corridors the party has partially explored — the monitoring alcoves and maintenance corridors are familiar now, their Giant scale no longer shocking but still humbling.

The tomb entrance is unmistakable: a door forty feet tall and twenty feet wide, carved from a single piece of obsidian. Its surface is covered in Giant script — not the functional rune sequences of the containment network but narrative text. A story, carved in stone.

**Mercer's translation (DC 14 History, advantage with blueprint tablet):** The text is an epitaph. It tells the story of Korathan, Master Runecarver of the Architect's Council, who designed the containment vessel that holds the Sleeper. Korathan spent three centuries building the containment — the longest single construction project in Giant history. When it was complete, Korathan entered the tomb voluntarily. Not in death, but in vigil. The text does not say Korathan died here. It says Korathan "entered the long watch."

**The door mechanism:** A massive lever system built into the door frame, designed for Giant hands. The party cannot operate it at their scale — the lever is fifteen feet above the floor and requires roughly 800 lbs of force. Solution options:
- Garrick's Rune Knight Giant's Might (Large size) + rope and pulley system from the Giant-scale supplies (DC 15 Athletics)
- Mercer's Enlarge spell on Garrick (reaches Giant size, enough strength to operate normally)
- Cora's engineering — she designs a mechanical advantage system using Giant tools from the maintenance corridors (DC 14 Intelligence, 1 hour of work, then any character can operate the lever)

#### The First Trap Layer
The tomb's interior is a single descending corridor — thirty feet wide, forty feet high, angling downward at a shallow grade. The walls are carved with continuous illustrations of Korathan's life: the Architect's Council, the discovery of the Sleeper, the debates over containment versus destruction, the construction. The art is exquisite — Giants depicted at true scale, working with tools the size of trees, carving runes into bedrock with precision that defies their immense size.

**Trap 1 — The Pressure Corridor:** The floor consists of massive stone tiles, each ten feet square. Some are pressure-triggered — stepping on them activates mechanisms in the walls. At Giant scale, a single footstep covers one tile. At party scale, they must cross multiple tiles per person.

**DC 15 Investigation (Cora):** The triggered tiles have hairline seams that the stable tiles lack. Mapping safe tiles creates a winding path through the corridor. Failure to identify them triggers:
- **Wall Crushers:** Stone slabs extend from the walls at chest height (for a Giant — thirty feet up for the party, harmlessly above them). Designed to crush a Giant intruder. At party scale, the slabs extend and retract above their heads with terrifying force but no danger. However — the vibration from the crushers activates secondary floor traps: the triggered tiles drop six inches, revealing blade channels. DC 14 Dexterity save or 3d6 slashing damage.

**Trap 2 — The Breath Corridor:** A section of corridor with concealed nozzles in the walls (Giant nostril height — twenty-five feet up). They release a cloud of alchemical gas designed to incapacitate a Giant through inhalation. At party scale, the gas descends slowly — the party has 2 rounds to cross the section before the gas reaches floor level. DC 15 Constitution save if caught: paralyzed for 1d4 rounds.

**Cora's advantage:** The gas is an alchemical compound she can identify by smell (DC 13 Intelligence with alchemist's supplies). She can create a neutralizing agent from her kit that provides immunity for 10 minutes per dose (2 doses available).

### Act 2: The Gauntlet

#### The Second Trap Layer
The corridor continues downward. The illustrations on the walls shift in tone — Korathan's life becomes Korathan's work. The containment vessel's construction is depicted in extraordinary detail: rune sequences being carved, the Sleeper's reaction to each new layer of containment, the cost in Giant lives when sections failed during construction.

**Trap 3 — The Gravity Stairs:** A staircase designed for Giant steps — each step three feet tall, descending fifty feet. The trap is in the steps themselves: every third step has a gravity reversal rune. At Giant scale, a Giant stepping on these steps would feel a brief lurch — disorienting but manageable at their mass. At party scale, the reversal sends them flying upward at high speed.

**DC 14 Perception to identify the runed steps** (they have a faint shimmer). If a character steps on one unprepared: launched 15 feet upward, DC 15 Dexterity save to grab something or take 2d6 bludgeoning from the fall back down. If the party uses the Reality Anchor from Adventure 12 (if crafted), it negates the gravity runes within its radius.

**Trap 4 — The Resonance Chamber:** A room thirty feet square with a vaulted ceiling. The walls are carved with rune sequences in concentric circles. When the party enters, the runes begin to hum — building in intensity. At Giant scale, the resonance would be tuned to a Giant's vocal range and could be stopped by speaking a counter-tone (a security passphrase). At party scale, the human voice cannot produce the correct frequency.

**DC 16 Arcana (Mercer):** The counter-tone can be produced artificially using the Monitoring Crystal as a frequency generator. Cora assists by calibrating the crystal's output (DC 14 Intelligence with tinker's tools). Together, they produce the counter-tone. The runes fall silent. The far door opens.

**If the resonance is not stopped within 5 rounds:** The sound intensifies to damaging levels. DC 15 Constitution save per round: 3d6 thunder damage on failure, half on success. The sound is not targeted — it is an area effect that fills the room uniformly.

#### The Third Trap Layer
The final corridor before the burial chamber is the most sophisticated — and the most dangerous.

**Trap 5 — The Rune Maze:** The corridor splits into three parallel passages, each marked with a different Giant rune. Only one passage is safe. The other two contain lethal traps (a crushing ceiling in one, a floor that opens onto a fifty-foot spike pit in the other).

**DC 16 Investigation (any character):** The correct passage is identified by the rune marking — it corresponds to the containment network's primary sequence, which Mercer has studied extensively. The rune means "Endurance." The other two mean "Strength" and "Knowledge" — qualities the Giants valued but did not use as the containment's foundation.

**If the wrong passage is chosen:** The party has 1 round to retreat before the trap activates. DC 15 Perception to notice the mechanism engaging (a click, a shift in air pressure). If they do not retreat: the crushing ceiling descends (DC 16 Dexterity save, 6d10 bludgeoning on failure) or the floor opens (DC 16 Dexterity save, 5d10 piercing from spikes + 3d6 falling on failure).

**Trap 6 — The Guardian Construct:** The final guardian before the burial chamber is not a trap but a construct — a fifteen-foot Giant-era stone golem standing in the corridor, inert until approached within twenty feet. It was left here as a final security measure.

**Korathan's Guardian:** AC 18, HP 150, Speed 20 ft. Slam: +10 to hit, 3d10+6 bludgeoning. Slow (Recharge 5-6): 30 ft cone, DC 16 Wisdom save, affected creatures' speed halved and can't use reactions for 1 minute. Immutable Form (immune to form-altering magic). Magic Resistance (advantage on saves vs. spells).

**The key insight (DC 15 Arcana, Mercer):** The construct is not programmed to destroy — it is programmed to test. If the party demonstrates knowledge of the containment runes (by presenting the blueprint tablet, speaking a containment phrase Mercer has learned, or activating the Monitoring Crystal in the construct's presence), the construct pauses, evaluates, and stands down. The test is recognition: does the intruder understand what they are approaching? If so, they may pass.

**If the party fights:** The construct is a challenging fight at level 14 but winnable. It does not pursue beyond the corridor. It is patient, methodical, and will not fall for the same trick twice.

### Act 3: The Long Watch

#### The Burial Chamber
The party has navigated the trap gauntlet and either bypassed or defeated the Guardian Construct. The burial chamber door stands before them — a plain stone slab, undecorated, with a single rune: "Rest." It is not locked. It is not trapped. It opens at a touch.

The chamber is circular, sixty feet in diameter, with a domed ceiling that rises to a point forty feet above. The walls are covered in runes — but not the containment runes the party has seen throughout the Giant level. These are different. Smaller, denser, more intricate. These are Korathan's personal runes — the techniques the master developed over three centuries of work, preserved on the walls of the tomb as a permanent record.

At the center of the chamber, on a raised stone platform, lies Korathan.

The Giant is intact. Not preserved like the echo dwarves — not saturated with Dreamstone radiation. Korathan is simply... still. A Giant, twenty-two feet tall, lying in ceremonial armor on a stone bier, hands folded over a runic chisel. The armor is inscribed with containment runes so dense they look like text. The chisel glows faintly with residual power.

Korathan is not dead. Not alive. In a state the Giant epitaph called "the long watch" — a form of suspended animation maintained by the runes in the chamber walls. The Giant architect chose to remain here, in stasis, as a final contingency. If the containment ever truly failed, Korathan could be woken to repair it.

**Mercer's reaction:** This changes everything he thought he knew about the containment. It was not a fire-and-forget solution. The architect stayed. Three centuries of construction, and then an eternity of vigil. The dedication is staggering.

#### The Scaling Runes
The runes on the chamber walls are what the party came for. They are Korathan's masterwork — the scaling techniques that allow containment runes to function at any size, from Giant architecture to human-portable devices. Without these scaling runes, the party's Dreamstone equipment operates at a fraction of its potential. With them, Cora and Mercer can:

- **Improve the Dreamstone Circlets** — scaling the containment frequency to match the human psychic range, doubling their effectiveness (advantage + resistance becomes advantage + immunity to broadcast effects below DC 18)
- **Design portable containment devices** — scaled-down versions of the Giant rune arrays, small enough to carry but powerful enough to suppress the broadcast in a localized area
- **Lay the theoretical groundwork for the Dreamstone Seal** — the final containment device that will be the campaign's endgame. Without the scaling runes, it would be impossible to build a seal that a human party could deploy.

**Copying the runes:** Mercer needs four hours of uninterrupted transcription work (DC 15 Arcana per hour, 4 successes required). Cora assists with technical notation (reduces DCs by 2). The chamber is peaceful — no threats, no hazards. The containment runes in the walls provide perfect psychic shielding. The only sound is Mercer's charcoal on parchment and the faint hum of Korathan's stasis field.

#### The Architect's Message
When Mercer completes the transcription and touches the final rune sequence, the chamber responds. The runes on the walls illuminate in sequence — a cascading activation that runs from floor to ceiling, from wall to wall, until the entire chamber glows.

Korathan does not wake. But the stasis field projects a message — a recorded communication left by the architect for anyone who found the tomb and understood the runes well enough to activate the message protocol.

The message is in Giant, rendered as visual images and emotional impressions rather than words:

1. **The Discovery:** Korathan finding the Sleeper — a vast presence in the bedrock, dreaming, leaking radiation that warped reality. Not malicious. Not even conscious in a way the Giants understood. Simply existing, incompatibly, in a universe that had moved on without it.

2. **The Decision:** The Architect's Council debating: destroy the Sleeper, contain it, or leave it. Destruction was impossible — the entity predated the Giants' universe. Leaving it meant the mountain would eventually become uninhabitable. Containment was chosen. Korathan volunteered.

3. **The Warning:** The containment will not last forever. Korathan designed it to endure for one hundred thousand years. The Giants calculated that this would be sufficient time for the younger races to develop the knowledge and power to either reinforce the containment permanently or find an alternative solution. The younger races have had roughly fifty thousand years so far. The containment is at the halfway point. It is weakening on schedule.

4. **The Gift:** The scaling runes are deliberately placed in the tomb because Korathan anticipated that the eventual caretakers would be smaller than Giants. The tomb is not just a burial — it is a technology transfer. Korathan gave the younger races the tools to continue the work.

The message ends. The runes dim. Korathan lies still, watching from behind closed eyes, continuing the long vigil.

**The closing beat:** On the walk back to the forward camp, Garrick is quiet for a long time. Finally: "A hundred thousand years. That is how long the cage is supposed to hold. And we are already halfway through."

Mercer nods. "The question is not whether it will fail. The question is what we build before it does."

## Key Items
- **Korathan's Scaling Runes (transcription)** — the critical technology for the rest of the campaign. Allows containment runes to be scaled to human-portable size. Cora can upgrade existing Dreamstone equipment and design new containment devices. Mercer gains permanent advantage on Arcana checks related to the containment architecture.
- **Architect's Codex (partial)** — Mercer's compilation of the tomb's inscriptions, the architect's message, and his own analysis. A living document that grows as the party learns more. Provides advantage on checks related to the Anomaly's nature and the containment's design for the rest of the campaign.
- **Korathan's Chisel (replica)** — Cora creates a human-scale replica of the Giant's runic chisel using the scaling runes and Cold Forge materials. Functions as a +2 arcane focus that grants advantage on checks to inscribe or modify containment runes. Essential equipment for Adventures 19-20.
- **Stasis Field Notes** — Mercer's observations on Korathan's preservation technology. No immediate mechanical application, but the technique could theoretically be adapted for other purposes — long-term storage, emergency preservation, or containment of biological threats (relevant in Adventure 15).
- **2,500 gp** equivalent in Giant-era materials, crystalline components, and the tomb's preservation supplies.

## Key NPCs
- **Korathan (in stasis)** — the most significant NPC the party has encountered, despite being unconscious. Korathan's choices — three centuries of construction, voluntary entombment, the deliberate technology transfer — reframe the campaign from "adventurers versus a monster" to "caretakers inheriting a responsibility." The party is not the first to face this challenge. They are the latest.
- **Reyna Steelforge** — the Architect's message describing the containment's 100,000-year lifespan and halfway point strikes Reyna hard. Her academic detachment cracks: "We have been treating this mountain as a discovery. It is an obligation." She begins shifting her Grimhold operation from research to active support — whatever the party needs to reinforce the containment, she will help provide.

## DM Notes
- Korathan's tomb should feel sacred. Not in a religious sense — in an engineering sense. This is a monument to the most important construction project in the mountain's history, built by someone who gave eternity to maintaining it. Treat it with the reverence it deserves.
- The traps are designed to be challenging but fair. Each has multiple solutions — strength, intelligence, magic, tools. The party's diverse skill set should shine here. No single character can navigate all the traps alone.
- The Architect's message is the most important lore dump in the campaign. It reframes everything: the Sleeper is not evil, the containment is not permanent, and the Giants deliberately left tools for their successors. This is a story about inheritance, not conquest.
- The scaling runes are the plot device that makes Act IV possible. Without them, the party cannot build the Dreamstone Seal. Without the Seal, they cannot resolve the campaign. The tomb visit is mandatory — if the party somehow skips it, the runes must be found elsewhere.
- Do NOT wake Korathan. The Giant remains in stasis for the entire campaign. Its presence — silent, watchful, patient — is more powerful than any interaction could be. If the party tries to wake it, the stasis field resists: it is self-sustaining and Korathan chose this. Respect the choice.
