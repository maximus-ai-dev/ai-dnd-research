# Adventure 4: The Sealed Archives (Level 4, Act I Finale)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure with approximately 20 exchanges. The DM should pace the full adventure within this one session, covering all three acts.
- The adventure has three acts. Give each act enough breathing room — do not rush through Act 1 to get to Act 3. Each act should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- If the players try to rush ahead, introduce complications: magical darkness that cannot be dispelled without anchoring the light source, false walls that lead to dead ends, the Amalgamation stalking them through walls they cannot see.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
The Brotherhood sanctum lies forty feet below the Salt-Vaults preparation kitchen, behind a door inscribed with a closed eye and the words: *The Grand Archive. Entry by rank only. The Amalgamation watches.*

The party has the Brotherhood insignias. They know what waits inside. The question is whether they are ready for it.

The Secretive Brotherhood were a cabal of arcane scholars who burrowed into the hollow spaces of the Iron Warlords' ruins, building a hidden sanctum that no one on the surface knew existed. They catalogued everything they found: the Iron Warlords' military doctrine, the Giant runes above their ceiling, fragments of the deep aura they could sense but not explain. Their library — the Sealed Archives — contains centuries of scholarship that Mercer would consider the most significant academic discovery of the age.

When they finally abandoned the site, they left their most dangerous creation behind to guard it: the Amalgamation, a flesh-golem constructed from the Brotherhood's own dead. It cannot see. It has never needed to. The sanctum's magical darkness is its natural environment, and it has been waiting in the dark for two hundred years.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Navigate the Sealed Archives, recover the spellbooks, defeat the Amalgamation, and open the Grand Stairwell to the levels below.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** The Amalgamation is not a mindless monster. It follows the last orders it was given — *guard the Archives, destroy unauthorized intruders.* The Brotherhood insignias do not grant permission to be in the sanctum. They only open the door. Once inside, the party is trespassers unless they can find a way to be recognized as authorized. The Wardens' knowledge (from Adventure 3) may be relevant here.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 5. This also triggers Intermission I — the workforce clears the Grand Stairwell and the peak becomes a stable surface camp.

### Act 1: The Grand Archive

#### The Door
The Brotherhood door is at the end of the interstitial corridor, forty feet from the preparation kitchen. It is three inches of stone fitted with Brotherhood arcane script — the closed-eye symbol in the center. The three insignias from Adventure 3 fit into three recesses arranged in a triangle. When all three are placed simultaneously, the arcane lock releases with a sound like a held breath finally exhaled.

The door opens inward. Cold air rushes out — not the stale cold of the Iron Warlords' corridors, but something sharper. Preserved. The smell is old ink, old leather, and something else: a faint animal warmth, like something large has been sleeping nearby for a very long time.

**The Wardens (if cooperative from Adventure 3):** Warden Aldric will not enter the sanctum. This is above his authorization level. He will tell the party one useful thing before they go: *"The Grand Archive is divided into three wings. The Amalgamation patrols the Central Hall. The East Wing contains the primary collections. The West Wing was restricted — Commander's level only. We were never told why."* He does not know what the Amalgamation will do when it encounters them. He has never seen it. He only knew it existed.

#### The Antechamber
The first room beyond the door is a rectangular antechamber — roughly thirty feet by twenty feet. Stone benches along the walls. A rank-insignia board mounted near the entrance showing Brotherhood hierarchies: Initiate, Scholar, Archivist, Commander. The insignias the party carries are Scholar-rank.

On the benches: personal effects left behind in a hurry. A half-written letter in archaic Common (Mercer can read it: *"...the deep readings have intensified again. Brother Cassen believes the resonance is climbing toward the Iron Warlords' level. I believe we have three months before—"* it stops mid-sentence). Two empty lantern hooks on the wall. No lanterns.

**Key detail:** There is no magical darkness in the antechamber. The ceiling here is intact Brotherhood stonework. But through the archway at the far end, the air is noticeably darker. Not dim — dark in a specific way, as if light itself becomes reluctant at that threshold.

#### The Central Hall
Beyond the antechamber, the Central Hall is a long corridor — sixty feet, ten feet wide — with archways branching left (East Wing), right (West Wing), and straight ahead (the Restricted Vault). The stone floor is smooth, worn down by centuries of foot traffic.

The darkness here is real. Light sources work, but their radius is halved — a torch that should illuminate thirty feet illuminates fifteen. A lantern that should illuminate sixty feet illuminates thirty. The effect worsens as the party moves farther in.

**What the party can see in the Central Hall:**
- Extensive damage. Shelving units ripped from walls. Stone benches overturned. Personal items scattered and ground under something heavy.
- Claw marks — massive, wide-set, four-inch deep gouges in the stone floor at regular intervals. The stride pattern is large: roughly eight feet between sets.
- A dark stain spreading from the center of the hall in a wide radius. Not blood — something older. It smells faintly of the same animal warmth as the door.
- One Brotherhood body, partially preserved by the sanctum's ambient transmutation. It has been here a very long time. Mercer can identify Brotherhood Commander-rank insignia on the robe. The body shows no visible wounds. It is simply lying in the middle of the floor, as if it sat down and did not get up.

**Mercer's Arcana/History check (DC 14):** The Amalgamation is a flesh-golem — a Brotherhood specialty. Unlike standard golems, flesh-golems experience rage states when damaged. They are immune to most magic. The magical darkness in this sanctum is structural — woven into the stone itself by the Brothers when they built this level. It cannot be dispelled. It can only be anchored (by attaching a light source to the Amalgamation directly) or countered with radiant damage.

#### The East Wing (Early Exploration)
The East Wing holds the primary collections — rows of stone shelving, once filled with books and scrolls. The damage here is less severe than the Central Hall, suggesting the Amalgamation has less reason to enter. Most of the collection is destroyed: water damage from above over the centuries, fungal growth, and simple decay have rendered the majority to mulch. But at the back of the East Wing, behind a false wall (DC 13 Investigation to detect from the slightly different stonework), is a secondary preservation chamber.

The chamber is intact. Inside:
- A sealed stone cabinet containing **7 Brotherhood spellbooks** — preserved in the same transmutation field as everything in the vaults. Three are general magical theory (minor academic value, Mercer finds them interesting but not groundbreaking). Two are specialized Brotherhood research into the deep aura — extremely relevant to the campaign's larger mystery. **Two are practical arcane collections with spells Mercer doesn't currently have** (DM's discretion, suggest Web, Enlarge/Reduce, Counterspell, Hypnotic Pattern as appropriate additions).
- A Brotherhood Commander's logbook, also preserved. Written by the same hand as the unfinished letter in the antechamber. **This is the most important item in the archive** — it contains a partial record of the Brotherhood's research, including what they found when they opened the West Wing's restricted vault, and why they sealed it back up and left.

#### The Near-Miss
Before the party can exit the East Wing with their finds, they hear it: a rhythmic impact from the Central Hall. Heavy. Regular. Stone floor yielding slightly to each footfall. Something is moving through the hall.

They cannot see it — the darkness in the Central Hall is complete from this angle. But the stride pattern matches the claw marks exactly. Eight feet per step. And it is moving slowly, methodically, in a pattern that suggests a search pattern rather than a patrol.

**The party must choose:** hide in the preservation chamber (it is behind a false wall), move to the West Wing and risk making noise, or try to get back to the antechamber. If they move without making noise (DC 14 Stealth for Garrick in chain mail — he has disadvantage), they may get through. If they fail, the footsteps pause. The darkness seems to thicken. Then, slowly, resume in a new direction — toward them.

**If the Amalgamation locates them early:** it should be a terrifying brush, not a fight. The party sees it for the first time in the half-light at the edge of the antechamber — massive, wrong-proportioned, made of too many bodies arranged into one shape. It fills the archway. Its face is featureless. It reaches one arm toward them.

Then they are through the door and the stone seals behind them.

### Act 2: What the Commander Knew

#### The Logbook
Before the party goes back in, Mercer reads the Commander's logbook. It takes him the better part of the night. What he finds:

The Brotherhood knew about the aura. They called it "the Deep Resonance." They spent decades trying to understand it, catalogue it, map its intensity. The West Wing's restricted vault was opened by the Commander himself after he noticed the resonance intensifying — climbing toward the Iron Warlords' layer. Behind the restricted vault door, he found a passage. Not a Brotherhood passage, not an Iron Warlords' passage. Older. Much older. He describes the stonework: "proportions no human hand designed. Steps the height of a man's knee. A smell of deep water and very old iron." He sent one scholar in. The scholar did not come back. The Commander sealed the vault, dismissed the junior scholars, and left with the senior staff. He left the Commander's body behind. The logbook doesn't explain why.

**Mercer's conclusion:** The restricted vault opens onto the Military Empire's level — the layer below the Brotherhood. This is the Grand Stairwell. The party does not need to defeat the Amalgamation to open the Stairwell door — they just need to reach it.

**The tactical question:** Can they run straight through the Central Hall, past the Amalgamation, and reach the Restricted Vault? Possibly. But the Restricted Vault likely has its own lock, the Amalgamation will be on them before they can work it, and whatever is in the West Wing (the Commander sealed it for a reason) may be worse. The best path is to deal with the Amalgamation directly.

#### The Amalgamation's Weakness
From the Brotherhood texts in the spellbooks and the logbook, Mercer can piece together the Amalgamation's construction:
- **Blind.** It has no sensory organs. It navigates entirely by vibration, sound, and air pressure. It is exquisitely sensitive to all three.
- **Magic immune.** Standard Brotherhood construction — spells of 6th level or lower that require a saving throw fail automatically. Magic Missile, Thunderwave, Faerie Fire: none of these will work directly on it.
- **Vulnerable to radiant damage.** The Brotherhood used necromantic processes to construct it. Radiant damage disrupts these bindings. Unfortunately, none of the party currently have access to radiant damage — but Cora's Experimental Elixir can produce unexpected results.
- **Rage state.** When damaged, the Amalgamation enters a rage: it moves faster, hits harder, and begins destroying its environment indiscriminately. This is how it destroyed most of the Central Hall.
- **Command pause.** If a Brotherhood command phrase is spoken at sufficient volume, the Amalgamation pauses for one round — long enough to reposition. The phrase is in the logbook: *"Stand Guardian. The Brotherhood commands it."* It only works once per encounter before the Amalgamation recognizes the voice as unauthorized.

**Garrick's approach:** The Amalgamation is a guardian, not a predator. It follows orders. This means it has rules. Garrick's enforcer instincts recognize that something with rules has predictable behavior — and predictable behavior can be exploited.

#### Returning to the Sanctum
The party enters the Brotherhood sanctum for the second time knowing considerably more. Their goals:
1. Reach the Restricted Vault
2. Engage and defeat the Amalgamation on their terms rather than its

**The Central Hall, second entry:** The Amalgamation has rearranged the Hall since their first visit. The overturned benches form crude barriers. The claw marks on the floor now show a more complex patrol pattern — the creature has been active. And the magical darkness is noticeably thicker. The Amalgamation generates it slowly through proximity. The longer it has been in a space, the darker that space becomes.

#### The West Wing (Optional)
If the party explores the West Wing before engaging the Amalgamation, they find the reason the Commander sealed it: behind the Brotherhood-era door is a short passage, and at the end, a door of clearly different construction. No Brotherhood stonework. Something older, heavier, more careful. The Military Empire's stonework — this is the other side of what the campaign bible calls "The Relic Basements."

The door is sealed from this side with iron bars (the Commander's work) and from the other side by what Mercer identifies as "an institutional lock — something that was meant to stay shut for a very long time." The Brotherhood bars can be removed. The institutional lock will need to be dealt with from the other side, or with tools the party doesn't currently have.

This is a door for the future, not for now. But it means there are TWO entrances to the lower level — the Grand Stairwell (official, wide, designed for foot traffic) and this West Wing passage (unofficial, narrow, originally a service access). The party now knows both exist.

> **HOSTILE-ONLY ENCOUNTER — THE AMALGAMATION ATTACKS INSTANTLY**
> The Amalgamation is a broken machine following broken orders. It responds to proximity like a pressure plate responds to weight. It charges the nearest sound source and swings. The command phrase is a kill switch — a hard reboot code, like pulling a power cord. The party FIGHTS or RETREATS.

#### The Amalgamation, First Full Encounter
Partway through exploring the West Wing, the Amalgamation finds them. This is the first real combat — but not the decisive one. Use these stats as guidelines:

**The Amalgamation (First Encounter — Patrol Mode)**
- HP: 60 (of its full 105)
- AC: 9 (it relies on its magic immunity, not its armor class)
- Speed: 30 ft., blindsight 60 ft. (deaf and blind beyond this range)
- Multiattack: Two slam attacks (+7 to hit, 2d8+5 bludgeoning)
- Magic Immunity: spells of 6th level or lower that require saving throws automatically fail
- Vulnerability: radiant damage (double damage)
- Rage State trigger: when it takes 15+ damage in a single turn
- **NOT in Rage State this encounter**

**Tactical notes:**
- The Amalgamation moves toward sound. Garrick in chain mail moving is trivially easy for it to track. Mercer in soft-soled boots can attempt DC 12 Stealth.
- Cora's Faerie Fire doesn't affect it directly (magic immunity), but it illuminates the darkness around it, letting allies see it clearly.
- The command pause (*"Stand Guardian. The Brotherhood commands it."*) works here for one round.
- Garrick's Stone Rune (Fighter Rune Knight feature) is the key tool: on a hit, he can expend the rune to potentially restrain the Amalgamation for a round — giving the party a chance to disengage.

**The goal of this encounter is to retreat, not to win.** The party should deal meaningful damage (trigger Rage State), survive the enraged version briefly, then get out using the command pause as a last resort. They now know:
- Its speed and reach
- That rage is a real threat
- That restraining it is possible
- The exact dimensions of the Central Hall fight space

### Act 3: The Blind Dark

#### Preparation
Before entering for the third time, the party can prepare. Cora's Experimental Elixir can produce a Potion of Radiance (a home-brew alchemical that allows a melee strike to deal an additional 2d6 radiant damage on the next hit — use this to give Garrick radiant damage access for the fight). Mercer should prepare Mage Armor and have Misty Step and Invisibility ready. Garrick should have Action Surge and Second Wind available.

**One useful item if the party thinks of it:** attaching a lit torch directly to the Amalgamation would halve the darkness it generates in the fight space. Cora can rig a torch to a thrown weighted hook with DC 12 tinker's tools — it won't deal damage but if it hits (ranged attack, improvised weapon, DC 12) it attaches and burns for one minute. The Amalgamation will eventually tear it off but it buys time.

#### The Final Run
The party enters the Central Hall for the last time. The darkness is thicker than before — the Amalgamation has been here undisturbed. Light sources now illuminate only ten feet. The Amalgamation is somewhere in the hall.

**The fight structure:**

**Phase 1 (Amalgamation at full capacity):**
- The Amalgamation stalks toward the largest sound source (Garrick)
- Its attacks are powerful but predictable in pattern — Garrick can use Shield Block (Fighter) to reduce two attacks per round
- Cora's radiance-enhanced strikes are the primary damage source — she needs to get in range, which means Garrick holds the line while she closes
- Mercer supports from range: the torch-hook attack, Misty Step to reposition, Invisibility to remove himself from the Amalgamation's blindsight range
- The command pause is the emergency button — Mercer speaks the phrase only when the Amalgamation would drop Garrick or Cora

**Phase 2 (Rage State — triggered at 52 HP or below):**
- The Amalgamation's speed increases to 40 ft.
- Its slams deal an additional die of damage (3d8+5)
- It begins destroying the environment: shelving units, stone benches, part of the ceiling. Each round, one randomly determined area of the Central Hall becomes difficult terrain (roll d4: 1-3 it's between the party, 4 it's between the Amalgamation and the party — lucky)
- The command phrase does NOT work in Rage State

**Phase 3 (Below 25 HP — Dying):**
- The Amalgamation's movements become erratic rather than purposeful
- Its blindsight range drops to 30 ft.
- On its final action before death, it reaches both arms toward the nearest creature — not to attack, but as if trying to hold on to something. It does not speak. It falls. This is a machine winding down. Narrate the shutdown: gears grinding to a halt, patrol loop stuttering, limbs locking in mid-stride.

**When the Amalgamation dies:**
The magical darkness begins to fade — slowly, from the edges of the sanctum inward, taking about ten minutes to clear completely. The sanctum's remaining ambient magic dissipates. The preservation field weakens slightly (the books are fine, but if there were any living things preserved by it, like the Commander's body, they finally decay).

From the Amalgamation's chest cavity — because it is, in the end, a construction of flesh — the party finds **the Amalgamation's Core:** a pulsing arcane battery the size of a fist, warm, geometric, and glowing faintly blue. Mercer identifies it immediately: this was the Brotherhood's most advanced construct-powering technology. It can be installed in the fortress to power magical defenses, or broken down for individual use. It is valuable and unprecedented.

#### The Grand Stairwell
The Restricted Vault door opens with a Scholar-rank insignia (the Brotherhood was nothing if not hierarchical about which keys opened which doors — the Restricted Vault opens at Scholar level because it was meant to be studied, not used).

The door opens onto a stairwell of entirely different construction: wide, broad steps, high ceilings, torch brackets still in their original Iron Warlords' positions (the Brotherhood built around this stairwell, not over it). The air from below is cool and dry with a complexity that speaks of scale — this is not a corridor or a room. This is the top of a very long descent.

The stairwell descends into darkness. In the faint glow of the party's light sources, they can see it going down for at least fifty feet before curving out of sight. The walls shift from Iron Warlords' stone to something older — more precise, more deliberate, with different proportions. Military Empire stonework.

At the landing, a rusted iron plaque reads in old Common: *LOWER PALACE. AUTHORIZED MILITARY PERSONNEL ONLY. NO EXCAVATION BELOW THIS LEVEL WITHOUT GENERAL'S SEAL.*

Mercer's quill is already writing. Below this landing, somewhere in the dark: the Relic Basements. The Sealed Crypt. And further, much further, the Dwarven Delve.

**The party does not go down today.** They have thirty people upstairs who need to know about this, and they have earned the right to come back to the surface and breathe clean air.

#### The Act I Closing Beat
When the party returns to the surface, the reaction from the workforce matters. The Amalgamation is dead. The Grand Stairwell is open. The Salt-Vaults contract with the Psychic Guardian is complete. The fortress has food, shelter, a functional defensive structure, and now a door to what lies below.

Brenna gathers everyone in the courtyard for a genuine meal — the first real one since the fortress was taken. Rolf, characteristically, says very little. The Wardens (if alive and cooperative) stand at the edges, uncertain of their role in a world without the Brotherhood.

And Mercer reads from the Commander's logbook by firelight: *"The resonance climbs. Whatever is sleeping below this mountain, it is not sleeping well."*

**Adventure 4 complete. The party advances to Level 5.**

#### Intermission I: The Frontier Outpost
Before Adventure 5 begins, the workforce spends three in-game months on surface improvements. This is handled off-screen as a brief narrative beat at the start of the next adventure:
- The Grand Stairwell is cleared of debris and secured with a new door (the workforce's carpentry)
- The surface curtain walls are patched — the fortress is now genuinely defensible
- The Salt-Vaults become the party's permanent food supply, with rotation schedules managed by Cora
- Rolf organizes a scouting network in the Starmetal Hills — they'll know if Kregg comes back
- The Wardens take up residence as fortress guards — strange, formal, two hundred years out of time, but competent
- Grimhold's relationship with the party changes: Reyna Steelforge begins calling it "the operation at the mountain" rather than "the bandit camp"

The peak is now a stable surface camp. The party is level 5. Below, the Grand Stairwell waits.

## Key Items
- **The Amalgamation's Core** — pulsing arcane battery. Install in fortress to power magical defenses (requires Cora's Artificer work, takes one week, provides a permanent magical ward against uninvited entry). OR break it down: yields enough components for one uncommon magic item per party member (DM's discretion, appropriate to each character).
- **2 practical spellbooks** — new spells for Mercer. Suggested: Web, Counterspell, Enlarge/Reduce, Hypnotic Pattern.
- **The Commander's Logbook** — primary source on the Deep Resonance. Mercer will want to study this for weeks. Seeds Act II.
- **Brotherhood Researcher spellbooks (2)** — detailed notes on the Deep Resonance aura. If Mercer spends downtime studying these, he gains advantage on Arcana checks related to the Anomaly for the next three adventures.
- **500 gp** in Brotherhood relics, salvage, and rare alchemical components from the preservation chamber.

## Key NPCs
- **The Amalgamation** — give it presence through its mechanical persistence. Two hundred years of the same patrol loop in the dark. It is the last Brotherhood creation. It does what it was made to do. Its death acknowledges its mechanical persistence — a machine finally allowed to stop.
- **Warden Aldric** — the most human of the three Wardens. He will want to know when it is over. He will want to see the Core — it is, in a sense, all that is left of the Brotherhood.
- **Brenna** — she has been managing thirty people through winter while the party was underground. She is exhausted. She is also proud in a way she won't say aloud.

## DM Notes
- The magical darkness is the dungeon's central mechanic. Make it atmospheric. Light should feel precious in here.
- The Amalgamation is a machine. It was given one job and it executes that job. It has no awareness of time passing, no loneliness, no understanding that the Brotherhood is gone. Garrick in particular may recognize this — he was an enforcer once, following orders mechanically. The parallel is in the futility of the routine, not in shared suffering.
- Cora's radiant damage access is the intended solution to the magic immunity. If the party doesn't think of the Experimental Elixir, let Mercer's research hint at it: "radiant disruption of the necromantic bindings" is in the spellbooks.
- Do NOT let the party skip the Intermission. The fortress upgrade and the transition to Act II need to feel earned.
- Kregg is still out there. He may have been watching. He knows about the Stairwell now. He does not know what to do about it, but he knows.
