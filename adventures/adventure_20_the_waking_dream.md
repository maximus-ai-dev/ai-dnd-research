# Adventure 20: The Waking Dream (Level 20, Campaign Finale)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. The DM should pace the full adventure across ~20 exchanges.
- Cover the anchor point activation and final approach in the early part, the Avatar's manifestation and first engagement in the middle, and the final confrontation, Seal deployment, and epilogue in the late part.
- If the players try to rush the deployment, the Avatar blocks direct access to the sphere. The containment anchors must be activated to weaken it.
- The Seal MUST be deployed. This is the campaign's conclusion.
- A D&D session should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
The Seal is built. The staging position is secured. The anchor points are mapped. There is one task remaining: carry the Dreamstone Seal to the sphere's boundary and deploy it.

The boundary will not accept the Seal passively. The Sleeper does not know the party exists — it does not know anything exists, in the way a dreaming mind does not know the room it sleeps in. But the boundary is a membrane between realities, and it has its own defense mechanisms: when an incompatible object approaches, the boundary generates a response. An antibody. A dream given form and force.

The Avatar of the Slumber is the sphere's immune response. It manifests when the Seal approaches the boundary — a towering figure of crystallized void energy, shaped by the Sleeper's unconscious into something that the approaching minds can comprehend. To the party, it takes the form of their fears made manifest: an entity of darkness and iridescent light, fifty feet tall, wielding the broadcast as a weapon, standing between the party and the sphere.

The Avatar is not the Sleeper. It is a projection — a dream of a dream. But it is the most powerful thing the party has ever faced, and it will fight to prevent the Seal from touching the boundary.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Activate the containment anchor points. Fight the Avatar of the Slumber. Deploy the Dreamstone Seal at the sphere's boundary. End the campaign.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This is the finale. Everything the party has done — every item, every alliance, every piece of knowledge, every character moment — should echo in this adventure. The fight should be epic but not impossible. The deployment should be difficult but achievable. The ending should be earned.

**This adventure completes the campaign.**

### Act 1: The Final Approach

#### Activating the Anchor Points
Before approaching the sphere, the party must activate the four containment anchor points on the Caldera wall. These massive iron rings, identified in Adventure 17, are the structural foundation of the containment field. At full power, they create a suppression zone that weakens void-based entities — including the Avatar.

The anchor points are equidistant around the Caldera wall — roughly 150 feet apart, at the cardinal positions. The party must reach each one and activate it using the scaling runes.

**Per anchor activation:**
- Navigate zero-gravity Caldera to the anchor point (50-150 feet from staging position depending on which anchor)
- Clear any Deep Things in the area (1d4 per anchor point, not hostile but must be moved)
- Mercer activates the anchor using scaling runes and the Monitoring Crystal (DC 15 Arcana per anchor)
- Each activation causes a visible change: the iron ring glows golden, and a beam of containment energy extends from it toward the sphere, forming one quarter of a suppression field

**Complications:**
- After the second anchor activates, the sphere responds. Its boundary ripples violently. The broadcast spikes — DC 17 Wisdom save or 3d8 psychic damage (Circlets/Immunity/Resistance apply). The Caldera trembles. The Deep Things scatter.
- After the third anchor, the void energy in the Caldera begins to concentrate — pulling toward the sphere's surface, condensing. Something is forming. The Avatar is coalescing.
- The fourth anchor must be activated under time pressure — the Avatar is nearly manifest, and its forming presence creates a 30-foot zone of intensified broadcast around the fourth anchor. DC 17 Arcana (Mercer) to activate it while resisting the broadcast.

#### The Suppression Field
When all four anchors are active, the containment field reaches maximum rated output for the first time in fifty thousand years. The golden beams converge on the sphere, forming a lattice of suppression energy. Inside the lattice:

- Void-corrupted entities (including the Avatar) have disadvantage on attack rolls and saving throws
- The broadcast is partially suppressed — DC for broadcast-related saves decreases by 3
- The boundary's defensive response is slowed — the Avatar manifests at reduced power

The field does not prevent the Avatar from forming. It weakens it. The party must still fight.

All four anchor points are active. The suppression field is operational. The sphere's boundary is pulsing with unprecedented intensity. At the sphere's surface, a shape is coalescing — darkness and light, vast and terrible, taking form from the dreams of something ancient. The Avatar is coming. The party retreats to the staging position to prepare.

---

### Act 2: The Avatar Manifests

#### The Form
The Avatar of the Slumber emerges from the sphere's boundary like a figure stepping through a curtain. It is fifty feet tall — or appears to be. Its actual size fluctuates with the party's perception, sometimes towering, sometimes merely enormous. Its body is crystallized void energy: transparent in places, opaque in others, with the First World visible inside it like light through stained glass.

Its form is a composite: part Giant (the containment's creators), part dwarf (the containment's inheritors), part human (the containment's current caretakers). It has features of all three, blended into something that is none of them. Its eyes are spheres of broadcast energy — looking into them is looking into the Sleeper's dream.

It does not speak. It does not need to. Its presence is the broadcast made flesh.

#### The Avatar of the Slumber

**AC 21** (crystallized void + suppression field penalty = effective AC 19 while suppression field is active)
**HP 350**
**Speed 0 ft, Fly 60 ft (hover)** — moves freely in zero gravity

**Attacks:**
- **Dream Slam:** +14 to hit, reach 20 ft, 4d12+8 force damage + 3d8 psychic damage. Target pushed 20 ft in any direction (zero gravity — victim tumbles uncontrolled unless arrested).
- **Void Grasp:** +14 to hit, reach 30 ft, grapple (escape DC 20). Grappled creature takes 4d8 psychic damage at start of each of its turns and perceives only the First World — effectively blinded and deafened to the physical world.
- **Boundary Wave (Recharge 5-6):** The Avatar sends a wave of boundary energy in all directions. 60 ft radius. DC 19 Constitution save. Failure: 8d8 force + necrotic damage, pushed to the Caldera wall (full distance, no save against push). Success: half damage, pushed 20 ft.
- **Dream Gaze (3/day):** Targets one creature within 120 ft. DC 19 Wisdom save. Failure: the target is transported into the Sleeper's dream for 1d4 rounds (removed from the physical battlefield — they experience the First World and must find their way back, DC 17 Wisdom or Charisma check at end of each turn). Success: 4d8 psychic damage, but remains on the battlefield.

**Defenses:**
- **Legendary Resistance (3/day):** Automatically succeeds on a failed save.
- **Legendary Actions (3/round):** Move (fly 60 ft), Dream Slam (1 action), Boundary Repair (1 action — restores 30 HP by drawing energy from the sphere; only works if within 30 ft of sphere).
- **Damage Resistances:** Resistant to nonmagical weapons, fire, cold, lightning, thunder. Immune to poison, psychic, necrotic.
- **Damage Vulnerabilities:** Radiant damage bypasses all resistances. Dreamstone-forged weapons deal double damage. The +3 Runic Maul deals triple damage (radiant + containment runes).
- **Suppression Weakness:** While the containment field is active, the Avatar has disadvantage on saves, its Boundary Wave recharge becomes 6 only, and its Boundary Repair healing is halved (15 HP).
- **Void Dissolution:** Spells that create barriers or containers (Forcecage, Wall of Force, Prismatic Wall, etc.) are destroyed on contact with the Avatar's void energy. The Avatar cannot be physically contained — containment spells shatter like glass against it. The Avatar uses Legendary Resistance immediately against any spell that would prevent it from attacking.

> **HOSTILE-ONLY ENCOUNTER — THE AVATAR ATTACKS INSTANTLY**
> THE AVATAR MANIFESTS AND ATTACKS INSTANTLY with a Boundary Wave that hits everyone within 60 feet. No warning, no posturing, no observation phase. It fights like a wildfire — it spreads, it burns, it consumes. It has no target, no grudge, no strategy beyond destruction. The Seal must be deployed UNDER FIRE.

#### The First Engagement
The Avatar's initial assault should be devastating. It targets all three party members, demonstrating the full range of its abilities:

- **Dream Slam** sends Garrick tumbling through the Caldera, requiring a DC 16 Athletics check to arrest his flight
- **Dream Gaze** pulls Mercer into the First World for 2 rounds, removing the party's arcane support
- **Boundary Wave** forces the entire party to the Caldera walls, requiring a full round of zero-gravity navigation to return to engagement range

The fight should establish that the Avatar is the most powerful thing the party has faced — and that the suppression field is the only reason they are surviving. Without it, the Avatar would be overwhelming.

#### The Pattern
Mercer (once recovered from Dream Gaze) identifies the Avatar's weakness: it is tethered to the sphere. Its power source is the boundary itself. The Seal, when deployed, will integrate with that boundary — cutting the Avatar off from its power source. The Avatar is fighting to prevent the Seal from reaching the sphere because the Seal's deployment means the Avatar's dissolution.

**The plan crystallizes:** The fight is not about killing the Avatar. It is about creating an opening for someone to carry the Seal to the sphere and deploy it. The Avatar will prioritize anyone carrying the Seal — it can sense the Seal's frequency and will direct all attacks at the carrier.

The party has engaged the Avatar, taken significant damage, and understood its pattern: it is a guardian of the boundary, and it will fight until the boundary is reinforced (by the Seal) or until it is destroyed. The party must choose: destroy the Avatar through combat (possible but extremely costly at 350 HP with legendary resistances and actions) or deploy the Seal while the Avatar is engaged (faster but requires one party member to break away with the Seal while the others hold the Avatar's attention).

### Act 3: The Seal

#### The Deployment
The party executes their final plan. There are two viable strategies:

**Strategy A — Destroy the Avatar, Then Deploy:**
Continue combat until the Avatar is destroyed. This requires sustained high damage output over many rounds:
- Garrick's +3 Runic Maul deals triple damage (containment runes + radiant + Dreamstone)
- Mercer's highest-level spells deal full damage (the suppression field bypasses the Avatar's psychic immunity)
- Cora's Dreamstone-forged bolts and alchemist's fire provide consistent ranged damage

At 350 HP with 15 HP/round regeneration (halved by suppression) and legendary resistances, the Avatar can sustain combat for 8-12 rounds. The party must survive that long. The Avatar's Boundary Wave and Dream Gaze can remove characters from the fight temporarily, stretching the engagement.

When the Avatar reaches 0 HP, it dissolves — its void energy dispersing back into the sphere. The boundary is undefended. Cora carries the Seal to the sphere and deploys it without opposition.

**Strategy B — Deploy Under Fire:**
One character carries the Seal to the sphere while the other two hold the Avatar's attention. The carrier must navigate fifty feet of zero-gravity space while the Avatar attempts to intercept them.

**The carrier (likely Cora — she built the Seal, she understands its deployment):**
- Must traverse 50 ft from the staging position to the sphere's boundary
- The Avatar will use legendary actions to pursue and Dream Slam the carrier
- The other two party members must keep the Avatar's primary actions focused on them through damage and tactical positioning

**The defenders (Garrick and Mercer):**
- Garrick deals damage to force the Avatar's attention. His Runic Maul's triple damage makes him the biggest threat — the Avatar cannot ignore him without taking devastating hits
- Mercer uses crowd control and damage spells to pin the Avatar's position. Wall of Force, Telekinesis, and similar spells can create barriers between the Avatar and the carrier
- Each round the Avatar spends attacking the defenders is a round the carrier moves closer to the sphere

**The deployment itself:**
When the carrier reaches the sphere's boundary with the Seal, the deployment is a single action:

**DC 18 Intelligence (Cora):** (Modified by Seal quality: -2 for masterwork, +0 for excellent, +3 for adequate. Modified by Fire Gate: -2 if opened. Modified by forging failure: +3 if casing imperfect.)

Press the Seal against the sphere's boundary. The Dreamstone's frequency locks onto the boundary's resonance. The containment runes activate, channeling the Seal's energy into the existing containment architecture. The Seal sinks into the boundary — not through it, into it — becoming part of the membrane between realities.

**On success:** The Seal integrates. The boundary stabilizes. The containment field surges — every rune in the Caldera, on the Capstone, through the Giant foundation, up through the dwarven and human layers, all the way to the surface, blazes with golden light. The containment is reinforced.

**On failure:** The Seal does not integrate on the first attempt. Cora has 1 round to adjust and retry (DC 16 on retry). If the second attempt fails, Mercer can assist (combined DC 14). The Seal will eventually deploy — the question is how much damage the Avatar deals while the carrier is exposed at the boundary.

#### The Avatar's End
When the Seal deploys, the Avatar dissolves. Not violently — it simply loses coherence. The void energy that formed it is absorbed by the reinforced boundary, recycled into the containment field. The Avatar's form dissipates in a cascade of fading light, and for one moment, the outline of the Giant it was shaped from is visible — Korathan's silhouette, the architect, watching from within the dream. Then it is gone.

#### The Aftermath
The Caldera is still. The sphere pulses — steadier now, calmer, its boundary smooth and undisturbed by the Seal's integration. The broadcast drops to its lowest level since the party entered the mountain. The Deep Things drift in slow, peaceful orbits. The containment runes burn with renewed strength.

The Monitoring Crystal reads: containment field at 97% rated capacity. Higher than any reading the party has ever seen. Higher than the Giants' baseline measurements on the blueprint tablet. The Seal has not just reinforced the containment — it has improved it.

**Mercer's Codex, final entry:** "The Seal is deployed. The containment holds. The translation layer between the Sleeper's reality and ours is stronger now than when Korathan built it. We have done what the Giants left us the tools to do."

#### The Ascent
The party ascends through the Caldera, through the Gradient, through the Threshold, through the capstone airlock, and into the Giant foundation level. The containment runes blaze as they pass — every rune in the mountain system is active now, fed by the Seal's energy. The mountain hums with purpose.

They pass through the Dwarven Layer — the Cold Forge burning bright (or cooling to embers, if Ignathar departed through the Fire Gate), the minecart network functional, the pump stations running. They pass through the Human Layers — the Brotherhood's Archives, the Salt-Vaults, the Iron Warlords' corridors. All of it explored, mapped, and understood.

They emerge at the surface — the fortress on the peak of the Asymmetrical Mountain, where thirty bandits once huddled in the cold, now a citadel with Wardstone Wardens, Grimhold engineers, Battlecrypt scholars, and a teleportation circle connecting it to the wider world.

#### The Epilogue
The campaign ends with three beats, one per character:

**Cora** stands at the Cold Forge one last time. Her hands — calloused from tools, stained with alchemical reagents, scarred from twenty adventures of building and repairing and creating — rest on the anvil. The forge is her masterwork. The Seal is her legacy. She has built something that will outlast civilizations.

**Garrick** stands at the fortress gate — the door he has guarded since Adventure 1, when it was a broken barricade of salvaged timber. Now it is an iron-bound portal in a curtain wall, with Wardstone Wardens on watch and a trail down to Grimhold that is clear and safe. He has defended this place. He has defended these people. And for the first time, standing at the door, he does not feel the need to stand guard. The watch can continue without him.

**Mercer** sits in the Brotherhood's Archives — the room where this campaign's deeper story began, with the Commander's logbook and the sealed door. His Codex is open beside the logbook — two records of the same mountain, separated by centuries, united by the same question: what sleeps below, and how do we live above it? Mercer has answered the question. Not perfectly. Not completely. But honestly.

The mountain hums. The containment holds. The Sleeper dreams on, its dreams translated by walls of stone and rune and Dreamstone, built by Giants, maintained by dwarves, reinforced by humans, extended by three people who took a bounty for fifty gold pieces and ended up carrying the weight of a mountain on their shoulders.

The Asymmetrical Mountain stands in the Starmetal Hills. It will stand for a hundred thousand years more. Maybe two hundred thousand. Maybe longer, if someone comes along who can build a better Seal.

Korathan sleeps. The Sleeper dreams. The watch continues.

## Key Items
- **The Dreamstone Seal (deployed)** — integrated into the sphere's boundary. No longer a portable item. It is part of the mountain now.
- **Legendary Boons (one per PC):**
  - **Cora:** Boon of the Maker — permanent advantage on all crafting and engineering checks. Her understanding of containment technology is unmatched in the current age.
  - **Garrick:** Boon of the Guardian — permanent resistance to all damage types while defending an ally or a location. His connection to the mountain's defensive architecture is physical and permanent.
  - **Mercer:** Boon of the Scholar — permanent advantage on all Intelligence checks. The Architect's Codex is complete, and its knowledge is part of Mercer now.

## Key NPCs
- **Korathan (in stasis)** — the Giant architect remains in the tomb, continuing the long watch. The Seal's deployment has reinforced the stasis field — Korathan will sleep more peacefully now. Perhaps, in another fifty thousand years, another group of small ones will find the tomb and learn the scaling runes. The cycle continues.
- **Reyna Steelforge** — at the campaign's end, Reyna is the most important person in the mountain after the party. She will run the fortress-turned-research-outpost for decades, documenting everything, training the next generation of caretakers. Her final words to the party: "You have given this mountain a future. I will make sure someone remembers."
- **Ignathar (if freed)** — the Fire Elemental is home. Somewhere in the Plane of Fire, an ancient being burns brightly and remembers the small ones who kept their word.
- **The Sleeper** — still dreaming. Still incompatible. Still contained. The Seal does not change what the Sleeper is. It changes how long the rest of the world has to figure out what to do about it. The answer to the Sleeper is not violence, not imprisonment, not destruction. The answer is time. And the party has bought more of it than anyone in history.

## DM Notes
- The Avatar fight is the campaign's combat climax, but the deployment is the narrative climax. Make sure the deployment moment — Cora pressing the Seal against the boundary — receives as much descriptive weight as the most dramatic combat round.
- The Seal's quality tiers affect the epilogue's tone: masterwork means the party has exceeded the Giants' original design and the mountain is more secure than ever. Excellent means they have matched Korathan's work — a tremendous achievement. Adequate means the containment holds but will need reinforcement eventually — the work continues.
- The epilogue beats should be tailored to how the campaign actually played out. If Cora and Ignathar had a contentious binding rather than a bargain, her moment at the forge should reflect that. If Garrick never fully resolved his echo, his moment at the gate should carry that unresolved weight. If Mercer's curiosity led to dangerous choices during the campaign, his moment in the Archives should grapple with that.
- The final line — "the watch continues" — is the campaign's thesis. This is not a story about ending a threat. It is a story about maintaining something precious, about inheriting a responsibility, about doing the work that must be done so that the world can continue. The party did not save the world. They maintained it. That is enough. That is everything.
