# Adventure 6: The Leaking Crypt (Level 6)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. Pace the full adventure within ~20 exchanges.
- Cover all three acts in this session. Do not compress excessively — each act should receive meaningful screen time — but do not stretch or pad either.
- The early part of the adventure should focus on the transition into the Dwarven Layer and the discovery of the lead-sealed mausoleum.
- The middle part should cover the mausoleum interior, the first direct encounter with corruption, and the discovery that something is leaking upward from below.
- The late part should be the containment crisis — tracing the leak to its source, fighting corrupted imperial dead, and sealing the breach before the corruption spreads to the human layers above.
- NEVER let the party descend below the upper Dwarven Layer this adventure. The deeper Delve is gated behind the Cold Forge (Adventure 7).
- A D&D session should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
The Commander's Letter was clear: the Sealed Crypt contained corruption, and the Military Empire chose to ignore it. Now the party knows better. They have the Giant Blueprint Tablet showing the mountain's full structure, and they know the Dwarven Layer sits between the human strata and the Giant foundation. If the Crypt's corruption is leaking, it is leaking downward — into the dwarves' domain.

Reyna Steelforge's Grimhold contacts have brought disturbing news: miners working the Starmetal Hills have reported an unusual metallic sweetness on the wind, the same scent the party encountered near the Sealed Crypt. It is faint, intermittent, and always from the direction of the mountain. Something below is venting.

The party descends via the Giant stairway discovered in Adventure 5. The upper Dwarven Layer is a transitional zone — dwarven construction layered atop Giant foundations, the ceiling heights shifting from human scale to something larger. Here they find the mausoleum: a lead-sealed burial chamber that the dwarves themselves sealed centuries ago, long before the Military Empire built above them. The lead seals are cracked. The corruption is leaking.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Locate the source of the corruption leak in the upper Dwarven Layer. Contain it. Survive the first direct exposure to the Anomaly's influence.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This is the party's first encounter with the Black Slumber's corruption as a tangible, physical force. It should feel wrong — not just dangerous but fundamentally alien. The corrupted dead are not undead in the traditional sense. They are preserved bodies that have been saturated with Dreamstone radiation until something in them began to move again. They do not think. They echo.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 7.

### Act 1: The Upper Delve

#### The Giant Stairway
The descent from the Hall of the Giants is immediately disorienting. The steps are three feet high — the party must climb down each one rather than walk. The stonework transitions from Military Empire masonry to something older and heavier: dwarven construction, built to last millennia. Iron brackets the size of wagon wheels held torches that have long since burned out. The air changes — cooler, damper, with a mineral sharpness that coats the tongue.

After forty feet of descent, the stairway opens into a landing. The ceiling is fifteen feet high — dwarven scale, not human. A corridor runs east and west, wide enough for three dwarves abreast. The walls are carved with geometric patterns that Mercer recognizes from the commemorative plaque in the Hall of the Conquered Peoples: this is Kazad-Khrom design, the lost dwarven empire that once stretched from the Frostfang Mountains to the Sentinels.

**Garrick's read:** The stonework is superior to anything in the human layers. These corridors were built to withstand siege. But something has damaged them — not age, not combat. The stone itself is discolored in places, darkened as if stained from within.

**Cora's assessment:** The discoloration follows a pattern — it radiates from a point deeper in the corridor, roughly to the east. Whatever stained this stone, it has been spreading slowly outward for a very long time.

#### The Upper Dwarven Corridors
The eastern corridor leads through a series of chambers that were clearly residential — low stone beds carved into alcoves, communal cooking hearths with iron chimney flues running into the rock above, a bathhouse with stone basins connected to a dry pipe network. Everything is sized for dwarves: the party moves through rooms designed for people four feet tall.

The discoloration intensifies as they move east. The darkened stone has a faint iridescence — blues, purples, greens — that catches torchlight in ways stone should not. Mercer identifies this immediately: Dreamstone radiation. Not raw Dreamstone ore, but the ambient effect of prolonged exposure to the Anomaly's emanations. The Giant Blueprint Tablet shows this area as sitting directly above a junction in the containment architecture. If any section of the upper Dwarven Layer would show corruption first, it would be here.

**DC 12 Perception (any character):** The metallic sweetness from the Sealed Crypt is present here too. Stronger. Not coming from above — coming from ahead.

#### The Mausoleum Door
At the end of the eastern corridor, past a collapsed storage chamber and a dry well shaft, the party finds it: a door sealed with lead plates riveted directly into the stone frame. The lead is thick — half an inch — and covers every seam. This was not a hasty seal. Someone took great care to ensure nothing could pass through this door in either direction.

The lead is cracked. Not broken — cracked, along stress fractures that follow the Dreamstone radiation pattern in the surrounding stone. The corruption has been working on this seal for centuries, and it is beginning to fail. Through the widest crack — barely a finger's width — a faint exhalation of air carries that metallic sweetness, concentrated enough to taste.

**Mercer's analysis (DC 14 Arcana):** The lead was specifically chosen because it blocks the Anomaly's radiation. The dwarves understood what they were sealing. This is not a crypt in the religious sense — it is a containment vessel. Something inside was too corrupted to destroy and too dangerous to leave exposed.

**Cora's engineering assessment:** The lead is failing because the radiation from below has been slowly transmuting the metal's crystalline structure. In another century, the seal will be completely compromised. The party has found the source of the smell that reached the Starmetal Hills.

### Act 2: What Lies Preserved

#### Breaching the Seal
The party must decide how to open the mausoleum. The lead seal, while cracked, still blocks the worst of the corruption. Removing it entirely would flood the corridor with concentrated Dreamstone radiation.

**Cora's solution (DC 13 Intelligence with tinker's tools):** Cut a controlled opening in the weakest section of the lead — large enough to enter but small enough to reseal. She can fashion a temporary patch from the lead sheeting itself, folded double. This limits exposure time and gives them an exit strategy.

Inside, the mausoleum is a single chamber roughly thirty feet square and ten feet high. The walls are lined with stone niches, each containing a body. Dwarven dead, laid out in ceremonial armor, hands folded over weapons. There are fourteen of them.

They are perfectly preserved. Not mummified — preserved, as if they died yesterday. Their skin has a faint iridescent sheen, the same blue-purple-green as the corridor stone. Their veins are visible beneath the skin, darkened to black. They are saturated with Dreamstone radiation.

**Critical DM note:** These are not animated. Not yet. They are dead dwarves whose bodies have been so thoroughly permeated by the Anomaly's radiation that normal decomposition has been replaced by a kind of crystalline suspension. They are not the Twisted — the Twisted are alive and corrupted. These are something else: echoes.

#### The First Echo
While Mercer examines the bodies and Cora studies the chamber's construction, Garrick notices something. One of the dwarves — a larger figure in a captain's ceremonial plate — has moved. Not much. Its hand has shifted from its chest to its side. When Garrick points this out, the others cannot confirm or deny it. They were not watching that body.

Then the captain opens its eyes. They are solid black, shot through with iridescent veins.

The captain does not attack. It sits up slowly, turns its head toward the party, and speaks. The words are Dwarvish, but Mercer can parse them: it is repeating a phrase, over and over, without inflection or awareness. A loop. A memory preserved in corrupted tissue, playing back. The phrase translates roughly: *"Seal the lower halls. The dreaming is too loud. Seal the lower halls."*

After thirty seconds of repetition, the captain lies back down and closes its eyes. The echo has ended.

**DC 15 Wisdom save for each party member present:** The Dreamstone radiation in this chamber is concentrated enough to cause mild psychic dissonance. On a failure, the character experiences a brief flash of alien sensation — a feeling of immense weight pressing from below, a sound like breathing through stone, a momentary certainty that they are dreaming and have always been dreaming. The effect passes in seconds. It is disturbing, not damaging. This time.

#### The Source of the Leak
Cora finds it on the chamber floor: a crack in the stone that extends below the mausoleum into the rock beneath. The crack is narrow but deep — it descends at least twenty feet before curving out of sight. The metallic sweetness pours from this crack like breath from a sleeping mouth.

**DC 16 Investigation (Cora):** The crack is not natural. It follows a structural line in the Giant architecture below — a seam in the containment network that has shifted over millennia. The mausoleum was built directly above this seam, probably because the dwarves who sealed these bodies understood that the radiation was strongest here. They concentrated their dead in the worst spot, behind lead, and hoped the seal would hold.

From the crack, occasionally, a sound: low, rhythmic, organic. Not the mechanical sound from the Dwarven Layer entrance. This is different. This is the mountain breathing.

### Act 3: The Containment

#### The Echoes Wake
The Dreamstone radiation in the mausoleum is intensifying. The party's presence — their warmth, their breath, their psychic noise — has agitated the preserved dead. The remaining thirteen bodies begin their echo cycles: sitting up, speaking fragments of Dwarvish, reaching for weapons that are no longer sharp, and lying back down. The cycles are getting shorter and more agitated.

**Combat encounter: The Mausoleum Guard**
The captain rises fully. This time it does not speak. It lifts its ceremonial war-pick (treat as a +1 war pick, 1d8+4 damage) and moves toward the party with mechanical precision. Three other bodies follow — soldiers in corroded chain, wielding rusted axes (1d6+3 damage each).

**The Dwarven Echoes (4 total):**
- **Captain Echo:** AC 16 (ceremonial plate), HP 52, Speed 25 ft. War-pick: +6 to hit, 1d8+4. Radiant vulnerability (double damage). On hit, target makes DC 13 Wisdom save or hears the captain's loop phrase for 1 round (no mechanical effect beyond distraction — disadvantage on next attack roll).
- **3 Soldier Echoes:** AC 14 (corroded chain), HP 28 each, Speed 25 ft. Rusty axe: +5 to hit, 1d6+3. Radiant vulnerability. No special abilities.

**Key combat note:** These are not intelligent undead. They fight in formation because that is what their bodies remember. They cannot be reasoned with, turned, or commanded. They can only be destroyed or contained. If reduced to 0 HP, they collapse and do not rise again — the echo is spent.

**Cora's observation mid-fight:** The echoes are drawing energy from the crack in the floor. As long as the crack is open, more bodies may activate. There are ten remaining.

#### Sealing the Crack
While Garrick and the party hold off the echoes, Cora must seal the crack. This is a multi-step process:

1. **Clear the debris** around the crack to expose its full width (DC 12 Strength or Dexterity)
2. **Apply a temporary sealant** using materials from the mausoleum: melted lead from the door seal, mixed with stone dust (DC 14 Intelligence with tinker's tools or alchemist's supplies)
3. **Compress the seal** by activating a dwarven pressure clamp built into the chamber floor — a mechanism Cora identifies as purpose-built for exactly this situation, though centuries of disuse have seized it (DC 15 Strength from Garrick, or DC 13 if Cora applies lubricant from her kit first)

Each step takes one round. During each round, 1d2 additional soldier echoes activate and join the fight (to a maximum of the 10 remaining).

When the seal is complete, the effect is immediate: the metallic sweetness cuts off. The radiation level in the chamber drops perceptibly. Any echoes still animated lose their energy source — they stumble, slow, and collapse within two rounds. The captain, if still standing, speaks one final phrase before falling: *"The dreaming stops. The dreaming stops."*

#### The Aftermath
The mausoleum is sealed. The crack is plugged — not permanently, but well enough to halt the leak for years. Cora estimates the lead-and-stone seal will hold for at least a decade before the radiation begins to compromise it again. A permanent solution would require Giant-era containment materials, which the party does not have. Yet.

The fourteen dwarven dead lie still. Whatever animating force the radiation provided has been cut off. They will not rise again unless the seal fails.

**Mercer's insight:** The blueprint tablet shows this mausoleum as one of dozens of similar structures throughout the Dwarven Layer. The dwarves did not just bury their dead here — they used lead-sealed crypts as radiation monitors. When the bodies began to move, it meant the containment was failing at that location. This mausoleum is a warning system, and it just fired.

The party returns to the surface with their first Dreamstone fragment — a shard of crystallized radiation that Cora carefully extracted from the crack before sealing it, wrapped in lead sheeting from the door. It pulses with a faint inner light, cold to the touch, and Mercer can feel it humming at the edge of his perception.

## Key Items
- **Dreamstone Fragment** — a shard of raw Dreamstone ore, roughly palm-sized, wrapped in lead. Radiates faint psychic energy. Can be used as a material component for enchantment (Cora's artificer work) or studied for Arcana insights (gives Mercer advantage on checks related to the Anomaly's nature for two adventure cycles). Handle with care — prolonged unshielded contact causes the echo effect in nearby organic matter.
- **Captain's Ceremonial War-Pick** — a +1 war pick of dwarven make. Garrick can use this. The iridescent sheen fades once removed from the mausoleum, but the weapon retains its enchantment. Inscribed with the captain's name in Dwarvish: *Torvan Deepwatch.*
- **Dwarven Radiation Survey Notes** — found in a sealed iron box near the mausoleum entrance. Written in Dwarvish (Mercer can translate slowly). Contains a systematic survey of radiation levels throughout the upper Dwarven Layer, with annotations marking which crypts are "active" and which are "stable." Provides a partial map of the upper Delve and identifies the Cold Forge as a landmark deeper in the layer.
- **300 gp** in salvageable dwarven ceremonial goods (armor fittings, iron torcs, carved stone tokens).

## Key NPCs
- **Torvan Deepwatch (Echo)** — the captain of the mausoleum guard. Not truly an NPC — an echo of one. His repeated phrase and his final words provide the party's first direct evidence that the dwarves experienced the Anomaly as a "dreaming" force. His name appears in the Radiation Survey Notes as the officer responsible for monitoring the upper Delve's containment seals.
- **Warden Aldric** — should be consulted before and after the descent. He knows the Brotherhood feared what lay below the Military Empire layer. After hearing the party's account, he becomes visibly shaken: "The Brotherhood sealed the Archives because the books whispered to us. If the dead are speaking too, the entity's reach has grown."

## DM Notes
- The metallic sweetness is the signature sensory detail of the Black Slumber's influence. Use it consistently — it should become a warning signal the party learns to recognize and dread.
- The echo phenomenon is not undeath. Make this distinction clear through description. Traditional undead are animated by necromantic energy. These bodies are animated by psychic residue — the Anomaly's dreams imprinting on saturated tissue. They replay memories, not malice.
- Cora's engineering skills are central to this adventure. The combat is secondary to the containment problem. Reward players who prioritize sealing the crack over killing echoes.
- The Dreamstone Fragment is the first tangible piece of the Anomaly the party has held. Mercer should be fascinated. Cora should be cautious. Garrick should be uncomfortable. Let their reactions define the tone.
- The Radiation Survey Notes are the bridge to Adventure 7. They identify the Cold Forge as a major dwarven landmark deeper in the layer — and note that its fires went out centuries ago when the fuel source was cut off. Reigniting it is the next step in making the Dwarven Layer operational.
