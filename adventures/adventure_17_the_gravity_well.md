# Adventure 17: The Gravity Well (Level 17)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. The DM should pace the full adventure across ~20 exchanges.
- Cover the descent through the Threshold and Gradient in the early part, the zero-gravity Caldera exploration in the middle, and the Deep Things encounter and forward position establishment in the late part.
- If the players try to rush deeper, the caldera's disorienting physics make rapid movement dangerous — velocity without gravity means uncontrolled flight into unknown territory.
- NEVER let the party reach the Anomaly itself this adventure. The caldera is enormous and the Anomaly is at its center.
- A D&D session should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

---

## Adventure Overview
The capstone is secure. The fortress above is a citadel. The party has the tools, the knowledge, and the allies they need. There is nothing left to do but descend.

The Architect's Codex describes the space below the capstone as the "Caldera" — a roughly cylindrical void carved by the Giants as the innermost layer of the containment vessel. Two hundred feet across and of unknown depth, it is the buffer zone between the capstone above and the Anomaly's core below. The Giants designed it as a dead zone — empty, sealed, and monitored. It has been undisturbed for fifty thousand years.

It is no longer empty.

The descent past the capstone transitions through three zones: the Threshold (where gravity weakens), the Gradient (where gravity rotates and eventually fails), and the Caldera proper (zero gravity, three-dimensional navigation, the Anomaly's raw broadcast filling every direction). The party must learn to navigate a world without up or down, where the only landmarks are containment runes on the walls and the faint, terrible glow at the center of everything.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Descend past the capstone into the Caldera. Navigate the zero-gravity environment. Establish a forward position for Act IV operations.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This adventure is about disorientation and awe. The Caldera is unlike anything the party has experienced — a vast, three-dimensional space where the normal rules do not apply. Movement is swimming, not walking. Direction is arbitrary. The broadcast fills the space like water fills a bowl. The horror is not in what attacks them — it is in the fundamental wrongness of existing in a place where existence was never meant to happen.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 18.

### Act 1: Below the Seal

#### The Controlled Descent
Cora and the Grimhold engineers have built an access point through the repaired capstone — a sealed airlock-style passage with lead-lined walls, designed to prevent void energy from leaking upward when the party enters and exits. The passage descends ten feet through solid capstone before opening into the transition zone.

The Dreamstone Circlets are essential. The Capstone Resonator is charged. The Reality Anchor is in Cora's pack. Garrick's +3 Runic Maul hums with anticipation. Mercer carries the Architect's Codex and Korathan's Chisel replica. They are as prepared as three people can be for what lies below.

**The airlock opens downward.** The first thing the party notices: the air is warm. Not geothermally warm — the warmth has no source. It simply is. The second thing: the light. Not torchlight, not runelight. A faint, diffuse luminescence that comes from everywhere and nowhere, casting no shadows. The third thing: the smell is gone. The metallic sweetness that has followed them since Adventure 5 is absent. In the Caldera, the Anomaly's presence is so total that the radiation is not a contamination — it is the atmosphere.

#### The Threshold
The first fifty feet below the capstone are the Threshold — a transition zone where the Giant containment runes are densest and the physics are mostly normal. Gravity functions but weakens with each step. By the bottom of the Threshold, the party weighs roughly half what they should.

**Observations in the Threshold:**
- The containment runes on the walls are the brightest the party has ever seen — burning with golden light, packed so densely they overlap. This is the containment's primary barrier, the strongest point in the entire system.
- The walls curve. The cylindrical shape of the Caldera is visible here — a gentle curve that makes the corridor feel like the inside of a massive pipe.
- Giant handholds are carved into the walls — footholds and grip points designed for a twenty-foot-tall being descending in weakening gravity. At party scale, they are ledges and platforms, useful for resting and anchoring.

#### The Gradient
Below the Threshold, gravity does not simply weaken — it rotates. The sensation is profoundly disorienting: "down" shifts by ten degrees, then twenty, then forty. The party must transition from walking to climbing to crawling on what was the wall, which is now the floor, which is now the ceiling.

**DC 14 Dexterity save (all characters, first time entering the Gradient):** Failure: the character loses their grip during the gravity rotation and falls sideways — which is now "down" — tumbling ten feet before catching a Giant handhold. 1d6 bludgeoning damage and prone (relative to the new gravity direction).

**Cora's countermeasure:** The Reality Anchor, deployed as a tethering point. All party members rope themselves to the Anchor, which provides a stable gravitational reference within its radius. Within fifteen feet of the Anchor, gravity points toward it regardless of the surrounding distortion. This makes the Gradient navigable — the party moves as a roped team, the Anchor serving as their "down."

#### The Edge of Zero
At the bottom of the Gradient, gravity fails entirely. The party reaches the lip of the Caldera proper — the point where the walls end and the void begins. The transition is stark: one moment they are crawling along a curved wall with the Reality Anchor providing gravity. The next, they are floating.

The Caldera opens before them. It is two hundred feet across — a cylindrical space carved into the bedrock, its walls covered in containment runes that provide the only landmarks. The runes cast golden light into the void, illuminating floating debris: chunks of stone from ancient collapses, fragments of Giant tools left during construction, and — drifting slowly in the windless air — objects that should not exist. Crystallized formations of impossible geometry. Dreamstone growths that spiral in patterns no crystal structure should allow. Small pools of liquid suspended in midair, reflecting light that does not come from the runes.

At the center of the Caldera, far below (a direction the party must choose arbitrarily), the glow. Not the golden light of the containment runes. Something else. Something that pulls at the eye the way gravity should pull at the body. The Anomaly's surface expression. The shadow the Sleeper casts on physical space.

**DC 16 Wisdom save (all characters, first sight of the Caldera):** Failure: awe. Not fear, not madness — awe so intense it paralyzes for 1 round. The character cannot look away from the glow. The Dreamstone Circlets provide advantage. On recovery, the character is shaken but functional. This is not a combat debuff — it is the emotional impact of seeing something truly beyond human comprehension.

The party has descended through the Threshold and Gradient, reached the Caldera's edge, and experienced zero gravity for the first time. They are anchored to the Caldera wall by ropes and the Reality Anchor. They can see the void's full extent. The transition zone is their base of operations — sheltered by the densest containment runes and close enough to the capstone access for emergency retreat.

### Act 2: Swimming in Nothing

#### Zero-Gravity Navigation
Movement in the Caldera requires a complete adjustment to the party's understanding of motion. There is no walking. There is only pushing off surfaces, drifting, and arresting momentum by grabbing containment rune arrays on the walls.

**Navigation mechanics:**
- **Pushing off:** A character can launch from a wall in any direction. Speed: 30 ft per round in the desired direction. No way to change direction mid-flight without pushing off another surface or using a spell.
- **Arresting momentum:** DC 12 Athletics or Dexterity to grab a wall handhold while drifting. Failure: continue drifting past the intended grab point, requiring a longer recovery.
- **Rope work:** Characters roped together can pull each other to positions. Garrick's strength makes him the party's anchor — he can arrest the entire team's drift with a DC 14 Athletics check.
- **Spell movement:** Mercer's spells that produce force (Mage Hand, Telekinesis, Gust) can serve as thrusters. Creative spell use is encouraged.
- **The Reality Anchor:** Creates a 15-foot gravity bubble. Within this bubble, there is "down" — toward the Anchor. The party can carry it, creating a small island of normalcy as they move through the void.

#### Exploring the Caldera Walls
The party traverses the Caldera wall — moving laterally around the cylinder's circumference, studying the containment runes and looking for structural features noted in the Architect's Codex.

**Key discoveries:**

- **The Fire Gate:** A sealed conduit in the Caldera wall, marked with the Giant rune for "fire." This is the planar anchor Ignathar spoke of — a passage that once connected to the Elemental Plane of Fire, used by the Giants during construction and then sealed. If the party promised Ignathar freedom, this is where the promise can be fulfilled. The seal is intact but the scaling runes could open it. Opening it now would be premature — but noting its location is critical. (This can be fulfilled during the return trip after the campaign climax, or during Intermission if the party prioritizes it.)

- **The Observation Platform:** A Giant-era structure built into the Caldera wall — a platform with crystalline viewing panels and monitoring equipment, designed for long-term observation of the Anomaly. The panels still function, providing magnified views of the Caldera's center. Through the panels, the glow resolves into something more specific: a sphere. Not a perfect sphere — slightly asymmetrical, its surface rippling with slow, organic motion. It is roughly fifty feet in diameter. It radiates the broadcast. It is the Sleeper's physical manifestation in normal space — the part of it that exists in this universe.

- **The Anchor Points:** Four massive iron rings set into the Caldera wall at equidistant points around the circumference. The Architect's Codex identifies these as structural anchors — the points where the containment field concentrates. If the party activates all four using the scaling runes, the containment field strengthens to its maximum rated output. This will be critical for the final confrontation.

#### The Broadcast Unshielded
At the Caldera wall, the Dreamstone Circlets provide adequate protection. But the center of the Caldera is different. The broadcast intensifies exponentially as one approaches the Anomaly's sphere. At fifty feet from the wall, the Circlets strain. At a hundred feet (halfway to center), they barely hold. At the center, nothing short of the Capstone Resonator's pulse would provide temporary protection.

**DC 17 Wisdom save (any character who moves more than 50 feet from the Caldera wall):** Failure: the broadcast overwhelms the Circlet. The character experiences a full sensory replacement — for 1d4 rounds, they do not perceive the Caldera. Instead, they perceive the First World: a reality of liquid stone and singing crystal, where time moves in spirals and consciousness is a shared medium. The experience is transcendent and terrifying. When it ends, the character is disoriented (disadvantage on all checks for 1 minute) and has gained a fragment of alien knowledge — a single piece of information about the Sleeper's nature or the containment's design (DM's choice, should be genuinely useful).

The party has explored the Caldera wall, located the Fire Gate, the Observation Platform, and the Anchor Points, and tested the broadcast intensity at distance. They have a functional understanding of zero-gravity navigation and the Caldera's layout. They have not approached the sphere or encountered the deep things. They are anchored to the Observation Platform, which serves as their operating base.

### Act 3: The Deep Things

#### First Contact
The party has been in the Caldera for hours. The floating debris has been drifting slowly around them — inert, harmless. Then Garrick notices that the debris has rearranged itself. Chunks of stone that were scattered randomly are now grouped. The crystalline formations have rotated to face the party's position. The suspended liquid pools have merged into larger masses and begun to pulse.

Something is aware of them.

**The Deep Things** are not creatures in any conventional sense. They are expressions of the Sleeper's dreaming — fragments of its consciousness given temporary form by the void energy in the Caldera. They manifest as geometric shapes: floating polyhedra of crystallized void, each roughly three feet across, with surfaces that shift between transparent and opaque. They are beautiful. They are wrong.

**Deep Thing Manifestation (6-8 individuals):**
- AC 15 (crystalline), HP 35 each
- Speed 0 ft, Fly 40 ft (hover, zero gravity native — they move freely in any direction)
- **Void Touch:** +8 to hit, reach 5 ft, 2d8+4 force damage + DC 15 Wisdom save or 2d6 psychic damage
- **Broadcast Echo (1/day each):** The Deep Thing replicates the Sleeper's broadcast in a 15 ft radius. DC 16 Wisdom save or hallucination for 1d4 rounds (as Adventure 12 hallucination table). Dreamstone Circlets provide advantage.
- **Spatial Distortion:** As a reaction to being attacked, shifts position by 10 ft in any direction. DC 16 Dexterity to track and maintain aim.
- **Resistances:** Resistant to fire, cold, lightning, nonmagical weapons. Immune to poison, psychic, necrotic.
- **Vulnerabilities:** Radiant damage. The Capstone Resonator's pulse destroys all Deep Things within its radius instantly.

**Combat in zero gravity:** The fight is three-dimensional. Deep Things approach from every direction — above, below, behind. The party's weapons work, but connecting with floating targets while floating yourself requires adjustment. Melee attacks have disadvantage unless the attacker is anchored to a surface. Ranged attacks are unaffected. Spells function normally.

The Deep Things do not fight tactically. They approach, touch, observe, and withdraw. They are more curious than aggressive — testing the party's psychic resilience, probing their minds through Void Touch, echoing the broadcast at close range. If repelled (half or more destroyed), the survivors drift back toward the sphere, losing form as they go, dissolving back into the ambient void energy.

#### The Message
During or after the Deep Things encounter, Mercer receives another broadcast fragment — clearer than anything before, transmitted through the lingering psychic contact of the Void Touch:

The Sleeper is not trying to break free. It cannot break free — it does not understand the concept of "free" versus "contained." It simply exists, dreaming, and its dreams are incompatible with the physical universe. The containment does not cage the Sleeper. The containment translates. It converts the Sleeper's reality-warping emissions into manageable frequencies — the Dreamstone radiation, the broadcast, the gravity distortions. Without the containment, the Sleeper's raw presence would rewrite the mountain. Not intentionally. Automatically. The way the sun does not intend to burn.

This changes the campaign's endgame. The party is not here to kill the Sleeper or imprison it more tightly. They are here to repair and strengthen the translation layer — the containment — so it lasts another hundred thousand years. The Dreamstone Seal is not a weapon. It is an upgrade to the translator.

#### Establishing the Forward Position
The party secures the Observation Platform as their Act IV operating base:

- Anchored to the wall with ropes and the Reality Anchor
- Protected by the densest containment runes on the Caldera wall
- Equipped with the observation panels for monitoring the sphere
- Connected to the capstone access via the Gradient and Threshold
- Dreamstone supplies and the Capstone Resonator stored in lead-lined cases

**The closing beat:** The party rests on the Observation Platform. Through the crystalline panels, they watch the sphere pulse. The Deep Things drift in slow orbits around it, formless now, their curiosity satisfied. The broadcast fills the Caldera like music from an orchestra too large to see.

Cora checks the Monitoring Crystal. The containment readings are stable. The capstone above holds. The runes burn. Everything is in order.

Mercer looks at the sphere and writes in the Codex: "It is not what I expected. It is not a monster. It is a dreamer, and its dreams are too large for the room it sleeps in. We are not here to wake it. We are here to make the room larger."

Garrick says nothing. He watches the deep things drift. He is thinking about the room he sleeps in, and what dreams he might have that are too large for it.

## Key Items
- **Caldera Navigation Charts** — Mercer's maps of the zero-gravity Caldera, including the anchor points, Fire Gate, Observation Platform, and the sphere's approximate dimensions and position. Essential for navigation in Adventures 18-20.
- **Deep Thing Fragment** — a crystalline shard from a destroyed Deep Thing. It retains a faint connection to the broadcast, functioning as a compass that always points toward the Anomaly's sphere. Also functions as a +2 arcane focus for spells related to psychic effects or planar phenomena.
- **Observation Panel Data** — detailed measurements of the sphere's dimensions, pulsation frequency, and energy output. Mercer uses this to refine the Dreamstone Seal's specifications in Adventure 19.
- **Fire Gate Location** — the sealed conduit's exact position on the Caldera wall. If Ignathar's bargain must be honored, this is where it happens. Opening the Fire Gate would also provide a source of elemental fire for the Seal's forging process in Adventure 19.
- **1,500 gp** equivalent in crystalline formations, void-touched minerals, and salvaged Giant-era observation equipment.

## Key NPCs
- **The Deep Things** — the Sleeper's unconscious expressions. They are not enemies. They are not allies. They are phenomena — like weather, like tides. They will appear throughout Act IV as the party operates in the Caldera, sometimes curious, sometimes indifferent, occasionally dangerous if the party disrupts the broadcast's equilibrium.
- **Ignathar (Fire Elemental)** — if the party locates the Fire Gate and reports back, Ignathar's response depends on the relationship: if bargained, it burns brighter with hope — "The gate. You found it. Remember your oath." If bound, it strains against the binding with renewed desperation. The Fire Gate is real. The promise can be kept.
- **Korathan (in stasis)** — the Giant architect's tomb is above the Caldera, separated by the capstone. Mercer reflects that Korathan chose the long watch knowing exactly what lay below. The dedication is even more humbling now that the party has seen the void firsthand.

## DM Notes
- Zero gravity is the defining mechanic of Act IV. Every combat, every exploration, every moment of rest must contend with the absence of "down." Describe the floating, the drifting, the constant small corrections needed to stay in position. Make it feel alien and eventually, grudgingly, navigable.
- The Deep Things are not combat encounters — they are environmental features. Their curiosity is genuine. Their danger is incidental. They do not want to hurt the party. They do not want anything. They exist because the Sleeper dreams, and sometimes the dreams have shapes.
- Mercer's revelation about the containment as a "translator" is the campaign's core thematic insight. The Sleeper is not evil. The containment is not a prison. It is a compatibility layer between two versions of reality. This reframe should make the party's mission feel noble rather than martial — they are engineers, not soldiers.
- The Fire Gate is a significant story beat. If the party bargained with Ignathar, they now have the means to fulfill their promise — but opening a portal to the Plane of Fire inside the Anomaly's containment vessel is not without risk. The decision to open it (or not) should be weighed carefully. It can provide critical resources for the Seal's forging but could also disrupt the containment.
- The closing beat's personal reflections matter. Each character should have a moment of quiet contemplation. They are inside the deepest, oldest structure in the mountain, looking at something that predates their entire civilization. The scale should be humbling, not terrifying.
