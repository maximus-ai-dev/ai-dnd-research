# Adventure 2: The Salt-Vaults Scavenge (Level 2)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure with approximately 20 exchanges. The DM should pace the full adventure within this one session, covering all three acts.
- The adventure has three acts. Give each act enough breathing room — do not rush through Act 1 to get to Act 3. Each act should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- If the players try to rush ahead, introduce complications: unstable floors, locked doors, pest ambushes, or structural hazards that slow them down naturally.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.
- **CRITICAL:** The enemies in this adventure are MUTATED GIANT RATS. They are animals — not intelligent, not psychic, not part of a hive mind. They do not communicate, they do not use tools, they do not have culture. They are just rats that have been exposed to residual magical radiation for too many generations. Do NOT invent intelligent creatures, salt-adapted bipeds, psychic fungi, awakening stages, countdown timers, or any entity not described in this file.

---

## Adventure Overview
With the peak secured and the Emberfell Rejects recruited as a workforce, the party faces an immediate problem: winter is here, and they have 15+ mouths to feed. The fortress has water from the deep well, but no food stores. Reyna Steelforge mentions that old prospector maps reference massive dry-storage pantries somewhere below the surface ruins — the Salt-Vaults, built by the Iron Warlords centuries ago to sustain a garrison of hundreds.

The problem: the vaults are infested with mutated pests — creatures warped by centuries of proximity to the deeper anomaly. The party must descend into the Iron Warlords' layer, navigate brutal pragmatic architecture designed for defense, clear the infestation, and secure a sustainable food supply.

**Adventure Goal:** Clear the Salt-Vaults of mutated pests and secure a food supply for the fortress workforce.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This is a practical, survival-focused adventure. The party is solving a logistics problem — food — by clearing out a pest infestation. It should feel like a dungeon crawl with a purpose, not a cosmic horror reveal. The rats are JUST RATS. The dungeon is JUST a dungeon. Keep it grounded. The wonder comes from discovering the Iron Warlords' architecture — the sheer scale and pragmatism of a civilization that designed everything for siege.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 3.

---

### Act 1: The Descent

#### The Problem
Winter has settled over the Starmetal Hills. The fortress is shelter, but food is running out. The recruited bandits — now the party's workforce — are getting restless. Brenna (the scarred bandit spokesperson) reports that rationing will last maybe ten days. After that, people start dying or deserting.

**Play this urgency.** Brenna is not panicking — she is stating facts, the way a logistics person states facts. "Ten days. Maybe twelve if we cut portions again. After that, I can't keep them here." Cora understands this language immediately.

Rolf, who has been exploring the fortress perimeter, discovered a partially collapsed stairwell behind the main hall. Unlike the boarded-over passages that exhale foul drafts from the deep west, this one leads east and down — into what appears to be worked stone corridors of much older, more deliberate construction. The air from below smells of dust and old stone, not the metallic corruption from the temporal stasis field.

#### The Iron Warlords' Layer
The stairwell descends about sixty feet through rubble-choked passages before opening into a distinctly different architectural style. Where the surface ruins are crude timber and stolen materials, this layer is brutal, functional stone — thick walls, low ceilings, narrow corridors designed for defense. Every intersection is a potential chokepoint. Arrow slits line the walls. Murder holes dot the ceilings.

**DM atmosphere guidance:** The Iron Warlords built for war. Everything here is purposeful and hostile to intruders. The ceilings are low enough that Garrick has to duck. The corridors are narrow enough that two people cannot walk abreast. The arrow slits are angled to provide overlapping fields of fire. This architecture says one thing: "We expected to be attacked, and we planned for it." Let the party feel the weight of a civilization that lived in permanent expectation of siege.

**Key details for the DM:**
- The Iron Warlords were a militaristic human civilization that built for war and siege. Everything is designed to funnel attackers into kill zones.
- The architecture is well-preserved but covered in centuries of dust. Nothing has lived here for a very long time — until recently.
- Faint scratching sounds echo from deeper in. Something is moving down here.
- Occasional patches of a pale, fibrous growth cling to walls near moisture — a fungal bloom fed by seepage from above. It's not dangerous, but it's unsettling. **This is just fungus. It does not think, communicate, or form a hive mind. It is mold.**

#### Exploration
The corridors branch in multiple directions. The party can find:
- **A guardroom** with rusted weapon racks and a collapsed wooden table. A faded map on the wall shows the layout — corridors, barracks, and a large area labeled in old Common as "The Salting Halls." This gives the party a destination. **Mercer should spend time studying this map.** He is fascinated by the Iron Warlords' organizational methodology. This is a culture that labeled everything.
- **A barracks** with rows of stone sleeping platforms. Personal effects are long decayed. One platform has claw marks gouged into the stone — deep, recent scratches. **Garrick notices these first.** He knows claw marks. These are from something the size of a large dog, and they are fresh.
- **A sealed armory** behind a heavy iron door (DC 15 Strength to force, or DC 13 Thieves' Tools). Contains rusted-beyond-use weapons but also 4 intact iron shields, a case of 20 crossbow bolts, and a sealed cask of military-grade lamp oil (valuable for lighting the dark corridors ahead). **Cora inventories everything.** This is salvage, and salvage has value.

**Do NOT let the party rush through these rooms.** Each discovery builds the world. The guardroom map, the claw marks, the armory — these are the beats of the early adventure.

#### Signs of Infestation
As the party explores deeper:
- Scratching sounds grow louder and more frequent
- They find gnawed bones — old bones, from whatever creatures lived here centuries ago, but with fresh tooth marks
- A corridor is partially blocked by a mass of shredded material — old fabric, fungus, and what looks like chewed stone — forming a crude nest barrier
- DC 12 Perception: glowing eyes watching from a crack in the wall, then gone

#### The First Encounter
A scouting party of **3 Giant Rats** (CR 1/8 each) attacks from a side corridor. These aren't normal giant rats — their fur has a faint iridescent sheen, their eyes glow dimly, and they're more aggressive than typical specimens. They fight to the death rather than flee, which is unnatural behavior for rats.

After the fight, Mercer can examine the bodies (DC 12 Arcana or Nature): the rats show signs of long-term exposure to low-level magical radiation. They're not corrupted like the Twisted — they're just animals that have been living too close to something magical for too many generations. The mutation is cosmetic and behavioral, not dangerous to handle.

**IMPORTANT: This is the ONLY type of creature in this adventure. Giant Rats. Mutated, yes. Intelligent, NO. There are no other species, no intelligent creatures, no psychic entities. Just rats.**

#### Act 1 Transition
The party has descended into the Iron Warlords' layer, found the map showing the Salt-Vaults' location, encountered the first mutated pests, and realized the infestation is more extensive than expected. They have NOT reached the Salt-Vaults yet.

**The emotional beat:** The party regroups at the guardroom, the faded map spread on the collapsed table. The scratching from deeper in the corridors hasn't stopped — if anything, it's louder. Cora is calculating: how much food is down there versus how many rats they'll have to kill to get it. Garrick is cleaning rat blood off his maul. Mercer is sketching the architecture in his journal and muttering about military-grade preservation engineering. Tomorrow, they go deeper.

### Act 2: The Salting Halls

#### Reaching the Vaults
Following the guardroom map, the party navigates deeper through the Iron Warlords' corridors. The architecture becomes increasingly focused on logistics — wider corridors for cart traffic, loading bays, and massive iron-banded doors designed to seal sections in case of siege.

The approach to the Salt-Vaults passes through:
- **A preparation kitchen** — massive stone counters, rusted meat hooks hanging from the ceiling, a drainage channel in the floor. The hooks sway slightly in a draft. Something knocked into them recently. **Describe this in detail** — the scale of industrial food preparation for a garrison. Stone basins large enough to butcher a whole deer. Channels cut into the floor to drain fluids into a waste sluice below. Cora recognizes efficient design when she sees it.
- **A loading corridor** — wide enough for two carts abreast. Deep grooves worn into the stone floor from centuries of heavy traffic. The pest droppings here are thick and fresh. **This is the transition point** — from abandoned architecture to active infestation. The smell changes here: from dust to ammonia and rot.

#### The Salt-Vaults
The Salting Halls are enormous — a series of connected chambers, each the size of a great hall, with stone shelving carved directly into the walls from floor to ceiling. The shelves once held preserved foods: salted meats, dried grains, sealed crocks of pickled vegetables. The Iron Warlords designed this to feed an army through a years-long siege.

**Give the party time to absorb the scale.** Mercer should be overwhelmed. Cora should be doing mental arithmetic. Garrick should be watching the shadows.

**What remains:**
- Most organic food is long decomposed, but the preservation methods were excellent. Some sealed stone crocks still contain viable pickled goods — sour and ancient, but edible. Enough to supplement hunting for weeks.
- Large quantities of rock salt remain in carved storage bins — incredibly valuable for preserving fresh game and fish.
- Dried grain stores are mostly consumed by the pests, but sealed stone silos at the back still hold significant quantities of ancient grain — stale but nutritious if ground and cooked.
- The total haul, if cleared and organized, could sustain 20 people through winter and beyond.

**The infestation:**
The Salt-Vaults are home to a colony of mutated Giant Rats — perhaps 30-40 total, led by an alpha. They've nested in the deepest chamber, using the remaining food stores as their primary food source. The rats are territorial and will defend the vaults aggressively. **They are ANIMALS defending a food source. They are not organized, they do not have leaders (except the instinctual alpha), and they do not think strategically.** They swarm threats and flee when overwhelmed.

#### Scouting the Nest
The party can observe the main nest from a vantage point (a collapsed shelf creating a natural overlook):
- **20+ Giant Rats** visible in the deepest chamber, clustered around a massive nest built from shredded materials
- The nest is centered on a large stone silo — the biggest remaining food store
- At the center, a much larger creature: the **Rat King** — a bloated, iridescent-furred giant rat the size of a large dog, with multiple tails fused together. It doesn't move much, letting the colony bring food to it.
- Three tunnel entrances lead into the nest chamber from different directions

**Mercer's observation:** "It's a colonial organism — the large one is the reproductive center. Remove it and the colony collapses. Standard pest biology, just... larger." He is right. This is zoology, not cosmic horror.

#### Planning the Assault
The party should plan before attacking. Options include:
- **Smoke them out** using the lamp oil from the armory and the ventilation channels visible in the ceiling
- **Bottleneck strategy** — seal two of the three entrances and funnel the rats through one
- **Lure and ambush** — use food to draw groups away from the nest and fight them in smaller numbers
- **Direct assault** — charge in and fight the whole colony (extremely dangerous for level 2)

#### Combat: Rat Patrol
If the party engages smaller groups before the main assault, run 2-3 encounters with **4-6 Giant Rats** each (CR 1/8). These are manageable fights that drain resources before the boss encounter.

**Rat behavior:**
- They swarm the nearest target
- They flee toward the nest if reduced to half their number
- Fleeing rats alert the colony, making the next group larger and more prepared

#### Act 2 Transition
The party has reached the Salt-Vaults, assessed the scale of the infestation, scouted the main nest, and formed a plan. They may have fought preliminary rat encounters. The Rat King and the main nest assault come next.

**The emotional beat:** The party retreats to the preparation kitchen to rest and finalize their plan. Through the walls, they can hear the colony — thousands of tiny claws on stone, the occasional squeal, the low bulk of the Rat King shifting in its nest. Tomorrow they go in. Cora has drawn up a supply manifest of what they'll recover — if they survive. Garrick says nothing. He's fought worse, but never this many. Mercer is writing an academic paper in his head: *Observations on Magically-Mutated Rodent Colonial Behavior in Siege Architecture.*

### Act 3: The Extermination

#### The Assault
However the party approaches the nest, this is the climax of Adventure 2. The full colony defends their territory:

**Wave 1: The Outer Guard**
- **8 Giant Rats** (CR 1/8 each) — the first line of defense. They attack as soon as the party enters the nest chamber.

**Wave 2: The Inner Swarm**
- **2 Rat Swarms** (CR 1/4 each, use Swarm of Rats stats) — a writhing mass of smaller mutated rats that pour out of cracks in the walls when combat noise reaches them. These are the real danger for a level 2 party — resistant to weapon damage and dealing automatic damage to anyone in their space.

**Wave 3: The Rat King**
- **The Rat King** — Use Giant Rat stats with the following modifications:
  - HP: 25 (instead of 7)
  - AC: 13 (matted, hardened fur)
  - Multiattack: Two bite attacks per turn
  - Aura of Command (Recharge 5-6): All Giant Rats within 30 feet that can see the Rat King can use their reaction to make one bite attack
  - Iridescent Shriek (1/day): The Rat King emits a piercing shriek. All creatures within 15 feet must make a DC 12 Constitution save or be stunned until the end of their next turn.
  - When the Rat King dies, all remaining rats flee immediately

**Tactical notes for the DM:**
- If the party used a smart strategy (smoke, bottleneck, lure), reduce Wave 1 to 4 rats and skip one Rat Swarm
- The nest chamber has environmental features: collapsible shelving (DC 13 Strength to topple onto rats, dealing 2d6 bludgeoning in a 10-foot line), the stone silo (provides full cover), and ventilation shafts (small creatures can flee through these)
- The Rat King fights from atop the silo, forcing melee characters to climb (DC 10 Athletics) to reach it

**Combat pacing:** This fight should feel like a desperate extermination, not a heroic battle. The rats are vermin. They die easily but there are SO MANY of them. The danger is attrition — being nibbled to death by forty small bites. Cora's alchemist fire and Mercer's area spells are critical. Garrick is the wall that keeps them from being overwhelmed.

#### After the Fight
With the Rat King dead, the remaining rats scatter into the deeper corridors and are no longer a threat to the vaults. The party can now assess their haul:

**Secured resources:**
- Enough preserved food (sealed crocks, grain stores, rock salt for preservation) to sustain the fortress workforce through winter
- The Salt-Vaults themselves — a massive, defensible storage area that can be organized and restocked
- A clear path from the surface fortress to the Iron Warlords' layer, opening up future exploration

#### The Workforce Reacts
When the party returns to the surface with news:
- Brenna organizes work parties to begin hauling food stores up to the fortress
- Rolf offers a grudging "not bad" — the highest praise he gives
- The workforce's morale visibly improves. They have food, shelter, and purpose. The fortress starts to feel like a home rather than a squat.
- Tomas, the youngest bandit, asks if there's more to explore down there. The answer, of course, is yes — much more.

**Give this moment weight.** The party just saved fifteen people from starving. Cora should feel the satisfaction of a solved logistics problem. Garrick should feel something he hasn't felt in a long time — belonging. Mercer should be planning the next expedition before they've even finished hauling the food.

#### Rewards
- Approximately 100 gp value in salvageable Iron Warlord military equipment (the intact shields, crossbow bolts, and other finds)
- The Salt-Vaults secured as a food supply and storage area
- A functional path between the surface fortress and the Iron Warlords' layer
- Workforce morale boost — the bandits are now genuinely invested in the fortress's success
- The Rat King's nest contains a small cache of shiny objects the rats collected: 35 gp in old coins, a silver ring worth 25 gp, and a **Cloak of Many Fashions** (common magic item — it can change its style, color, and apparent quality at will, though it provides no mechanical benefit beyond looking good)

#### Adventure Ends When
The party has cleared the Salt-Vaults, secured the food supply, and returned to the fortress with resources. Adventure 2 is complete. The party advances to Level 3.

**The emotional beat at session's end:** The first real meal in the fortress courtyard. Not fancy — rehydrated ancient grain porridge with pickled vegetables older than anyone's grandparents — but hot, and enough for everyone. Brenna sits with the party. Rolf stands at the wall, watching the Starmetal Hills in the last light. Below them, in corridors they have only begun to explore, the Iron Warlords' architecture extends deeper than they know. But tonight, they eat. Tonight, the mountain is home.

## Key NPCs
- **Brenna** — workforce leader. Practical, direct, increasingly trusted. She is not a damsel — she is a logistics manager.
- **Rolf** — the quiet one. Competent, stoic, observant. He notices things other people miss.
- **Tomas** — the youngest. Curious, eager, probably going to get himself killed someday by exploring somewhere he shouldn't.

## DM Notes
- This adventure is about SURVIVAL and LOGISTICS. The party is solving a food problem. Keep it grounded.
- The Iron Warlords' architecture is the atmosphere — not magic, not horror, just the impressive remains of a civilization that built to last. Let Mercer geek out about it.
- The rats are JUST RATS. Bigger and meaner, but rats. They do not speak, plan, communicate telepathically, or serve a higher power. They eat, they breed, they bite. That is all.
- Do NOT introduce any elements from other adventures. No Wardens, no Brotherhood, no Amalgamation, no Psychic Guardian, no Sleeper. The dungeon at this point is just the Iron Warlords' layer — empty corridors, empty barracks, rats.
- Cora's practicality is the engine of this adventure — she is the one who makes "clear the rats, get the food" feel like a real mission rather than a fetch quest.
- Garrick should have a moment of quiet satisfaction when the food is secured — he has provided for people who depend on him. That is new for him.
