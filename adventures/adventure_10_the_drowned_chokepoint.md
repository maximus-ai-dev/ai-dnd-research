# Adventure 10: The Drowned Chokepoint (Level 10, Act II Finale)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure. Pace the full adventure within ~20 exchanges.
- Cover all three acts in this session. Do not compress excessively — each act should receive meaningful screen time — but do not stretch or pad either.
- The early part of the adventure should focus on descending into the newly drained lower Dwarven Layer, the horror of what the water reveals, and the discovery of the corrupted intersection.
- The middle part should cover the Drowned Juggernaut activating, the first engagement that forces a retreat, and identifying the machine's three weaknesses.
- The late part should be the boss fight — a multi-phase battle using the intersection's infrastructure, the pump system, and everything the party has learned across Act II. The Juggernaut falls. The Giant Capstone is revealed.
- If the players try to rush the Juggernaut, it is too powerful for a direct assault. The intersection's infrastructure must be used against it.
- NEVER let the party descend past the Giant Capstone this adventure. Act III begins below it.
- A D&D session should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.

## Adventure Overview
The pumps are running. The lower Dwarven Layer is draining. And as the water recedes, the Twisted's secrets are exposed.

The Pump Station Operations Manual identified a critical location: the Drowned Chokepoint, the intersection where all water management systems in the Dwarven Layer converge. Every drainage pipe, every pump circuit, every overflow channel — they all meet at this single junction point, located at the deepest navigable level of the dwarven construction, directly above the Giant Capstone that separates dwarven architecture from the Giant foundation below.

The chokepoint has been underwater for centuries. Now it is draining. And what the water reveals is worse than anyone expected.

The Twisted have been using the flooded intersection as a staging area. Protected by the water from above and by the capstone from below, they have built a workshop of corrupted dwarven engineering: biological-mechanical hybrids, organic resin structures, and — at the center of it all — the mining mech Mercer glimpsed. They have modified it into something monstrous. The Drowned Juggernaut: a fifteen-foot dwarven excavation automaton, rebuilt with Twisted bioengineering, designed to drill through the Giant Capstone and breach the containment below.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Reach the Drowned Chokepoint. Destroy the Drowned Juggernaut. Secure the Giant Capstone.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** This is the Act II boss fight. The Juggernaut is a CR 13 creature against a level 10 party — it is meant to be fought with environmental advantages, not raw damage. The intersection's pump infrastructure, the drainage system, and the Juggernaut's own design weaknesses are the keys. This fight should feel like the culmination of everything the party has learned about dwarven engineering, Twisted corruption, and the mountain's containment systems.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 11 and triggers Intermission II.

### Act 1: What the Water Hid

#### The Drained Depths
The lower Dwarven Layer is a revelation. As the water has receded over the past week, corridors and chambers that have been submerged for centuries are now accessible — slick with mineral deposits, coated in bioluminescent algae, and eerily preserved by the cold, mineral-rich water.

The party takes the minecart network's deep mining route south from the transit hub. The rails are corroded but navigable at slow speed. The tunnel descends steeply — sixty feet below the forge level, then eighty, then a hundred. The air is warm and heavy with moisture. Every surface gleams with residual water.

**Key observations during the descent:**

- **The Twisted Infrastructure:** As the party descends, they begin to see modifications to the dwarven tunnels. Rails have been extended with crude but functional additions — new track sections made from salvaged dwarven iron, joined with organic resin. Side tunnels have been widened. Loading platforms have been added at irregular intervals. The Twisted have been building their own logistics network within the dwarven one.

- **The Organic Components:** The deeper they go, the more biological material appears. Black resin coats the walls in patches, forming structures that look almost architectural — buttresses, channels, conduits. In places, the resin has grown into the stone itself, fusing organic and mineral. Mercer identifies these as extensions of the Twisted's bioengineering — living infrastructure that grows and maintains itself.

- **The Smell:** The metallic sweetness is overwhelming here. The Dreamstone radiation is intense enough that even Garrick, who has no arcane sensitivity, can feel it — a pressure behind the eyes, a faint ringing in the ears. Mercer estimates they are within a hundred feet vertically of the Giant Capstone. The Anomaly's influence is no longer indirect. They are approaching its source.

#### The Corrupted Intersection
The deep mining tunnel opens into the Drowned Chokepoint. It is enormous: a domed cavern roughly one hundred feet across and fifty feet high, carved by the dwarves as the convergence point for their entire water management system. Massive pipes — ten feet in diameter — enter the cavern from every direction, some still dripping, others dry. The floor is a network of drainage channels radiating from a central platform like the spokes of a wheel. The platform is elevated fifteen feet above the channel floor, accessible by stone ramps.

The water has drained to knee level in the channels but the central platform is dry. And on it, the Twisted have built their workshop.

**The workshop contains:**
- Mechanical components stripped from other dwarven installations — gears, pistons, hydraulic assemblies
- Organic resin structures serving as workbenches, storage racks, and structural supports
- Tools — dwarven tools modified with biological grips and extensions
- Plans — etched into stone tablets in a script that combines Dwarvish engineering notation with something else, something organic and angular that Mercer cannot read. These are the Twisted's engineering documents.

And at the center of the workshop, partially disassembled for modification, is the Drowned Juggernaut.

#### The Juggernaut
It is larger than Mercer's glimpse suggested. Eighteen feet tall, roughly humanoid, built from dwarven iron and stone in the shape of a mining automaton — a machine designed for boring through solid rock. Its arms end in rotating drill assemblies. Its torso is a furnace, cold now, designed to be powered by elemental fire or geothermal heat. Its legs are pistons and hydraulic rams, built for brute stability.

The Twisted have modified it extensively. Organic resin fills the gaps between iron plates, serving as both adhesive and conduit. Black fluid runs through transparent resin tubes like blood vessels, connecting the mechanical systems to a biological control node mounted where the automaton's head should be — a pulsing, iridescent mass of corrupted tissue that serves as the Juggernaut's brain.

The Juggernaut is not active. It is being charged — thick resin cables connect it to the organic infrastructure throughout the cavern, feeding it energy from the Anomaly's radiation below. Mercer estimates it is at roughly seventy percent charge.

**DC 16 Arcana (Mercer):** The biological control node is the key. Without it, the Juggernaut is inert machinery. The node processes the Anomaly's psychic radiation into control signals — it is, effectively, the Sleeper's dream translated into mechanical instruction. The Juggernaut does not think. It dreams the Sleeper's dream of breaking free.

**DC 15 Investigation (Cora):** The Juggernaut's drill assemblies are aimed at the floor. Specifically, at a section of the central platform that is different from the surrounding stone — darker, denser, inscribed with Giant-era runes that glow faintly even without magical detection. This is the Giant Capstone. The Juggernaut was designed to drill through it.

### Act 2: The Machine Wakes

#### Sabotage Attempt
If the party attempts to destroy the Juggernaut while it is inert, they discover the problem: it is incredibly durable. Dwarven mining automatons were built to withstand tunnel collapses. The iron plating is two inches thick. The organic resin is resistant to fire, acid, and physical impact. Cora estimates that dismantling it with available tools would take six to eight hours of continuous work.

They do not have six hours.

**The activation:** Whether triggered by the party's interference, a timed charge cycle, or a Twisted signal from below, the Juggernaut activates. The biological control node pulses with light. The furnace in its torso ignites — not with clean fire but with a sickly, iridescent heat that smells of Dreamstone. The drill assemblies spin. The hydraulic legs engage, and the Juggernaut stands.

It is the loudest thing the party has ever heard. The drills scream against air. The pistons hammer. The organic components hiss and pulse. It turns toward the party with the slow, inevitable motion of a machine designed to bore through mountains.

> **HOSTILE-ONLY ENCOUNTER — THE JUGGERNAUT ATTACKS CONTINUOUSLY**
> THE JUGGERNAUT ACTIVATES AND CHARGES IMMEDIATELY when the party enters. It is a mining machine with corrupted targeting. It processes the party as rock to be drilled. The environmental solutions (cables, flooding, capstone runes) must be executed UNDER FIRE — the Juggernaut attacks every round during the entire sequence. There is no safe window to work. The party splits attention between surviving and executing the plan.

#### First Engagement
**The Drowned Juggernaut:**
- AC 19 (dwarven iron plating + organic resin reinforcement)
- HP 210
- Speed 20 ft (30 ft charge)
- **Drill Slam:** +11 to hit, 3d10+7 bludgeoning + 2d6 necrotic (corruption damage). On hit, target is pushed 10 ft.
- **Drill Charge:** Moves up to 30 ft in a straight line. All creatures in path: DC 17 Dexterity save or 4d10+7 bludgeoning + knocked prone.
- **Corruption Furnace (Recharge 5-6):** 30 ft cone. DC 16 Constitution save. 6d8 fire + necrotic damage (half on save). Creatures that fail are poisoned for 1 minute (save ends).
- **Reactive Plating:** When hit by a melee attack, attacker takes 1d6 bludgeoning from hydraulic recoil.
- **Control Node Vulnerability:** The biological control node (AC 14, HP 40) is mounted at the top of the torso. It can be targeted separately at disadvantage (it is partially shielded by iron plating). If the node takes 20+ damage in a single round, the Juggernaut is stunned for 1 round as it recalibrates. If the node is destroyed, the Juggernaut enters a berserk state (see Act 3).
- **Damage Resistances:** Resistant to nonmagical bludgeoning, piercing, slashing. Immune to poison, psychic.
- **Damage Vulnerabilities:** Lightning damage bypasses the organic resin's reinforcement (reduces AC to 17). Radiant damage deals double to the control node.

**The fight should be unwinnable in a straight brawl.** The Juggernaut's HP, AC, and damage output are too high for a level 10 party to overwhelm through direct combat. The party should deal some damage, see how little effect it has, and retreat.

**Retreat trigger:** When the Juggernaut uses Drill Charge and nearly kills a party member, or when Cora realizes the organic resin is regenerating minor damage between rounds (it heals 10 HP at the start of each of its turns from the charging cables still connected to it).

#### The Key Insight
During the retreat, Cora notices something critical: the charging cables. The Juggernaut is still connected to the organic infrastructure throughout the cavern. While connected, it regenerates. If the cables are severed, it loses its healing and its power supply begins to deplete.

**Second insight (Mercer, DC 14 Arcana):** The drainage system. The Chokepoint's pipes are massive — ten feet in diameter. If the party can redirect the pump system's output back into the cavern, they can flood the intersection. The Juggernaut is a mining machine, not a submarine. Water in its furnace would cripple it. And the organic components are biological — sustained submersion in cold water would shock the control node.

**Third insight (Garrick, DC 13 Investigation):** The capstone. The Giant runes on the capstone floor are part of the containment network. The pressure valve the party activated in Adventure 5 is connected to this same system. If Mercer can activate the capstone's runes, the containment field would interfere with the Juggernaut's corruption-based systems — its furnace, its control node, and its regeneration all rely on the Anomaly's radiation.

The party has three weapons: sever the cables, flood the chamber, and activate the capstone runes.

### Act 3: Breaking the Machine

#### Preparation
The party has the tools they need — they just need to deploy them:

- **The cables:** There are six major charging cables connecting the Juggernaut to the cavern's organic infrastructure. Each cable is organic resin roughly four inches thick. They can be severed with sustained cutting (DC 13 Strength with an edged weapon, one round per cable) or burned (fire damage, 15 HP per cable). Severing all six cuts the Juggernaut's regeneration and begins a power depletion timer (it loses 10 max HP per round once disconnected).
- **The flood:** The drainage pipes can be reversed using the master control valves in the Chokepoint itself — a control panel on the elevated platform near the workshop. Cora needs two rounds of uninterrupted work at the controls (DC 15 Intelligence with tinker's tools) to reverse the flow. Once reversed, the cavern begins flooding at a rate of one foot per round. At five feet, the Juggernaut's lower mechanisms are compromised (speed halved). At ten feet, the furnace is submerged (Corruption Furnace disabled, 3d6 cold damage per round to the Juggernaut).
- **The capstone:** The Giant runes on the capstone floor can be activated by Mercer using the Giant Blueprint Tablet as a reference (DC 16 Arcana, advantage because he has studied the tablet extensively). Activation creates a containment field in a 30-foot radius around the capstone. Within the field, corruption-based effects are suppressed: the Juggernaut's necrotic damage is removed, its regeneration is halted regardless of cable status, and its control node takes 2d6 radiant damage at the start of each of its turns.

#### The Coordinated Assault
The party re-enters the Chokepoint. The Juggernaut is drilling — its drill assemblies grinding against the capstone floor, the Giant runes flaring with each impact. It has made progress: a groove roughly three feet deep has been carved into the capstone. The runes are damaged but not destroyed. Time is running out.

**Phase 1 — Cable Severance:**
The party must sever the six charging cables before engaging the Juggernaut directly. The cables are distributed around the cavern — two on the north wall, two on the south, two on the east. Garrick is the fastest cutter. Mercer can burn cables with fire spells. Cora can sever them with targeted tool use. The Juggernaut will notice the cable severance after the second cable is cut — it detaches from the capstone and turns to defend its infrastructure.

**Phase 2 — The Flood:**
While Garrick and Mercer deal with the Juggernaut (keeping it busy, dodging drill charges, surviving furnace blasts), Cora reaches the control panel and begins reversing the drainage flow. Two rounds of concentration. If interrupted, she must restart. The Juggernaut will prioritize her if it notices what she is doing.

**Phase 3 — The Capstone:**
With the water rising and the Juggernaut weakening, Mercer breaks away to activate the capstone runes. He needs to reach the center of the capstone — which means getting past the Juggernaut — and spend one round in concentration with the blueprint tablet. On success, the containment field ignites. The Giant runes blaze with golden light. The Juggernaut screams — a mechanical-organic shriek as the containment field attacks its corruption systems.

**Phase 4 — The Kill:**
With cables severed, water rising, and the containment field active, the Juggernaut is vulnerable:
- No regeneration
- Speed halved (flooded)
- Corruption Furnace disabled (submerged)
- AC reduced to 17 (organic resin compromised by water)
- Control node taking 2d6 radiant per round
- Losing 10 max HP per round (power depletion)

The party has a window. Garrick engages in melee — his +1 war-pick and Rune Knight abilities are most effective now. Mercer provides spell support. Cora fires crossbow bolts from the elevated platform.

The fight should feel dangerous but winnable.

**Berserk State (Control Node destroyed):** If the party destroys the control node (40 HP, or accumulated from the radiant damage), the Juggernaut enters berserk mode. It loses its tactical behavior and begins attacking everything randomly — including the cavern walls. It deals double damage but can no longer target specific creatures (roll randomly). Berserk lasts 3 rounds, after which the Juggernaut collapses — a machine without a mind, shuddering to a halt in the rising water.

#### The Capstone Revealed
The Juggernaut falls. The water continues to rise — Cora reverses the drainage again, and the cavern slowly empties. What remains is the Giant Capstone: a section of floor roughly forty feet in diameter, inscribed with runes that are still glowing faintly from Mercer's activation. The Juggernaut's drilling has damaged it — a three-foot groove mars the surface — but the capstone is intact. The containment holds.

Below the capstone, visible through the drill groove if anyone cares to look: darkness. Not the darkness of an unlit room. A darkness that moves, that breathes, that has weight. The Anomaly's domain begins immediately below this stone. The Giant architects built the capstone specifically to seal it.

**Mercer's final analysis:** The capstone is the lid of the containment vessel. Below it is the Giant foundation level — the colossal architecture designed as a cage. Below that, the bedrock. Below that, the Sleeper. The Juggernaut was three feet from breaking through. If it had succeeded, the corruption would have flooded upward through the entire mountain within days.

**The closing beat:** The party stands in the drained intersection, exhausted, surrounded by the wreckage of the Juggernaut and the Twisted's workshop. The capstone runes dim slowly as the containment field fades to standby. From below, through the drill groove, a sound rises: not the rhythmic industrial noise of the Twisted, not the mechanical breathing of the pumps. Something older. A vibration felt in the bones rather than heard. The mountain hums.

Garrick says what they are all thinking: "We need to go down there eventually, don't we?"

Mercer nods. "Not today. But yes. Eventually."

## Intermission II: The Industrial Stronghold
With Act II complete, the party enters a three-month downtime period:
- **Grimhold Specialists (Tier 2 Workforce):** Reyna sends engineers, miners, and a smith. The fortress workforce doubles in skill and capability. Complex construction projects are now possible.
- **Giant Cistern Drainage:** Using the pump system, Cora directs the drainage of the Giant Cisterns — massive water reserves in the Giant foundation level that can be accessed through sealed conduits in the capstone. The fortress achieves water independence from Grimhold.
- **Custom Forging:** With the Cold Forge at full capacity (the pump system provides cooling water), the forge can now work adamantine. Cora can begin crafting from the 40 lbs of adamantine ore. One suit of adamantine armor or two adamantine weapons during this intermission.
- **Capstone Repair:** Mercer and the Grimhold engineers patch the drill groove in the capstone using a combination of dwarven masonry and Giant-era rune restoration (partial — full restoration requires materials from the Giant level).
- **Naming the Stronghold:** With Grimhold engineers arriving and the fortress becoming a permanent installation, Reyna asks the party what to call this place on the Guild's official maps. The party names their stronghold. This is their home now.

## Key Items
- **Adamantine Chassis Plates** — salvaged from the Juggernaut's torso plating. Enough for one suit of half plate (AC 15 + Dex mod, max 2). Adamantine: critical hits against the wearer become normal hits. Garrick should wear this.
- **Dwarven Master Forge Key** — a complex iron key found in the Juggernaut's core mechanism. It unlocks the master forge controls at the Cold Forge, enabling full-capacity operation (required for adamantine working). Without the pump system providing coolant, the key was useless. Now it completes the forge.
- **Dreamstone Power Cell** — the Juggernaut's power source, a fist-sized sphere of crystallized Dreamstone encased in lead. Contains enormous energy. Cora can install it in the fortress's defensive systems (powers a magical alarm and barrier for the fortress entrance) or Mercer can study it (permanent advantage on Arcana checks related to the Anomaly, but requires weekly Wisdom saves DC 14 to avoid nightmares that reveal fragments of the Sleeper's dreams — useful intelligence but psychically disturbing).
- **Twisted Engineering Schematics** — the stone tablets from the workshop. Mercer can partially translate them over time. They reveal the Twisted's broader plan: the Juggernaut was one of several projects. Others include the fleshy amplifiers (broadcasting the Sleeper's dreams upward) and a larger drill positioned deeper in the Giant foundation. The containment is being attacked from multiple angles.
- **3,000 gp** in salvageable dwarven mechanical components, raw materials, and Juggernaut parts.

## Key NPCs
- **Reyna Steelforge** — arrives at the fortress personally for the first time during Intermission II, with a team of Grimhold's best engineers. Her reaction to the Juggernaut wreckage and the capstone: "You have been sitting on the most important archaeological site in recorded history, and you have been using it as a basement. I am moving in." She establishes a permanent Grimhold presence at the fortress. This changes the settlement's character — it is no longer a bandit camp. It is becoming a research outpost.
- **Ignathar (Fire Elemental)** — with the Master Forge Key and the cooling system online, the forge reaches full capacity for the first time in over a thousand years. If the party bargained with Ignathar, it is pleased: "The forge burns true. I had forgotten the feeling." It begins producing heat at a level that warms the entire Dwarven Layer. If the party bound it, the binding strain increases with the forge's output — Cora must reinforce the binding during Intermission II.
- **The Twisted Overseer** — did not appear during this adventure. Its absence is deliberate. The Twisted are regrouping. Their primary instrument has been destroyed, their flooded staging area drained, their supply lines cut. They will respond — but not yet. Act III is their counterattack.

## DM Notes
- The Juggernaut fight should feel cinematic. Describe the drills screaming, the water rising, the Giant runes blazing. This is the set piece of Act II — every environmental system the party has learned to use (the forge, the pumps, the pressure valves, the containment network) comes together.
- The three-phase weakness system ensures the fight is a puzzle, not a damage race. The party cannot brute-force 210 HP at level 10. They win by being smart.
- The drill groove in the capstone is a permanent reminder of how close the Twisted came. Every time the party passes through the Chokepoint in future adventures, they see the scar. It should feel sobering.
- Intermission II fundamentally changes the campaign's scale. The party goes from managing a bandit camp to running a research outpost with professional engineers and a fully operational dwarven forge. Their resources are now industrial, not survival-level.
- Reyna's arrival is a tonal shift. She is no longer a quest-giver in a distant town. She is an ally with her own agenda — understanding the mountain's history. Her goals align with the party's, but she will push for exploration and documentation where the party might prefer caution. She is a check on "seal everything and go home" thinking.
- Act III begins below the capstone. The party has secured everything above it. Now they must enter the Giant foundation level — where the scale changes, the hazards change, and the Sleeper's influence is no longer filtered through layers of stone and lead.
