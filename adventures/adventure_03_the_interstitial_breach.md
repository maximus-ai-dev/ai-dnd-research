# Adventure 3: The Interstitial Breach (Level 3)

## PACING RULES (CRITICAL — DM MUST FOLLOW)
- This is a SINGLE SESSION adventure with approximately 20 exchanges. The DM should pace the full adventure within this one session, covering all three acts.
- The adventure has three acts. Give each act enough breathing room — do not rush through Act 1 to get to Act 3. Each act should feel like a chapter of a book, not a summary of one.
- If the players try to rush ahead, introduce complications: structural instability, workers ignoring orders, the Psychic Guardian's Stone-Tenders behaving strangely, secondary breaches in other rooms.
- Only use events, NPCs, and locations described in this adventure file. Do not invent major plot elements not in the source material.
- **CRITICAL:** The entities behind the wall are THREE HUMAN WARDENS in magical stasis. They are NOT undead, NOT monsters, NOT cosmic entities. They are confused, disoriented soldiers following two-hundred-year-old orders. Do NOT invent additional creatures, psychic phenomena, reality-warping effects, dreamscapes, heartstone artifacts, or any entity not described in this file. The interstitial space is a CORRIDOR with three ALCOVES. That is all.

---

## Adventure Overview
The repair contract with the Psychic Guardian is in progress. Five workers from Brenna's crew — supervised by Stone-Tenders — are patching cracked support pillars and compromised ventilation seals in the Salt-Vaults preparation kitchen. It is careful, exhausting work. On the third day of repairs, one of the workers swings a mattock into a section of wall that sounds hollow. The wall is thin — thinner than it should be. One more swing and it gives way.

Behind it: a sealed corridor sealed from the other side. The air that rushes out is cold and stale and old in a way that is different from the Iron Warlords' dust. It carries a faint sweetness — preserved organic matter, long dormant. And from somewhere in the dark beyond, a low rhythmic sound begins. Like breathing. Like something waking up.

The party must investigate and contain the breach before whatever is on the other side reaches the workforce or the food stores. They cannot seal it with stone — the Psychic Guardian's contract requires the ventilation system remain intact, and the breached wall is load-bearing. They will need another solution.

**Adventure Goal (to be achieved in this session):** Investigate and contain the breach. Keep the workforce casualties to zero. Do not damage the Salt-Vaults' structural integrity.

**IMPORTANT FOR DM:** The goal is NOT simply to kill everything on the other side. The party has 30 workers who are not fighters. Worker safety is the primary constraint. A solution that kills the threat but costs five workers is a failure. A solution that contains the threat and loses nobody is a success. The Wardens are PEOPLE — confused, scared, operating on obsolete orders. They deserve to be treated as characters, not monsters.

**Milestone:** Completing this adventure advances the party to Level 4.

### Act 1: The Repair Work

#### The Setup
The Salt-Vaults preparation kitchen is now a worksite. Five of Brenna's most capable workers — led by Rolf — are following the instructions the Stone-Tenders laid out: mixing clay-limestone-salt mortar from the recipe on the wall, applying it to cracked support pillars, replacing rotted ventilation membrane with flattened iron scavenged from broken barrel hoops. The Stone-Tenders observe, clicking approval or disapproval in binary pulses.

The work is going well. Day one and two passed without incident. Brenna reports the food has been hauled up — thirty people are eating again. The mood in the fortress is better than it has been since the party arrived.

**DM atmosphere guidance:** Start this adventure SLOW. The repair work is mundane, methodical, and important. Give the party time to interact with the workforce, observe the Stone-Tenders' behavior, and feel the normalcy of what they've built. This calm is what makes the breach terrifying — it interrupts something that was finally working.

**Play the domestic beats:**
- Rolf supervises quietly, correcting a worker's mortar application with a wordless adjustment of their trowel angle.
- Cora reviews Brenna's supply ledger and finds two discrepancies. They argue about it — professionally, not personally.
- Mercer studies the Stone-Tenders with academic fascination, sketching their clicking patterns and trying to map their communication.
- Garrick stands watch at the corridor junction because that's what he does — he watches.

**Key NPCs active this adventure:**
- **Rolf** — quiet, competent, not given to panic. He will be the one who makes the breach. He feels guilty about it and says so plainly: "Should've tested the wall first. That one's on me."
- **Brenna** — upstairs managing the fortress. She is not present at the breach but will need to be told. Her reaction matters: she trusts the party, but she is responsible for her people.
- **The Psychic Guardian** — communicates only through Mercer, and only when the Stone-Tenders are disturbed. It is not hostile, but it is not patient. The contract has terms.

#### The Breach
On the third day, Rolf is chipping mortar from a ventilation channel in the eastern wall of the preparation kitchen — a section the Stone-Tenders indicated needed clearing. His mattock swings true three times, then punches through. The wall is only four inches thick here. The air pressure differential pulls a whistle of cold air through the hole.

Rolf freezes. He does not expand the hole. He comes to find the party.

**Play this moment carefully.** Rolf is not panicking. He is a professional reporting a problem: "Hit hollow. Four inches of stone where there should be six feet. Something's on the other side. Air's moving." He does not speculate. He waits for the party to decide.

When the party investigates, Mercer can confirm: the hollow section is not original Iron Warlords construction. It was added later — much later — from the other side. Someone built a false wall to conceal this passage from anyone in the preparation kitchen. The mortar is a different composition: no salt, heavy on lime, with trace amounts of a mineral that glows faint blue-white under Detect Magic. Preservation mortar. The same type used to seal the Salt-Vaults' stone crocks.

**The decision the party faces:** They can try to reseal the hole quietly and pretend it didn't happen. Or they can expand it carefully and look. The Stone-Tenders' reaction to the breach is immediate and unambiguous — they position themselves between the hole and the silo, clicking rapidly. They are not attacking. They are guarding.

When Mercer reaches out to the Psychic Guardian, it communicates urgency: *Sealed. Old. Not ours. Do not let it spread.*

#### What Is Behind the Wall
This is the interstitial space — a corridor about eight feet wide and forty feet long, running parallel to the preparation kitchen. It was built by the Secretive Brotherhood (the human-era mage faction who occupied the level above the Iron Warlords' layer) as a concealed access route to their sanctum below. They sealed it from the Iron Warlords' side using preservation mortar when they abandoned it, leaving behind what they could not take.

What they left: three Brotherhood Wardens — human guards who volunteered for a preservation ritual, sealed in stasis within alcoves in the corridor walls. They are not undead. They are not dead. They are preserved-living, in a state of suspended animation maintained by the same residual transmutation aura that mutated the rats. The breathing sound is them, very slowly, beginning to register that their dormancy has ended.

**CRITICAL: The interstitial space is JUST A CORRIDOR. Forty feet long, eight feet wide, three alcoves with three preserved humans. There are no additional creatures, no psychic phenomena, no reality-warping effects, no stairways to other dimensions, no dream sequences, and no cosmic revelations. A corridor. Three alcoves. Three sleeping soldiers. That is the entire space.**

The party cannot see any of this at first — the hole is too small and it is dark on the other side. What they can see, if they put a light source through: stone floor, a narrow corridor, and the edge of an alcove with something upright inside it that might be a suit of armor. Or something wearing armor.

### Act 2: The Wardens Waken

#### The Opening
The party must decide how to handle the breach before the repair workers return in the morning. Option A: expand the hole enough to enter and investigate before anything comes through. Option B: attempt to re-seal it immediately. Option C: set a watch and see what happens.

Whatever they choose, by mid-morning the decision is made for them: the first Warden emerges.

**The Wardens** were soldiers of a Brotherhood garrison, preserved in magical stasis for approximately two hundred years. They are not hostile by nature — they are disoriented, deeply confused, and operating on the last orders they received before stasis: *guard the passage, allow no unauthorized access to the sanctum below.* They do not know their Brotherhood is gone. They do not know two centuries have passed. They are dangerous not because they are evil but because they will follow their orders until someone convinces them otherwise — or stops them by force.

**Name these characters correctly — this is non-negotiable:**
- **Warden Aldric** — the oldest, most articulate, most suspicious. He is the first to wake.
- **Warden Petra** — quick to act, slow to trust. She is the one who shoots Rolf.
- **Warden Cassen** — the third, still half-dormant. He has had the most time to process and is the easiest to reason with.
**Do NOT rename these characters. Do NOT substitute "Brother Valen" or "Brother Arin" or any other names. They are Aldric, Petra, and Cassen. Period.**

**Warden stat notes for DM (approximate Challenge 2 each):**
- Medium humanoid, preserved by magic — they do not bleed normally, their wounds close slowly
- Equipped with Brotherhood-issue equipment: ring mail, longsword, hand crossbow with six bolts
- Each Warden carries a brass insignia with the Brotherhood's symbol — a closed eye within a triangle
- They speak archaic Common — Mercer can understand them with effort
- They respond to Brotherhood command phrases — but no one knows what those are yet

#### The Workforce Problem
The repair workers are in the kitchen when the first Warden comes through. Rolf keeps his people calm — barely. Two workers bolt for the stairs. One freezes. The Wardens, upon encountering people in the preparation kitchen, do not attack immediately — they issue a challenge in archaic Common: *"Identify yourself to the Brotherhood or stand aside."*

This is the party's window. If they can communicate before violence breaks out, they may be able to negotiate. If the workers panic and scatter into the lower corridor where other Wardens are stirring, it becomes a chase.

**The key tension this adventure:** Worker safety vs. investigation. The party cannot do both at once. Someone needs to manage the panicking workers while someone else talks to (or fights) the Wardens.

#### Key Scene: The First Negotiation
**Warden Aldric** is confused but rational. He recognizes that significant time has passed — the preparation kitchen smells different, the equipment is wrong, the Stone-Tenders are present (which he finds alarming). He does not know the Brotherhood is gone but begins to suspect it when the party cannot name a single Brotherhood officer or command phrase. He defaults to protocol: he will not allow access to the sanctum passage and will detain anyone who tries to pass.

**Give Aldric personality.** He is not a robot executing orders. He is a man who went to sleep two hundred years ago and woke up to a world he doesn't recognize. His stoicism is a mask for profound disorientation. When he speaks, his voice is steady but his hands are not. When Mercer mentions the passage of time — "Sir, the Brotherhood has been gone for two centuries" — Aldric goes very still. He does not argue. He processes. Then: *"That... changes the terms of engagement. But the orders stand until I receive countermanding authority."*

Mercer's Giant and Dwarven languages are useless here, but his History and Arcana give him context: the Brotherhood was a secretive order of arcane scholars who occupied the level above the Iron Warlords' ruins roughly 150-200 years ago. They were known to use preservation magic on their guards. They are believed to have abandoned this site.

#### Complications
- The second Warden — **Petra** — emerges from the corridor while the party is negotiating with Aldric. She does not wait for explanations — she sees non-Brotherhood personnel in a restricted area and draws her crossbow.
- **Petra shoots Rolf.** Crossbow bolt in the shoulder. Not life-threatening, but it escalates everything. Rolf doesn't go down — he staggers, grabs the bolt, and looks at the party with an expression that says "handle this."
- One of the repair workers, panicking, runs into the corridor to escape and nearly reaches the third alcove. If **Cassen** (the third Warden) wakes while an untrained worker is directly in front of him, the worker will almost certainly be injured.
- The Stone-Tenders refuse to enter the corridor. Their patrol routes end at the preparation kitchen wall. They will guard the silo and the kitchen but cannot help beyond.

**Combat if it erupts:** Two Wardens vs. the party while also protecting workers. Garrick is the obvious frontline but the room is constrained — the corridor is eight feet wide. Cora's Faerie Fire is valuable here (the Wardens' preservation gives them odd regeneration). Mercer can try to talk while Garrick and Cora hold the line.

**Garrick's moment:** When Petra shoots Rolf, Garrick's instinct is to fight. He has to choose: follow his instincts (violence) or follow the party's plan (negotiation). This is a character moment. The old Garrick — the enforcer — would have put Petra down before she could reload. The new Garrick — the one who is building something here — should hesitate. Let the player decide.

#### Act 2 Transition
The first two Wardens are either subdued (unconscious, not dead — the party should realize these are living people) or a tense standoff exists, and the third Warden — **Cassen** — is still dormant in his alcove. Rolf has a crossbow bolt in his shoulder. The workers are shaken but alive.

**The emotional beat:** The preparation kitchen is a mess — overturned mortar buckets, scattered tools, a blood smear where Rolf was hit. Through the hole in the wall, the corridor is visible in torchlight: two empty alcoves, one still occupied. The figure in the third alcove — **Cassen** — is breathing faster now. Whatever the party is going to do, they have until morning before the third one wakes.

### Act 3: The Resolution

#### The State of Things
Two Wardens are subdued or in standoff. The third — **Cassen** — has not yet woken fully. The party knows:
- These are living humans, not undead
- They are operating on two-hundred-year-old orders
- They are guarding a passage to a sanctum below — the Secretive Brotherhood's sanctum
- They will not stand down without a Brotherhood command phrase or proof that the Brotherhood is dissolved

#### The Path to Resolution
There are several ways to resolve this:

**Option A: Negotiate a new agreement.** If Mercer can find Brotherhood documentation — there are records in the Wardens' alcoves, sealed in preservation tubes — he can piece together enough context to convince them their order is gone and their duty is complete. This requires a successful Arcana and History check over 1-2 hours while the other two party members maintain the standoff. Success means the Wardens stand down voluntarily. They become a source of information about the sanctum below and can be offered a place in the workforce as guards — they have nowhere else to go.

**Option B: Locate the command phrase.** One of the preservation tubes in the alcoves contains a partial operations log. A careful read (Investigation check, Mercer only) reveals the Brotherhood's standard dismissal phrase in archaic Common: *"The eye closes. The watch is ended."* Speaking this phrase causes the Wardens to enter a formal stand-down posture. They will not fight. They will not help. They will simply wait.

**Option C: Force.** The party can subdue all three Wardens. This is possible but costs resources — each Warden is a capable fighter and their preservation means they do not go down easily. Risk of worker casualties goes up significantly if combat spills into the kitchen. This is the "bad ending" path — it works but leaves questions unanswered and a passage to the sanctum below that is now unguarded.

**Give the resolution weight.** This is not a puzzle to solve — it is a moral question. The Wardens are people out of time. They volunteered for stasis expecting to be woken by their commanders. Instead they wake to strangers in their home. Aldric, if talked to honestly, will ask: *"If the Brotherhood is gone... then what were we guarding? What was it for?"* There is no good answer.

#### The Sanctum Passage
At the end of the forty-foot corridor, a stone door bears the Brotherhood's closed-eye symbol and a message in archaic Common: *"The Grand Archive. Entry by rank only. The Amalgamation watches."*

Mercer can read this. He should recognize "Amalgamation" as a Brotherhood term for a bound construct — typically a flesh-golem variant used as a guardian. The door is sealed with a Brotherhood arcane lock that the Wardens' insignias could bypass.

**HARD STOP — THIS ADVENTURE ENDS AT THE DOOR. DO NOT OPEN IT.**
The party does NOT enter the sanctum this adventure. This is absolute. If the party attempts to open the door, the Wardens physically block them and refuse: *"Entry requires Commander authorization. We do not grant it. Not yet. Not until we know what we are guarding again."* The adventure ends with the party standing at the sealed door, holding three brass insignias, knowing what waits beyond. Adventure 4 is for that. End the adventure here. If you find yourself writing about the door opening or the archive beyond, STOP and write the closing beat instead.

#### The Workforce Outcome
After resolution, the party must return to Brenna with a full account. Rolf is alive and recovering. The two other workers who bolted made it upstairs safely. No one died — if the party played it right. Brenna's reaction:

If zero casualties: cautious relief. She is not happy about the breach or the danger, but she trusts the party made the best call. "Next time, maybe tell me before it gets to crossbow bolts."

If one or more workers seriously injured: Brenna is angry. Not unreasonably. "You brought thirty people into this mountain. That means something." The workforce morale drops — some workers start asking if the deal is worth it.

#### The Psychic Guardian's Assessment
When Mercer reports the outcome to the Psychic Guardian through the Stone-Tenders, it communicates something new: it knew the Brotherhood passage was there. It sealed the preparation kitchen wall from its side as a secondary barrier. It did not expect the party's workers to remove its seal. It is not angry — it acknowledges the party acted in good faith. But it adds a new term to their agreement: the Brotherhood passage must be re-sealed before the repair contract is considered complete. The party now has the Wardens (if cooperative), their insignias, and the knowledge of what lies below.

#### Closing Beat
The repairs are completed. The breach is re-sealed — either with the Wardens' cooperation (they help repair the wall, understanding the logic) or by force with new mortar. The contract with the Psychic Guardian is fulfilled. The Salt-Vaults hum with restored preservation magic.

The Wardens, if cooperative, take up residence in the preparation kitchen as self-appointed guards. They are strange and formal and two hundred years out of time, but they are not dangerous. They call Garrick "the large one" and Mercer "the scholar" and Cora "the ledger-keeper." They do not understand the Stone-Tenders but are willing to coexist.

On Mercer's table: three brass insignias with the closed-eye symbol, a partial Brotherhood operations log, and the knowledge of a locked door forty feet below the Salt-Vaults with the words: *The Amalgamation watches.*

Adventure 4 awaits.

## Key Items and Information
- **Three Brotherhood Insignias** — brass, closed-eye symbol. Function as keys to the Brotherhood sanctum door.
- **Partial Operations Log** — archaic Common, damaged. Contains the dismissal phrase and partial roster of Brotherhood personnel. Mercer can extract more information with time.
- **The Dismissal Phrase** — *"The eye closes. The watch is ended."* Causes Wardens to stand down.
- **Brotherhood Sanctum Door** — sealed arcane lock. Insignias bypass it. Do not open this adventure.
- **The Amalgamation** — referenced but not encountered. A bound construct. Guardian of the Grand Archive. This is the Act I finale boss.

## Key NPCs
- **Warden Aldric** — oldest of the three, most articulate, most suspicious. If any Warden can be persuaded, it is him. He speaks carefully and formally. He is afraid and hiding it well.
- **Warden Petra** — the one who shoots Rolf. Quick to act, slow to trust. Not cruel, just trained. She follows protocol because protocol is all she has left.
- **Warden Cassen** — the third, still half-dormant at session's end. The easiest to reason with because he has had the most time to process the situation. When he finally speaks, he is the most human of the three — he asks simple questions: "How long? Is anyone left? What happened to the Commander?"
- **Rolf** — takes a crossbow bolt but survives. Doesn't blame anyone. "Walls that hollow should have been suspicious." This is the most words he's said in the entire campaign.

## DM Notes
- The Wardens should feel like PEOPLE, not monsters. They are confused and afraid under the stoicism. Two hundred years of stasis ends and the world they knew is gone. Give them moments of humanity — Aldric touching the wall and finding it unfamiliar, Petra looking at the Stone-Tenders with genuine bewilderment, Cassen asking "Is it still winter?"
- The key tension is worker safety vs. curiosity about the sanctum below. The party will want to go through that door. They should not — not yet. Adventure 4 is for that.
- Garrick's enforcer background gives him instinctive understanding of the Wardens' situation — soldiers following orders they no longer understand. This is worth leaning on.
- Cora's ledger-brain is useful here: she can calculate exactly what the workforce can and cannot absorb in terms of morale hits.
- Do NOT reveal "The Amalgamation" beyond the name on the door. Mystery is the point. The party should leave this adventure knowing only: it is there, it is a construct, and the door can be opened with the insignias. Nothing more.
- Do NOT invent additional content beyond what is in this file. No Heartstone artifacts, no Descent of Dreams, no reality-warping, no chase sequences through impossible architecture. The interstitial space is a corridor with three alcoves. The sanctum door is at the end. That is the entire geography.
- HARD STOP: This adventure ends at the sanctum door. The door does not open. The Amalgamation does not appear. The archive is not described. If you find yourself writing any of those things, stop and write the closing beat instead.
